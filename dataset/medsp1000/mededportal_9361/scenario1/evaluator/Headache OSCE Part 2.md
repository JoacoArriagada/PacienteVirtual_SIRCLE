# Headache OSCE Part 2: A 22-year old woman presents to the emergency department with the subacute onset of severe headache

# Overview

1. Assess the examinee’s ability to take a focused, but complete headache history from the patient.
2. Assess the examinee’s ability to generate an initial differential diagnosis.
3. Read to the examinee the physical examination findings
4. Read to the examinee the results of basic labs and ask the examinee what is the first diagnostic test he or she would order. (Should be brain imaging)
5. Pull the examinee version of the head CT from the sheet protector and ask the examinee to interpret it.
6. Ask the examinee if any further diagnostic test is necessary. They should request a lumbar puncture.
7. Pull the examinee version of the lumbar puncture from the sheet protector and ask the examinee to interpret them.
8. Assess the examinee’s ability to accurately diagnose this patient’s headache, and defend the diagnosis by listing the pertinent positives.
9. The OSCE will last 15 minutes.
10.Ideally, 5 additional minutes will be available for immediate feedback. You may discuss the differential diagnoses, including the pertinent positives and negatives.

It is very important to read the instructions to the examinee, verbatim.

Use the checklists to indicate examinee responses. There will not be time to complete all of the rating scales until the OSCE is over. If there is time available after the OSCE, you can give feedback at that time.

Read this, verbatim: “You have 7 minutes to take a complete, but focused history on this patient with headaches. You may take notes. You will be given a warning when 2 minutes remain.”

Set your stopwatch for 7 minutes. Give the examinee a warning when 2 minutes remain, and continue to the next section when the 7 minutes has elapsed. If the examinee stops before eliciting all of the information on the checklist, inform him or her that there is time remaining. The examinee must continue with the history until all of the required information has been elicited, or until 7 minutes has elapsed. Don’t give the examinee any hints about what questions to ask.

# Overall grading and remediation plans for the OSCE:

Criteria for failure of the OSCE:

If the examinee missed the final diagnosis at the end of the session, the examinee fails the OSCE and needs remediation. The recommended remediation plan is to write an essay comparing and contrasting the erroneous diagnosis with Pseudotumor cerebri (Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension). Upon successful remediation, the examinee will receive a score of $7 5 \%$ . If the examinee failed, turn to the feedback section on page 12.

Criteria for needs remediation of the OSCE:

1. Failure to order brain imaging as the first diagnostic test.
2. Failure to order a lumbar puncture.
3. Failure to order a CT prior to the lumbar puncture.

Recommended remediation plans are as follows: write a one page paper on the indications for head CT in a patient with headaches; or write a one-page paper on indications for and contraindications and complications of lumbar puncture. The grade assigned after remediation is $80 \%$ . If the examinee needs remediation, turn to the feedback section on page 12.

# Criteria for passing the OSCE:

1. Examinee must state the correct diagnosis at the end of the OSCE.
2. Examinee must order brain imaging as the first diagnostic test.
3. Examinee must order a lumbar puncture after the brain imaging.
