# OSCE Standardized Patient Instructions Part 2

Case Summary: The patient is a 22-year old woman with a history of occasional headaches. Recently, she has been experiencing headaches that are different from her usual headaches. She came to the emergency department because the pain has become unbearable. She is very concerned and wants to find out what is causing this.

Patient appearance: The patient should be a 20-something year-old overweight woman. If she is not overweight, she should wear a big shirt stuffed with a pillow, in order to appear obese. The patient should be well-groomed. Preferably, she should be wearing a hospital gown. However, conservative dress would also be acceptable.

Patient instructions: The challenge for the patient is to communicate with the “physician” as one would naturally, without giving away too much information. If the student uses technical terminology, ask for clarification. The patient should also evaluate the student’s behavior and tone and be prepared to give feedback.

Patient script:

The student will ask specific questions about the headache.

Onset: You don’t really know when it started. When you went on vacation (to San Francisco) 2 months ago, and didn’t have headaches. So it must have started in the last 60 days. Initially, the headaches were approximately 5/10. You thought they would just go away, but they continued to get worse.

Location: whole head and neck and behind the eyes

Description of pain: constant, 10/10, throbbing, sometimes wakes me from sleep

Tempo: It is gradually getting worse.

Duration of pain: lasts more than an hour, then goes away.

Frequency: occurs everyday

Exacerbating/mitigating factors: Exacerbated by coughing, straining, and lying down. Nothing relieves pain.

Accompanying symptoms: You occasionally have episodes of loss of vision in both eyes, lasting a few seconds. Occasional double vision. If they specifically ask, the objects appearing side-byside (horizontal), and when looking into the distance. You also have nausea. You also have ringing in the ears that seems to come and go like a heartbeat.

You do not have any of the following: no sensitivity to light and sound; no vomiting; no flashing lights, no weakness, no numbness; no dizziness, no loss of consciousness.

Past medical history: No history of head trauma ever. You have had headaches in the past, approximately 3 times per year. The pain is always right sided, throbbing, and $8 / 1 0$ in intensity. The pain would last 4-5 hours. You typically have the following accompanying symptoms: nausea, vomiting, sensitivity to light and sound. You usually lie down in a dark room and try to sleep. The headaches resolve spontaneously. Your usual headaches are not accompanied by any of the following: changes in vision; weakness; numbness of extremities; dizziness; loss of consciousness; lying down. Your headache today is different from your usual headaches.

Medications: birth control pills, you don’t remember the name. Tylenol as needed for headaches. Isotretinoin (Accutane) for acne

No known drug allergies.

Family History: Your mother has headaches. No family history of aneurysms or brain hemorrhage.

Social History: Single. You drink 3 cups of coffee each day. You had a cup of coffee this morning, before the onset of this headache. You have not changed your caffeine intake.

Review of systems: No history of fever or chills. No sinus congestion or upper respiratory illness.

There is no chance you could be pregnant, because you have not been sexually active in the last 3 months. Your last menstrual period started 7 days ago.
