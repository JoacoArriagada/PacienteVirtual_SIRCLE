# Headache OSCE Part 2: A 22-year old woman presents to the emergency department with the subacute onset of severe headache

# Overview

Read this, verbatim: “You have 7 minutes to take a complete, but focused history on this patient with headaches. You may take notes. You will be given a warning when 2 minutes remain.”

Set your stopwatch for 7 minutes. Give the examinee a warning when 2 minutes remain, and continue to the next section when the 7 minutes has elapsed. If the examinee stops before eliciting all of the information on the checklist, inform him or her that there is time remaining. The examinee must continue with the history until all of the required information has been elicited, or until 7 minutes has elapsed. Don’t give the examinee any hints about what questions to ask.

Read this to the examinee, verbatim: “You examine the patient. You are not sure, but she may have some mild blurring of the optic disc margins, bilaterally. Grossly, her visual fields seem a little constricted. You cannot detect diplopia at this time. Visual acuity is normal. Otherwise, the complete neurological and head and neck exam is normal and non-focal. You have 90 seconds to think about the differential diagnosis. You will then state the 4 most likely diagnoses, from most likely to least likely. Don’t list the pertinent positives and negatives at this time.” Don’t give any hints.

Set your timer for 2 minutes. After 90 seconds, the examinee must start listing the diagnoses. This section must be completed within 2 minutes.

Read this, verbatim “Basic labs were already ordered, including CBC, platelets, chem 7, PT, PTT, and INR. All were normal. Urine pregnancy test was also negative. What is the first test you would order on this patient?” The examinee should respond immediately. If examinee does not respond, prompt him or her.

Regardless of what the examinee orders, read this verbatim $" \mathsf { A }$ head CT was performed on this patient during the last shift.” Remove the CT scan from the page protector and hand it to the examinee.

Read this, verbatim: “Please interpret this image. You may think aloud, and then state your final interpretation, and whether the study is normal or abnormal.”

Set your stopwatch for 1 minute. This section must be completed within that time.

Regardless of the examinee’s interpretation, read the following: “The head CT was read by the neuro-radiologist as normal. An MRI and magnetic resonance venogram were also performed, and venous sinus thrombosis was excluded. Is there any other diagnostic test you would like to order at this time, or is the evaluation complete?” The examinee must respond immediately. If examinee hesitates, prompt examinee.

ASKS FOR Multiple TESTS If the examinee wants to order more than one test, read this. “You may order only one more test. What would you like to order?”

Whether the examinee ordered a lumbar puncture or not, read this, verbatim: “A lumbar puncture was performed on this patient. You may think out loud, and then state your final interpretation.” Hand the lumbar puncture results to the examinee. No hints.

Set your stopwatch for one minute, as this segment must be completed within that time period. If the examinee hesitates, prompt him or her.

Read this to the examinee, verbatim: “You may take a moment to think about your final diagnosis, including the pertinent positives. As soon as you are ready, please state the final diagnosis, and list the pertinent positives. Do not list any pertinent negatives.”

Set your stopwatch for 3 minutes. Encourage the examinee to keep listing pertinent positives, but end the session after 3 minutes. You can give the following prompts regarding the pertinent positives. “Are there any other clinical features that support the diagnosis?” Or, “Are there any diagnostic evaluations that support the diagnosis?” and “Please continue.”
