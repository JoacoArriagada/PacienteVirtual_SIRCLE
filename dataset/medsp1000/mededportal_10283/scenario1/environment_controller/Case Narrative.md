1. **TITLE OF CASE:** Toxic Ingestion/Ethylene Glycol Ingestion
2. **ENVIRONMENT:**

**A. Lab set up** - emergency department bed in simulation center or true emergency department.

**B. Manikin set up** - The manikin will be a clothed adult size manikin. At the beginning of the simulation, there are no monitor leads on the patient, no IV access has been obtained, and the patient is not on oxygen.

**C. Props** - Available for use will be a heart monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, and pulse oximeter. Supplemental oxygen by nasal cannula and face mask will be available. There will be a medicine cart or tray with a full complement of vasoactive agents, ACLS medications, medicines necessary for sedation, rapid sequence intubation, and analgesia. In addition, a fully stocked code cart with defibrillator will be available for use, along with a selection of direct laryngoscopes and intubation supplies, including suction, bag valve mask, bougie, backup airway devices, end-tidal carbon dioxide detector, etc.

**D. Audiovisual** - (See Appendix A) Available for review when asked for by participants will be chest x-rays and laboratory studies.

**E. Distracters** - Distracters can be added at the director's discretion. Choices could include obtrusive or difficult family members or Nephrology consult challenging the need for dialysis. However, as written, there is no need for distractors as the goal is simply to manage a medically complex patient.

1. **ACTORS:**
   1. Roles can vary depending on the number of participants in the simulation session and on potential actor availability. This case can be implemented using a single participant and one facilitator/operator who can provide oral feedback via overhead audio and play additional historical or consultant roles via voice only. Realism can be enhanced by using physical actors, such as nursing staff members, but these are not required to successfully implement this case.
   2. Roles may be played by resident or faculty physicians, nurses, or medical students.
   3. Actions for the roles will be as follows:
      1. Primary physician: The main scenario participant will act as the primary physician and do the primary evaluation of the patient to include obtaining a history, conducting a physical exam, and ordering any necessary medications or other interventions. The primary physician may perform any needed procedures, or may delegate these to other providers if multiple participants are present.
      2. Secondary physicians: Other participants in the scenario will serve as collaborators, assistants for any necessary procedures, and consultants.
      3. Nursing staff: The role of the nurse will be to administer medications, verify orders, and perform other tasks as directed by the physicians. The nurse may also make observations as needed to stimulate case progression.
2. **CASE NARRATIVE:**
3. **Scenario Background**
   1. Chief Complaint: Altered mental status (AMS)
   2. Triage Nursing Note: Patient's family called EMS after finding the patient confused at home.
   3. Vital Signs: T 98.4**°**F (rectal), HR 110 bpm, RR 24/min, BP 108/62 mmHg, Sats 100% on RA
   4. Past Medical History: Diabetes
   5. Past Surgical History: none
   6. Medications: Metformin
   7. Allergies: No known allergies
   8. Family and Social History: Father with history of alcohol abuse
4. **Initial Scenario Conditions**
   1. **History given by family/EMS:** The patient was last seen normal 18 hours ago. Family reports that the patient has recently developed depression after retirement and has been under the care of a psychiatrist. However, the patient still expressed loss of interest in activities and seemed despondent. Family called to check on her, and when she didn't answer the phone, drove to her house. The patient was found minimally responsive in bed. No empty medication bottles were found and there was no evidence of trauma or assault.
   2. **Circumstances at symptom onset:** The patient saw a psychiatrist approximately 2 weeks ago for depression, and was started on an unknown antidepressant.
   3. **Associated symptoms (given by system as review of system is completed):**

Review of systems is unobtainable secondary to altered mental status.

* 1. **Initial Exam** (physical exam findings should be revealed to the learner upon direct questioning)
     1. **General**: Patient has a depressed level of consciousness. No evidence of trauma. She is mildly tachypneic.
     2. **HEENT**: Atraumatic and normocephalic. Pupils are 4mm, equal, and reactive to light. Conjunctivae are normal. Mucous membranes are moist.
     3. **Skin:** Warm and dry. No diaphoresis or rashes noted.
     4. **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, regular rhythm. No murmurs or rubs noted.
     5. **Respiratory**: Tachypnea is present; otherwise lungs are clear to auscultation.
     6. **Abdominal:** Normal bowel sounds. Non-distended bladder. No grimacing with palpation.
     7. **Genitourinary:** Guaiac negative stool.
     8. **Back:** Normal.
     9. **Extremities:** No cyanosis, clubbing, edema.
     10. **Neurological:** No response to verbal stimuli, but withdraws to pain. No speech or eye movement to stimulation (GCS 7). Corneal reflex intact, no gag reflex is present.
  2. **Physiology (data points should appear when patient is placed on the appropriate monitoring devices)**
     1. Heart Rate 110 bpm, sinus
     2. Blood Pressure 108/62 mmHg
     3. Respiratory Rate 24/min
     4. Pulse Oximetry 100 on RA%
     5. T 98.4**°**F (rectal)

1. **Scenario Branch points**
   1. Changes in patient condition:
      1. The patient requires intubation secondary to her depressed mental status. If the patient is not intubated, then her respiratory status should worsen (repeat VS - HR 120 bpm, BP 100/56 mmHg, RR 30/min, Sats 94%).
      2. The patient's heart will initially improve with an isotonic fluid bolus (repeat VS- HR 92 bpm, BP 110/64 mmHg). However, the patient's VS should deteriorate (repeat VS - HR 130 bpm, BP 96/50 mmHg) if more aggressive measures to correct metabolic derangements are not initiated, including administration of NaHCO3 drip, fomepizole and dialysis. Ultimately, the patient requires dialysis to correct her severe acid-base abnormalities.
   2. Request for old records: Baseline creatinine is 1.4.
   4. Intravenous fluid administration: An IV fluid bolus is indicated by the physical exam showing tachycardia. The patient's heart rate should improve with fluid administration (repeat VS- HR 92 bpm, BP 110/64 mmHg), but ultimately the hemodynamics should worsen until efforts to correct the profound metabolic derangements are initiated (bicarbonate drip, fomepizole, and dialysis).
   6. Laboratory studies: Laboratory studies can be found in Appendix A.
   8. Consultation of specialists: An intensivist and nephrologist will be available by telephone consult. They should neither direct management, nor be obstructive. If any consultant is requested prior to diagnosis, this should be unavailable until the diagnosis of presumptive toxic alcohol ingestion. If the poison center is consulted for management, they should only be available to confirm treatment decisions that have already occurred. They should not be available to lead/direct management. Allowance of pocket references or specialists to provide specific treatment information (i.e. drug dosages) can be adjusted to the level of expertise of the learner.

**VII. Instructor Notes:**

1. **Scenario flow** - Instructors can directly influence the flow of the scenario by providing the initial patient history.
2. **Information for actors** - This case can easily be presented without formal "actors" and by using other participants to serve as the nursing staff or other collaborating physicians.
3. **Scenario programming** - The settings for a high-fidelity patient simulator are fairly straightforward for this scenario and do not require specific programming. Our program typically presents this scenario with the initial vital signs as presented above, with the major branch points of fluid resuscitation and recognition of need for dialysis.
