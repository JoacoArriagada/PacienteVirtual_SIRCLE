1. **TITLE OF CASE:** Toxic Ingestion/Ethylene Glycol Ingestion
2. **TARGET AUDIENCE:** Resident physicians and fellows
3. **LEARNING OBJECTIVES:**
   1. **Primary Learning Objectives**
      1. Interpret arterial blood gas and recognize acid base disturbance
      2. Recognize toxic alcohol (ethylene glycol) poisoning
      3. Identify the need for fluid resuscitation
      4. Understand the need to start fomepizole
   2. **Secondary Learning Objectives**
      1. Consider NaHCO3 for acidosis
      2. Discuss indications for dialysis
      3. Discuss usefulness of co-factor therapy
      4. Demonstrate the need for hyperventilation to prevent worsening acidemia after intubation
   3. **Critical Actions**
      1. Check blood sugar in a patient with altered mental status
      2. Perform focused history and physical exam based on the presentation
      3. Intubate patient secondary to altered mental status
      4. Initiate fluid resuscitation
      5. Troubleshoot differential of anion gap metabolic acidosis (AGMA) by sending lactic acid and salicylate levels
      6. Start fomepizole
      7. Discuss the case with nephrology for hemodialysis
      8. Discuss the case with intensive care unit for admission

**VII. Instructor Notes:**

1. **Scenario flow** - Instructors can directly influence the flow of the scenario by providing the initial patient history.
   1. Key aspect of case - Identification of toxic alcohol/ethylene glycol ingestion and recognition of the clinical pattern of presentation is essential. Fluid resuscitation must be performed; alkalinization with bicarbonate infusion should be considered. Fomepizole should be started after toxic alcohols are considered; this should be initiated prior to results of definite testing. Ultimately, this patient requires dialysis for ethylene glycol toxicity.
2. **Information for actors** - This case can easily be presented without formal "actors" and by using other participants to serve as the nursing staff or other collaborating physicians. Any specific actors used outside of participants should be briefed about the critical actions and anticipated flow of the case ahead of time.

**VIII. Debriefing Plan:**

1. **Method of debriefing** - A post-case debriefing conference is recommended immediately following the end of the scenario. Consider including some of the following elements:
   1. Open-ended questions by facilitator - Consider beginning the session with a question to the primary participant about how they felt the scenario went. This often leads to extensive participant-led discussion that will touch on many of the major issues in the case. Invite any secondary participants and/or observers to comment constructively about the case.
   2. Brief didactic review - Some possible materials for review after the scenario have been provided in Appendix B. They address several major teaching points related to the case; however, individual participants may raise additional questions.
   3. Formal participant evaluation - Our group does not use this case for formal evaluations and we have no standardized form or format for such feedback. However, this case can easily be adapted to the standardized evaluation method used at your institution using the objectives in Section III (above).
2. **Actual debriefing materials** - See Appendix B for debriefing materials.
3. **Rules for the debriefing** - Often an open-ended question to the participants will provoke a discussion of aspects of the case management that were good, and those areas where improvement can be made. Encourage your participants to discuss the case management decisions in a non-judgmental way. When this case is used for experiential learning (without formal participant evaluation) discussions may vary as compared to situations where formal feedback or grading is anticipated. We have found that placing an emphasis on the learning that occurs from experiencing a case like this is very effective, rather than focusing on the "right answer" or whether the specific management decisions were exactly as the leader would have performed.
4. **Questions to facilitate the debriefing -**
   1. What is the differential for AGMA?
   2. What diagnostic tests will help evaluate the etiology of AGMA?
   3. Discuss pros/cons of NaHCO3 administration in an acidotic patient.
   4. Most labs cannot perform real time toxic alcohol testing. What other laboratory tests/clues in this case point to EG toxicity?
   5. In this scenario, after intubation, why is it important to consider the minute ventilation during selection of the ventilator settings?
   6. Discuss indications for fomepizole in EG toxicity.
   7. Discuss indications for dialysis in EG toxicity.
   8. Discuss the use of thiamine and pyridoxine co-factor administration in EG toxicity.
