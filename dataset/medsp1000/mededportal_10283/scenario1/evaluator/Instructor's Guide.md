**Instructor Guide for Toxic Ingestion/Ethylene Glycol Ingestion**

**Resource Files and Use of Resource Files**

The case submission contains four elements to help run the simulation. The first element is the case narrative, which includes all pertinent case information, interpretations of the case information, and anticipated case branch points. The second element is Appendix A, a PowerPoint that contains pertinent case lab information, x-ray, and EKG to be used during the case simulation. The third element is Appendix B, a PowerPoint that contains teaching points for the debriefing session. Finally, an evaluation form is included, that contains recommended care actions.

**Purpose and Education Objectives**

Acute ethylene glycol overdose is an uncommon but life threatening ingestion. The purpose of this simulation is to present an acute ethylene glycol overdose case that allows residents and fellows to test their knowledge base and critically evaluate the management of this type of ingestion.

Primary learning objectives include

1. Interpret arterial blood gas and recognize acid base disturbance
2. Recognize toxic alcohol (ethylene glycol) poisoning
3. Identify the need for fluid resuscitation
4. Understand the need to start fomepizole

Secondary learning objectives include

1. Consider NaHCO3 for acidosis
2. Discuss indications for dialysis
3. Discuss usefulness of co-factor therapy
4. Demonstrate the need for hyperventilation to prevent worsening academia after intubation

**Conceptual Background**

Ethylene glycol toxicity is an infrequent but life threatening ingestion that requires an adequate knowledge base and critical thinking skills to manage. A simulation case allows real-time, hands on training for residents and fellows to practice managing this type of ingestion.

**Practical Implementation**

The simulation programming is not difficult, but the instructor should be familiar with their simulator software. Approximately 30 minutes is required for this simulation, 15 minutes to do the case, and approximately 15 minutes for debriefing and discussion. The debriefing session should be conducted in a nonjudgmental manner, while focusing on identifying knowledge gaps and critically evaluating the case management.

**Deployment and Limitations**

As this is a less frequently encountered ingestion, most residents and fellows are not familiar with the management of this ingestion. Expected discussion topics include a basic overview and evaluation of an ethylene glycol ingestion, resuscitative efforts, use of antidote (fomepizole), and indications of dialysis. The limitation of this case is the lack of realism, a shared problem with all simulation-based instruction. During deployment at our institution, common educational gaps included: non- recognition of the ingestion; wanting to defer treatment until definitive testing; lack of familiarity with the antidote and indications for administration; as well as hesitancy to institute dialysis.

**Effectiveness and Significance**

This case was developed to review and teach PGY1-PGY3 Emergency Medicine residents and Pediatric Emergency Medicine fellows critical aspects to the evaluation and management of a an ethylene glycol overdose. This case has been used numerous times for group simulation training; additionally, the case content has been used more than 20 times as an oral board type case.

Feedback of the case scenario occurred during these uses. The combination of participation in the simulation scenario, interactive feedback session that focused on debriefing questions, and review of the brief PowerPoint lecture addressed all of the listed objectives for the learners.n Additionally, the majority of participants identified a general lack of comfort in managing this ingestion (even when the management decisions were correct) secondary to 1) lack of familiarity as this is an uncommon ingestion and 2) need to make treatment decisions without confirmatory testing (even thought this is realistic to community and academic practice settings). In this regard, the case was received well as it is an opportunity to critically think through and review the management decisions.
