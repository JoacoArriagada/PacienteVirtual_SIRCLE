# Standardized Patient Training Materials

**Part 3 Encounters include:**

* History/interview
* NO PHYSICAL EXAM
* Time outs

**Case Overview: Kim Lee**

**Patient Name:** Kim Lee

**Presenting Problem:** abdominal pain

**Actual Diagnosis:** the cancer which has metastasized (spread) to your liver will be discovered to be a melanoma (skin cancer), originally from the back of your scalp, under your hair

**Patient Demographics:**

**Age:** 45 years old

**Sex:** female

**Ethnicity:** open

**Height:** Average

**Weight:** Average

**Overview of Case:**

Kim Lee is a 45 year old woman with a several month history of abdominal pain that has been getting progressively worse. While her primary care doctor was away on vacation, she went to urgent care for the pain, where she saw a medical student first, followed by the urgent care doctor. They recommended and she agreed to several blood tests and imaging studies. All results were forwarded to her primary care doctor. An abdominal CT scan revealed several “spots” on the liver that were worrisome for a cancer (either from the liver originally or from somewhere else that spread to the liver). At the point of the case that you will be engaged with the students, you do not yet know that there were any abnormalities on the abdominal CT scan.

**Goals for Encounter:**

During the interview, the students will be expected to:

* Convey the results of the CT scan
* Address the patient’s concerns as best as they can
* Describe next steps
* Students have a 20 minute interview including "time-outs"
* Only the interviewer and the facilitator(s) may call “time-out” during the interview. If the student runs out of questions or is stuck, he/she may call a “time-out” and ask for help. The facilitator(s) may want to call a time out to check in on process, clarify something, or make suggestions to the interviewer.
* After 20 minutes, students will end the interview by saying that your preceptor will want to see her in a few minutes.
* In contrast to other PBL cases, the SP will “break role” at the end of the encounter to give feedback to the interviewer from the perspective of the patient. She will exit the room and then immediately walk back in for a 5 minute feedback session.

**Feedback Checklist**

You should begin by:

* asking the student to reflect on his/her performance, and
* offer feedback about how you felt with the questioning

**Note:** The checklist below is for your convenience and to help generally guide your feedback to the student. You will NOT be asked to complete an actual checklist for each student. When you provide verbal feedback to the students, you may wish to touch on the some of the points listed below.

**General**

* Was the student’s appearance and demeanor professional?
* Did the student introduce him/herself?
* Did the student ask how you were feeling/doing today?
* Did the students put you at ease and treat you with respect?

**Interview**

* **Did the** student explain the test (abdominal CT) results in understandable language?
* Did the student check for your understanding of what was said?
* Did the student describe what the next step(s) would be?
* Did the student mention the possibility of cancer?
* Did the student mention the possibility that this could be a cancer that has spread (metastasized) to your liver?
* Did the student respond to your emotions in an appropriate way?
