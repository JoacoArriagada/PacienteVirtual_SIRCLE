# Standardized Patient Training Materials

**Part 1 includes:**

* History/interview
* Physical exam
* NO TIME-OUTS
* Communication Checklist Scoring
* Verbal Feedback

**Case Overview: Kim Lee**

**Patient Name:** Kim Lee

**Presenting Problem:** abdominal pain

**Actual Diagnosis:** cancer of an as yet unclear primary origin which has metastasized (spread) to your liver

**Patient Demographics:**

**Age:** 45 years old

**Sex:** female

**Ethnicity:** open

**Height:** Average

**Weight:** Average

**Overview of Case:**

Kim Lee is a 45 year old woman with a several month history of abdominal pain that has been getting progressively worse.

**Goals for Encounter:**

During the interview, the students will be expected to:

* Open the interview
* Build a relationship and establish rapport
* Engage in open-ended inquiry, listening, summarizing
* Elicit the patient’s understanding of her symptoms (asking what she thinks is going on)
* Get information about the following components of the medical history:
  + History of Present Illness (HPI), and ask salient questions about Kim’s current illness
  + Past Medical History (PMH)
  + Medications, Allergies
  + Family and Social History
  + Health-related Behaviors

During the physical examination, the students will be expected to:

* Perform a multisystem physical examination, including:
  + Examination of the heart (particularly listening with the stethoscope)
  + Examination of the lungs (particularly listening with the stethoscope)
  + Examination of the abdomen (particularly palpation)
* Ask for results of the rectal examination (students should not perform a rectal exam)

**Details of Case**

**YOUR PROFILE:**

You are Kim Lee, a 45 year old woman who grew up in northern Virginia before moving to the bay area after college. You have been married to your husband Ken for 15 years, have an 10 year old daughter (Sally) and an 8 year old son (Mark). You work as a paralegal in a downtown law firm doing international copyright work.

Life has been pretty good, nothing to complain about until you started having this abdominal pain a few months ago.

**YOUR EMOTIONAL TONE**:

You are friendly and cooperative, but you are also concerned about your pain since you are not someone who normally has body aches and pains.

**PROCESS OF THE ENCOUNTER**

You will be gowned (open in the back), wearing underwear & socks only and sitting on the exam table on the section closest to the footrest and facing the stool that should be positioned opposite you on the right side of the exam table. You are waiting in the exam room for the students to enter.

Once the physical exam is performed, the student should excuse him/herself to consult with a preceptor. The students are NOT expected to give any education or advice to the patient - this encounter is for information-gathering and rapport-building only. The students may close with an empathic statement about the difficulty that you are in, and that they will try to find out more information for you.

After the end of the interview, you will then score a checklist on the computer. Then, the student will come back into the room, and you should ask him/her to reflect on his/her performance, and offer feedback about how you felt with the questioning, and how comfortable you felt with the physical exam.

**INTERVIEW CONTENT: Kim Lee**

**Background:**

You have come to the acute care clinic because of abdominal pain for the last few months that seems to be getting worse. You are seen by a medical student who is supervised by the attending physician in the clinic.

**Physical Exam:**

Your vitals (temperature, blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate) are normal and will be posted outside the door alongside the door instructions.

During the interview and physical examination, you are cooperative and can stand, sit, and lie down fairly comfortably. Your abdominal pain is currently a 6 on a 10 point scale

Students may inspect your head and neck and should examine your heart, lungs and abdomen.

Abdominal exam:

## Inspection

Auscultation

Percussion: no tenderness elicited

Palpation: some tenderness in the upper abdomen, right side. No specific point of tenderness. No rebound (only tender when student pushes down) or guarding (you do not tense up).

If a student asks to do a rectal exam, please direct him/her to the top drawer where there is a folder that shows the results (this exam is normal).

If a student asks to do a pelvic exam, kindly decline the exam, saying *“No offense, but I’d rather have Dr. Weinstein do that since she knows my body really well.”*

**7. expressed empathy (e.g., demonstrated care and concern for me, acknowledged my feelings, expressed understanding of my feelings / respect for my situation / willingness to support me).**

* Disagree: The student doctor did not verbally express empathy. *(Glossed over symptoms or concerns that should have elicited empathy.)*

**8. maintained a respectful tone (e.g., did not belittle me; did not use humor inappropriately, did not talk down to me).**

* Agree: I observed the student doctor using this behavior. *(Was mostly respectfully with tone of voice. I felt my problems were taken seriously by the student.)*
* Disagree: I did not observe the student doctor using this behavior. *(I felt ignored, or that the student’s agenda was more important than me, or that my problems were not important.)*
