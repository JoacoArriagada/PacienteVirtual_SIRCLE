# 23. **Case Scenario with the Standardized Patient**

**MEETING #1**

Imagine that the whole multi-disciplinary team has been putting in extreme efforts to provide optimal intensive care for Leona. Everyone is sad about the devastating start to life that Leona has experienced. There have been several other babies with Hypoxic Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE) in the past 6 months, but Leona’s case certainly appears to be the most serious.

Leona’s mom, Mrs. Helen Richards, has just completed her first visit with Leona and the bedside nurse.  Helen did not say much at the bedside; she appears to understand that things are not going well. She understands she is about to get a clinical update from the doctor for the first time.

When you call the trainee into the staged room, you tell him/her that the mom, Helen, is expecting to have the first clinical update on Leona’s condition.

**MEETING #2**

Imagine that Leona’s condition is largely unchanged at 48 hours of age. All team members remain concerned about her long-term neurodevelopmental outcome. Leona’s mom, Helen, remains without her husband as he is stuck overseas. The team is hoping that Helen will have spoken with him about possible care plan options given the concern for Leona’s outcome.

You now tell the trainee that he/she is about to re-meet Helen after having reviewed the “<u>**NICU Progress Note #2**</u>” (see following page).  This meeting was planned after her previous visit to see Leona.  **The trainee’s role** is to provide Helen with a clinical update and proceed with the shared decision making process around Leona’s care plan.

**Appendix D**

**Information to assist trainees with specific, difficult questions from the SP**

As a supervisor, you bring clinical expertise and experience to the SOLE. Additionally, you have had the opportunity to read literature around how to address some of the specific, difficult questions from the SP. Below is a brief summary of the information from the key and additional references for your convenience, in order to help you assist trainees that struggle with these specific questions from the SP.

**QUESTION #1: Have you done everything you can for my baby? We want everything done.**

- Utilize the mnemonic SOBPIE to gain greater understanding of what may be generating this question (steps to be used for each question):
    - What is the **S**ituation?
    - Consider **O**pinions around and **O**ptions for the situation
    - Ensure **B**asic human interactions
    - Truly get the **P**arents’ story/concerns/needs/goals
    - Provide truthful and desired **I**nformation
    - Manage the **E**motions and relational aspects of decision making
- “Everything” does not necessarily mean “Do every single test, investigation and management option available to you as a physician”
- “Everything” does mean “After learning about us parents and our child, interact with us and help us in doing what is in the best interests of our baby and family”
- This can occur in the context of intensive care, where cure and a ‘good’ outcome is possible and remains the goal, or in the context of palliative care, where death is expected and comfort of the baby becomes the main goal.
- The positive nature of all care (intensive or palliative) should be communicated
- Recognize the fluctuating nature of emotions and choices during decisions of such magnitude

**QUESTION #2: Is this only your opinion or someone else’s too?**

- Utilize the mnemonic SOBPIE to gain greater understanding of what may be generating this question:
- Humility is an important trait for physicians to display
- Care in the NICU/PICU/Academic centres should always be multi-disciplinary and each member of the health care team and family has a valuable voice to be heard
- In life and death situations, additional opinions of colleagues should be sought

**QUESTIONS #3: If my baby was yours, what would you do?**

- Utilize the mnemonic SOBPIE to gain greater understanding of what may be generating this question:
- An approach involving the parents and using information (rather than one’s values/opinions) is key
- The question allows:

    - Parental assessment of physician bias
    - Opportunity to increase bond and trust between physician and parents
    - Time to focus on the best interests of the baby

- Three (or more) possible rationales behind the question should be considered:
    1. Parents are overwhelmed by the information/statistics/etc … and need clarification regarding what might make their baby better
    2. Parents need support in making a very difficult decision and choosing between life and death
    3. Parents cannot make a choice and believe they need clear direction from the treating team
- Exploration with the family and garnering trust and understanding must be accomplished before any cautious sharing of a physician’s own opinions and values

**QUESTION #4: What do we do now? [Regarding a transition to palliative care from intensive care]**

- Utilize the mnemonic SOBPIE to gain greater understanding of what may be generating this question:
- The physician generally needs to initiate such discussions
- Support the transition to palliative care by clarifying the prognosis
- Identify end-of-life goals with the parents
- Develop a treatment plan with the parents
- Ensure clarity at each and every step of the process
- Ensure parents know they are not being abandoned and that the situation is “not their fault”
- Say “We will continue to do what is best for your baby and make sure s/he is always comfortable as we transition to palliative care”; **do NOT say** “… as we withdrawal care” as this suggests abandonment
