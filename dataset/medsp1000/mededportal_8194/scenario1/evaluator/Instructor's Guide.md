# Title:

# A seizing infant in the post anesthesia care unit

# Resource files:

Word Document

# Resource utilization:

The document submitted provides a detailed outline for the utilization of the simulation scenario. The scenario would ideally be performed in the post anesthesia care unit (PACU) using a high fidelity baby simulator.

# Goals:

Simulation is well recognized as giving excellent hands on experience and practice in crisis management, teamwork, collaboration and communication. We have produced a simulation scenario that challenges the capacity of the modern day simulator to the maximum

# Learning Objectives:

# Primary:

1. Recognition and management of infant seizures   
2. Institution of basic life support (BLS)   
3. Institution of appropriate pediatric advanced life support (PALS)   
algorithm for cardiac arrythmias   
4. Institution of appropriate treatment of local anesthetic toxicity   
5. Plan and institute necessary post-seizure/arrest care and transfer to the   
intensive care unit (ICU)

# Secondary: Technical Goals

Basic and advanced airway management with bag mask and oral   
intubation   
Specific cardiac arrhythmia management – bradycardia and ventricular   
tachycardia (VT) induced by resuscitation epinephrine bolus   
Pharmacological seizure management   
Pharmacological local anesthetic toxicity management

# Behavioral Goals

Directed, closed-loop communication Communication directed at specific individuals Individuals communicate when action is complete Individuals in assessment roles continuously communicate with team leader   
There is evidence of good teamwork among the members, e.g., it is clear who is in charge but all team members contribute their ideas tasks are delegated efficiently and clearly.

members share with team what they are thinking and doing members announce what tasks they have done Recognition that initially there is minimal information available for patient management Deal with the difficult and obstructive nurse/colleague who is initially caring for the patient

# Didactic points

Differential diagnosis of failure to wake up after anesthesia e.g.   
hypoglycemia, neurological insult etc.   
Differential diagnosis of seizure activity (e.g. hypocalcaemia, electrolyte imbalance)   
Differential diagnosis of VT (e.g. epinephrine bolus)   
‘Lateral thinking’ to determine the actual cause of the seizures (local anesthesia toxicity)

# Why we created the scenario?

The scenario was created for the ongoing education and training of our pediatric anesthesia trainees at our institution: both residents and fellows. We felt this scenario had so much potential that we took it to the Society for Pediatric Anesthesiologists meeting and utilized it during the Simulation workshop at that meeting.

# Practical Implementation:

The scenario requires a high fidelity infant manekin and technician to operate the computer. Necessary adjuncts to the manekin include, airway management kit, intravenous access and fluid kit, code cart.

We have always run the scenario within the post anesthesia care unit where we have found a more realistic environment can be achieved.

The session faculty requires a facilitator, a distracter and a technician to run successfully.

We have always used at least 3 trainees/candidates – one as a team leader, an airway manager and an external cardiac massager. Further trainees can be utilized as necessary as medications administrators, documenters or observers.

Once the facilitators have set up the scenario the candidates are introduced to the environment and given the case briefing as described in the submission background. The candidates can either be introduced as a group to the scenario or if the development of a good working team is to be challenged further then just the initial team leader can be introduced to the scenario. The principle facilitator should introduce the PACU nurse distracter and the SIM baby technician to the team prior to the start of the scenario going “live”.

The set up takes approximately 15 minutes and due to the complexity of the scenario a pre scenario discussion should take place involving the faculty.

The simulation itself takes between 15 and 20 minutes.

The debrief afterwards is open ended, but usually takes at least 20 minutes.

# Deployment:

We have utilized this scenario as described above on Anesthesia residents, Pediatric anesthesia fellows and at a national meeting workshop on simulation. Providing the facilitators are motivated then we have found the simulation to work very well, but enthusiasm and motivation amongst the facilitators/faculty is imperative as is invariably the case with any form of simulation.

# Limitations:

This is a reasonably high intensity simulation. We designed it to challenge the candidates and it does that. One could almost say at times we have been obstructive, but when we run the scenario the consistent feedback has been good and by not being too liberal with information the candidates have described how realistic the scenario is.

The scenario is not designed for all medical practitioners and really is only of benefit to anesthesiologists and anesthesiology trainees.
