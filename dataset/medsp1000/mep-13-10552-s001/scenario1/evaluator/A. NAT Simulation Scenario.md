**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The learners enter the room to find an infant in distress with altered mental status. They immediately place the patient on bedside monitors and recognize that the patient is mildly hypoxic and tachypnic. A primary survey occurs and the following are identified: Airway is patient but patient may have decreased ability to protect it given mental status. Breathing is rapid and saturations are mildly decreased. Circulation appears diminished. Supplemental oxygen is provided and an IV is placed. A fluid bolus is ordered. The participants began considering possible causes and making a plan for evaluation including laboratory evaluation and neuroimaging. Before studies can be completed, the patient develops worsening respiratory distress as evident by hypopnea and worsening desaturations. The patient also develops worsening hypotension requiring fluid resuscitation. Without delaying care to complete the studies, the team must emergently address the changes including ventilator support through bag-mask ventilation, oxygen delivery and IV fluid resuscitation. The team may contemplate intubation at that time but the patient develops seizure before it can be completed. Management must then focus on continued support of airway, breathing and circulation as well as administration of an anti-epileptic such as a benzodiazepine. When the seizure resolves, the team must continue to provide ventilator support and oxygen to the patient. Initial laboratory evaluation possibly including blood gas, complete blood count, electrolytes, renal function, liver function tests, blood cultures, urinalysis and urine cultures are sent. Empiric antibiotics are administered given the possibility of serious bacterial infection as the underlying cause. The airway is secured by intubation prior to complete of evaluation including chest radiograph and head CT.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Failure to recognize changes in clinical status: We found that medical students do not always notice changes in vital signs on the monitor or as portrayed through the simulation. If changes were not noted in 1-2 minutes a facilitator would point out that changes occurred without specifying the direction of change.
2. Failure to recognize the need for intubation prior to transportation: Some of our learners did not immediately recognize that the patient required a definitive airway before being transferred to another setting such as the CT scanner. Given that our learners here medical students, we were satisfied with them identifying the need to provide ventilator support through either bag-mask ventilation or intubation. If students did not intubate patient but other care had been completed we stopped the scenario and discussed why you would proceed to intubation but did not focus on the technical skill of intubation.
3. Failure to recognize seizure activity: Mannequin capabilities regarding seizure activity vary widely amongst products. We find that people often do not notice the shaking movements of the infant or do not interpret it as seizure activity. When the scenario transitions to the seizure portion, we find it best to have a facilitator state, “The patient has developed stiffening and shaking in the arms and legs.” The facilitator may also demonstrate the activity. Alternatively a vibrating pad, such as those used for back massage, may be placed under the mannequin and turned on to simulation seizure activity.
