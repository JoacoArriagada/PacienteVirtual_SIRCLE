# DEBRIEF NOTES FOR CEREBRAL VENOUS SINUS THROMBOSIS CASE

(Organized by learning objectives)

 Include cerebral venous sinus thrombosis as a cause of headache in postpartum patients and describe its presentation.

Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis can be difficult to diagnose because of its nonspecific presentation.   
It can result in cerebral infarction and carries the risk of significant morbidity. Maintaining an index of suspicion for all patients presenting with headache is important.   
A thorough history, including screening for risk factors, and careful physical examination are critical in such patients.   
Pathophysiology: Obstruction of the veins leads to increased pressure, decreased capillary perfusion and increased cerebral blood volume and pressure. This eventually results in vasogenic edema causing parenchymal changes, cerebral edema and venous hemorrhage from venous or capillary disruption.   
Cerebral venous thrombosis causes impaired CSF absorption which also elevated intracranial pressure.

Presentation:

 Specific presentation features are affected by the particular venous sinu that is occluded and the extent.   
 The onset of symptoms can be acute, subacute or even chronic.   
 Headache: o This is the most common symptom and is usually the first symptom. o Pain is usually localized, not global. o Headache onset is most often gradual.

Encephalopathy: o Altered mentation and confusion can be present.  Seizures: o Can be focal or generalized.  Focal deficits:

o Motor weakness is the most common focal deficit.   
o Speech deficits can occur.   
o Visual changes and sensory deficits are less common.

 Develop a differential diagnosis for a patient with a seizure in the immediate postpartum period beyond eclampsia

Late postpartum eclampsia, eclamptic seizures occurring between 48 hours and 4 weeks after delivery, accounts for approximately $1 / 7$ of eclampsia cases. Since pregnancy and the peripartum period can unmask or worsen a number of neurological disorders, one should consider other entities besides eclampsia in pregnant or postpartum women who seize.

Considerations include but are not limited to:

 Reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome (imaging can help differentiate from cerebral venous sinus thrombosis)   
 Stroke   
 Hypertensive emergency   
 Infection   
 Subdural hematoma as a complication of epidural placement for childbirth   
 Brain tumor or abscess   
 Metabolic disorder such as hypoglycemia or uremia   
 Infection   
 Drug use   
 Vasculitis

Perform a problem-focused neurological history and examination in appropriate patients.

Much of the critical information can be obtained via history.  This depends on the reliability of the source.

Organization of the exam:

 No consensus about the correct order. o Providers should determine their preferred order and use this approach consistently.

 Components:

o Mental status (includes language)   
o Cranial nerves   
o Motor   
o Reflexes   
o Sensory

 Identify the most appropriate imaging modalities to diagnose cerebral venous sinus thrombosis and identify the diagnostic findings

Modality-specific findings:

 Noncontrast CT:  Delta sign: seen as a dense triangle (from hyperdense clot) within the superior sagittal sinus

o This can also be seen occasionally with a nonthrombosed sinus or with a markedly elevated hematocrit

 Contrast-enhanced CT:

 Reverse delta sign: seen in the superior sagittal sinus from enhancement around the less dense thrombosed sinus.

MRI/MRV:

$\bigcirc$ The preferred method of imaging $\bigcirc$ The diagnosis can usually be made even without contrast. $\bigcirc$ The thrombus can be directly visualized. $\bigcirc$ Restricted diffusion may or may not be seen.

Ultrasound: Generally not helpful; not recommended.

CT scan should be obtained first; this allows for screening of bleeding. If the CT is negative, an MRI may be obtained to evaluate for thrombus. A MRI may also be obtained after a positive CT scan to evaluate for diffusion.

 Explain the treatment principles for cerebral venous sinus thrombosis

The patient should receive standard stabilization measures that stroke patients receive : i.e. airway support when necessary

Anticoagulation: though there is some concern for increasing hemorrhage, the current recommendation is to proceed with anticoagulation even in patients with existing hemorrhage.

 Systemic anticoagulation is indicated.   
 Some centers perform dural venous sinus thrombolytic therapy. Patients with severe deficit may benefit from this therapy offered at limited specialized centers.

Antiepileptic agents should be administered to patients with seizures.  Benzodiazepines can be administered to break an acute seizure but patients should receive longer acting agents as well.

Neurology should be consulted.

Correctly disposition patients with this diagnosis.

Given the high morbidity of this condition and recommended anticoagulation, patients should be admitted to an ICU setting for careful monitoring.
