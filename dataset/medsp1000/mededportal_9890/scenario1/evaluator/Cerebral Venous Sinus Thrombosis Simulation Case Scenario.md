# Simulation Interest Group Scenario Template

I. Title : Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis II. Target Audience: Residents (emergency medicine, internal medicine, neurology, obstetrics/gynecology, family practice and others), medical students

# III. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives

A. Upon completion of the simulated case and debrief, learners will be able to :

 Include cerebral venous sinus thrombosis as a cause of headache in postpartum patients and describe its presentation.   
 Develop a differential diagnosis for a patient with a seizure in the immediate postpartum period beyond eclampsia   
 Perform a problem-focused neurological examination in appropriate patients   
 Recognize the risk factors for cerebral venous sinus thrombosis and evaluate patients for these risk factors Identify the most appropriate imaging modalities to diagnose cerebral venous sinus thrombosis and identify the diagnostic findings Explain the treatment principles for cerebral venous sinus thrombosis

VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

1. Scenario Background Given to Participants Patient identification/chief complaint: 32 y.o. female with diarrhea, weakness, headache The patient is brought to the ED by car by her mother.   
2. Past medical history (must be asked for): Migraines, Crohn’s disease, hypothyroidism, 12 days $\mathrm { s / p }$ NSVD – she did not have an epidural placed   
3. Meds and allergies: Synthroid, Compazine prn headaches, Asacol/NKDA   
4. Family/social history: Father with Crohn’s disease; no tobacco or alcohol or drugs; works as an engineer; lives with her family; her mother is currently visiting to help with the new baby   
5. Review of systems (must be asked for): mild blurry vision (she is not sure if it is monocular or binocular)

A. Scenario conditions initially 1. History patient gives

 For the past 6 days, she has had up to 10 episodes of diarrhea a day. Small amount of blood in her stool as well as some mucous.   
 No vomiting or abdominal pain.   
 No fevers.   
 She feels weak.   
 Over the last three days, she has developed a persistent right-sided headache. This headache is different than her migraines (must be asked for). Her mother reports that she is “just not herself.”

2. Patients initial exam

 Vital signs: T 97.6, HR 108, RR 18, BP 96/50, O2 sat 98RA   
 General: She is mildly lethargic but arousable   
 HEENT: NCAT, PERRL, EOMI, MM somewhat dry   
 Neck: Supple   
 Lungs: CTA b/l   
 Heart: Mildly tachycardic, regular rhythm   
 Abdomen: Soft, nontender, mild fullness in lower half   
 Neuro: Oriented, lethargic but appropriate, CNs intact, 4/5 on her left side (when asked about this she says that she didn’t mention it earlier because she thought it was from holding her baby in her left arm when she is right handed), 5/5 on the right, normal reflexes

3. Patients physiology

Patient has a cerebral venous sinus thrombus in the setting of being postpartum and dehydrated. She also has a history of Crohn’s disease which predisposes her to this entity.

Her tachycardia is due to dehydration – she will respond to IVF   
Her thrombus causes a seizure that will likely be thought to be eclamptic but will not break with Magnesium; she requires benzodiazepines.

B. Scenario branch points

Patient seizes in the ED

o The participants will likely administer Magnesium which does not break the seizure.   
o Benzodiazepines are required.   
$\bigcirc$ Her fingerstick glucose is $1 2 0 \mathrm { m g / d L }$ .   
$\bigcirc$ Once she starts seizing, for each minute that she does not receive benzodiazepines, her O2 sat should decrease by $2 \%$ .

 Decision to obtain imaging: Consideration of cerebral venous sinus thrombus as a cause of the patient’s presentation and evolving neurological symptoms and ordering appropriate imaging

o Participants may discuss the best imaging modality with the neurology consultant.   
o Further information about imaging modalities can be found in the Debrief Notes.

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer:

The patient’s mother is also present in the ED. The mother can speak up to redirect if there is a lull in the history-taking or the participants are moving off-course.

B. Tips to direct actors

 If another student or resident is given information to play the part of the mother, he/she should be asked to interject as necessary to provide the history, especially about the fact that she is postpartum.

 If the manikin cannot be programmed to have asymmetric strength on the neurological examination, the mother should state that she thinks her daughter is not moving her left arm as well as she normally does.

C. Scenario programming

1. Optimal management path

a. Initiates IV, O2, monitor   
b. IVFs are administered   
c. Takes a complete history and elicits the history of the patient being postpartum   
d. Performs a complete neurological examination   
e. Glucose checked when the patient has a seizure   
f. Consideration of eclampsia and administration of Magnesium   
g. Administration of benzodiazepines   
h. Neurology consultation   
i. Neuroimaging   
j. Discussion about anticoagulation   
k. Admission to the hospital

2. Potential complications path(s)/potential errors path(s)

a. Not taking a complete history   
b. Not performing a complete examination   
c. Not eliciting the fact that the patient is postpartum in a timely enough fashion to consider eclampsia   
d. Not considering the diagnosis of cerebral venous sinus thrombosis
