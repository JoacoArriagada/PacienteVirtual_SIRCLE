**Appendix K: EAC Workshop**

**Patient Centered Communication**

**Scenarios:** The role play exercises follow a virtual patient through a typical ambulatory visit starting with an exercise on Agenda Setting, followed by an exercise on Interrupting and finishing with an exercise on Co-creating the Plan.

**Scenario #1: Agenda Setting** **Provider**

JD is an 18 year old patient with history of asthma and obesity. In Epic, it says she is coming in for back pain and personal. Not been seen since 2012.

Your MA rooms JD and notes chief complaint as back pain. P - 90, BP = 120/80, T = 99, Pulse Ox = 97, BMI = 35

You enter the room and introduce yourself and start the interview.

**Scenario #1: Agenda Setting** **Patient**

History:

You have been struggling for the last 2 years. Been tough at home - not getting along with your parents. You will graduate this year and plan to enroll in Minnesota School of Cosmetology but not sure yet if you can afford it. You live with your parents (is cheaper) but don't want to. You have asthma which got worse in the last 2 years; last few weeks you wake up coughing a lot. You made the appointment to get meds for your low back (making it so hard to sleep) and because you think you might be depressed or have ADD (read the criteria on the internet) because you can't concentrate and can't sleep. You also need albuterol refill. You don't want your parents knowing about your health and so glad you could come on your own without them. Last few times you were at the doctor, you didn't say anything because your mom was with you. Personal issues: you smoke now (like your mom but she'll kill you if you find out), also cutting about once a month. You also make yourself throw up once in awhile because you know you are too fat. Hoping there are medications to help you lose weight.

*Role playing:*

*When asked why you are here, you launch into your* ***back pain*** *(launch into the whole story). You keep going until you are asked "anything else?" and then you switch to the next issue on your list. Make stuff up if you have to. Each time you hear "anything else" you switch to the next concern. You leave the personal to the end and don't go there unless asked:*

* Back pain: real bad, both sides, like being punched, started 1 month ago, makes it hard to sleep and wakes you up at night. Taking a ton of ibuprofen, probably too much. Wonder if need an xray or MRI. Mom and aunt with bad backs. Don't want to be like them.
* Asthma: 2 months, coughing all the time and some shortness of breath from coughing and running out of inhalers. Scared have pneumonia. Wonder what pneumonia is - a friend had it.
* Insomnia: sleep all messed up - back hurts but also just lie there awake. Can't fall asleep and then wake up in pain, early.
* Feeling fat: tired all the time so don't exercise. Also can't breath. Wanting a medication to help you lose weight. Maybe will help my asthma.
* Maybe I have ADD: can't concentrate and tried friends Ritalin. Helped. Wanting own prescription. Could help study when in cosmetology school.
* Maybe depressed: your mom says you are depressed. She has depression.

*You have set the visit agenda and know that JD is here for back pain, asthma and meds, possible depression, obesity. You have together decided to start with the back pain and plan to address asthma and depression too.*

*Start taking her history in as efficient and patient centered way possible. Practice interrupting. Plan to take about 5 minutes*

**Scenario #2: Interrupting Patient**
