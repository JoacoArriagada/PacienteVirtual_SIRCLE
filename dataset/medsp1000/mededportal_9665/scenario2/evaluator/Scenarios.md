1. **Case Title:** Undiagnosed Aortic Stenosis (AS) in a Patient Undergoing Emergent Exploratory Laparotomy (VERSIONS A and B)
2. **Target Audience:** Anesthesia Residents (CA-1 to CA-3 years), Faculty for MOCA/CME credit
3. **Learning Objectives:** At the conclusion of the simulation and debriefing session, the learner will have met the following core competencies from the Accreditation Committee of Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) as it applies to this specific simulation scenario.
   1. **Primary Learning Objectives:**
      * 1. **Medical knowledge:** Identify signs and symptoms of severe AS; outline the hemodynamic goals for induction and intraoperative management of patients with AS.
        2. **Patient Care:** Performs a complete pre-anesthetic physical exam. Generates an anesthetic induction plan for a patient with signs and symptoms of severe AS.
        3. **Interpersonal and Communication Skills:** Communicates with the operating room team that there is severe hypotension and EKG changes.
        4. **Professionalism:** During the debriefing after the simulation, discuss the importance of leadership and communication among operating room personnel (anesthesiologist, surgeon, RN, surgical technicians) during times of crisis.
   2. **Secondary Learning Objectives:**
      * 1. Discuss the pathophysiological changes that occur with severe AS and how these changes impact the left ventricle pressure volume loop.
        2. Identify the cardiovascular effects of induction agents and how these effects relate to the hemodynamic goals for AS.
        3. Implement appropriate treatment strategies for cardiovascular derangements that commonly occur in patients with AS under general anesthesia.
   3. **Critical Actions Checklist:**
      * Prior to Induction
        + Confirms NPO status
        + Confirms allergies
        + Confirms medication list
        + Confirms history
        + Reviews labs and radiology images
        + Auscultation of the heart
        + Auscultation of the lungs
        + (optional) Places arterial line
        + (optional) Places central line
      * Induction
        + Induces general anesthesia
        + Secures the airway
      * Crisis Presentation
        + Recognizes hypotension
        + Treats hypotension
        + Recognizes change in EKG
        + Alerts surgical team about cardiovascular changes
        + Calls for help and institutes advanced cardiac life support (ACLS)

1. **Case Narrative (VERSION B)**
   1. **General Synopsis:** You are an anesthesiologist at a large community hospital taking in-house call. The trauma surgeon is booking an emergent exploratory laparotomy. The patient, Ernest Milford, is a 79 year old retired farmer who presented to the emergency room (ER) after having been kicked by a steer and falling on a cattle prodding rod. He was brought to the ER by ambulance with the rod in place. In the ER, his Focus Abdominal Sonography for Trauma (FAST) exam revealed a significant amount of blood in the left retroperitoneal space. The non-contrast abdominal CT showed the rod impaling the descending and transverse colon, possible small inferior renal injury. No obvious splenic damage. He has been prepped and brought to the operating room (OR) where you meet your team.
   2. **Past Medical History:** None
   3. **Social History:** The patient is a widower and lives alone on his farm with his dog. He has two adult sons, both of whom are alive and well. He feels like he has been slowing down over the past few years
   4. **Allergies and Medications:** The patient has no known drug allergies. The patient takes no routine medications.
   5. **Review of Systems:**
      1. GENERAL: increasing fatigue over the past 18-24 months
      2. HEAD/NECK: negative
      3. CARDIOVASCULAR: occasional chest discomfort over the past 12 months when lifting heavy objects and climbing in and out of the combine
      4. PULMONARY: gets winded easier than he used to, endorses shortness of breath with mild exertion, denies dyspnea at rest, negative for productive cough
      5. NEUROLOGIC: negative
      6. GASTROINTESTINAL: has had nothing to eat or drink for 4 hours, accident happened 2 hours prior to ER presentation
      7. RENAL/HEPATIC: negative
      8. ENDOCRINE: negative
      9. HEME/COAG: negative
   6. **Physical Exam:**
      1. GENERAL: elderly male stabilized on a backboard with a cattle prod rod protruding from left lower quadrant packed with an occlusive dressing that is saturated with blood. Moderate distress due to pain
      2. HEIGHT/WEIGHT: 170cm/83kg (BMI: 28.72)
      3. HEART: 2/6 systolic murmur; regular rate and rhythm
      4. LUNGS: non-smoker; distant, diminished breath sounds throughout, fine crackles at bases, no wheezes
      5. AIRWAY: Mallampati I, normal mouth opening, slightly limited ROM of neck, thyromental distance > 3 finger-breadths, positive subluxation of the jaw
      6. NEUROLOGIC: negative
      7. GASTROINTESTINAL: foreign body seen protruding from the L side of abdomen
      8. VITALS: HR 74 ; BP 97/71; RR 20; Temp 37.7oC; SpO2 100% on 10 L non-rebreather

1. **Instructional Notes:**
   1. **Facilitator Background and Briefing Information:**
      * In these scenarios, the learner is presented with an elderly patient who requires emergent general anesthesia with rapid sequence intubation (RSI) for an exploratory laparotomy. The presence of occasional chest pain, fatigue, and systolic murmur should alert the trainee to possible aortic stenosis, however it may not be obvious to them.
      * The learner should plan an induction that matches the goal of care for a patient with AS. The learner may opt to place a central line for central venous pressure (CVP) monitoring and route of administration for pressors prior to induction.
      * The learner may understand the need for an RSI in both scenarios.
        2. VERSION B:
           + Given the trauma with associated blood loss and pain, the patient is likely hypovolemic and tachycardic. He has a full stomach due to eating 4 hours ago
           + (Figure 3) From the EKG given, the learner should recognize: non-specific T-wave abnormalities, and evidence of LVH
