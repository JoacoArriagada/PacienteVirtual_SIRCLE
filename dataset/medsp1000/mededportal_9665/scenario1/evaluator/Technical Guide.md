| **VERSION A: General Anesthesia with Etomidate** | | | |
| State | Patient Status | Student learning outcomes or actions desired and trigger to move to next state | |
| Phase II:  Induction with Etomidate | HR 74, NSR  BP 101/73 (via Aline)  RR 16  SpO2 99% on 3L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner may choose GA with RSI with etomidate as induction agent. | **Operator:**  Learner may place A-line prior to induction and/or CVP monitor.  **Trigger:**  If the learner chooses etomidate for induction, proceed to maintenance of anesthesia and start surgery.  Teaching Points:  It would be ideal to have this patient euvolemic to hypervolemic prior to induction. Volume status can be assessed by CVP monitor, systolic pressure variation from A-line once intubated, LiDCO Rapid©, or TEE. |
| Phase III:  Maintain anesthesia | HR 92, NSR  BP 98/72  RR 16  SpO2 99% on vent | **Learner Actions:**  If learner maintains anesthesia with 1 MAC of volatile or more, then patient will go into junctional rhythm, become hypotensive, and arrest unless volatile is reduced and hypotension is treated.  If learner maintains anesthesia with 0.5 MAC of volatile, case will proceed. | **Trigger:**  1+ MAC: patient goes into junctional rhythm and becomes hypotensive. Go to Phase IV.  < 0.5 MAC: patient proceeds with case.  **Teaching Points:**  Goals of anesthetic management for patients with AS. |
| Phase IV:  Junctional Rhythm & BP  | HR 51, junctional rhythm  BP 72/58  RR 14  SpO2 99% on vent | **Learner Actions:**  Learner may instruct surgeons to stop surgery until patient becomes more stable.  Learner may identify precipitating events: hypovolemia, onset of junctional rhythm with loss of atrial kick.  Learner may treat hypotension with alpha-agonist (phenylephrine) or alpha and beta agonist (norepinephrine) or may give a fluid bolus.  Learner may attempt cardioversion to regain sinus rhythm.  Learner may call for TEE probe. | **Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs surgeons to stop case and appropriately treats hypotension and dysrhythmia then patient progressively becomes more stable (return to baseline vitals) → *END (Debrief)*  If after 3 minutes, the learner fails to treat hypotension and the dysrhythmia → proceed to Phase V (arrest).  **Teaching Points:**  Management of aortic stenosis intra-op complications. |
| Phase V:  Cardiopulmonary  arrest | HR 32, PEA  BP 48/32  SpO2 92% | **Learner Actions:**  Learner may call for help.  Learner may ask the CPR and ACLS be started.  Learner may request crash cart brought into the OR and paddles placed on patient.  Learner may increase FiO2. | **Trigger:**  If learner correctly calls for help and institutes ACLS → *END (Debrief)* |

| Phase II:  Induction with Propofol | HR 94, NSR  BP 101/73 (via Aline)  RR 16  SpO2 99% on 3L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner may choose GA with RSI with propofol as induction agent. | **Operator:**  Learner may place A-line prior to induction and/or CVP monitor.  **Trigger:**  If the learner chooses propofol for induction. Learner may call for TEE probe. | **Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs surgeons to stop case and appropriately treats hypotension and dysrhythmia then patient progressively becomes more stable (return to baseline vitals) → *END (Debrief)*  If after 3 minutes, the learner fails to treat hypotension and the dysrhythmia → proceed to Phase IV (arrest)  **Teaching Points:**  Management of aortic stenosis intra-op complications. |

Learner may request crash cart brought into the OR and paddles placed on patient.  Learner may increase FiO2. | **Operator:**  If learner auscultates the chest to listen to breath sounds, state that lung sounds are clear bilaterally.  **Trigger:**  If learner correctly calls for help and institutes ACLS → *END (Debrief)* |
