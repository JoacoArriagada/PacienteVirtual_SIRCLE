**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

*First Responder actions and Team Entry:*

First responder checks for responsiveness with verbal and gentle shake, states: “No response, get the code cart.” Remainder of team is allowed in after 10 seconds and team initiates high quality CPR, attaches defibrillator, and appropriately defibrillates patient. Team obtains lab testing, administers epinephrine, and continues providing advanced life support while searching for reversible causes of arrest through history, physical examination, and lab testing.

Anticipated Management Mistakes

1. *Prioritization: One example includes checking airway and beginning ventilations before checking a pulse. Another common example trying to administer epinephrine prior to analyzing the rhythm. When prioritization issues arise in this assessment, it is important to spend more time during the Rapid Cycle Deliberate Practice (RCDP) rounds that follow on initial choreography team coordination.*
2. *Equipment operation or location: Examples of this are inability to attach oxygen to a self-inflating mask, problems with use of the defibrillator, inability to draw up epinephrine. These types of errors require more time spent on the specific procedural skill involved.*
3. *Team roles: Examples of this error include duplication of work (two team members attempt to draw up medications) and omission of tasks (no one starts a timer). This requires more time spent discussing what the roles are along with use of closed loop communication to confirm which person is fulfilling which role.*
4. *Team leadership: Most common mistake early if for all interns to work on the patient and for no one to organize the roles. If this type of problem is most prevalent, more time is spent on role assignment, closed loop communication, and mental modeling. Another common error is giving out multiple orders to individuals which leads to confusion. This can be corrected through modeling of leadership during the RCDP cycles.*
