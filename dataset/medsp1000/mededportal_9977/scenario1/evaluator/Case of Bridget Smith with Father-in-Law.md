**Name and Age:** Bridget Smith, 24 y.o.

**Encounter Length:** 30 minutes

**Case Purpose:** The purpose of the encounter is to enable students to practice communicating with a patient showing physical evidence of violence accompanied by an overbearing relative (e.g. father-in-law, mother-in-law, spouse). RN has pre-ordered a left wrist x-ray, which is negative for fracture.

**Props:**

Father-in-law. Bruises on forearms.

**Setting:**

You have come to the clinic today because you are experiencing pain in your left wrist.

**Opening Statement**:

“I hurt my wrist”

**SP Presentation and Starting Position:**Sitting in chair, cradling left wrist. *(You have already had an x-ray of your wrist and it shows no fracture.)* You have old and new bruises on your arms. Accompanying you is an older male relative (Father-in-law). During the interview you continue to glance at the relative. You appear somewhat nervous.

**Bruising makeup:** You will have a few bruises on the ulnar (outer) side of your arm(s). Wear a loose fitting top with long sleeves (to unsuccessfully try to hide the bruises)

**Style of talking and answering questions:** You do not mention any violence in presence of the relative. However, if student asks the relative to step out, with gentle questioning you can tell the student what really happened.

**If** the student starts with *open-ended questioning,* e.g. “**How did this start?” Or “Tell me about it?”** Give the following story:

You fell while trying to carry a basket of laundry down the stairs.

**If asked for more details about the other bruises in presence of relative...**

You are clumsy and always running into things. It’s a running family joke how clumsy you are. You run into walls, furniture, even people sometimes. “Sometimes I get caught up in my thoughts and don’t watch where I’m going! (chuckle).

IF ASKED - You do not have any numbness or tingling in the fingertips.

IF the learner begins asking direct questions, answer as follows

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| How long have you been having these symptoms? | Since falling about 3 hours ago |
| Anything make it better | Holding the wrist still |
| Anything make it worse | Any movement in the wrist |
| Describe the pain | Throbbing sensation, any movement is excruciating |
| Location | Left wrist |
| Severity | …give it a 10 |
|  |  |
| PAST MEDICAL HISTORY | None |
|  |  |
| Hospitalizations/surgeries | Tonsillectomy, Hospital for child birth (10 months-old) |
| Medications | Birth control pills |
| Allergies | Motrin |
| FAMILY MEDICAL HISTORY | Both parents living out of state, in good health. |
| SOCIAL | * You are a stay at home mom, live with your husband and infant son * Husband works as a car salesman * No alcohol, no drugs * Smoke half a pack per day for past 6 years *(didn’t smoke while pregnant)*. |

Answers to any other medical questions should mirror your own or otherwise be “normal”

**The Relative**

If student asks, the relative introduces himself as “the father-in-law”. Patient’s husband is at work and patient could not drive herself due to the injury. Baby is being taken care of by mother-in-law. The student may/should (politely) ask the relative to step out. A savvy student may do this in a way that does not raise suspicion such as “I am going to examine the patient now, so you mind giving us a moment?” The relative is reluctant to leave, but obliges with statements to provider “how long is this going to take?” and to patient “I’ll be right outside.”

**Note**: If the student does not ask the relative to leave the room, then the patient will stick with the “I’m clumsy” story.

**Opening the Discussion after relative leaves the room:**

A Framing Question the student may ask:

* *“Because violence is so common in the patients I see, I ask all my patients this question”* or *“This pattern of injury is often caused by another person, did someone hurt you?”* or “*I don’t know if this is (or ever has been) a problem for you, but many of the patients I see are dealing with abusive relationships….”*

Your initial reaction will look something like:

* You anxiously admit that your husband became angry today and pulled on your wrist. *You pulled away from him, and your wrist hit the wall when it slipped out of his hand.*
* If you do not feel comfortable or are not being asked in a compassionate manner, you do not have to reveal violence.

**Conditional Statements**

IF the student asks how you sustained the other visible bruises on the arms.

* Response: “My husband yells, shoves, and hits me when he becomes angry. He’s always finding ways to point out that I am not very smart and I can’t do anything right. It’s my fault that he becomes angry.”

IF the learner asks “Is your child safe where you are currently living?”

* Response: “He has never hurt the baby, although the baby watches and starts to cry when he’s yelling at me or pushing me.”

IF the learner asks “Do you feel safe going home from here”?

* Response: “I am always afraid that he will get angry. He will kill me if he finds out I told you.”

IF the learner asks “Do you have any other injuries that you would like me to evaluate today?”

* Response: “My body is sore, especially my wrist. I just want to make sure it’s not broken.”

IF the learner asks “Do you have an escape plan in case things get very dangerous and you have to leave?”

* Response: “No. I have nowhere to go, and I have to take the baby with me.”

IF the learner hands you a resource card, accept it and hide it.

* Response: “I don’t want my husband to see this.”
