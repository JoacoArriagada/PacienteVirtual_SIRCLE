**Summary of Case:**

You are Anne Mercer, a 30-45 year old mother of 3 children. Eight weeks ago, you gave birth to your third child (first daughter)—Carrie Anne. At your 3-week post-partum check-up with your obstetrician, you mentioned feeling sad, anxious, and having low-energy since the birth of Carrie Anne. At the time, you were crying a lot. Dr. Martin (your obstetrician) believed that this was most likely a case of post-partum “blues” and prescribed a low dose of Zoloft. You were not nursing.

It is now 5 weeks later. Although you are no longer crying, you are feeling worse and Dr. Martin has referred you to the psychiatry clinic.

About a month ago, you noticed that you began to feel anxious about routine tasks. You had to meet with the teachers at your son’s school a few weeks ago and found yourself feeling panicky about being able to successfully accomplish this simple task. Initially, your experienced anxiety is as a feeling that “things aren’t safe,” “things feel too hard,” but the discomfort has progressed to feelings of dread. If asked, you describe dread about having to get up and feed the baby, dread about the morning and the need to get out of bed, dress and shower, and dread about going back to work. You are sleeping poorly, and you do not want to get out of bed in the morning. The thought of returning to your job—a position that you had previously found stimulating and gratifying—is overwhelming. You don’t understand why you are feeling this way. You can’t seem to feel any better and you have begun to believe it will never change.
