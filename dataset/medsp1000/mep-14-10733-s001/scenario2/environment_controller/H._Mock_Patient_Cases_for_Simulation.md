**Patient Identifier:** Amy Smith is a 4-year-old, otherwise healthy girl admitted four days ago for dehydration secondary to viral gastroenteritis.

**Subjective/Interval Events**:

* Amy did well yesterday
* Tolerated PO intake and fluids stopped yesterday evening
* She ate a small meal for dinner and breakfast this morning.
* No emesis since yesterday morning.
* Few small volume, loose stools over the course of the evening and into the morning.
* This morning, Mom asked about discharge plans.
* Not vomiting but getting Zofran every 6-8 hours as needed (four times in past 24 hours)

**Objective:**

*Vital signs*: Afebrile, HR 80s-90s, other vital signs within normal limits

*Intake/Output:*

Intake: 1.7 L (1000 mL IV, 700 mL PO)

Output: Only one diapers charted overnight, stool x4

*PE:*

General: Well-appearing young girl, alert and active.

HEENT: Well hydrated with moist mucous membranes and good cap refill.

Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally

Cardiovascular: Regular rate and rhythm. No murmurs, rubs or gallops

Abdomen: Abdomen soft, non-tender, non-distended

Skin: Capillary refill less than 2 seconds.

*Labs*:

BMP: Na 139, K 4.0, Cl 108, Bicarb 22, BUN 5, creatinine 0.4, glucose 106

**Assessment:** Amy is a healthy 4-year-old girl admitted for dehydration in the setting of emesis and diarrhea due to viral gastroenteritis. She is now euvolemic after IV fluid rehydration, which was discontinued last night, but she has had decreased urinary output and is not vomiting but continues to use Zofran every 6-8 hours.

**Plan:**

Viral gastroenteritis: Presented for one day of fever and significant emesis. Developed dehydration due to emesis and inability to tolerate PO. She is now s/p rehydration with IV fluids. She is tolerating PO well. Now has loose stools with decreased urinary output but overall appears euvolemic with improved PO intake. Supportive care, including Tylenol/ibuprofen prn

* Discontinue Zofran and monitor for further nausea/emesis
* Advance diet as tolerated, starting with bland foods
* Continue to encourage PO fluids
* Monitor urinary output

Disposition: Discuss with family and rest of the team
