The SP case evaluation tools used by the Tulane SP Program are long-established and reliable: they undergo review by the clinical skills staff and faculty, as well as by veteran SPs who weigh in on the logistical considerations of the cases, and they have been in use with large classes of students for multiple years. ***Patient Background:***

This patient (J. Landry) is a 25 - 50 year old male/female. S/he has come to the clinic as a new patient and has a complaint of a cough for 2 weeks that is getting worse.

S/he moved to this city from across the country and started waiting tables 2 months ago after being laid off from the job s/he had had for 5 years. S/he says waiting tables is always physically demanding but has become increasingly difficult due to his/her worsening cough. S/he cannot hold a conversation without coughing profusely, and it is hard for him/her to sleep through the night. S/he is worried about losing his/her job at the restaurant if his/her illness becomes worse.

S/he did not see a doctor initially because s/he lost health insurance when s/he was laid-off and could not afford an appointment with his/her old primary care physician. Since losing his/her job, his/her financial situation has become tenuous. S/he is living with a roommate whom s/he found on Craigslist to split rent, and borrowed money from a family member to pay for this appointment. Other than the one family member, no other family lives nearby – most are still across the country.

S/he is having a difficult time adjusting to his/her new financial/working situation. S/he has never been wealthy, however s/he has been financially independent from a young age. S/he says that s/he has been feeling depressed lately due to his/her current situation. S/he is also stressed because s/he is does not know of any resources or medical services available to help him/her until s/he gets better and is able to find more stable employment. Because of the depression and stress, the patient has been smoking more lately.

The patient broke up with his/her long-term partner 3 months ago and has recently started dating someone locally. S/He had 2 previous sexual partners. S/He was HIV tested 2 years ago when donated blood - was negative. The patient has completed three semesters of college. He/she likes his/her job as a waiter but hopes to save enough money to finish his/her degree.

*If asked, the SP will be forthcoming with all of the information provided above. The SP will use as much of their own history as needed to make the role believable and give the students ready answers to questions other than those on the checklist.*

***Goals for this case:***

We are particularly interested in making sure students understand the contributing social factors affecting this patient’s condition: a difficult job situation / financial instability, increased stress triggering increased smoking (likely exacerbating the cough), and a minimal social support network in the city.

As you complete the evaluation & add written comments for the students, please particularly assess whether students followed up regarding inability to pay for medication (even over the counter), and increased smoking under stress. Ideally, the student should tell patient that they will find or connect them with resources to facilitate obtaining the needed medication, and comment about smoking as a health risk and/or provide smoking cessation counseling.

***Session Logistics:***

Students have been advised that they have 20 minutes to conduct a full patient history. Prior to the session, they will be given a brief patient chart with the patient’s name and chief complaint. They are not required to perform a physical exam.

Once the student has concluded the session, or when time runs out, give the student the “Post-Session Key Points for Students” document; they will review this prior to completing a self-evaluation exercise.

Finally, complete the “Contexts of Care Case Checklist,” giving students feedback on their history-taking and communication skills, as well as their ability to elicit and address the social considerations of the case. This document will be given to them once you have completed it, for them to review after their self-evaluation.

**Standardized Patient Self-Evaluation Exercise:**

**Expectations for Students**

This SP session is intended to give you extra practice in taking patient histories, and to help you assess your strengths & weaknesses. During the session, the SP will score your performance in terms of communication skills and thoroughness regarding history-taking and counseling. After the session, you will read a summary of the case’s key points, watch your video (if one was taken), complete a self-evaluation exercise, and review the SP evaluation of your performance.

***Goals*:**

* Practice the components of the general medical interview
* Practice communication skills relevant to medical history taking
* Identify strengths and prioritize areas for improvement

***Learning Objectives*:**

By the end of this session, you will be able to:

* Take a complete patient history: chief complaint, history of present illness, past medical history, social history, family history, and review of systems
* Provide any counseling you feel is appropriate
* Review and reflect on your own communication and interviewing skills

***Expectations & Grading*:**

* Attendance (sign the sign-in sheet) & review of patient chart & student instructions
* Completion of patient history during the SP encounter: you may use any reference materials you choose. Take notes!
* Completion of the documentation exercise (type a note) after the SP encounter
* Performance on checklist (scored by the SP)
* Completion of post-session tasks:
  1. Review the post-session key points document
  2. Review of video (if taken) and completion of self-evaluation assignment
  3. Review the SP’s evaluation: see if your assessment of your performance lines up with the SP’s
