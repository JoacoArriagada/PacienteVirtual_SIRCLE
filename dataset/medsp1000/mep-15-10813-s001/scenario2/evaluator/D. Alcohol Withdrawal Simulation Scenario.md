**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The Lead Fellow and Support Fellow enter the room, where the ICU nurse provides a brief patient history and notes worsening agitation, hypertension, and tachycardia. The Lead Fellow asks for fingerstick glucose check, full vital signs and asks the Support Fellow to examine the patient, including a focused neurologic exam, while obtaining further history from the ICU intern. The Lead Fellow considers multiple etiologies for agitated delirium, but has a high suspicion for alcohol withdrawal given the available information. After seizure develops, the Lead Fellow requests benzodiazepine administration. The nurse questions the management decision, as he/she is concerned that benzodiazepines will worsen delirium. The Lead Fellow navigates the conflict by acknowledging the concern and agreeing benzodiazepines are typically avoided in delirium, but feels this is the treatment of choice for the patient given the suspicion for alcohol withdrawal. After one dose of benzodiazepine is given and there is no clinical response, the Lead Fellow requests an additional dose, understanding multiple doses may be necessary. The Lead Fellow also discusses with the team other medical treatments for refractory seizure. After benzodiazepines are administered, the patient becomes obtunded and hypoxemic. The Lead Fellow recognizes the patient’s inability to protect her airway and proceeds with intubation. After intubation and stabilization, plans for head imaging and potential CSF sampling are made to evaluate for alternative etiologies of refractory seizure. Throughout the simulation, the team exhibits closed loop communication with call-outs and check-backs, and the team has a shared mental model. At the end of the scenario, the Lead Fellow leads a team debrief to discuss what went well and what could be improved in the future.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Inability to recognize status epilepticus: If the fellow does not recognize status epilepticus and uses haloperidol for agitated delirium, this will lower the seizure threshold and result in increased seizures.
2. Uncertainty about doses of antiepileptics: Some learners may be uncomfortable with dosing of antiepileptics. One option is to have the intern ask if they should call the pharmacist for recommendations, leave the room, and return shortly thereafter with the appropriate dosing information.
3. Inability to navigate conflict: The learner will be challenged by having a team member who disagrees with their approach to management. A successful learner will acknowledge the nurse’s suggestion and discomfort with benzodiazepines, integrate their input, and build a shared mental model.

**Debriefing Materials: Alcohol Withdrawal**

Status epilepticus is defined as:

* Continuous seizure lasting ≥ 5 minutes
* ≥2 seizures with incomplete recovery of consciousness

Metabolic causes of seizure should always be considered in refractory seizures and treated aggressively.

* Hypo- or hypernatremia
* Hypo- or hyperglycemia
* Hypo- or hypercalcemia
* Hypomagnesemia
* Withdrawal from alcohol or benzodiazepines
* Drug intoxication
* Hyperammonemia/acute liver failure

Alcohol enhances inhibitory tone (via GABA) and inhibits excitatory tone (via amino acids); abrupt cessation leads to CNS over-reactivity.

Alcohol withdrawal timeline:

* 6-36 hours: tremulousness, headache, diaphoresis
* 12-48 hours: seizures, hallucinations
* 48-96 hours: delirium tremens with fevers, delirium, hypertension

Delirium Tremens (DT)

* DT is defined by the presence of disorientation, in addition to hallucinations and sympathetic changes such as tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia, diaphoresis
* DT can be life threatening, with death typically due to arrhythmia or concomitant illness, though mortality from DT is less than 5% if treated appropriately
* Prevention is by treating withdrawal symptoms

Alcohol withdrawal management:

* Benzodiazepines: remain mainstay of therapy; increase frequency of GABA channel opening
  + Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV (~2-4 mg IV q15-20)
  + Diazepam 0.2 mg/kg IV (~5-10 mg IV q5-10 minutes)
  + Midazolam 0.2 mg/kg IV (or 10 mg IM)
  + Chlordiazepoxide 25-100 mg PO q1h
* Phenobarbital increases duration of GABA channel opening and suppresses glutamate activity: 130-260 mg IV q15-20 minutes (t1/2 50-140 hours)
* Propofol: opens chloride channels in absence of GABA
