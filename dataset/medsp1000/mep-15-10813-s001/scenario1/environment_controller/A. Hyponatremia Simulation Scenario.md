| **Actors and roles in the room at case start** | *Who is present at the beginning and what is their role? Who may play them?*  ICU fellow/primary learner:  Lead Fellow who will be responsible for directing the care of the patient.  Procedure/support fellow: Optional Support Fellow to assist the Lead Fellow as needed. The role should be played by a critical care fellow without prior knowledge of the scenario.  The patient (manikin):  Represented by a manikin with voice portrayed by a simulation staff member playing through speakers into the room. The staff member provides the voice of the patient, who is sleepy but responds to loud voice, noxious stimuli, and answers yes/no questions occasionally.  ICU intern (confederate):  A faculty or simulation staff member should perform the role of intern. The intern has additional patient history and can page staff if requested by the fellow.  ICU nurse (confederate):  The ICU nurse should be performed by a critical care nurse who will represent the Medical ICU nurse for the recently admitted the patient with confusion. The nurse will call the Lead Fellow to evaluate the patient’s acute change in mental status.  ICU respiratory therapist (confederate):  A respiratory therapist should perform this role and assist with respiratory care needs.  Observer (confederate):  A faculty member who will observe in the room and end the scenario. | | |
| **HPI** | The patient is a 68 year-old man admitted to the Medical ICU 30 minutes ago with confusion and severe hyponatremia. He becomes progressively more confused while in the ICU. His family brought him to the hospital after he was acting strangely at home, and initial evaluation in the Emergency Department revealed a large left lung mass and a serum sodium level of 108 mEq/L.  Additional information available if requested (provided by the ICU intern):  Labs from the Emergency Department:  Complete blood count: within normal limits  Basic metabolic panel: Na: 108 mEq/L; Cl: 75 mEq/L; otherwise normal  Liver function testing: within normal limits  Blood and urine toxicology: no substances detected  Urinalysis: within normal limits  Urine sodium: 45 mEq/L  Serum osmolality: 235 mOsm/kg  Urine osmolality: 650 mOsm/kg  Chest radiograph: 5 cm mass in the mid-left lung adjacent to the hilum.  Head CT: no acute intracranial abnormality.  Emergency Department neurological examination:  He was lethargic, but easily arousable. Only oriented to self. Moved all extremities. No abnormal movements.  Emergency Department course:  1.5 liters of 0.9% NaCl was given. Vital signs remained stable with heart rate in the 70s/min, blood pressure 130s/60s mmHg, and respiratory rate 12-14/min with oxygen saturation of 98%. | | |

| Possible Interventions:   * Adjustments to patient monitor requested: the nurse makes the change * Anesthesiology requested: the intern will leave the room to page, and return one minute later letting the team know that they are performing an emergent intubation and will arrive as soon as possible * Neurology consult requested: the intern will leave the room to page, and return one minute later letting the team know the neurologist is on the way * Benzodiazepine administration: no change in scenario with 1 or 2 doses, or total dose less than 2 mg; if 3 or more doses given and total dose is 2 mg or greater, then progress to Act 3 * Antiepileptic administration: no change to scenario * Hypertonic saline requested: saline at concentrations ≥ 2% can be ordered but will not arrive until Act 3 * Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3) administered: no change in scenario with 1 ampule of NaHCO3, seizures stop with 2 ampules of NaHCO3 * Other sedatives administered: proceed to Act 3 * Decision made to proceed with intubation: no change in scenario; proceed with uncomplicated intubation |
