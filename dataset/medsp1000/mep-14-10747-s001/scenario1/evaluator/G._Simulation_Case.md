Ideal Scenario Flow

Initial Session:

The learner watches a demonstration video immediately prior to entering the simulation environment while using skills checklist (Appendix F). Then the learner enters the room and receives the patient case and history. Next the learner discusses the need for a diagnostic and therapeutic paracentesis with the patient, including the risks and benefits and obtains informed consent. The learner then washes his/her hands, positions patient and examines the abdomen. The learner uses physical exam and ultrasound to determine site of needle insertion. The learner then performs a “time out”, washes his/her hands and puts on sterile gloves. The learner then cleanses the site of needle insertion and sterilely performs a diagnostic and therapeutic paracentesis including all steps outlined in Appendix F. After fluid removal, the learner applies a bandage to the site of needle insertion. Performance of the procedure is done under close guidance and feedback of the instructor who evaluates the learner using the Paracentesis Competency Assessment Tool (PCAT) (Appendix F). Feedback is given when steps are omitted or performed incorrectly. The learner can then repeat such steps. The simulation is complete when the learner reaches the Minimum Passing Standard, which is defined as the aggregate score on the PCAT that signifies ability to perform the procedure on a live patient under direct supervision.

Subsequent Session(s):

This session is identical to previous however does not include the initial “practice session” where the learner reviews the steps of the procedure with the instructor or watches a pre-procedural video. Instead the learner moves immediately into an observed procedure without any intervention by the instructor until the procedure is complete. At that point the instructor uses the skills checklist (Appendix F) to go over missed or incorrectly performed steps and the learner can repeat these steps. The simulation is complete when the learner has no additional questions and reaches the Minimum Passing Standard.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

* Not obtaining informed consent from patient before performing procedure: Paracentesis cannot be ethically performed in this way, learner cannot pass simulation unless repeated with proper informed consent
* Not washing hands before paracentesis and/or not cleaning needle insertion area with sterilized solution before procedure: Procedure is considered unsterile and learner cannot pass the simulation unless repeated in sterile technique
* While advancing the catheter/needle complex, not continually aspirating until peritoneal fluid is aspirated: Procedure is considered unsafe and learner cannot pass simulation unless repeated with proper technique
* Use of incorrect ultrasound probe: Some trainees selected the linear array probe instead of the preferred phased array or curvilinear probe. During the first session this should be corrected in real-time, in subsequent sessions trainees should be made aware of the incorrect choice after completion of the paracentesis
* Forgetting to perform time out: Trainees often forget to perform this pre-procedural session, they should be reminded of this safety feature and its importance
* Not washing hands with soap and water: While still considered sterile, trainees should be reminded of improved sterility by washing just prior to gloving
* When cleaning, placing swabs on sterile field or back in pouch: Trainees should be instructed to place used swabs in the trash
* Touching areas of gown/unsterile field with sterile gloves: Trainees should be notified of broken sterility and need for new gloves
* For anesthesia, forgetting to switch to smaller needle: Trainees should be reminded to start anesthesia with the smallest gauge needle available, keeping the comfort of the patient in mind
* When advancing catheter, remove needle to early so not possible to make catheter flush with skin: Trainees should be instructed to not retract the needle until the catheter is flush with the skin
