4. **Environment**
   1. **Simulation Environment Setup:** Simulation lab Emergency Department room, medication area (Pyxis or second area), audio-visual control room, debrief room/area
   2. **Manikin Setup:** No manikin (standardized patient with virtual vital signs)
   3. **Audiovisual Material:** audio-visual feed from room with capture of standardized patient and vital signs monitor. See **Appendix A** for additional setup details.
   4. **Props:** See **Appendix C** for additional setup details.
5. **Actors**
   1. **Roles & Role Activities:**
      1. Patient: Adult standardized patient with simulated vital signs. EKG 3-lead, BP, Pulse oximetry and IV. See **Appendix B** for actor notes and presentation.
      2. Physician(s): One to three participant physicians. Multiple configurations are possible.
      3. Course Director: Physician educator primarily responsible for participant learning.
      4. Simulation Education Specialist: Clinician facilitating education from a technical and environmental perspective.
      5. Nurse (confederate): At least one nurse is required for this scenario. The nurse will carry out orders and, when needed, redirect the learners.
      6. Respiratory Therapist: This role may not be needed if the scenario progresses quickly.
      7. Family member: Adult standardized patient (or faculty member playing the role) who will deliver an empty medication bottle to the physician team several minutes into the simulation.
6. **Case Narrative**
   1. **Scenario Background**
      1. Chief Complaint: The patient presents with hypotension, bradycardia and somnolence. He states he intentionally took painkillers to ‘party’ but he doesn’t feel right.
      2. History: Narcotic Drug abuse, use of street drugs. No other history.
      3. Medications: None
      4. Allergies: NKDA
      5. Family and Social History: The patient is unable to provide further information due to increasing somnolence and onset of mild delirium.
   2. **Scenario Initial State**
      1. Patient Habitus and Presentation: Patient reclining in ED bed having difficulty staying awake.
      2. **Physiology:** T 37.2, P 30-40, BP 100/60 R 12 SPO2 98%. Narrow complex rhythm with bradycardia on monitor.
   3. **Scenario Branch Points**
      1. **Scenario B:** The patient is experiencing severe somnolence and progressive delirium.
         1. **Changes in Patient Condition:** The patient's condition will deteriorate over the course of the scenario. He will become increasingly somnolent and delirious.
         2. **Ineffective interventions:** If naloxone is given, the heart rate will not respond to the medication and the patient will continue to vomit. If activated charcoal is given, the actor is to portray having an aspiration episode. If cardiac pacing is used, it will fail to capture. If a trans venous pacer is requested, the equipment is unavailable.
      3. **New Vital Signs:** SPO2 98% with Oxygen, BP 100/60 P30-40, RR20
      4. **Pacing:** The patient’s cardiac status will not stabilize with transcutaneous pacing. Transvenous pacing is not the focus of this scenario and if attempted, equipment is unavailable.
      5. **Laboratory Studies:** Laboratory results are located in **Appendix H**.
      6. **X-ray and EKG:** Results are located in **Appendix G**.
      7. **Medications:**
         1. **IV Normal Saline Bolus**: Has no appreciable impact on patient condition.
         2. **Calcium:** Calcium gluconate, 30 to 60mLs of 10% solution, infused over 10-15 minutes or Calcium chloride 10-20mLs of a 10% solution, infused over 20 minutes. In this scenario, the calcium infusion will have only a minimal effect in raising the heart rate (heart rate 45-55).
         3. **Glucagon:** 3-5 mg IV in this scenario will have minimal effect. Glucagon in these high dosages often causes vomiting. If no antiemetic was given, the patient will vomit.
         4. **Naloxone:** 0.4-2mg IV push. This will have no effect on the patient’s delirium or bradycardia.
         5. **Atropine:** 0.5mg IV push. This will have no effect on the patient’s bradycardia.
         6. **High-Dose Insulin:** A bolus push of 1u/kg IV of regular insulin followed by a continuous infusion of 1u/kg/hr. This is to be administered concurrently with a bolus of 25g of dextrose followed by an infusion of D10% solution. In this scenario, the rhythm will remain bradycardic but the blood pressure will increase to 110/70. Follow-up glucose and potassium measurements may be needed during treatment.
         7. **Intravenous Lipid emulsion:** A bolus of 1.5mL/kg IV of a 20% solution followed by a continuous infusion of 0.25mL/kg/min. In this scenario, this will improve the rhythm to a heart rate of 50-60 with a BP of 105/70.
         8. **Activated Charcoal:** If given, the patient is to portray having an aspiration event.
         9. **Whole bowel irrigation:** Polyethylene glycol may be recommended in overdoses of sustained release preparations, not necessary in this setting.
         10. **Vasopressors:** Dopamine, Epinephrine, and norepinephrine: These drugs, if given in this scenario, the BP will increase to 105/70.
         11. **ECMO and IABP:** Unnecessary in this case and unavailable if requested.
      8. **Consultation of Specialist(s):** Consultation with the Critical Care service, Cardiology service, and Toxicology service (or Poison Control Center) is required.
7. **Instructor Notes**
   1. **Scenario Flow:** Instructors should provide the scenario background card from **Appendix D** to the learners just before the start of the scenario.
   2. **Virtual Vital Signs:** Instructors familiar with virtual vital signs may wish to directly control, or work side by side with a simulation technician to allow flexible response to participant actions.
   3. **Scenario Programming:** Scenario programming for virtual vital signs can be accomplished prior to the event; triggers may include time and medications.

**Appendix B**

Moulage & Standardized Patient Support

**Moulage** included glycerine mist to simulate sweating. If activated charcoal given and simulation of vomiting is wanted: simulated vomit created by mixing oatmeal with cocoa powder and a small amount of Karo syrup. If the patient is not actively vomiting, fill a vomitus bag and smudge the patient’s gown or skin with a small amount.

The **standardized patient** (SP) is a 40 year old man or woman with a history of narcotic drug abuse who presents with somnolence and delirium. The SP will present as sleepy and appearing drugged and will only say limited responses as described in the source text. The standardized patient will deny suicidal thoughts or intent, deny taking any other medications, and if pressed may indicate taking 20 pills and liking Oxy.

During the scenario, the SP will become increasingly delirious (as if drunk or drugged) and will not be able to provide any further coherent information. The SP is unable to provide a health history.
