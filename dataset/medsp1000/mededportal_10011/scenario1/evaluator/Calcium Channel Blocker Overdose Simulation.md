1. **Internal Medicine Pre-Code Scenario: Calcium Channel Blocker Overdose**
2. **Target audience:** Emergency Medicine residents as well as Internal Medicine / Family Medicine Residents and 3rd/4th year medical students doing an Emergency Medicine rotation
3. **Learning Objectives**
   1. **Primary**
      1. Demonstrate appropriate assessment of a patient with hypotension, bradycardia, and hyperglycemia
      2. Demonstrate initial approach to evaluating a poisoned patient
      3. Consider underlying causes of the presenting condition: Calcium channel blocker Toxicity
      4. Identify causative agent through history, physical, exam and laboratory values
      5. Initiate appropriate treatment to reverse cause including fluids and calcium
      6. Consideration of other modalities of treatment including high dose insulin therapy, lipid emulsion therapy, glucagon, and vasopressors.
   2. **Secondary**
      1. Develop and discuss differential diagnosis for patient presentation such as digoxin, beta blocker, clonidine or opioid toxicity as well as heart block
      2. Determine a reason for ingestion
      3. Identify appropriate specialties for consult including Cardiology, Toxicology, Poison Control, and Critical Care
   3. **Critical Actions Checklist**
      1. Perform a rapid assessment: CAB, vital signs
      2. Perform a focused history & examination
      3. Initiate treatment:
         1. Oxygen
         2. IV Fluids
         3. Place patient on cardiac monitor
         4. Bedside glucose testing
         5. Administration of calcium; consideration of additional therapies
         6. Referral to critical care and Cardiology
      4. Request appropriate tests
         1. Lab tests , Chest x-ray and EKG: See Appendix F-H
      5. Perform organized and focused hand-off to the Critical Care Service
      6. Contact inpatient Toxicology or a Poison Control Center to seek expert advice
4. **Environment**
   1. **Simulation Environment Setup:** Simulation lab Emergency Department room, medication area (Pyxis or second area), audio-visual control room, debrief room/area
   2. **Manikin Setup:**  No manikin (standardized patient with virtual vital signs)
   3. **Audiovisual Material:**  audio-visual feed from room with capture of standardized patient and vital signs monitor See **Appendix A** for additional setup details
   4. **Props:** See **Appendix C** for additional setup details
5. **Actors**
   1. **Roles & Role Activities:**
      1. Patient: Adult standardized patient with simulated vital signs. EKG 3-lead, BP, Pulse oximetry and IV. See **Appendix B** for actor notes and presentation.
      2. Physician(s): One to three participant physicians. Multiple configurations are possible. The Course Director may choose a primary or may have learners work in groups.
      3. Course Director: Physician educator primarily responsible for participant learning
      4. Simulation Education Specialist: Clinician facilitating education from a technical and environmental perspective.
      5. Nurse (confederate): At least one nurse who is familiar with the learning objectives is required for this scenario. The nurse will carry out orders and, when needed, redirect the learners toward the learning objectives.
      6. Respiratory Therapist: This role may not be needed if the scenario progresses quickly. A trained professional who is capable of setting up breathing treatment equipment may play this role.
      7. Family member: Adult standardized patient (or faculty member playing the role) who will deliver an empty medication bottle to the physician team several minutes into the simulation
6. **Case Narrative**
   1. **Scenario Background**
      1. Chief Complaint: The patient presents with hypotension, bradycardia and somnolence. He states he intentionally took painkillers to ‘party’ but he doesn’t feel right.
      2. History: Narcotic Drug abuse, use of street drugs. No other history.
      3. Medications: None
      4. Allergies: NKDA
      5. Family and Social History: The patient is unable to provide further information due to increasing somnolence and onset of mild delirium.
   2. **Scenario Initial State**
      1. Patient Habitus and Presentation: Patient reclining in ED bed having difficulty staying awake.
      2. **Physiology:** T 37.2, P 30-40, BP 100/60 R 12 SPO2 98%. Narrow complex rhythm with bradycardia on monitor.
   3. **Scenario Branch Points**
      1. **Scenario A:**  The patient is experiencing very mild delirium and somnolence. Expectations for this scenario include making a timely diagnosis of the current condition (especially after information is provided by the family member)and then instituting a treatment plan focused on aggressive treatment of the bradycardia, likely first with IV atropine and IV calcium, and then with high dose insulin therapy and lipid emulsion therapy. The IV atropine should have little effect on the patient’s bradycardia. The IV calcium will have a larger effect and the heart rate should rise in response. The use of the high dose insulin therapy and the lipid emulsion therapy, should be considered for refractory symptoms but will not be necessary to stabilize the patient in this branch.
         1. **Changes in Patient Condition:** The patient will not deteriorate over the course of this scenario. A combination of IV fluid, calcium, atropine, high dose insulin therapy, and lipid emulsion therapy
         2. **Ineffective interventions: I**f naloxone is given, the heart rate will not respond to the medication (bradycardia to continue) and patient will continue to vomit. If activated charcoal is asked for the nurse will ask if giving it is a good idea based on his metal status (mild delirium). If physician is insistent on giving activated charcoal, actor is to portray having an aspiration episode.
      3. **New Vital Signs:** SPO2 98% with Oxygen, BP 100/60 P30-40, RR20
      4. **Pacing:** The patient’s cardiac status will not stabilize with transcutaneous pacing. Transvenous pacing is not the focus of this scenario and if attempted, equipment is unavailable.
      5. **Laboratory Studies:** Laboratory results are located in **Appendix H**
      6. **X-ray and EKG:** Results are located in **Appendix G**
      7. **Medications:**
         1. **IV Normal Saline Bolus**: Has no appreciable impact on patient condition
         2. **Calcium:** Calcium gluconate, 30 to 60mLs of 10% solution, infused over 10-15 minutes or Calcium chloride 10-20mLs of a 10% solution, infused over 20 minutes. In this scenario, it will improve the rhythm to normal sinus (heart rate 65-75).
         3. **Glucagon:** 3-5 mg IV in this scenario will improve rhythm to normal sinus. Glucagon in these high dosages often causes vomiting. If no antiemetic was given, the patient will vomit.
         4. **Naloxone:** 0.4-2mg IV push. This will have no effect on the patient’s delirium or bradycardia
         5. **Atropine:** 0.5mg IV push. This will have no effect on the patient’s bradycardia
         6. **High-Dose Insulin:** a bolus push of1u/kg IV of regular insulin followed by a continuous infusion of 1u/kg/hr. This is to be administered concurrently with a bolus of 25g of dextrose followed by an infusion of D10% solution (starting rate usually 100mL/hr) to achieve euglycemia. In this scenario, the rhythm will remain bradycardic but the blood pressure (BP) will increase to 120/80. High dose insulin is typically given with glucose but additional glucose may not be necessary in calcium channel blocker overdoses if significant hyperglycemia is present. Participants should follow glucose and potassium measurements every 20 minutes. Calcium channel blocker overdoses initially present with hyperglycemia but may require increased glucose supplementation as they clinically improve.
         7. **Intravenous Lipid emulsion:** a bolus of 1.5mL/kg IV of a 20% solution followed by a continuous infusion of 0.25mL/kg/min. In this scenario, if given, this will improve the rhythm to normal sinus (heart rate 65-75) and the blood pressure (BP) will increase to 120/80. It may be unnecessary due to clinical improvement with calcium alone.
         8. **Activated Charcoal**: to be discouraged from using this by the nurse; if given, the patient is to portray having an aspiration event. Activated charcoal may be appropriate in patients who present within one hour of an acute ingestion who have no mental status changes. Multi-dose activated charcoal, is also inappropriate in this patient due to aspiration risk and risk of ileus.
         9. **Whole bowel irrigation:** polyethylene glycol may be recommended in overdoses of sustained release preparations, not necessary in this setting.
         11. **ECMO and IABP:** There are case reports to support both but unnecessary in this case as patient will improve with medical management and are unavailable if requested.
      8. **Consultation of Specialist(s**): Depending on the stability of the patient and the knowledge base of the learners, we strongly suggest consultation with the Critical Care service and Cardiology service. Toxicology service (and/or Poison Control Center) should be consulted (see appendix J, K,L for materials from Florida Poison Control).
7. **Instructor Notes**
   1. **Scenario Flow:**  Instructors should provide the scenario background card from **Appendix D** to the learners just before the start of the scenario.
   2. **Virtual Vital Signs:** Instructors familiar with virtual vital signs may wish to directly control, or work side by side with a simulation technician to allow flexible response to participant actions. It has been this author’s experience that having the capability to vary the patient response on-the-fly enhances the learning experience.
* 1. **Key Learner Events:**
     1. Order oxygen
     2. Request appropriate tests: Lab, EKG, Chest x-ray, ABG
     3. Identification of the causative agent: Calcium channel blocker overdose
     4. Early cardiovascular support with initiation of IV fluids
     5. Order IV calcium infusion
     6. Consideration of high dose insulin therapy, lipid emulsion therapy, glucagon
     7. Identification as to reason for ingestion (suicide attempt vs. accidental)
     8. Request appropriate consults: Critical Care service, Cardiology service, and Toxicology service (and/or Poison Control Center).
  2. **Information for Actors:**  See Actor Prompt Sheet in **Appendix B**
  3. **Scenario Programming:** Scenario programming for virtual vital signs can be accomplished prior to the event, however, we recommend a flexible approach with variable vital signs directed by the instructor or planned for execution based on a trigger event. Triggers may include time and medications.
**Appendix B**

Moulage & Standardized Patient Support

**Moulage** included glycerine mist to simulate sweating. If activated charcoal given and simulation of vomiting is wanted: simulated vomit created by mixing oatmeal with cocoa powder and a small amount of Karo syrup. If the patient is not actively vomiting, fill a vomitus bag and smudge the patient’s gown or skin with a small amount.

The **standardized patient** (SP) is a 40 year old man or woman with a history of narcotic drug abuse who presents with somnolence and delirium. The SP will present as sleepy and appearing drugged and will only say things like, “I wanted to get high so I took a lot of my mother’s pain pills, they began with a “D,” and “I don’t feel well. The standardized patient will deny suicidal thoughts or intent. The standardized patient will deny taking any other medications and will admit that they have never been hurt by getting high before. If pressed the SP may indicate that he or she, “took 20 pills” and that he or she likes taking Oxy. The SP motivation is that she or he buys prescription pain killers on the street, prefers Oxy, but will take anything he or she can get.

During this scenario, the SP has very mild delirium and somnolence and will not deteriorate. The SP is unable to provide a health history.
