Pediatric Emergency Medicine Simulation Curriculum: Hyperkalemia due to   
Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia

# 2. Description of Scenario

# Scenario Overview

This scenario teaches learners to recognize and manage hyperkalemia secondary to congenital adrenal hyperplasia.

The scenario is simulation-based with a focus on medical management of a complex patient.

Learners will participate in a simulation scenario and be asked to identify the signs and symptoms of adrenal crisis and hyperkalemia as well as direct appropriate management (See Appendix A).

At the end of training, the learners should be able to recognize the seriousness of the situation and recruit a full resuscitation team. The primary complication for the team to recognize and manage is dysrhythmia due to hyperkalemia as a presentation of the patient’s undiagnosed congenital adrenal hyperplasia.

# 5. Goals and Objectives

# Goal 1: Recognition and Management of Undiagnosed Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH)/Adrenal Crisis

The learner will demonstrate recognition of adrenal crisis in a safe and professional manner. (ACGME Competencies: Medical KnowledgeA, Patient CareB, Interpersonal and Communication SkillsC, ProfessionalismD)

# Objective 1a - Initial patient assessment [A, B, C, D]

The learner will be expected to discuss what s/he would look for in an initial physical examination (primary survey e.g. airway, breathing, circulation, disability, exposure) and history (SAMPLE- Signs and symptoms, Allergies, Medications, Past medical history, Last meal, and Events) of any pediatric patient s/he is evaluating.

# Objective 1b – Appropriate monitoring [A, B, C]

The learner should apply basic monitoring standards for a patient in shock (cardiorespiratory monitors, pulse oximetry, blood pressure, and temperature). S/he should obtain a patient weight or accurate estimate (e.g. Broselow-Luten Tape).

# Objective 1c - Identification of adrenal crisis[A, B, C]

The learner should identify symptoms (vomiting, lethargy, failure to thrive) and physical exam findings (tachycardia, dry mucous membranes, poor tone) consistent with adrenal crisis as well as laboratory abnormalities (hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, hypoglycemia).

# Objective 1d - Management of adrenal crisis with hydrocortisone [A, B]

The learner should administer intravenous or intraosseous hydrocortisone. Pediatric dosing for the initial hydrocortisone dose is $5 0 ~ \mathsf { m g } /$ /meter squared, and neonatal dosing is $2 5 { \mathsf { m g } }$ .3 Volume depletion, hyponatremia, and hypoglycemia should also be addressed with isotonic fluid boluses and dextrose infusion as needed.

# Goal 2: Prompt Establishment of Intravenous or Intraosseous Access

The learner will identify the need for prompt intravenous or intraosseous access to facilitate further treatment. (ACGME Competencies: Medical KnowledgeA, Patient CareB, Technical SkillsF)

# Objective 2a - Demonstrate understanding of the relevant anatomy for intravenous or interosseous access [A, B, F]

The learner should be able to identify pediatric anatomy necessary for intravenous or intraosseous access.

Objective 2b - Demonstrate the ability to gain intravenous or intraosseous access. [A, B, F]

The learner should position the patient for intravenous/ intraosseous access. Using appropriate clean/sterile technique, the learner should insert either an intravenous or intraosseous line. Intravenous fluids and medications should be administered rapidly.

# Goal 3: Recognition and Management of Hyperkalemia

The learner will identify and treat hyperkalemia in adrenal crisis in a safe and professional manner. (ACGME Competencies: Medical KnowledgeA, Patient CareB, Interpersonal and Communication SkillsC, ProfessionalismD, Systems-based PracticeE, Technical SkillsF)

# 5. Goals and Objectives

# Objective 3a – Identification of hyperkalemia [A, B, F]

The learner should identify the peaked T waves on cardiac monitoring and/or identify the risk for hyperkalemia given the presentation of adrenal crisis. Further confirmation should be made using bedside electrolyte evaluation.

# Objective 3b – Management of hyperkalemia [A, B]

The learner should recognize that hyperkalemia with associated cardiac conduction abnormalities is a medical emergency. The learner should administer intravenous or intraosseous calcium, as either calcium chloride $( 2 0 \mathrm { m g } / \mathrm { k g } )$ or calcium gluconate $\lvert 1 0 0 \mathrm { m g / k g } \rvert$ , to stabilize the cardiac membranes and prevent deterioration into a ventricular arrhythmia.4 They should then proceed with measures to shift potassium intracellularly (bicarbonate, insulin/glucose, albuterol).2

Optional Goal 4: Management of Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (If optional stage 3 is used) The learner will identify ventricular tachycardia and manage it according to PALS guidelines.5 (ACGME Competencies: Medical KnowledgeA, Patient CareB, Technical SkillsF)

# Objective 4a – Identification of ventricular tachycardia [A, B]

The learner will identify ventricular tachycardia on cardiac monitoring.

# Objective 4b- Cardiopulmonary resuscitation [A, B, F]

The learner will provide high quality chest compressions at an appropriate rate and depth. Chest compressions should be interrupted for adequate bag-mask ventilation but other interruptions will be kept to a minimum. Quality of CPR will be monitored and adjustments made as needed.

# Objective 4c- Defibrillation for pulseless ventricular tachycardia [A, B, F]

The learner will demonstrate correct placement of pediatric defibrillation pads and the ability to properly engage defibrillator. Situational awareness and safety will be demonstrated when discharging unit. The learner should demonstrate knowledge of appropriate doses and delivery of electricity for the treatment of pulseless ventricular tachycardia.

# Goal 5: Teamwork and Communication Skills

The learner will become more skilled in the management and leadership of emergency personnel including physicians, nurses, and ancillary personnel. (ACGME Competencies: Interpersonal and Communication SkillsC, ProfessionalismD, System-based PracticeE)

# Objective 5a – Team structure and leadership [C, D, E]

The learner will be exposed to a full-scale manikin-based simulation in which the learners are faced with a life threatening emergency due to hyperkalemia caused by adrenal crisis. They will be expected to clearly identify (verbally or with visual cues) and maintain a team leader (orders, priorities verbally stated by team leader) and team member roles (questions, information directed to team leader).

# 7. Common Errors and Prevention Strategies

# Common Errors and Prevention Strategies:

# a. Failure to recognize adrenal crisis.

Strategy: Review signs of adrenal crisis: failure to thrive, ambiguous genitalia or genital hyperpigmentation, vomiting, shock (hypotension, dry mucous membranes, altered mental status), hyperkalemia (peaked T waves on monitor, abnormal laboratory values, ventricular tachyarrhythmia), hypoglycemia, hyponatremia.

# b. Failure to treat adrenal crisis.

Strategy: Review treatment for adrenal crisis: intravenous or intraosseous hydrocortisone, fluids and oxygen for treatment of shock, and correction of electrolyte abnormalities, namely hyperkalemia (calcium, glucose, insulin, bicarbonate, albuterol, furosemide, sodium polystyrene), hyponatremia (isotonic fluids), and hypoglycemia (dextrose boluses and ongoing continuous infusion).

c. Failure to provide supplemental oxygen.

Strategy: Review goal of supplemental oxygen: to improve oxygen delivery and meet metabolic demand, preventing progression to cardiac arrest. This is not dependent on measured oxygen saturation. Review equipment available for oxygen administration.

d. Failure to achieve timely intravenous or intraosseous access.

Strategy: Review anatomy, equipment and process for insertion of IV/IO. Review PALS goal of IV or IO access within 90 seconds of cardiopulmonary failure. In this scenario, the patient is not arresting, however is at risk for rapid decompensation.

e. Failure to rapidly administer intravenous or intraosseous isotonic crystalloid fluids. Strategy: Review goal of administration of isotonic crystalloid fluids, $2 0 \mathrm { m L } / \kappa \varrho$ boluses over 5- 20 min until blood pressure is restored and tissue perfusion improved. Review equipment available to achieve this (e.g. pressure bag, 3-way stop-cock and $6 0 ~ \mathsf { m L }$ syringe, etc).

f. Failure to follow PALS guidelines for pulseless ventricular tachycardia. (if optional stage 3 used)

Strategy: Review PALS guidelines including appropriate chest compression to breath ratio and initiation of rapid defibrillation for pulseless ventricular tachycardia.

g. Inefficient teamwork

Strategy: Review need to brief (discuss team roles) prior to a critical situation and huddle (adhoc planning to re-establish situation awareness) during a crisis.

h. Inefficient communication: lack of call-out Strategy: Review importance of directed communication: “Survey physician- What’s the airway status?”

i. Inefficient communication: lack of check-back Strategy: Review use of closed-loop communication: Team Leader: “Give normal saline, $2 0 0 \mathsf { m l }$ , IV push over $1 0 \mathsf { m i n ^ { \prime \prime } }$ .

# To begin:

This scenario works best if learners function in their normal roles (physicians function as physicians, nurses as nurses). If your learners consist only of physicians, please ask 1-2 of them to function as nurses during the scenario. To maximize the realism of the scenario, it helps to ask them to do everything a nurse would normally do at your institution. If your learners have not been introduced to the concept of specific team roles (including a team leader) and responsibilities, we recommend discussing your institutional standard prior to the start of the scenario. If your learners are already familiar with this concept, we recommend reminding them of your institution’s code team roles and responsibilities prior to the start of the scenario. Based on your learners needs/experience, the instructor can decide whether to assign roles or allow them to determine their own roles during the brief. Depending on the needs of your learners, you may choose to have the learners remain in the same roles throughout the scenario or rotate. Please state your expectation to the group.
