**Sam Rodilla, Knee Pain – Instructor’s Guide**

<u>**Resource**</u>: 	Sam Rodilla, Knee Pain

<u>**How to use the resource file**</u>:	The resource file contains information necessary for training standardized patients, presented in a table format. It describes a case, the way it is used, and contains SP training notes and additional details.

<u>**Purpose of the resource**</u>: 	To enrich the sources of case scenarios available to standardized patient trainers and medical school faculty for objective structured clinical examinations.

<u>**Educational Objectives**</u>:

- Apply the principles of effective communication to facilitate relationship building, data gathering and patient education
- Use appropriate questioning and physical examination to assess the condition of a patient with joint pain

<u>**Conceptual background**</u>: 	This case was created for use with standardized patients, for teaching and/or evaluation purposes. The case is intended for teaching/examination of medical students, years 1 through 4 (depending on medical school’s type of curriculum), as well as residents and faculty development.

<u>**Practical implementation advice**</u>: 	All original materials/patient exam room needs are described in the case, <u>users are free to change the original case to better suit their needs</u>, as long as due credit is applied.

<u>**How to use the “Example SOAP Note”**</u>

The example written note is meant as a possible guide for completeness of information inclusion. While the parts of a SOAP note are standardized, the individual writing styles are varied, and we cannot stipulate that there is only one correct way to complete the SOAP note.

The “Example SOAP Note,” if used in a teaching context, should be provided to the students to <u>compare with their own notes</u>, and help them in organizing the note. When the SOAP note is used for evaluative purposes, there are a number of ways in which it could be utilized, depending on the faculty expectations, student learning level and desired complexity. Traditionally, the faculty designing the evaluative experience has the freedom and responsibility to decide on these details.

<u>**Recommended length of session:**</u>	15 minutes for learner in patient room, 10 minutes for patient note, 30 min for debriefing with faculty-observer (for teaching purposes only). The debriefing session may include:

- Discussion of what went well
- Discussion of what could be improved
- Review of questions that presented difficulty for the student(s)
- Specific recommendations on:

- Time management
- Patient examination
- Data gathering
- Closing of the encounter with provision of summary to the patient

In simulated environments, the most learning occurs during the debriefing of a scenario. In general, it is recommended that the length of the debriefing session is as long as or longer than the time devoted to patient encounter and note writing. Such debriefing may be purposefully omitted if the case is used for high-stakes exams.
