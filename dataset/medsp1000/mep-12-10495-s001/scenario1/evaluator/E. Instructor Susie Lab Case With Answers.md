**Susie’s Sippy Cup Dilemma: Early Childhood Caries**

**CASE DESCRIPTION:**

**Part A:** Susie Smith is an ex-preemie, born at 28 weeks (6 months gestation). She is presently 6 months chronological age (adjusted age 3 months) and is coming in to see you for her 6 month vaccinations. She has an associated mild subglottic stenosis, having been intubated for the first month of her life. She is growing very well. Susie’s mother breast fed Susie for the first month, but she now gives Susie six – 6 oz bottles of Enfamil formula daily. Her mother sends Susie to bed with a bottle and she adds a spoonful of sugar to it to make it taste better. She positions Susie on her back, which she was told to do to prevent SIDS. She props the bottle into her mouth by resting the bottle on a pillow. Susie’s mother says its helps Susie to get to sleep. This allows her mother to get some homework done.

Susie’s mother is an 18-year old single parent who is living at her parents’ rural Levy County, FL homestead. They have a well for drinking water and other household use. She is receiving Medicaid assistance and her parents, who are both retired, have a very limited income. Susie’s mother took a year off from high school during her pregnancy and has now returned to finish her senior year of high school. She admits to having some difficulties with her course work and finds the evenings difficult in balancing homework with looking after Susie. When Susie’s mother smiles, you notice she has visible carious lesions (cavities) between her two maxillary central incisors (# 8 & 9).

While Susie’s mother has brought Susie in for her vaccinations, her mother has expressed concern that Susie has no teeth yet and that she is six months of age.

**Answer:** No. Normal eruption time for the earliest teeth would be 6-10 months of age. Susie is a premature baby. Her chronologic age is different from her developmental age. She was born at 28 weeks (6 months development), 3 months short of a 40 week term pregnancy. She should have 3 months subtracted from her chronologic age to determine her adjusted age = 3 months. Normal tooth eruption for Susie would probably begin in approx. 3-7 months. Children vary in their tooth eruption times and sequences.

**ANSWER:** Susie’s risk for ECC: High

Susie’s Risk Factors (AAP – High risk: Susie’s mother has caries – Other Risk factors – mom does not have a dentist, special health care needs, Medicaid eligible, continual bottle use. No protective factors.) Note: Several factors are not applicable since the patient does not yet have erupted teeth.

**ANSWER:**

* Stop sending Susie to bed with the bottle.
* Stop sweetening the milk.
* Stop bottle propping, a significant choking risk and can lead to ear infections. Remember that Susie’s mother needs support to do her homework. Her mother was receptive to the counseling regarding the appropriate positioning of Susie due to the risk of SIDS, so she may be amenable to removing the bottle due to the choking risk.
* Encourage Susie’s mother to start to get Susie accustomed to her mother cleaning her mouth by either wiping the gums daily with a soft, clean washcloth or small, soft toothbrush. This helps set the stage for tooth brushing once the teeth begin to erupt.
* Encourage Susie’s mother to seek dental care to have her own teeth repaired. This is to reduce the potential of the inoculation of Susie’s mouth with S. Mutans & other cariogenic bacteria through vertical transmission from her mother to Susie.
* Susie’s mom can also chew a Xylitol containing gum. This has been shown to be effective in reducing the vertical transmission of S. Mutans. (Soderling,E. et al, Influence of Maternal Xylitol Consumption on acquisition of Mutans Streptococci by Infants. JDR 79 (3): 882 - 887, 2000) BTW: How much gum would Susie’s mom need to chew daily? Depends on amount of Xylitol in the gum. If it is listed as the first ingredient, probably there is enough Xylitol to be effective. You have to chew in the range of at least 2 pieces, three times a day to have an effect.
