**Susie’s Sippy Cup Dilemma: Early Childhood Caries**

**CASE DESCRIPTION:**

**Part A:** Susie Smith is an ex-preemie, born at 28 weeks (6 months gestation). She is presently 6 months chronological age (adjusted age 3 months) and is coming in to see you for her 6 month vaccinations. She has an associated mild subglottic stenosis, having been intubated for the first month of her life. She is growing very well. Susie’s mother breast fed Susie for the first month, but she now gives Susie six – 6oz bottles of Enfamil formula daily. Her mother sends Susie to bed with a bottle and she adds a spoonful of sugar to it to make it taste better. She positions Susie on her back, which she was told to do to prevent SIDS. She props the bottle into her mouth by resting the bottle on a pillow. Susie’s mother says its helps Susie to get to sleep. This allows her mother to get some homework done.

Susie’s mother is an 18-year old single parent who is living at her parents’ rural Levy County, FL homestead. They have a well for drinking water and other household use. She is receiving Medicaid assistance and her parents, who are both retired, have a very limited income. Susie’s mother took a year off from high school during her pregnancy and has now returned to finish her senior year of high school. She admits to having some difficulties with her course work and finds the evenings difficult in balancing homework with looking after Susie. When Susie’s mother smiles, you notice she has visible carious lesions (cavities) between her two maxillary central incisors (# 8 & 9).

While Susie’s mother has brought Susie in for her vaccinations, her mother has expressed concern that Susie has no teeth yet and that she is six months of age.
