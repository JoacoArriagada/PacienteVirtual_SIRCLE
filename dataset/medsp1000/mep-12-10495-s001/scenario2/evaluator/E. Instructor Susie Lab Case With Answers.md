**Susie’s Sippy Cup Dilemma: Early Childhood Caries**

**Part B:**

Susie returns with her mother at age 12 months for her MMR (Measles, Mumps & Rubella) vaccination. She is now in the 25th percentile for growth. Susie is not walking yet, but she is crawling and will grasp onto a table and stand herself up and even let go for a moment and then fall to a sit. Susie’s mother has introduced solids and because of your earlier advice she no longer sends Susie to bed with the bottle. She has introduced Susie to a sippy cup and Susie’s favorite drink is apple juice, which she sips on happily throughout the day. Susie now has 6 teeth (teeth #D, E, F, G, O, P). Susie’s mother says Susie is brushing her own teeth twice a day. She is using a non-fluoridated toothpaste. Susie’s mother has not seen a dentist. (Note: Medicaid does not cover fillings for cavities after age 18.) Sitting on the examining table in your office, Susie is very squirmy and she cries as she sits on your examination table and you examine her. It is difficult to examine herhead and neck area.

* decreases separation anxiety for the child because the caregiver can be seen.

**ANSWER:** Susie’s risk for ECC **High** (AAPD –High risk: mom has active caries, Susie’s mother’s low socioeconomic status, bed with bottle with natural and added sugar, white spot lesions, Also moderate risk factors –premature birth. No protective factors.)

**ANSWER:** The cells that manufacture enamel are sensitive to systemic insults. Disruption in the production of enamel will result in a defect which may be micro or macroscopic. It is difficult to distinguish early decalcification from enamel defects, but this does not affect management. Enamel defects are associated with substantially increased risk of ECC.

Discuss enamel dysplasia “white spots” versus decalcification “white spots” relative to caries risk assessment. It is possible that Susie has had some disruption of normal tooth development related to her premature birth and the white areas represent enamel abnormalities. This is not unusual in premature babies. This type of “white spot” anomaly is also a risk factor for the development of ECC. So the etiology of the white spot does not matter in caries risk assessment.

**ANSWER:** Fluoridated Water, Fluoridated toothpaste, fluoride varnish, fluoride gels, foams and mouthwashes, Fluoride supplements

**ANSWER:** Fluoridated water, a systemic and topical source of fluoride is very appropriate at this age as a number of her permanent teeth are calcifying, her primary teeth complete calcification, and her newly erupting primary teeth complete calcification after eruption.

Susie’s mom is brushing Susie’s teeth twice daily with a smear of fluoridated toothpaste which provides a regular source of topical fluoride.

As Susie is at high risk of caries, Fluoride varnish, a topical form of fluoride, can be applied every 3 months.

She is drinking well water and you may assume that fluoride supplements are indicated for this high risk child. They MAY be. However, because fluoride works mainly topically, the guidelines really have not been emphasizing F supplements. They are not recommended at all for children at low risk for caries, even if they live in non-fluoridated communities. F supplements tend to have poor adherence to daily administration, marginal benefit if the other forms of F are used, and substantially increase the risk for fluorosis.

For a child at high risk (like Susie), parental behavior change would be better focused on brushing with F toothpaste every day, cleaning teeth, and feeding practices. Be aware:

fluoride supplements should NEVER be prescribed without a careful history regarding sources of water in the child’s diet – ex. Caregiver may have fluoridated water, caregiver/mom may use fluoridated bottled water for formula and drinking for Susie, her family’s well may have high enough levels of fluoride ion naturally (0.3-0.7 ppm). You should also take a careful history to be sure no other providers have already prescribed fluoride supplements already.

**ANSWER:** Dental fluorosis is a discoloring of the teeth due to chronic, excessive exposure to fluoride during tooth development. Fluorosis does not occur once teeth are fully developed. It is a cosmetic issue that does NOT affect systemic health. In its most common manifestation, it presents as a white mottling of the tooth enamel. It develops from exposure during tooth development to excessive fluoride at an intake of more than 0.06 milligrams per kilogram of body weight per day. Do not prescribe fluoride supplements without testing the fluoride content of the child’s water supply and a thorough history of all sources of water in the child’s diet, educate parents to use age appropriate amounts of toothpaste when brushing (for Susie a smear of toothpaste on her toothbrush). Fluoride varnish applied every two to four times per year is not a major risk factor for fluorosis since it is an episodic exposure, not chronic. Current clinical guidelines do not recommend use of fluoride supplements for children at low risk for dental caries because it increases the risk for fluorosis without adding significant prevention benefits.

**ANSWER:** You can apply Fluoride varnish to the ‘white spot’ lesions and all of Susie’s teeth. This can be applied after positioning Susie in the knee to knee position with Susie’s mom, lifting her lip and drying her teeth with cotton gauze.

**ANSWER:** Follow up with Susie within 3 months to reapply fluoride varnish and/or ensure that the dental referrals have been followed up on. White spot lesions can rapidly proceed to cavitated lesions (cavities) requiring restorative intervention (which is hard to do in a 12 – 36 month old!)
