**Susie’s Sippy Cup Dilemma: Early Childhood Caries**

**PART D**

Four months have passed. Susie is now 19 months of age. You are covering for emergencies at your regional hospital. Susie’s mom has come to the emergency room as she is very concerned. Susie kept pulling at her lip recently. She has stopped gaining weight, as she will not eat anything and will only drink. Susie’s mom reports she has not been sleeping well and has woken up crying and screaming for the last three nights in a row. Nobody has been sleeping well and her mother seems to be at her wits end. Susie’s mother gave Susie acetaminophen and this only helped a little. Susie awoke this morning with a swollen upper lip that is tender to touch. Her temperature is 39 C. Susie is very upset.

Susie’s mother reports that Susie did see the dentist. The primary care dentist tried very hard to fix Susie’s teeth, but Susie really kicked and screamed and the dentist referred her to a Pediatric Dentist. The Pediatric Dentist noted that Susie not only had cavities on all four of her primary maxillary incisors, but now has cavities on the biting surfaces of the following teeth: B, I, L and S and that these needed to have stainless steel crowns placed on them due to the very large size of the cavities. Because of her history of subglottic stenosis and Susie’s young age, the Pediatric Dentist was unwilling to sedate Susie for the dental treatment in office. It was recommended that Susie have all of her work done under a general anesthetic at the tertiary care hospital in the area. The Pediatric Dentist had noted that the wait list was quite long and had warned that front teeth might abscess during the wait. Susie’s mother was instructed to call immediately if this developed. Her mother is frightened and Susie is quite upset.

Susie has an acute labial cellulitis from an acute dental abscess. As the caries lesion increased in size, it encroached on the pulp of the tooth, causing an acute infection and pulpal necrosis. The infection spread outside the end of the tooth into the surrounding tissues causing a cellulitis. The mass is quite painful and fluctuant.

Caries extending to pulp with resultant pulpal necrosis and apical abscess or periodontitis

Depending on the virulence of the bacteria involved and the host defense mechanisms, a tooth may develop a dental abscess with a drainage point (gum boil). Left untreated, these abscesses can have acute exacerbations in the form of cellulitis, such as Susie is now experiencing. They can also cause damage to the developing permanent tooth. Rarely, dental abscesses left untreated can result in brain abscess formation.

Definitive management of abscesses through either tooth extraction, or if possible, root canal therapy are the only treatment options.
