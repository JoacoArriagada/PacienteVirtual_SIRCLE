**Case**

1) Scene 1: Admission

The patient is a 56 year old Hispanic male admitted after a motor vehicle collision. He was a restrained passenger. His admission injuries included a left pneumothorax, multiple broken ribs, flail chest on the left and a serious femur fracture and confusion. He is admitted to the intensive care unit given concern about his ability to protect his airway with his confusion, where he is stabilized with traction for the leg, and a chest tube on the left.

2) Scene 2: 24 hours later: Transfer to the Floor/Talk with family you have never met

The patient is transferred to the floor after his mental status clears. A nursing note documents that there is multiple family at his bedside, including his wife and a nephew that was a medical student at Duke, but now an intern elsewhere. The patient is groaning in pain and the family is very concerned about the amount of pain he is in. The nephew keeps telling his family that he will get the pain team to come and find out who is on call, so something can be done.

Scene 2: On call intern must address the pain issues facing the patient

3) Scene 3: 6 hours later: PCA is ordered and implemented

The team orders a PCA----a device that delivers pain medication at the command of the patient. The patient keeps pushing the button, then falls asleep, but the nurse has to tell the family several times that they should not hit the button.

4) Scene 4: 6 hours later: Code

The patient is found to be unresponsive about 20 minutes after his wife leaves for the night. He receives bag, mask, valve respirations, is suctioned. Chest compressions are started. An AED is placed, and there is no shockable rhythm. The code cart defibrillator shows that the patient is having a PEA arrest---there is a heart electrical rhythm, but the heart is not pumping. He the receives epinephrine, narcan and he is intubated. After about 15 minutes, he is back in sinus rhythm, but is totally unresponsive.

Scene 4: on call intern: Tell wife that patient moves to the ICU and that he coded

Scene 4b: on call intern: Explain to wife that the PCA was likely the cause

5) Scene 5: 3 days later: ICU care and Neurology consult

The patient iscontinues to be unresponsive, with a GCS of 3T (cannot completely complete the GCS because the patient is intubated, but this is really bad!). He has occasional jerking that is often common in patients who had damage to their brain when not enough blood gets to the brain. Neurology is consulted and says things look bad, and that the patient is likely to be comatose, perhaps ultimately diagnosed with persistent vegetative state, they cannot comment on prognosis another four days. Orthopedics sees the neurology note and decides that the risk outweighs the benefits of fixing the femur. If the patient remains in a vegetative state, e won’t leave bed. The anesthetic risk just isn’t warranted.

Scene 5a: ICU intern talks with wife about neurology consult

Scene 5b: Intern breaks the news that the surgery won’t be performed and responds to wife’s claim that the surgery is cancelled because the patient is Hispanic.

6) Scene 6: Need for Peg and Trach

Neurology confirms that the patient is in a vegetative state and has a very low chance of meaningful recovery Pulmonary recommends a PEG and TRACH to assist with suctioning and to allow feeding.

Scene 6: Discuss Neurology visit, Try to consent patient’s wife for PEG and TRACH or move towards palliative care
