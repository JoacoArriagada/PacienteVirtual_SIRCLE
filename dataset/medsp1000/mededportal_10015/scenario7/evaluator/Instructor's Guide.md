**A Breaking Bad News Exercise to Assess Student Competence prior to Graduation during a Capstone Course**

Elizabeth F. Ross, DPT, Nancy W. Knudsen, MD, Saumil M. Chudgar, MD, MS, Deborah L. Engle, EdD, MS, Colleen O’Conner Grochowski, PhD and Alison S. Clay, MD

**Short Description**

This is a breaking bad news exercise for graduating medical students that utilizes a standardized patient (SP) acting as a family member during a complex hospitalization. Students take turns meeting with a family member at multiple times during a single patient’s hospitalization to discuss changes in the patient’s condition. The exercise is unique in that it follows a patient’s family through several encounters, allowing the students to see the repeated impact of these conversations on the family over time. Direct observation by peers and faculty allow for assessment of student competence using the Transitional Year Milestones. Short written reflections offer insight into student learning about the impact of human emotions on encounters between patients and providers.

**Purpose of the Session**

* To utilize a Capstone course for assessment of communication skills in graduating medical students using a combination of direct observation and written reflection.
* To allow students with significant clinical experience to practice communication skills in more complex clinical scenarios, specifically in situations that require students to “break bad news.”

To assess learner competence in communication as judged by faculty and peers using the Transitional Year Milestones, and the Core Entrustable Professional Activities prior to Residency (CEPAERS).

**Objectives**

* Students will communicate using discreet communication skills (ask-tell-ask, use of silence, pacing, warning shots, etc.) to deliver bad news to a standardized patient’s family member.
* Students will demonstrate insight into the emotions of family members including the effect of these emotions on family’s perceptions of and responses to providers during difficult times through written reflections following the clinical exercise.
* Students will provide effective feedback to each other assessing the same communication skills they use to talk with patients.
