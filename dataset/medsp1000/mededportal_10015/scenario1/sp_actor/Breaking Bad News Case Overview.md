Background family dynamics:

The patient’s nephew is a recent Duke Graduate who still has access to Duke Information Technologies. His brother is a neurologist at Johns Hopkins. Occasionally the “standardized patient family” member brings up “conflict” between information from the family and from the care team.

**Case**

1) Scene 1: Admission

The patient is a 56 year old Hispanic male admitted after a motor vehicle collision. He was a restrained passenger. His admission injuries included a left pneumothorax, multiple broken ribs, flail chest on the left and a serious femur fracture and confusion. He is admitted to the intensive care unit given concern about his ability to protect his airway with his confusion, where he is stabilized with traction for the leg, and a chest tube on the left.

2) Scene 2: 24 hours later: Transfer to the Floor/Talk with family you have never met

The patient is transferred to the floor after his mental status clears. A nursing note documents that there is multiple family at his bedside, including his wife and a nephew that was a medical student at Duke, but now an intern elsewhere. The patient is groaning in pain and the family is very concerned about the amount of pain he is in. The nephew keeps telling his family that he will get the pain team to come and find out who is on call, so something can be done.
