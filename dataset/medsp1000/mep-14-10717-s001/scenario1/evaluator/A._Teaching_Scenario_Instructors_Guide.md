**Appendix A: Teaching Scenario Instructors Guide**

**Case 1**

Rationale: Given that many emergency medicine clerkships are mandatory, general interest in the learning activity may be variable and at times students are not engaged in the learning experience.

Objective: Recognize the lack of motivation in the student and provide feedback to the student on the expectations of the clerkship and provide context related to the student’s future career (consultant) as a means of increasing engagement.

Set-up: The student in this scenario has already matched in radiation oncology and is not putting his full effort into the rotation as a result. The choice of radiation oncology was intentional to minimize the overlap in practice between the ED and planned specialty. This also allows the simulated supervisor to identify interactions with consultants as a possible point of interest to the student. At the beginning of the scenario the student was late to the shift and is questioned by the simulated supervisor about why they are late and provide feedback.

The setting: It is a Monday and you are working as the staffing resident on the lower acuity side of the department and it’s 30 minutes after the student’s shift was scheduled to start. Waits are longer than normal. People are in the hall. Your faculty are carrying at least five patients a piece primarily and they are not happy about it.

The presentation: Arrive avoiding eye contact, take your time unpacking your things, walk over to get a drink, then just sit. When confronted, say you got caught in traffic or overslept, or both. Already matched into radiation oncology, so this rotation won’t really matter to you.

Debriefing Notes: The indifferent scenario is designed to encourage the supervisor to increase engagement of the learner in the activity by identifying their learning interests and pointing out areas that may be of more interest to them and also reinforcing the expectations of the clerkship.

* One thing the teaching resident should do in this case is reinforce the expectations of the clerkship and potentially include that passing this clerkship is a graduation requirement. The feedback should include as many specifics as possible. For this case, being on time for shifts, settled and ready to participate in patient care at the start time for your shift.
* Adult learners are more invested in their education when they understand the context of the information they are being provided, so in this case remind the student that when they start internship they will have the potential to be the first to evaluate a patient with an undifferentiated complaint and the emergency department is an excellent place to gain those skills. This should help get the learner to buy into the value of the rotation and increase motivation.
* Attempting to tailor a student’s experience toward what their planned field is also a great way to increase interest in the student needing motivation. While Radiation Oncology cases aren’t common in the department, they serve as consultants to other physicians. Pointing out the good and bad in consultants as they interact with them though the month are helpful learning points for them. Knowing what your learners are planning for their future is helpful to pull in relevant teaching points on their cases.
