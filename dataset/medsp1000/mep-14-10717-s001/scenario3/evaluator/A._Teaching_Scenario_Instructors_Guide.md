**Appendix A: Teaching Scenario Instructors Guide**

**Case 3**

Rationale: The ED can be a fast paced and chaotic environment that can be a challenge to adapt to for students not familiar with the pace. The overwhelmed by the system and the ED is designed for residents to identify tactics to empower the students to become more effective learners and to ensure safe care delivery in the department.

Objective: Recognize the need to balance providing effective patient care to patients while supporting the learner.

Set-up: The simulated supervisor is coming on at 9am and the student who started at 7 am was given multiple patients in change-over. The student is confronted with several challenges related to results coming back and systems issues and comes to the simulated supervisor for guidance on several tasks.

The setting: You come in at 9 am for your Saturday staffing shift. The night shift student and off service intern have left six change over patients with pending studies/labs to the student that started at 7am. The ED is already busy.

The presentation: Dr. I have these patients that were changed over to me and I just wanted to run them by you. I can’t figure out why this woman with RLQ pain hasn’t been for her CT yet. (Order entered by night shift student but not signed off by faculty yet) I’m also waiting for the GC/CT to come back on this girl with vaginal discharge. The nurses keep yelling at me that she is ready to go. The report on this guy who was in car accident says negative head CT and face CT, but I think he has a cut on his face and I have never done any suturing before. Also, I talked to the surgery person on this woman with abdominal pain and a lactate of 10 like they told me to, but he said they could go home since the CT was negative. How do I put in her prescription for pain medicine?

Debriefing Notes: The overwhelmed by the ED and the system scenario is designed to challenge the supervisor to help the student work through some challenging patients who have been changed over to them. There is a tendency for the supervisor to take over care for all of the patients. Without acknowledgement that the student should feel overwhelmed by the situation, the student will likely feel incompetent and that they should have been able to handle things. Ideally, the supervisor will support the student by helping them work thought the challenges and eventually coming back and doing some teaching on taking sign out.

* The teaching resident often did a poor job of acknowledging that the medical student was overwhelmed. Additionally, they did not reassure the medical student that they should indeed feel overwhelmed and that it is too much for a medical student to handle thus reassuring the student. Without the acknowledgement and support, the medical student starts to feel incompetent and that they should be able to handle everything.
* The teaching resident’s natural tendency was to take control of the situation. “Why don’t you let me take care of all of this while you start with a new patient?” There are two problems to this option. First, the medical student does not learn anything. Second, again this often makes the medical student feel as though the resident does not trust them.
* Occasionally, the teaching resident would get lost in the details of the “sick” abdominal pain case and start doing too much teaching about differentials, workups, mesenteric ischemia, etc. If this happens discuss the option of teaching by role modeling in these instances. Take the student with the resident to solve these problems. Then circle back later to do some teaching about the cases when there is more time.

Finally, we discussed the importance of circling back when time allows to discuss safe patient handoff and changeover and its implication for patient safety.
