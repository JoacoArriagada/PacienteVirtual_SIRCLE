| **Brief narrative description of case** | *In this simulation 2 learners playing the role of EMS providers, will evaluate a patient with diabetes mellitus (DM) after being called by their adult child for altered mental status. The patient will be hypoglycemic, provided glucose, and regain mentation. After this the patient will refuse transport to the hospital.* |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | * Evaluate a patient with altered mental status * Analyze blood glucose readings * Respond appropriately to hypoglycemia with medical management * Apply the Altered Mental Status protocol developed by the Regional Emergency Medical Advisory Committee. * Evaluate for capacity to refuse transportation * Discuss which patients can refuse transport * Review the legal issues surrounding transport after treatment |
| **Critical Actions** | * Perform a focused history and physical exam * Determine nature of the illness * Treat hypoglycemia using IV dextrose appropriately * Evaluate for capacity to refuse transport * Discuss with medical control * Communicate with patient and family |
| **Learner Preparation or Prework** | EMS Protocols  If facilitators want to “prime” the learners they can give them specific policies related to diabetes management. |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*The learners enter the room to find the patient slumped over on their chair, with their head on the table. Their adult child provides the medical history. After a physical examination, the learners request a blood glucose, which is low (40). They determine that the patient cannot tolerate oral solutions and place an IV and administer 50g of IV dextrose. They continue to monitor the patient until they wakes up. Repeat fingerstick glucose is 150. The learners offer patient food/drink with sugar. The learners offer patient transport which they decline. The learners perform a capacity assessment and determine the patient has capacity. They contact medical control and medical control also agrees. They leave the patient with their adult child and a plan to see their PMD later that day and call 911 if anything changes.*
