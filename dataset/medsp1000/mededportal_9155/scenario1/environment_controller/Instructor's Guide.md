# INSTRUCTOR’S GUIDE FOR TOTAL ANOMALOUS PULMONARY VENOUSCONNECTION SIMULATION SCENARIO

Title: “28-day-old male with non-obstructing supracardiac Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection and atrial septal defect presenting with pulmonary overcirculation”

IX. Environment

a. Lab Set Up: Emergency Department (ED) trauma bay or critical care room b. Manikin Set Up: High fidelity SimBaby manikin driven by Laerdal SimBaby 1.6.2 software

i. Patient’s attire: SimBaby is wearing a dry diaper and wrapped in a blanket ii. Initially, no intravenous access or supplemental oxygenation or monitoring leads are attached iii. Moulage: cyanosis of distal extremities, nailbeds, tongue, lips, peri-oral

c. Props: EMS run sheet, electrocardiogram, chest plain radiograph, laboratory results sheet

X. Actors

a. Roles: patient’s parent (mother or father), nurse, pediatric cardiologist
b. Who may play them: actor, nurse, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or physician
c. Distractors: none

XI. Case Narrative

a. Scenario background given to participants. Mode of arrival is by ambulance. An EMS run sheet is provided that lists the chief complaint, history of present illness and review of systems, past medical history, medication information and allergies.

Pertinent details (such as the particular extremity on which the blood pressure was obtained) are not specified for both purposes of realism and to provide learners with an opportunity for discovery during their own assessment. In the critical care room are the patient, parent (mother or father who can provide greater historical detail when asked by the participants), and a nurse.

# i. EMS management in transport:

1. No pre-hospital intravenous access is obtained. Vital signs obtained include: heart rate, blood pressure (extremity not specified), respiration rate, temperature (forehead), oxygen saturation.
2. By report, supplemental oxygen by face mask was employed briefly in transit, but this has come off during transfer to the emergency department

ii. EMS run sheet information includes: patient’s name, age, sex, race, chief complaint per parent, history of present illness and review of symptoms (some components intentionally incomplete – participant may ask parent for any missing details), medications, and drug allergies. Run sheet only indicates “none” for past medical history and there is no mention of family or social history

3. Sex: “male”

4. Race: “caucasian”

5. Chief complaint: “breathing problems”

6. History of Present Illness and Review of Systems: “4 week old male former 36 week infant with poor feeding history and poor weight gain, today less alert, producing fewer wet diapers. No diarrhea, vomiting. Breathing faster than usual. Mother describes 2 days of “seeming congested and having breathing problems”. New cough as of today and looks uncomfortable. Not tolerating feeds due to fussiness and fast breathing.”

7. Past Medical History: “None”

8. Allergies: “No known drug allergies”

9. Vital Signs: Heart rate: 195 Respiration rate: 60, Blood pressure: 80/55, Oxygen saturation: $8 8 \%$ on room air. Temperature: 37.4 C (99.3 F) forehead

1. History of Present Illness: No subjective or measured fever at home. Parent says that Jimmy feeds with effort and in general has not fed as much as her friend’s infant who is a similar age.

2. Review of Systems:

GENERAL: less active today, fussy, worsening apparent discomfort HEAD, EYES, EARS, NOSE, THROAT: no discharge from eyes or ears; lips and tongue sometimes blue when feeding or crying

PULMONARY: fast breathing, short of breath, occasional cough today CARDIOVASCULAR: skin feels clammy after feeds GASTROINTESTINAL: poor feeding, no diarrhea or vomiting, no blood in stool GENITOURINARY: makes wet diapers but fewer today (2 compared with 4-6 usually) DERMATOLOGIC: cyanotic lips/tongue during feeds, no rashes NEUROLOGIC: weak cry, moves all extremities
3. Past Medical and Birth History: “Ex-36 week, full term vaginal delivery, no NICU stay; Immunizations up-to-date; Mother group B Streptococcus negative, no maternal history of illicit drug use.”
4. Social and Family History: “Lives with mother and father, no siblings, no smokers at home, no sick contacts. Maternal grandmother has diabetes, paternal grandfather deceased at age 67 of stroke”

# b. Scenario conditions initially:

i. History: provided by EMS run sheet and additional information from parent
ii. Patient’s initial vital signs in the Emergency Department: Heart rate: 210, Respiration rate: 70, Blood pressure: 76/50 (right arm), 76/54 (left arm), 75/52 (right leg), 76/50 (left leg). Oxygen saturation: $8 5 \%$ on room air, Temperature: 37.5 C (99.5 F) rectal. Weight: $3 . 5 \mathrm { k g }$ (7.7 lb)

iii. Physical Exam in the Emergency Department:

1. GENERAL APPEARANCE: Crying, tachypneic infant with moderate intercostal and sternal retractions. Skin is warm and moist with evidence of cyanosis of lips, tongue, peri-oral, nailbeds, hands, and feet

2. HEAD, EYES, EARS, NOSE, THROAT: flat fontanelle, conjunctiva anicteric, nares clear, dry mucous membranes

3. RESPIRATORY: tachypneic, moderate intercostal and sternal retractions, no stridor, bilateral coarse sounds

4. CARDIOVASCULAR: sinus tachycardia, widely split and fixed second heart sound (S2), grade II systolic ejection murmur over left upper sternal border (LUSB), grade II mid-diastolic flow rumble at left lower sternal border (LLSB), no gallop or rub, hyperactive precordium with point of maximal impulse appreciated over left sternal border, $1 +$ bilateral femoral pulses, thready brachial pulses, 4 second capillary refill

5. ABDOMINAL: positive bowel sounds, soft, non-distended, liver edge palpable 6 cm below right costal margin, liver span $1 0 \mathrm { c m }$

6. SKIN: cool, moist; cyanosis of lips, tongue, peri-oral, nail beds, and distal upper and lower extremities

7. NEUROLOGIC: restless, irritable and poorly consolable

# iv. Initial State Summary:

1. Significant exam findings: Tachypneic, tachycardic, borderline hypotensive, crying, intercostal retractions, poor perfusion, central cyanosis, cool extremities, thready pulses, significant systolic and diastolic murmurs, widely split and fixed S2.

2. Information:

a. Chronically: poor feeding with history of poor weight gain
b. Acutely: 2 days fussiness and difficulty breathing today with new cough and respiratory distress but afebrile
c. Vital signs from EMS significant for tachycardia, tachypnea, and hypoxia. No pre-hospital glucose measurement was obtained

# 4. Response to treatment:

a. Oxygen administration does not improve saturation
b. Treating hypoglycemia will not result in clinical improvement, but a follow-up fingerstick will demonstrate resolution of hypoglycemia (fingerstick glucose $\# 2 = 8 8$ )

5. Trigger to advance:

a. Advance to a transition state (deterioration of vital signs) occurs at 5 minutes from start of scenario.

# c. Transition state

i. Vital signs: Begin trend to increase heart and respiration rates and decrease oxygen saturation over a five minute period (heart rate increases by 33 beats per minute, respiration rate increases by 11 breaths per minute, systolic and diastolic blood pressure decreases by $6 \ : \mathrm { m m H } \mathrm { g } .$ , and oxygen saturation decreases by $2 { - } 4 \%$ ). At end of five minute transition, heart rate will be as high as 243, respiration rate as high as 81, oxygen saturation as low as $8 1 \%$ .

ii. Exam: work of breathing appears to increase (infant is now grunting).

iii. Diagnostic test results return Electrocardiogram findings: sinus tachycardia, right atrial enlargement, and right ventricular hypertrophy Chest radiograph findings: pulmonary edema, cardiomegaly Hyperoxia test (bedside): oxygen by face mask does not improve saturation; if formal blood gas obtained, oxygen administration does not improve hypoxemia

Laboratory, imaging, and electrocardiographic findings are provided for interpretation

1. Blood gas: uncompensated respiratory acidosis with metabolic acidosis; hyperoxia test suggests cardiac etiology (repeat blood gas relatively unchanged after oxygen administration)
2. Hyponatremia due to water retention
3. Elevated serum lactate and decreased serum bicarbonate due to tissue hypoxia
4. Elevated blood urea nitrogen due to decreased intravascular volume and dehydration
5. Chest plain film radiography demonstrates cardiomegaly, increased pulmonary vascular markings, and pulmonary edema suggestive of congestive heart failure or pulmonary overcirculation
6. Electrocardiogram demonstrates sinus tachycardia, right atrial enlargement, and right ventricular hypertrophy

# v. Response to treatment:

1. Oxygen saturation and heart rate improve somewhat after intubation

vi. Trigger to advance: endotracheal intubation, furosemide, and pediatric cardiology consultation, $+ / -$ vasoactive agent, $+ / -$ positive inotrope.

# d. Partially Treated State

i. Vital signs: Interval improvement from Transition State. Heart rate decreases to 180 beats per minute, respiration rate is controlled by mechanical ventilator (patient paralyzed and sedated), oxygen saturation increased to $9 0 \%$ with positive pressure ventilation.

# iii. Response to treatment:

1. Oxygen saturation and heart rate improved with furosemide, intubation and/or vasoactive agent and/or positive inotrope

# iv. Trigger to advance/conclude scenario (all must occur)

1. Pediatric cardiology consultation
2. Furosemide administration
3. Vasoactive agent and/or inotrope administration
4. Admit to Pediatric Intensive Care Unit or transfer patient to
appropriate care facility

# XII. Instructor’s Notes

a. Tips to keep scenario flowing:

i. Chest radiography and electrocardiography will not require a technician to be present or the actual physical act of obtaining these studies. After they are requested and the scenario is set to advance, the nurse will present the results to the participant during the Transition State

b. Tips to direct actors:

i. If participants do not verbalize their differential diagnosis or plan or their interpretation of the data, the nurse actor may ask “what do you think is going on?” as a stimulus
ii. If audio from the manikin is compromised during the scenario and a cue for deterioration (e.g. grunting) is lost, an actor (nurse) may provide a stimulus to trigger reassessment of the patient (“Does he look worse to you?”) to keep the scenario moving forward, only as needed

iii. Pediatric Cardiology consultant: Consultation is by telephone. Consultant will require the participant to present a focused history, including testing and treatments in the ED, as well as the reason/question(s) for the consultation

c. Scenario programming:

i. The transition/patient deterioration trend begins 5 minutes from the start of the scenario. There is no pathway that will result in the death of the patient.

ii. When appropriate cardiac medication therapy is ordered by a participant, the operator may select “Appropriate Therapy” from within the program, which will result in an improvement in vital signs.

iii. Chest radiography and electrocardiogram results are delayed until the Transition State

iv. If an echocardiogram or bedside ultrasound is requested, the nurse will say that she/he will call for one and will tell participants after calling that “it may take about 15-20 minutes to get here”

v. The participants may administer empiric antibiotics, but this will not harm or help the patient in the scenario.

vi. Alternative treatments, such as prostaglandins in consideration of a ductal dependent lesion, if inappropriately administered by the participants, will not improve or harm the patient

vii. An initial single intravenous saline bolus in response to tachycardia and hypotension does not improve patient, but also does not result in deterioration. $> 2 0 \mathrm { c c } / \mathrm { k g }$ will cause worsening hypoxia and accelerated deterioration to transition state

viii. Vasoactive or inotropic agent may not be administered until the very end of the case (after cardiology consultation) depending upon the learners’ initiative, but it must be administered prior to the case concluding formally

d. Resource Files (total $= 1 2$ files)

i. Laerdal SimBaby program files: (3) 1. TAPVC.CHF.final.scb (main program) 2. HR RR SPO2.tnd (transition trend) 3. tapvc.hnb (handlers)

a. Oxygen: nasal canula or facemask ventilation: decreases oxygen saturation by $2 \%$ from room air reading
b. Intubation (endotracheal): increases oxygen saturation to 89- $9 0 \%$
c. Furosemide: increases oxygen saturation by $3 \%$
d. Inotrope or vasoactive agent: increases oxygen saturation by $3 \%$
