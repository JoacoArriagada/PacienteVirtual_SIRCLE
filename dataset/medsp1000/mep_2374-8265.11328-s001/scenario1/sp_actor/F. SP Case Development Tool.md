| THE PATIENT STORY | You are a lawyer in a rural town and a prominent part of the community. You retired 6 months ago and were looking forward to continuing cycling races/rides, hiking, and enjoying retirement. Three months ago, you were involved in a motor vehicle collision and were diagnosed with an acute lower back strain. Your x-rays and other diagnostic tests were normal with no emergent issues. You were given hydrocodone, then additional hydrocodone at follow-up, and about one month after the accident Dr. Larson increased the dose and added carisoprodol. You report, “I’ve been taking the medications around the clock ever since. They are the only things that help the pain and help me to sleep at night. I’m here to get those medications refilled again.” You are disappointed in yourself and how this injury has impacted your life. You are scared to exercise or do activities you used to enjoy for fear of reinjuring your back. You previously used exercise to help with past depression and cannot do that now. You feel emotionally tired and disappointed that this injury is preventing you from enjoying retirement. You are worried about your relationship with your spouse because “My spouse does not like the number of medications I’m taking.” You do not want others in the community to know about your difficulties. In between regularly scheduled visits, you have had to go to urgent care and the emergency room for pain because you ran out of pain medication. You also have taken extra medication from your recent dental appointment and left-over medication from your spouse’s knee surgery. You do not share this information unless specifically asked. |
