Appendix F: Standardized Patient Case Development Tool

Name of Case: Chronic Back Pain Management

Name of educational and or assessment activity: Interprofessional Team-based Opioid Education

Patient Name: Sam Jones

Chief Complaint: Refill medication request secondary to motor vehicle crash (MVC)

Case Objectives:

1. Describe the roles and responsibilities of the healthcare team and how they work together to provide team-based care to patients using opioids.
2. Utilize appropriate non-stigmatizing language when caring for patients taking or potentially misusing opioids.
3. Express one’s knowledge and opinions to healthcare team members with confidence, clarity, and respect, working to ensure common understanding of information, treatment, and care decisions.
4. Evaluate a patient for potential opioid misuse or opioid use disorder as a member of the healthcare team.
5. Differentiate between treatment options for a patient with an opioid use disorder and/or pain management.
6. Work collaboratively with the healthcare team and the patient to develop a patient care plan.

Most likely Diagnosis and Differential with rationale from history and/or physical exam:

- Chronic musculoskeletal back pain: patient history, PEG screening tool, SP interview.
- Depression: patient history, PHQ-9 screening tool, SP interview.
- Safety concerns: high morphine equivalent dose (MED); combining alcohol with opioids and acetaminophen; high daily dose of acetaminophen.
- Opioid use disorder (OUD): PMP report, SP interview.

Diagnosis with support from positive and negative history and PE findings:

- Chronic pain: not seeing clinically meaningful improvement in pain or function despite continued opioid use and high MED.
- Safety concerns: high MED with overdose risk, combining carisoprodol with opioids, combining alcohol with opioids and acetaminophen, and high daily dose of acetaminophen (3,900 mg/day).

Differential with support from positive and negative history and PE findings:

- Depression: PHQ-9 score of 13; unable to exercise, reduced physical activity, strained relationship with spouse, weight gain, difficulty sleeping.
- Potential opioid use disorder: concerning behaviors including taking medication more frequently than prescribed and receiving opioid prescriptions from other sources.

Management or diagnostic plan:

- Chronic pain: patient education about realistic goals, risks and benefits of opioids, consultation or referral to a pain specialist, consider tapering opioids without rapid taper, non-opioid medications such as NSAIDs or antidepressants, cognitive behavioral therapy, exercise, physical therapy, yoga, acupuncture, or music therapy.
- Depression: referral to behavioral health specialist, exercise or physical therapy, consider initiating an antidepressant.
- Safety concerns: prescribe naloxone and provide training to patient and spouse, discontinue carisoprodol, provide acetaminophen education, discourage alcohol use.
- Concerning behaviors: establish a trusting patient-provider relationship, discuss multiple prescribers and opioid volume, consider a pain agreement with single prescriber/single pharmacy and/or urine drug testing, and consider addiction-specialist referral if OUD assessment is needed.

Professionalism issues or challenges:

- During the SP interaction, student teams may have difficulty managing their time.
