**\* Unacceptable is used only for the few students who leave an unusually bad impression.**

**PATIENT PROVIDER INTERACTION (PPI) GUIDE**

**1.  Appeared professionally competent.**

- Appeared to know what s/he was doing.
- Inspired my confidence (I felt I could trust the student because of the student’s own level of self-confidence).
- Appeared to have my interests at heart.

**2.  Effectively gathered information.**

- Collected information in a way that seemed organized.
- Began with several open-ended questions (e.g., “What brings you in?” “Tell me more.” “Anything else you are concerned about?”) and progressed through interview using a balanced ratio of open- to closed-ended questions.

- Summarized periodically.

**3.  Listened actively.**

This item has to do with your sensing that the student understands what you are communicating verbally and/or non-verbally (e.g. why you don’t feel you can follow a particular regimen).

- Paid attention to both my verbal and non-verbal cues.
- Used comfortable eye contact, facial expressions, nodding, pauses, posture and body language to express encouragement.
- Avoided interruptions  (It is important to distinguish between supportive interruptions and interruptions that “cut you off”).

- Asked questions to make sure s/he understood what I said.

**4.  Established personal rapport.**

Showed interest in me as a “person,” not just in my condition.

- Introduced themselves warmly.
- Addressed me by name.
- Non-verbal expression of interest (warmth in tone of voice, eye contact, body language, etc.)

- Verbal expression of interest (social interest; personal, supportive or collaborative comment).

- Used understandable language; did not use unexplained technical jargon.

**5.  Appropriately explored my perspective.**

Encouraged me to identify everything that I needed to say.

(If the student does not find out your perspective on the problem/illness AND how you feel about what s/he is suggesting for treatment, the examinee cannot fully meet your needs [item #7].)  [This item and the last item in this section “Met my needs” work in tandem.]

- Determined my reason(s) – both stated and unstated - for the visit.
- Explored the reasons why I cannot follow the medical regimen.
- Explored for unexpressed feelings.

- Inquired about my ideas about the illness, concerns or expectations  (e.g. How well I am controlling my hypertension.  What I think are the long-term effects of hypertension.)
- Encouraged me to ask questions.

**6.  Addressed my feelings.**

Expressed interest in my personal feelings and experience.

- Acknowledged my personal feelings and experience (which were expressed verbally and/or non-verbally). (e.g. “I imagine it must be hard to have an illness which requires major lifestyle changes.”)
- Made me feel understood by using specific statements of empathy and support.

- Validated, legitimized and/or normalized my medical concerns and feelings.  (e.g. “Many heart failure patients have had similar difficulties as you.”

**7.  Met my needs.**

Worked toward a plan which takes into consideration both the student’s diagnosis and treatment plan (the examinee’s perspective) as well as your concerns about your illness and follow-up care (your perspective).

- Clearly explained diagnostic and/or therapeutic plan.
- Gave me some sense of:

- What s/he thought was going on with me.
- The anticipated course of the illness.
- What was going to happen next.

- Included my concerns in the treatment plan.
- Provided me with choices/options regarding my care.  (e.g. lifestyle changes that I could comply with).

- Negotiated with me to come to a mutually agreeable plan (i.e., checked to see how I felt about the plan.)
