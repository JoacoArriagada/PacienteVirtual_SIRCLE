During the exercise you will work in an interprofessional team of 4-5 students, each from a different profession (Dentistry, Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy, Physical Therapy) on a single standardized patient encounter.  Each student in the team will spend 15 minutes with the standardized patient while the other students observe the interaction.

You will have 20 minutes to meet your team and review the patient information for the encounter. In this meeting, you should briefly introduce yourselves and tell your fellow team members where you are in your training. Additionally, you should decide the order for the interviewers (i.e. which student will go first, second etc) and strategize about how your team wants to approach the visit. Consider what skills each team member brings to the team and how to capitalize on these strengths.

During the encounter most team members will watch the interaction remotely.  Depending upon the numbers of participants in your team, one member may be observing in the room with you.

- <u>**DO NOT take any equipment or supplies into the rooms with you - including pagers,  i phones, teaching prompts, or any other materials.**</u>  
- **Make sure that you are wearing your white coat (if you have one) and are dressed professionally. You should bring your own stethoscope if this is equipment you would typically use in your profession.**
- **All other equipment and supplies you will need are provided in each clinic room.**
- **DO NOT enter the rooms until you are instructed to do so by the hall proctor.** 

<u>**The patient encounters:**</u>

A hall proctor will let you know when to enter the room. Before entering the room, briefly review the presenting information posted on the door. This information is the same as the information your team reviewed prior to the encounter.  Please keep in mind that this information is also posted on the door <u>inside</u> each room.

<u>If you decide to leave the room before time is up, you may not go back into the patient room</u>.

All patient encounters should be dealt with as if they were being seen in an outpatient clinic. **We recognize that the encounter and room set-up may be different than how you would typically practice.  The goal of this exercise isn’t to perfectly recreate how you see patients in your setting, but to give you experience working in an interprofessional team.** Treat the standardized patient as a real patient and the encounter as real clinical encounter.  You may use information gathered from watching previous students interact with this patient.  However, keep in mind that if there is a question you feel is very important, you may want to ask it again as it is possible that you may have a specific way of asking the question in your profession that is different.

**For dental students: There will be no intraoral examination or periodontal probing as part of this exercise.  You should perform the rest of your examination as appropriate to the case. Then you may tell the patient, “Now I’d like to do an intraoral exam.”  The patient will then provide you with a folder of the examination results.**

<u>&nbsp;</u><u>**The encounter is a 15-minute encounter.**</u>

**There will be a 5-minute prompt towards the end of the encounters and “time is up” announcement at the end. When you hear this prompt** <u>**you must leave the room**</u>**.** 

There will be a 3-5 minute break between encounters.  During this break, the interviewing student will complete a brief written exercise on the case.  Subsequently, you can meet briefly with your team to discuss the case.

There will be no genital or rectal exams performed on any patient in this exercise.
