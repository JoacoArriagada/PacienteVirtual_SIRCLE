Discussing Life-Sustaining Treatment, Pediatrics

Faculty Guide

**Session Objectives:**

1. Demonstrate ability to establish trust and rapport prior to and during conversations about life-sustaining treatment.
2. Demonstrate a focus on the patient’s surrogate’s values and goals rather than on treatment options for advance care planning.
3. Use appropriate elements of informed consent in a conversation about life-sustaining treatment.
4. Probe for clear understanding of meaning of preferences and goals.

**Faculty Instructions**

Ask students if they have any questions about the scenario. Remind them that their end goal is to discuss preferences for CPR, but that may take several interviewers and the patient’s father may not come to a firm decision.

If the scenario is resolved after 2-3 students, the SP may restart the scenario with a different attitude while keeping the same details.

**Relevant Behaviors for Advance Care Planning Discussions**

**I Preparation**

Ensure appropriate setting and check that relevant people can all attend.

**II Introduction**

1. Explain why you have convened this meeting now.
2. Explain that its purpose is to respect the patient’s wishes.
3. Reassure that death is not believed to be imminent, if true, and avoid false reassurance.
4. Check how the surrogate feels about a discussion of advance care planning.
5. Establish trust.

Consider use of **PEARLS** for relationship building.

**P** - Partnership

**E** - Empathy

**A** - Apology

**R** - Respect

**L** - Legitimating

**S** - Support

**III Information**

1. Ask about understanding of the illness and prognosis.
2. Provide additional information using understandable terms.
3. Clarify shared understanding of illness and prognosis.
4. Ask for understanding of likely further curative or palliative treatments.
5. Provide additional information regarding likely treatments.
6. Ask if choices about life-sustaining treatment have ever been documented.

**IV Elicit Preferences**

1. Explore knowledge of other people’s end of life care and feelings about it.
2. Ask what makes life worth living or what should be the goals of treatment at the end of life.
3. Elicit specific preferences, and ask why.
4. Identify unacceptable life states and what risks are acceptable to avoid them.
5. Discuss probabilities and ask how uncertainty would be managed.
6. If the surrogate says they would not want any treatment, ask specifically about artificial nutrition and hydration.
7. Tell what you will do to meet the patient’s goals and emphasize that the team will remain involved regardless of which goals are chosen.

**V Proxies**

1. Identify who will be proxy.
2. Explore how much communication there has been with the proxy.
3. Stress the need for good communication with the proxy.
4. Ask how much leeway proxy should have in decision-making.

**VI Documentation**

1. Make chart note of key discussion points.
2. Arrange for drafting and signing relevant documentation where applicable.

**VII Relationship**

1. Watch for emotional signals and respond verbally and non-verbally.

Consider using **NURS** for emotion handling.

**N** - Name the emotion.

**U** - Understand the emotion.

**R** - Respect the emotion.

**S** - Support the surrogate.

1. Reassure that uncertainty and confusion are common and that there can be time to reflect as needed.
2. Summarize the conversation, including unresolved issues, and check for shared understanding.
