# **Life-Sustaining Treatment: Kelly Ward (parent: Sam Ward)**

**Patient Information: Kelly Ward**

**Your Role:**

You are a pediatrics intern doing a rotation in the pediatric intensive care unit (PICU). A 9-year-old girl was admitted two days ago with an intracranial hemorrhage. You just rotated onto the service and are on rounds this morning. The team discussed the fact that she had not made any progress towards recovery and may deteriorate at any time, including a possible cardiac arrest. The patient’s oncologist is out of town. The PICU attending asks if you would like to lead the discussion (with them present) with the patient’s father about whether or not cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) would be appropriate or desired.

**Situation:**

Kelly Ward is a 9-year-old girl diagnosed with a brain tumor (glioblastoma) eight months ago. She underwent surgical resection but unfortunately, the entire tumor could not be removed. She subsequently had a ventriculoperitoneal shunt placed for obstructive hydrocephalus.

Because the entire tumor could not be removed, Kelly has been undergoing chemotherapy and radiation, for almost the entire last seven months. This requires her to go to the hospital several times per week for treatment. She has been hospitalized twice since the initial surgery, both times for infections (pneumonia). She has never been intubated before until this hospitalization.

Two days ago, while playing on her handheld gaming device, she had the acute onset of dysarthria, confusion, and the inability to stand. She was immediately brought to the hospital, where she was found to have an acute hemorrhage in and around the tumor. She is now in the PICU and intubated for airway protection. She is still lethargic but responds to voice and stimulation by attempting to open her eyes.

Over the past 48 hours, Kelly has not shown any signs of improvement. The PICU physicians are concerned that she could deteriorate at any time. The neurosurgeons who have consulted do not think any further surgical intervention would be of benefit at this time.

Past Medical History: None other than stated above. Normal development, normal vaccination schedule.

Social History: Lives with her father (Sam Ward). Her mother died two years ago in a motor vehicle collision. Attends 3rd grade.

**Your Task:**

Discuss Kelly’s father’s wishes about CPR. You are not being asked to discuss removing the ventilator at this time. He was told by the team previously the medical facts to date.
