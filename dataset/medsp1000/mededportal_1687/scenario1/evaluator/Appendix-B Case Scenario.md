# Clinical Scenarios:

CASE 1: Called for "shortness of breath"

HPI "50 yo woman travelling with family from Florida with h/o HTN, and GERD presents to ER with progressively worsening dyspnea, PND and nocturia and cough with pink frothy sputum of 2 days duration"

H/o from husband: “he ran out of his prescriptions and has been dining out in restaurants daily”;   
essentially, patient is non-compliant with rx and diet.

Student Evaluation:

1. VS—present on monitor   
2. PE---"sim man" diaphoretic, pallor, peripheral cyanosis, tachycardia(adrenergic activity), rales, wheezes, S3gallop, loud P2 (high pulm art pressure). Second tier findings: “kussmals sign”, JVD, HJR (Rt heart failure)/RUQ tenderness, edema.   
3. Formulate initial ddx   
4. Quick management: upright position, O2, NTG, MSO4, lasix, consider inotropes? (dobutamine, dopamine, PDI (milrinone); note learning point: when to use or avoid betablockers & ACEI for acute decompensated CHF   
5. Quick orders: EKG(LVH,atrial dil, LBBB, old MI), CXR, BNP, CE, chem7, ABG, CBC   
6. Student consultants—during demonstration get few students to evaluate labs, EKG, CXR(cards and radiology "consultants")   
7. Etiology of acute CHF—MI, infxn, anemia etc

Group Discussion/Feedback:

review of pathophysiology and pharmacology for cases  review of evidence-based medicine including practice guidelines  overall feedback of session

# Case Discussion on CHF: Pathophysiology:

heart fails to pump blood to peripheral tissues,   
etiology of myocardial damage or high metabolic demands(anemia, thyrotoxicosis,   
myxedema, beri beri, pagets, myeloma, pregnancy etc)   
adaptive mechanisms include: Frank-Starling mech (SV and LVEDV/venous return),   
myocardial hypertrophy, adrenergic stim(vasoconstriction/afterload), RAAS stim(salt/water   
retention/preload)

# Treatment goals:

decrease preload, afterload, and inhibit RAAS/adrenergic response   
PRELOAD—NTG(SL), mso4, lasix(diuresis(20-90min), pulm art vasodilation and   
venodilation)   
AFTERLOAD—ACEI,ARB, hydralazine, nitroprusside(both preload and afterload reduction)   
RAAS—k-sparing diuretics, ACEI/ARBs
