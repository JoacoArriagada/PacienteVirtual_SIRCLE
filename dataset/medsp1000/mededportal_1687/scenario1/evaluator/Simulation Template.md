I. Title: A simulation-based curriculum for a 4th year medical students during an internal medicine acting internship

II. Target Audience: fourth-year medical students on internal medicine AI

# 1. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives

CASE SIMULATIONS

A. Primary (case simulation) – To teach and reinforce the skills required to perform a comprehensive evaluation and develop a treatment plan for a patient being admitted to the hospital.

Identify required level of care by obtaining appropriate information using history, physical exam findings, and other sources (labs/studies) Demonstrate clinical reasoning by integrating the obtained information Institute immediate interventions as required and form a short-term assessment and plan of care prioritizing actions . Document clinical reasoning by writing a brief assessment of the situation and a plan of care and admission orders for treatment and subsequent studies

B. Secondary (case simulation) –  Recognize the severely ill patient Recognize the mechanics of providing care, including appropriate and professional interactions with co-workers

C. Critical actions checklist (case simulation) – still under development

2. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – For case simulation: Setup involves a hospital gurney on the simulation manikin is placed. A mock portable ECG/vitals monitor (provided by the Laerdal simulation kit) is placed at the head of the gurney. The computer workstation controlling the manikin is located approximately 5 feet from the foot of the gurney. The computer is a standard PC loaded with the software provided by the company and is attached to the manikin and to the mock portable ECG/vitals monitor.   
B. Manikin Set Up – Laerdal SimMan manikin (Laerdal Corporation, Stockholm, Sweden SimMan; Laerdal Medical Corporation, Wappingers Falls, NY, USA) and Laerdal Chester Chest torso & LP body part trainers.  SimMan is a portable and advanced patient simulator for team training with realistic anatomy and clinical functionality. With SimMan, educators are able to test students’ clinical and decision-making skills during realistic patient care scenarios tailored through the use of software and interactive technology. SimMan also provides highly realistic patient simulation for the practice of teamwork, leadership and communication skills that gives immediate feedback to interventions. Please see Appendix A for more details regarding the SimMan manikin. Standard IV tubing, several triple-lumen catheter and lumbar puncture kits (type varies with the availability from hospital’s central supply), gauze, drapes, masks, gowns, gloves. We discuss the superiority of chlorhexidine when prepping the patient; however for cost purposes we use alcohol on the manikins.   
C. Props – ECGs and chest radiographs are projected on the computer screen or provided as printouts when needed.   
D. Distracters – few external distracters exist as the simulation center is selfcontained. The learners at neighboring modules can provide some distraction.

3. Actors

A. Roles – medicine team called to evaluate a patient in the emergency room for possible admission to the medicine service, specific roles not preidentified   
B. Who may play them – other students   
C. Action Role – Case simulations: approximately 4 students surround the manikin when a case simulation is in progress. They are instructed to behave as if they are the medicine team and are assessing a patient in the   
emergency room. Pre-defined roles are not described. The session instructor runs the computer at the foot of the bed and also voices various parts in the scenario: voice of the patient (manikin), nurses providing information, as well as the narrator, etc.   
Procedure module: 2 students surround each procedure manikin, one acts as a primary and the other the assistant. After adequate time, the learners change positions.

III. Case Narrative - describes what the learner will experience. Minor variations occur between iterations of the case, age change, male vs. female, etc.

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants - specify if given freely or must be asked for. For a more detailed description of a case scenario, please see Appendix B. 1. Chief complaint $=$ shortness of breath. Triage note is available: 50- year-old woman who presents with acute onset shortness of breath for 2 days. She reports subjective fever and chest pain (pleuritic chest pain – 7 out of 10); no radiation. Pain increases with inspiration. Prior to this she has had a non-productive cough for about 1 week. She is perimenopausal with no travel history (provided info) 2. Past medical history (must be requested): PMH is significant only for hypertension and GERD 3. Meds and allergies (must be requested): hormone replacement therapy and hydrochlorothiazide; no medication allergies, but she does have an allergy to contrast dye (hives)

4. Family/social history(must be requested):

Father with coronary artery disease – had a myocardial infarction at age 61 Mother had hypertension and diabetes mellitus (type 2) Smokes 1 pack per day for 25 years, but no history of alcohol or illicit drugs

B. Scenario conditions initially (everything aside from the initial handout must be requested specifically)

1. Patient history is voiced by the instructor playing the role of the patient. It is also given in a printed handout.   
2. Patients initial exam: on the handout as well as on the monitor   
3. Patient’s physiology – Generally the manikin simulator is programmed to have decreased breath sounds at the bilateral bases, with some mild bibasilar crackles. An extra heart sound is present (S3). Chest radiograph shows increased pulmonary markings with small pleural effusions.

4. Temp 100.1, tachycardia at 110, tachypnea at 22 breaths/minute, blood pressure 155/90

5. Labs that are available upon request: ABG, basic metabolic panel, liver studies, coagulation studies, serum Ca & Mg, complete blood count with diff

C. Scenario branch points

1. The patient’s condition does not change significantly unless the team makes drastic decisions.   
2. Responses to treatments: If the team decides to give diuretics, the patient may become less dyspneic. Reducing after load can also improve vital signs.

3. Typically learners focus on cardiac etiologies and try to control BP and give diuretics.

IV. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer:

 Things work better if the facilitator doesn’t try to provide “extra” help   
Just answer what the learners are asking   
Get in the roles – when you are voicing the patient, act like a patient. When voicing the nurse- it’s ok to say that a study is pending or hasn’t been drawn yet. This serves to keep the learners on task.

B. Scenario programming

1. Optimal management path – Once it appears that the learners are gravitating towards the cardiac path, the facilitator should keep to that path. We are not designing a trick-scenario. Scenarios generally take up to $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ .

3. Potential errors path(s) – n/a

4. Program debugging – There seems to be a proper sequence to setting up the SimMan simulator. If the air compressor that drives the lungs of the manikin is not primed then the manikin will not breathe correctly. Occasionally, the ECG monitor is not correctly attached. This requires shutting down the computer and reattaching the wires, testing the leads, and rebooting the system (about 5 min).

4. Debriefing Plan

A. Method of debriefing – Individual learners write a brief (1-2 paragraphs) assessment of the case with instructions to provide reasons for their decisions, alternative diagnoses they considered and reasons that they settled on the diagnosis. On a separate handout, individual learners are asked to provide medication orders, fluid orders (if appropriate), follow-up labs and tests (15 min). The instructor collects this information and then the learners begin a group debriefing session on various topics.   
B. Group debriefing topics are generally chosen by consensus of the learners. These have included discussion of heart failure (right vs. left), pulmonary embolism & right heart strain, Light’s criteria for effusions, etc. Group debriefing lasts approximately 20-30 minutes.   
C. During the course of the 4-week rotation, the learners have four afternoon sessions $( 2 { \cdot } 4 \mathrm { p m } )$ dedicated to these didactics. The Case Simulation sessions takes place in 1-2 of these sessions. The maximum number of AI’s per month at our site is 10. When there are more than 5 AI in a month, the Case Simulation is given twice - half of the AI’s in the first session and then repeated with the remaining AIs. The Procedure Module takes place in another session.
