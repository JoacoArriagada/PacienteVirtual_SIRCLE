# Part 1

A 20-40 year old female, 15 weeks pregnant, presents to her OB/GYN for a routine visit. She is noted to have a swollen, bruised left arm.

The patient discloses that she has been in a long-standing abusive relationship with her current partner. The pregnancy was unexpected, but she is happy about having the child. Her partner, however, is not. Historically, the partner had only been verbally abusive. He would tell her that she is worthless without him and that she is too stupid to hold a job or make it on her own. He had never been physically abusive until yesterday, when he grabbed her by her arm and threw her against the wall. She is scared to report him and feels she needs to forgive him because she relies on him financially. She is afraid she will not be able to support herself or the baby without him, since he has a “good job” as a banker. She has a sister who lives nearby, but she hates the idea of “crawling back to my family.”

HITS: Physically Hurt (rarely), Insult (fairly often), Threaten with physical harm (sometimes), Scream or curse (fairly often)

Change importance scale: 5 out of 10
Change confidence scale: 2 out of 10

The patient feels safe going home and is not open to discussing a plan for change. She definitely does not want to go to a shelter or call the police but will take a few handouts.

# Feedback and discussion

This patient exhibited resistance to change. Here are some tips to consider when debriefing this case:

Patients will often be less resistant to change if they are invited to actively participate in discussing and planning the change. Try opening with, “What do you think would be the best next step?”
As noted in the checklist, try to understand the true nature of the resistance by asking “Maybe we need to try a different way of approaching this. What makes sense to you at this point?” or “I can understand that you might be hesitant at this point. What do you think might be contributing to this?”
Don’t be surprised by resistance to change, and try to avoid being defensive. Remember that the resistance is not directed personally towards you, but towards the situation and the context.
Remember that you can’t change anyone else; you can only change yourself. As such, you can’t always change your patients, but you can change how you interact with them.
