**Title: Simulation of Cardiovascular and Respiratory Distress for the Pediatric Clerkship**

4. Preparation
   1. Programming
      1. Simulator programming should be completed prior to the start of the educational session. See individual section for programming recommendations.
   4. Orient Nursing Staff
      1. Nursing staff can be nursing students, inpatient, intensive care unit (ICU) or emergency department (ED) nurses. Nurses will function in the scenario as an inpatient or ED nurse. Nurses will perform requested tasks and, if requested, can provide advice or recommendations. Orient nurses to materials, equipment and medications in simulation lab.

Actors

- Roles: parent, nurse.
- Who may play them: resident physicians, attending physicians, nurses or actors.
- The simulated parent will provide patient history to students.
- The simulated nurse will provide IV access, medications, oxygen or other tasks requested by students.

Orientation and Shared Setup

- Students are oriented to the simulation lab including the mannequin, monitor, equipment, medications, and nursing staff. They are provided a telephone to call for help if needed. Students are instructed to ask for vital signs, as they will not be given initially. Blood pressures will not be automated throughout the simulation.
- Each group consists of four students. Students are encouraged to assign roles prior to the start of the simulation: group leader, recorder, examiner, and historian/family communicator.
- Students are provided with a brief 1-2 sentence description of the scenario prior to entering the simulation lab.
- Three resident/faculty observers are necessary: one annotates the scenario for debriefing, one observes the scenario, and one operates the high-fidelity simulator.

Scenario Overview

- Case #4: Hypovolemic Shock
         1. Students will be provided with brief history: 6-month old with vomiting and diarrhea presents to emergency department.
         2. Students must request vital signs initially. HR, RR and oxygen saturations will be continuous. BP and temperature must be requested after initial set of vitals.
         3. The parent will provide the past medical history, social history, family history and allergies.
         4. Additional history will be given upon request.

1. Instructor Notes
   1. Tips to keep scenarios flowing well.
      1. Scenario may vary depending upon direction students take the case, adjust as necessary.
      2. Offer a telephone to the students. They will be able to call and ask questions if they are having difficulty with the scenario.
   2. Time limit
      1. If students do not perform required tasks, the simulation will end within five minutes of last clinical decision.
   3. Tips to actors
      1. Students should ask for vitals upon entering the room.
      2. Nurses should repeat BP’s upon request from the students.
      6. Hypovolemic shock
         1. The parent is present throughout entire scenario and provides history.
         2. The nurse is unable to obtain IV access and will require IO placement.
         3. Nurses may give suggestions for management if requested by students.
   4. Anticipated Management Mistakes
      1. Monitor failure.
         1. Vital signs are called into room over speaker.
      2. Assessment of perfusion/capillary refill on mannequin.
         1. If students asked about perfusion/capillary refill, this was called into the room over the speaker.
      3. Students ask for laboratory exams or radiologic exams that are unavailable for the patient.
         1. Nurse will explain that these are unavailable.
