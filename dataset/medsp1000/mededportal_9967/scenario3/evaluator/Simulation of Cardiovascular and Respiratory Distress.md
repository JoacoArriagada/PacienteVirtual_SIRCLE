**Title: Simulation of Cardiovascular and Respiratory Distress for the Pediatric Clerkship**

1. Target Audience: MSIII students during Pediatric Clerkship
3. Learning Objectives
   1. Primary
      1. Recognize an acutely ill child, obtain pertinent history and perform focused physical exam.
      2. Recognize clinical signs of a child in respiratory or cardiovascular distress.
      3. Recognize the normal range of vital signs in pediatric patients.
      4. Identify pathophysiology of four common pediatric diseases.
      5. Demonstrate closed loop communication and promote a team approach in the management of an acutely ill patient.
   2. Secondary
      1. Obtain focused history from a parent or patient in emergency situation.
      2. Formulate a differential diagnosis for common pediatric diseases presenting with respiratory distress or cardiovascular compromise.
      3. Practice identifying and assigning roles in a team setting.
      4. Identify initial therapies needed to stabilize a patient in an emergency setting.
   3. Critical Actions Checklist
      1. Recognize a child in respiratory or cardiovascular distress.
      2. Perform focused physical exam.
      3. Take pertinent, focused history.
      4. Perform initial management.
      5. Observe and respond to patient’s response to therapies.
      6. Determine admission status.

Scenario Overview

- Case #3: Anaphylaxis
         1. Students will be provided with brief history: 13 year-old with dizziness and leg pain who presents to emergency department.
         2. Students must request vital signs initially. HR, RR and oxygen saturations will be continuous. BP and temperature must be requested after initial set of vitals.
         3. The parent will arrive five minutes into the scenario and provide the past medical history, social history, family history and allergies. Patient is light-headed with tongue swelling and unable to provide a thorough history.
         4. Additional history will be given upon request.
         5. Please refer to Anaphylaxis Simulation Sheet.

Potential Error Pathways for Students:

1. Students do not check vital signs upon entering the room.
   1. The students may not recognize the extent of tachypnea or desaturation & this may prolong intervention.
   2. Understand the importance of obtaining vital signs upon entering the room.
2. Students do not recognize hypotension.
   1. Patient will become progressively more confused and “light-headed.”
   2. Prompt students to recognize hypotension and provide initial treatment with a fluid bolus.
3. Students do not recognize bee sting.
   1. Nurse in room can lead students to bee sting.
   2. Encourage a thorough skin evaluation when the patient has complaints involving an extremity.
4. Students do not give steroids.
   1. Discuss therapies for anaphylaxis. Most are short-acting and steroids will prevent any relapse.

1. Instructor Notes
   1. Tips to keep scenarios flowing well.
      1. Scenario may vary depending upon direction students take the case, adjust as necessary.
      2. Offer a telephone to the students. They will be able to call and ask questions if they are having difficulty with the scenario.
   2. Time limit
      1. If students do not perform required tasks, the simulation will end within five minutes of last clinical decision.
   3. Tips to actors
      1. Students should ask for vitals upon entering the room.
      2. Nurses should repeat BP’s upon request from the students.
      5. Anaphylaxis
         1. Bee sting is over left leg and may be covered up by pants.
         2. Patient is dizzy/light-headed from hypotension and has difficulty talking due to tongue swelling. She isn’t able to describe bite until the students discover it upon exam. After that, she will recall bite and give HPI.
         3. Parent will arrive five minutes into scenario and provide history (PMH, SH, FH, allergies, medications) upon request.
         4. Nurses may give suggestions for management if requested by students.
   4. Anticipated Management Mistakes
      1. Monitor failure.
         1. Vital signs are called into room over speaker.
      2. Assessment of perfusion/capillary refill on mannequin.
         1. If students asked about perfusion/capillary refill, this was called into the room over the speaker.
      3. Students ask for laboratory exams or radiologic exams that are unavailable for the patient.
         1. Nurse will explain that these are unavailable.
2. Debriefing Plan
   1. Structure: group of four students debriefed in groups with 2-3 resident/faculty observers.
   2. Resident/Faculty observers may facilitate discussion as students are encouraged to reflect upon experience.
   3. Use white board to display three separate categories: Medical Decisions, Confidence, and Teamwork.
      1. Each category is separated into 2 subcategories: “Things Done Well” depicted by “+” sign and “Areas for Improvement” depicted by “- “sign. See attached debriefing chart for more details.
   4. Scenarios are recorded and the video is reviewed throughout the debriefing to discuss white board topics.
   5. End each debriefing session by identifying the concepts learned and emphasizing the progress the group makes in each scenario.
