# Stimulation 2:

1. Title: “Ankyloglossia”

4. Environment

1. Set Up: Office or clinic room   
2. Props: 3D dental mold   
3. Distractors: none   
4. Actor: female postdoctoral fellow   
5. Role: adult female who is the mother of a 3-year-old Michael. Michael was   
invisible and was represented by his dental mold.

# Case Study

Michael is a 3-year-old boy who was born with a short frenulum. Michael’s mother reported that she can only understand about half of what her son says. She also reported that he had some difficulty sucking as a young infant.

Dental Consequences: Ankyloglossia, lingual gingival recession, diastema between mandibular central incisors (#25 and #26).

Speech Consequences: Feeding consequences, He has difficulty producing /t/, /d/, /n/, and /l/.

# Scenario Background Given Freely to Participants

My son Michael is 3-years-old. When he was a baby he had some trouble sucking and getting the milk out of the bottle. Now that Michael is speaking, I can only understand about half of what he says. He also has difficulty licking an ice cream cone or a lollipop.

It is recommended that the actor is trained to provide all of the above information in their scenario to each team within two minutes of the encounter to ensure a fair and standard exposure.
