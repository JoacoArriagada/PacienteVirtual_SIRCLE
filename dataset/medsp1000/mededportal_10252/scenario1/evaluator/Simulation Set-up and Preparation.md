# Stimulation 1:

1. Title: “Missing Teeth”

2. Target Audience: SLP undergraduate students enrolled in Anatomy and Physiology of the Speech and Hearing Mechanism and Dental students in year 1-3 of their program.   
3. Learning Objectives Primary 1. Exchange clinically relevant information through interprofessional teams to identify and implement appropriate care for patients, based on comprehensive evaluations. 2. Apply interprofessional practice principles that lead to timely, efficient and effective delivery of patient-centered oral health care. 3. Promote an in-depth understanding of how dentistry and speech-language pathology both play a vital role in oral health and how one profession greatly influences the other. Secondary 1. Experience real-time interpretation of complications and recommend a treatment plan. 2. Recognize the importance of good communication across disciplines. 3. Learn how to be professional with a client and report information to the client in a way that he/she can easily understand.

Critical actions checklist—Examiner’s will have 15 minutes to:

1. Introduce yourself to client (1 min)   
2. Determine the issue the client is facing as a team (3 min)   
3. Inspect the 3D dental mold (1 min)   
4. Discuss the diagnosis and treatment as a team (4 min)   
5. Complete SOAP note (3 min)   
6. Report the treatment plan back to the client as a team (3 min)

4. Environment

1. Set Up: Office or clinic room   
2. Props: 3D dental mold   
3. Distractors: none   
4. Actor: male research assistant   
5. Role: adult male

Case Study Scenario Background Information: for faculty and actor only

Tim: 18 years old and has no other known medical complications.

One day, Tim was riding his bicycle to his friend’s house when he noticed a loud noise coming from the bike. Suddenly, his front wheel locked and Tim was thrown over the handlebars, landing face-first on the pavement in front of him. He did not have any serious neck or back injuries.

Dental Consequences: Missing his central incisors, as well as his right lateral incisor. Loss of alveolar bone leading to collapse of lower facial $1 / 3 ^ { \mathrm { r d } }$ , mesial drift of right canine (#6) and left lateral incisor (#10).

Speech Consequences: Some phonemes have been difficult to produce correctly. These include /s/, //, /v/, and likely all alveolar sounds. Reduced intelligibility.

It is recommended to have a speech-language pathologist have a brief training session with the actor to so that they may accurately portray these speech errors.

# Scenario Background Given Freely to Participants

One day, I was riding my bike to my friend’s house when I noticed a loud noise coming from the bike. Suddenly, my front wheel locked and I was thrown over the handlebars, landing face-first on the pavement. I went to the hospital for a check-up after and did not have any serious neck or back injuries. Now, I feel like my friends can’t understand a word I say.

It is recommended that the actor is trained to provide all of the above information in their scenario to each team within two minutes of the encounter to ensure a fair and standard exposure.
