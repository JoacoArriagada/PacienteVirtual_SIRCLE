# Stimulation 3:

1. Title: “Tongue Thruster”

2. Target Audience: SLP undergraduate students enrolled in Anatomy and Physiology of the Speech and Hearing Mechanism and Dental students in year 1-3 of their program.

3. Learning Objectives Primary 1. Exchange clinically relevant information through interprofessional teams to identify and implement appropriate care for patients, based on comprehensive evaluations. 2. Apply interprofessional practice principles that lead to timely, efficient and effective delivery of patient-centered oral health care. 3. Promote an in-depth understanding of how dentistry and speech-language pathology both play a vital role in oral health and how one profession greatly influences the other. Secondary 1. Experience real-time interpretation of complications and recommend a treatment plan. 2. Recognize the importance of good communication across disciplines. 3. Learn how to be professional with a client and report information to the client in a way they can easily understand.

Critical actions checklist—Examiner’s will have 15 minutes to:

1. Introduce yourself to client (1 min)   
2. Determine the issue the client is facing as a team (3 min)   
3. Inspect the 3D dental mold (1 min)   
4. Discuss the diagnosis and treatment as a team (4 min)   
5. Complete SOAP note (3 min)   
6. Report the treatment plan back to the client as a team (3 min)

4. Environment

1. Set Up: Office or clinic room   
2. Props: 3D dental mold   
3. Distractors: none   
4. Actor: female research assistant   
5. Role: 10-year old girl

Case Study Scenario Background Information: for faculty and actor only

Sally is 10 years old and demonstrates a tongue thrust when she swallows. Her dentist says that she needs braces, but her orthodontist will not put braces on until the tongue thrust is resolved. This is because when the braces are removed, if the tongue thrust continues, the teeth may move forward again due to the pressure of the tongue.

Speech consequences: She has difficulty producing /s/, /z/, “ch”, “sh” and “zh”

It is recommended to have a speech-language pathologist have a brief training session with the actor to so that they may accurately portray these speech errors.

Dental consequences: As a result of the tongue thrust, Sally’s top central incisors are pushed forward.   
Anterior open bite, protrusion and rotation of central incisors.

Scenario Background Given Freely to Participants

I am 10 years old and my dentist says that I need braces. My mother took me to the orthodontist and he said that he would not put braces on me until my tongue acts more normal or goes back or something. He said if he puts braces on and I don’t fix my tongue then my teeth may move again and I would need braces again!

It is recommended that the actor is trained to provide all of the above information in their scenario to each team within two minutes of the encounter to ensure a fair and standard exposure.
