**Dana Jones, physical and hearing – Instructor’s Guide**

<u>**Resource**</u>: 	Dana Jones, physical and hearing

<u>**How to use the resource file**</u>:	The resource file contains information necessary for training standardized patients, presented in a table format. It describes the case, the way used/validated, contains SP training notes and additional details.

<u>**Purpose of the resource**</u>: 	To enrich the sources of case scenarios available to standardized patient trainers and medical school faculty for formative training or objective structured clinical examinations.

<u>**Educational Objectives**</u>:

- Perform a general physical examination
- Appropriately use verbal and non-verbal communication skills to establish rapport with patient.
- Respond to a communication challenge (late disclosure of problems with hearing)
- Perform hearing exam – whispered voice test, Weber test and Rinne test.

<u>**Conceptual background**</u>: 	This case was created for use with standardized patients, for teaching and/or evaluation purposes. The case is intended for teaching/examination of medical students, years 1 through 4 (depending on medical school’s type of curriculum). This case could also be used for resident entry-level testing and faculty development in teaching.

<u>**Practical implementation advice**</u>: 	All original materials/patient exam room needs are described in the case, <u>users are free to change the original case</u> to better suit their needs, as long as due credit is applied.

<u>**Use of section “Standardizing the Unknown”**</u>:	This section is intended for use when training multiple SPs for portraying the same case. Its purpose is two-prong:

1. It is very hard to predict and standardize the answers to each and every question learners might decide to ask of the SP. Having more details to the portrait makes the character more complete, and the SPs can easier associate with and <u>think</u> as the patient portrayed.
2. The details referred to in this section might be quite different for different states, cities and schools. These questions are intended for the group of SPs to decide on. This gives them the chance to partake in the creation of the portrayal, bring local detail to the role, and take <u>ownership</u> of the role. The SP trainers should feel free to add/delete questions in this section to make it most applicable to their local specific circumstances.

In my experience with adding this section to the SP case, it takes less than 10 minutes of allotted training time for the SP group to decide on the details, and on numerous occasions has meaningfully contributed to the case presentation. Usually, the last question related to “What else would you like to know about this person?” brings up any parts of the role that might have not been clear or well described during training.

The notion of training standardized patients in the context of a role, rather than for specific interrogative props is gaining momentum across the nation and is considered a possibility for SP training for the USMLE Step 2 CS examination. Karen Lewis, Board of Directors Member for the Association for Standardized Patient Educators (ASPE), ASPE Publications Committee  member and Director of the SP Program at George Washington University, wrote in the September 2011 issue of “ASPE News”: “In my experience, simulated activities where the SP can perform within the confines of a behavior profile almost always accomplish better learning for our students, clearer educator understanding of what they have learned, and more informed ideas about the next steps.”

<u>**Recommended length of session:**</u>	15 to 45 minutes for learner in patient room, 10 minutes for patient note (if required), at least 30 min for debriefing with faculty-observer (for teaching purposes only). For new learners, it is helpful working in couples of two students with a faculty-observer and using longer sessions (e.g.30-45 min). For learners who have more experience, shorter time for patient encounter may be allotted (e.g. “standard” 15 min for OSCE-type exams).

<u>**Case has been successfully implemented with 40 MS 1 students at Paul Foster SOM.**</u> On a continuous basis at the originating program, inter-rater reliability was assessed, and for 20 consecutive cases at the Center, it was above 80%. Additionally, a case study was completed to review the inter-rater reliability between SPs and faculty-observers in a multiple-station high-stakes OSCE examination; results indicated faculty and SPs matched in scoring; manuscript is under preparation.
