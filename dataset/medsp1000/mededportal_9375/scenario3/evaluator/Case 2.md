**Simulation Session 2**

**Cognitive Decision Making**

**William Paolo, MD**

**SUNY Upstate Department of Emergency Medicine**

1. **TITLE:** PE or Pneumonia?
2. **Target Audience:** Emergency medicine residents, Medical Students
3. **Learning Objectives:**
   1. **Primary**
      1. This is the final scenario of the cognitive decisions curriculum and the interactions regarding the examiner and examinee occur immediately during the debriefing section. Unlike the previous 2 scenarios the learner is not blinded to the case insofar as he/she will understand the role is not the medical resolution of the case rather it is the real time identification of cognitive decision pitfalls as have been highlighted throughout the curriculum. The examinee should be able to determine the biasing influences his/her cognitive thought process has on their decisions and verbalize them immediately upon completion of the scenario. In addition feedback will be given after the scenario using the same fill in the blank form used for case# 2 in the 1st simulation.
4. **Critical Actions**
   1. Collect Appropriate History
   2. IV, O2, Monitor
   3. Obtain basic laboratory studies
      1. EKG/Troponin/Chest X-ray
   4. Properly Interpret EKG
      1. Normal sinus rhythm
   5. Interpret CXR
      1. Infiltrate
         1. CT-A if ordered reveals PE and pulmonary infarction
         2. CXR alone appears typical for either pneumonia or infarction
   6. Admit patient to appropriate service (ICU)
   7. Relay relevant information to examiner after completion of scenario
5. **Environment**
   1. **Lab Set Up:** Standard simulation room with one-way mirror. Directors may participate in multiple roles (i.e. nurse, EMS provider, cardiology consultant). Simulation participants may consult various services via telephone in the simulation room.
   2. **Manikin Set Up:** A standard simulation doll is required for this simulation. Basic programming allowing for control of essential vital signs and cardiac monitoring is essential to the realism of the scenario.
   3. **Props:**
      1. Resuscitation bay with standard resuscitation equipment (i.e. intubation tools, bag-valve-mask, IV equipment).
      2. EKG machine or ability to project and/or hand the participant the necessary EKG
      3. CXR display device (i.e. PACS machine, hard copy of relevant CXR)
      4. Medications
6. **Actors:**
   1. **Roles**
      1. Paramedic: The paramedic should radio ahead stating that he/she is bringing in a patient with pneumonia from a nursing home. The patient is delirious and it is difficult to obtain further information from the notes sent from the nursing home. He relays however that the patient had one episode of blood-tinged sputum.
      2. Nurse (RN): The nurse should do what the participant asks for but constantly remind the participant of the ill appearance of the patient and that the ED is quite busy. He/she should be helpful however will not contribute towards diagnosis.
      3. Patient: Manikin. Should be portrayed in distress by the course leader and/or assistant.
      4. MICU Consultant: Will be available via phone. Will not see the patient and/or the CXR or CT-A (if ordered) but will continually defer to the judgment of the provider. Will accept the patient to the ICU if either diagnosis (pneumonia or PE with infarction) is conveyed.

1. **Debriefing Plan:**
   1. As with the previous case the scenario is built in order for the learner to examine the multifactorial influence that contribute to bias and subsequent cognitive error in the simulation. The debriefing is intended for the learner to discuss both the perceived cognitive errors that he/she made and the potential for errors unbeknownst to the learner during the case. As with the previous cases the debriefing is not intended to highlight the medical teaching points of the case, rather, the discussion should focus on the assessment of cognitive error. The preceptor should then go over the questions listed below in a final exit interview.

**Cognitive Error Review**

1) Name 3 cognitive errors you committed in this scenario.

2) What internal factors contributed to your errors?

3) What external factors contributed to your errors?

4) What could you have done to avoid committing the above errors?

5) What most contributed to the error in your thought process?
