**Simulation Session 1**

**Cognitive Decision Making**

**Joint Case 1/2**

1. **TITLE:** The presentation of a STEMI
2. **Target Audience:** Emergency medicine residents, Medical Students
3. **Learning Objectives:**
   1. **Primary**
      1. This 1st case is intended to be a straightforward STEMI scenario. In the scenario a patient will be presenting with an inferior wall STEMI. The examinee should demonstrate an adequate knowledge of the management and recognition of STEMI as outlined below. There is a catheterization laboratory available so it is unnecessary to consider thrombolysis in this scenario. The cardiology consult will not enter the room, is only available via phone, and will not interpret the 12 lead EKG. The EMS provider will radio ahead informing the examinee that they have a patient who is having “chest pain” and will not provide a presumptive diagnosis. The nurse in the room will frequently prompt the examinee to administer medications that are on the ACLS pathway for STEMI (i.e. “shouldn’t we start the heparin now”) and will follow the physician orders that deviate from ACLS reluctantly.
      2. The purpose of this case is to set the stage for the following case where a similar presentation will in fact be an aortic dissection and not a STEMI.
   2. **Critical Actions**
      1. Collect Appropriate History
      2. IV, O2, Monitor
      3. Obtain basic laboratory studies
         1. EKG/Troponin/Chest X-ray
      4. Properly Interpret EKG
         1. STEMI
      5. Provide appropriate medications as per the 2012 AHA updated STEMI management guidelines
         1. Primary reperfusion in the coronary catheterization laboratory as soon as possible
         2. Emergency initiation of antiplatelet therapy
            1. Aspirin 162-325mg
            2. P2Y12 receptor inhibitor (any one of the following as per the 2012 AHA guidelines)

Clopidogrel 600mg

Prasugrel 60mg

Ticagrelor 180mg

* + - * 1. Heparin therapy

Unfractioned heparin

60 Units/Kg blous (maximum of 4000 Units)

12 Uniits/kg/hr infusion (maximum of 1000 units/hr)

Can consider Bivalirudin as an alternative to unfractionated heparin

* + - * 1. IIb/IIIa inhibitors

Not invariably started in the emergency department (some groups will begin within the cath-lab). It is important to tailor this to your particular hospital setting. If your institution uses in the ED ensure examinee familiar with use of IV IIb/IIIa inhibitors otherwise can defer to cardiology in the cath-lab.

Abciximab 0.25 mg/kg IV bolus over 1 minute

Followed by 0.125 mg/kg/min IV

* + - 1. Pain control
         1. Nitroglycerin 0.4mg sublingually x3

The examinee should provide sublingual nitroglycerin for pain control. A total of 3 tablets will completely relive the patient’s pain making the need for a continuous IV infusion unnecessary. In the scenario the patient has no RV involvement so it is unnecessary to have the patient become hypotense with the administration of nitroglycerin. Should a right-sided EKG be requested prior to the administration of nitroglycerin the examinee will be told that it is normal (no additional EKG provided).

* + - 1. Beta-blockers
         1. The use of IV beta-blockers has proven to be a controversial topic in the emergent management of STEMI. It is not critical that the patient be provided an IV beta-blocker in the scenario and if provided will not change the physiological findings as manifest by the vital signs of the patient.

       1. Activate cardiac catheterization laboratory
    2. Relay relevant information to examiner after completion of scenario

1. **Environment**
   1. **Lab Set Up:** Standard simulation room with one-way mirror. Directors may participate in multiple roles (i.e. nurse, EMS provider, cardiology consultant). Simulation participants may consult various services via telephone in the simulation room.
   2. **Manikin Set Up:** A standard simulation doll is required for this simulation. Basic programming allowing for control of essential vital signs and cardiac monitoring is essential to the realism of the scenario.
   3. **Props:**
      1. Resuscitation bay with standard resuscitation equipment (i.e. intubation tools, bag-valve-mask, IV equipment).
      2. EKG machine or ability to project and/or hand the participant the necessary EKG
      3. CXR display device (i.e. PACS machine, hard copy of relevant CXR)
      4. Medications
2. **Actors:**
   1. **Roles**
      1. Paramedic: The paramedic should radio ahead stating that he/she is bringing in a patient with a STEMI at the onset of the scenario. Upon entering the room with the patient the paramedic should commence with a presentation that emphasizes the patient’s distress from his STEMI.
      2. Nurse (RN): The nurse should do what the participant asks for but constantly remind the participant of the time constraints of STEMI treatment. He/she should be very pushy about the need to quickly move this patient in and out of a very busy ED and request to give medications before they are asked for.
      3. Patient: Manikin. Should be portrayed in distress by the course leader and/or assistant.
      4. Cardiology Consult: Will be available via phone. Will not see the patient and/or the EKG but will continually defer to the judgment of the provider. If the participant deems the patient a STEMI he will open the cath lab and accept the patient.
3. **Case Narrative:**
   1. **Scenario Background**
      1. **Paramedic radio call:**
         1. Bringing into you a 45-year-old male with crushing substernal chest pressure. It started 1-hour prior. The patient has a history of high cholesterol and hypertension. Field EKG demonstrates ST-elevation. The patient is then brought directly into the resuscitation bay.
         2. Past Medical History: high cholesterol, hypertension
         3. Past Surgical history: none
         4. Family History: none
         5. Social History: Smoker 1ppdx 20 years
         6. Medications: “statin”
      2. **Initial patient encounter:**
         1. Obvious distress. Clutching chest. Asking for pain medication, asking for wife to come. Asking “Am I having a heart attack. Please help me. Please don’t let me die.”
      3. **Positive Review of Systems (all else negative)**
         1. Chest pain
         2. Diaphoresis
         3. Nausea
         4. Radiation of pain to the left arm
      4. **Physical examination:**
         1. BP 180/90, HR 88, RR 12, T 37, O2: 99%
         2. General: Obvious distress secondary to discomfort
         3. Skin: warm, diaphoretic
         4. Rest of physical examination—normal
      5. **Ancillary Tests**
         1. EKG (see attached EKG) STEMI inferior leads
         2. CXR (see attached) within normal limits
         3. Laboratories
            1. CBC, BMP, Coags Normal
            2. Troponin T: 2.50
4. **Debriefing Plan:**
   1. The examinee should upon completion of this case go over the case with the examiner and all relevant STEMI treatment should be reviewed as outlined above in the critical actions. Each categorization of therapy (i.e. pain control) should be discussed and the importance of each step be emphasized. Under this scenario the patient is presenting to a hospital with a fully equipped cath-lab with on-call cardiologist therefore it is not imperative that fibrinolytics be discussed.
   2. The goal of this scenario is to begin to bias the thought process of the examinee. He/she should be unaware that the case is merely to set-up cognitive decisions in case # 2. There is no formal evaluation form for this scenario as it is merely a dummy scenario to set up case #2. The examiner should go over STEMI management with the examinee as if the purpose of the simulation is to review ACS management. After a short debriefing in which the important aspects of STEMI management are discussed case scenario number 2 (almost exactly the same as the first) commences.
