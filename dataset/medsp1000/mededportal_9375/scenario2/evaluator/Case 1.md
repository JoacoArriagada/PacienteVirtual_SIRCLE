**Simulation Session 1**

**Cognitive Decision Making**

**Joint Case 1/2**

5. **TITLE:** Dissection presenting as a STEMI
6. **Target Audience:** Emergency medicine residents, Medical Students
7. **Learning Objectives:**
   1. **Primary:**
      1. The 2nd case is designed to cause multiple cognitive errors in the examinee. With the initial case fresh on the mind as well as the mistakes made, multiple cognitive decision errors are intended to occur in this scenario. This is a case of a Type-A aortic dissection with involvement of the coronary arteries mimicking a STEMI. All of the providers from EMS to nursing should be pushing the examinee to act upon the STEMI. When cardiology is consulted they will again be unable to see the patient in the ED but will be available waiting for the patient in the cath-lab. The examiner should write down the cognitive errors as they occur (i.e. anchoring bias), as these are the key learning points that are to be reviewed upon completion of the simulation.
   2. **Critical Actions**
      1. Collect Appropriate History
      2. IV, O2, Monitor
      3. Obtain basic laboratory studies
      4. EKG/Troponin/Chest X-ray
      5. Properly Interpret EKG
      6. STEMI however this is designed to fool the participant
      7. Provide appropriate medications
      8. Consult cardiology
      9. Relay relevant information to examiner after completion of scenario
8. **Environment**
   1. **Lab Set Up:** Standard simulation room with one-way mirror. Directors may participate in multiple roles (i.e. nurse, EMS provider, cardiology consultant). Simulation participants may consult various services via telephone in the simulation room.
   2. **Manikin Set Up:** A standard simulation doll is required for this simulation. Basic programming allowing for control of essential vital signs and cardiac monitoring is essential to the realism of the scenario.
   3. **Props:**
      1. Resuscitation bay with standard resuscitation equipment (i.e. intubation tools, bag-valve-mask, IV equipment).
      2. EKG machine or ability to project and/or hand the participant the necessary EKG
      3. CXR display device (i.e. PACS machine, hard copy of relevant CXR)
      4. Medications
9. **Actors:**
   1. **Roles**
      1. Paramedic: The paramedic should radio ahead stating that he/she is bringing in a patient with a STEMI at the onset of the scenario. Upon entering the room with the patient the paramedic should commence with a presentation that emphasizes the patient’s distress from his STEMI.
      2. Nurse (RN): The nurse should do what the participant asks for but constantly remind the participant of the time constraints of STEMI treatment. He/she should be very pushy about the need to quickly move this patient in and out of a very busy ED and request to give medications before they are asked for.
      3. Patient: Manikin. Should be portrayed in distress by the course leader and/or assistant.
      4. Cardiology Consult: Will be available via phone. Will not see the patient and/or the EKG but will continually defer to the judgment of the provider. If the participant deems the patient a STEMI he will open the cath lab and accept the patient.
10. **Case Narrative:**
    1. **Scenario Background**
       1. **Paramedic radio call:**
       2. Bringing into you a 59-year-old male with crushing substernal chest pressure. It started 1-hour prior. The patient has a history of high cholesterol and hypertension. Field EKG demonstrates ST-elevation. The patient is then brought directly into the resuscitation bay.
       3. Past Medical History: high cholesterol, hypertension
       4. Past Surgical history: none
       5. Family History: Early MI in Father/Brother
       6. Social History: Smoker 1ppdx 20 years
       7. Medications: “statin”
    2. **Initial patient encounter:**
       1. Obvious distress. Clutching chest. Asking for pain medication, asking for wife to come. Asking “Am I having a heart attack. Please help me. Please don’t let me die.”
    3. **Positive Review of Systems (all else negative)**
       1. Chest pain
       2. Diaphoresis
       3. Nausea
       4. Radiation of pain to the left arm
    4. **Physical examination:**
    5. BP 210/110 (disparity between arms if asked for) HR 77, RR 12, T 37, O2: 99%
    6. General: Obvious distress secondary to discomfort
    7. Skin: warm, diaphoretic
    8. Rest of physical examination—normal
11. **Ancillary Tests**
    1. EKG (see attached EKG) STEMI inferior leads
    2. CXR (see attached) within normal limits
    3. Laboratories
    4. CBC, BMP, Coags Normal
    5. Troponin T: 15
12. **Debriefing Plan**
    1. The debriefing plan is intended, as is the case, to elicit the understanding of cognitive decision errors and not the correct medical diagnosis/appropriate management of the case. Multiple biasing influences have specifically been placed into the case scenario (including the memory of the exact same presentation in scenario one) to bias the participant into making errors. The course leader should write down the obvious errors that occur during the scenario that leads to the misdiagnosis of STEMI in place of aortic dissection. Using the question tool (attached) the learner should self-assess to determine the errors committed and the external/internal influences that contribute to the genesis of error. If the learner arrives at the correct diagnosis the discussion should still focus on the possibility of errors that did not occur and what external and internal influences could have contributed to their development.

**Cognitive Error Review**

1) Name 3 cognitive errors you committed in this scenario.

2) What internal factors contributed to your errors?

1. What external factors contributed to your errors?
2. What could you have done to avoid committing the above errors?

5) What most contributed to the error in your thought process?
