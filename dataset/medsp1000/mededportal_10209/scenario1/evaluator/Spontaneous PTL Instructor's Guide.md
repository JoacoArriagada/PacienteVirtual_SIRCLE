# 5. Goals and Objectives

ACGME Competencies: (1) Medical Knowledge, (2) Patient Care,(3) Interpersonal and Communication Skills, (4) Professionalism,(5) System-based Practice, (6) Practice-based Learning and Improvement

# Goal 1: Experience a Scenario Involving the Management of PTL

The learner will experience a scenario involving the management of PTL in a simulated setting.

Objective 1: Patient Assessment

The learner will take a history and conduct a physical examination to determine that a clinical diagnosis of PTL is appropriate.

Objective 2: Appropriate Monitoring   
The learner should assess both maternal and fetal well-being (contractions, maternal vital signs,   
and fetal heart rate monitoring)

Objective 3: Determination of Maternal and fetal status Order appropriate laboratory tests and ultrasound

Objective 4: Proper Management

The learner should be able to determine a diagnosis, counsel regarding the risks/benefits/prognosis and develop an appropriate plan of action for the patient.

# Goal 2: Teamwork and Interprofessional Communication

The learner will become more skilled in interacting with nursing staff and a simulated patient in a stressful simulated setting.

Objective 1: The learner will demonstrate components of communication with his or her team that is in line with TeamSTEPPS or a similar teamwork and team communication system\*. (1, 2, 3)

Objective 2: The learner will use components of communication that facilitate the patient’s understanding and allow the patient to maintain confidence in the team. (2, 3, 4)

# Goal 3: Self-assessment of Technical and Communication Skills

The learner will review the video-recorded simulation session with the instructor, and identify areas for improvement relating to technical and communication skills.

Objective 1: After reviewing the video, the learner will be asked to identify aspects of the simulation, including both communication and management, that were both successful and that need improvement. (3, 6)

Objective 2: The learner will be asked to evaluate his/her performance during the simulation event by completing a self-assessment form. (5)

\*TeamSTEPPS (Strategies and Tools to Enhance Performance and Patient Safety) is a teamwork system developed by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality with the DOD to improve patient safety through optimization of communication and teamwork skills among health care professionals.

# SCENARIO 1: Preterm contractions with intact membranes

Setting: L&D Triage

SBAR (by RN): 30 yo G2P1001 at 35 weeks presents to triage with complaints of contractions. Her pregnancy has been uncomplicated. She has contractions on the monitor and is uncomfortable. She needs to be evaluated.

# Resolution: (The scenario ends when the trainee)

1. Appropriately manages the scenario. Review of obstetric history, current pregnancy events and medical/surgical history. Review maternal medication allergies. Specifically address the nature of PCN allergy, which is anaphylaxis as a child. Review maternal vital signs Review fetal heart rate and tocometry Pelvic examination including speculum for nitrazine/pool/fern/wet mount, cervical exam Ultrasound for presentation Considers reassessment of cervical exam after a time interval Management could include IV hydration, labs (CBC, UA), GBS prophylaxis

2. Ideal management would include the above assessment of history, exam, ultrasound and a repeat cervical examination which is unchanged. The correct diagnosis is preterm contractions, therefore administration of GBS prophylaxis is unnecessary. If IV fluid are administered and the contractions slow down, the patient could be discharged from triage.Does not effectively manage the scenario, but no critical errors. This may require prompting from the RN or patient if the ideal evaluation or management has not yet occurred. Management should not include tocolysis, corticosteroids, magnesium sulfate, transvaginal cervical length, fetal fibronectin, GBS swab (due to history of bacteriuria). Management should not include administration of clindamycin without GBS sensitivity testing.   
3. Trainer calls an end due to severity of error. Administration of PCN or cephalexin (history of anaphylaxis) Participant calls for a cesarean section   
4. Stop scenario after 10 minutes if no critical error has occurred but the management has not lead to a resolution of contractions.

# Appendix B: Debriefing Guide

“Simulation creates the opportunity to debrief.”

Simulation creates the opportunity to examine our medical management, team dynamics, and communication skills. It allows us to talk about the challenges we face in a safe environment and improve the way we care for patients as a team.

For more extensive instruction on optimal debriefing please refer to the references.

# Debriefing Strategy

The Basic Assumption for the debriefing is:

“We believe that everyone participating in the simulation scenario is intelligent, welltrained, cares about doing their best, and wants to improve”.  Method of debriefing should be based on learning objectives and targeted to analyze critical actions and the impact of experience on the participants

General Facilitator Goals:

Try to facilitate the TEAM’s discussion or individual self-reflection if one-on-one debrief   
(avoid dominating the conversation)   
Ask open-ended questions (avoid yes/no questions)   
Discuss the performance (not the individual)

# Framework for debriefing:

Each debrief should consist of:

$\spadesuit$ Introduction   
$\spadesuit$ Discussion of Medical Management   
$\spadesuit$ Summary

1) Introduction

This “sets the stage” for debriefing and creates expectations. What you might say:

“This is an opportunity to reflect and learn, improve our medical care, team work, and communication.”   
“Everyone may ask questions and share their feelings.”   
“Once you leave this session, we encourage open discussions of the concepts you learn, but ask you to keep specifics about the scenario and individual’s performances within these walls.”

# 2) Medical Management

This portion of the discussion focuses on the medical aspects of the case. It’s usually more comfortable to begin with these “facts”.

Reactions $\bigcirc$ Clear the air and set the stage for discussion $\bigcirc$ Facts

What happened? $\bigcirc$ Participants often want to know "the answer" $\bigcirc$ Stick to the facts

o How did you feel about that?

o Accept expressions of feelings

“Acknowledge” is not the same as “agree” Try to mirror feelings rather than evaluate them Don't tell participants "that's OK" when it may not be o Give perspective if participant feelings are hurt, e.g.:

 I've seen this a dozen times and that happens nearly every time… or, I've made the same mistake… or, We all make mistakes and this is a good place to learn from them… or, Remind them of the Basic Assumption (above) and say that we’ll work together to figure out what happened…

# o Understanding

Remember to use Advocacy-Inquiry: be curious!

(a) Exploring (b) Applying (c) Generalizing

# o Exploring

(a) What were you thinking at the time?   
(b) It looked to me like… (Use this to discuss some error you observed and would like to find out why the trainee chose a particular course of action)   
(c) How do you account for that?   
(d) Why did that happen?   
(e) What led to it? What next?

# o Applying

(f) What drug or procedure or behavior might be best? (g) Have you ever done this clinically? (h) How might this be reflected in your clinical practice?

Generalizing

(i) Have you ever seen anything similar to this in your practice? (j) Are there analogies to the clinical world? (k) What can be done in a similar situation?

# Overview

(l) How did this scenario go in terms of teamwork and communication? (m) How did this scenario go in terms of patient safety? (n) How could this training scenario be improved?

3) Summarizing

$\bigcirc$ This is your opportunity to ensure the key learning points are highlighted $\bigcirc$ Try to choose approximately 3 take-home medical management and team management points.
