**PART 8**

Mr. Steven's symptoms suggest moderate dehydration. His reduced blood pressure and elevated heart rate are severe enough to be admitted to the hospital.

*Mr. Stevens needs IV fluid to both increase blood volume and rehydrate the cells; an isotonic saline solution (0.9% sodium chloride) should be administered first to correct extracellular fluid depletion and increase volume.*

*Hypotonic saline (half-isotonic in dextrose) or 5% dextrose in water (D5W) is another possible answer. Loss of water through severe sweating can cause a condition called hypernatremia (excess sodium). If there is evidence that Mr. Stevens's sodium levels were high, then hypotonic solutions could be administered AFTER the initial isotonic saline treatment.*

*A hypertonic solution would increase the blood volume by pulling water into the blood from the cells. Even though volume would be increased in this situation, the blood cells themselves could become further dehydrated. A strongly hypotonic solution would cause water to shift from within the blood to the cells. This would be beneficial for the cells, but blood volume may not be stabilized.*

*Pure water would be extremely hypotonic compared to cells in the body. Injection of pure water would cause local vascular cells and RBCs to swell and potentially burst.*

*The ECF osmolality would drop, and water would flow into the ICF. Lower ECF osmolality would cause cell volume to increase.*

*Definitely not. Mr. Stevens isn't suffering from a loss of blood, but a loss of fluids. Saline solutions are preferable as they are cheaper.*

*ECF osmolality will increase. Water will flow out of the ICF. Cell volume will shrink.*

**PART 10 Conclusion:**

After receiving an IV of isotonic saline solution, Mr. Stevens made a full recovery. He was discharged later that day and was still able to make his flight. He vowed to drink more water during the next marathon race.
