**Interpret the patient's CBC results and blood smear. Provide a physiologic explanation behind the changes.**

*WBC and platelet counts are normal. MCH and MCHC are normal.*

*The hematocrit is high and the mean corpuscular volume (MCV) is low. Erythrocytes look normal on the blood smear.*

*Mr. Stevens has lost water from his body through sweat. This loss of water leads to a contraction in the extracellular fluid volume and contraction of plasma volume. However, the RBC count is still the same as Mr. Stevens hasn't lost any blood. The same amount of RBCs in a smaller volume of fluid produces a higher hematocrit. As the water levels in the interstitial fluid and plasma decrease, the concentration of solutes dissolved inside (osmolarity) will increase. The higher concentrations of solutes in plasma makes it hypertonic relative to the RBCs. Consequently, water will flow out of the cells and reduce their volume. This process will lead to a reduced MCV. However, the MCV reduction is not severe enough to be obvious visually in a blood smear.*

**PART 8**

Mr. Steven's symptoms suggest moderate dehydration. His reduced blood pressure and elevated heart rate are severe enough to be admitted to the hospital.

**You decide that Mr. Stevens needs an intravenous fluid infusion. What fluid will you administer to the patient to treat his condition? Explain the rationale behind your choice.**

*Mr. Stevens needs IV fluid to both increase blood volume and rehydrate the cells; an isotonic saline solution (0.9% sodium chloride) should be administered first to correct extracellular fluid depletion and increase volume.*

*Hypotonic saline (half-isotonic in dextrose) or 5% dextrose in water (D5W) is another possible answer. Loss of water through severe sweating can cause a condition called hypernatremia (excess sodium). If there is evidence that Mr. Stevens's sodium levels were high, then hypotonic solutions could be administered AFTER the initial isotonic saline treatment.*

*A hypertonic solution would increase the blood volume by pulling water into the blood from the cells. Even though volume would be increased in this situation, the blood cells themselves could become further dehydrated. A strongly hypotonic solution would cause water to shift from within the blood to the cells. This would be beneficial for the cells, but blood volume may not be stabilized.*

*Students should note that pure water would be extremely hypotonic compared to cells in the body. Injection of pure water would cause local vascular cells and RBCs to swell and potentially burst.*

**How would fluids shift in the body if you gave him hypotonic (0.45% NaCl) saline or 5% dextrose (D5W)? What would happen to the volume of his cells?**

*The ECF osmolality would drop, and water would flow into the ICF. Lower ECF osmolality would cause cell volume to increase.*

**Would blood be the correct treatment for this condition? Defend your answer.**

*Definitely not. Mr. Stevens isn't suffering from a loss of blood, but a loss of fluids. Saline solutions are preferable as they are cheaper.*

**What do you think would happen to the patient if he were given hypertonic saline (3% NaCl)? Describe the shift in fluid compartments and the volume of his cells.**

*ECF osmolality will increase. Water will flow out of the ICF. Cell volume will shrink.*
