## <u>**STATION 10**</u> 

**TOBACCO DEPENDENCE**

<u>**SCENARIO:**</u>

You are working in a busy primary care clinic and are about to see a 32-year-old who complains of cough for the past two months. She was seen at an urgent care center two weeks ago and again last week.  The urgent care physician reported that there was no evidence of allergy, reflux, asthma, or cardiac disease, the examination was normal, and chest and sinus X-rays, and pulmonary function test were all normal.  In addition a negative tuberculin skin test was recorded.

A course of azithromycin and an albuterol inhaler was prescribed but did not help. The note from the urgent care says “smoker’s cough, quit smoking and follow up with primary care physician.”

<u>**DIRECTIONS:**</u>

1. DURING THE FIRST 5 MINUTES, TAKE A DIRECTED HISTORY RELATED TO THE PATIENT’S CHIEF COMPLAINT OF COUGH

1. DURING THE FINAL 6 MINUTES YOU WILL BE ASKED TO EXPLAIN YOUR TREATMENT OPTIONS TO THE PATIENT AND TO MOTIVATE THE PATIENT TO AGREE TO A TREATMENT PLAN
