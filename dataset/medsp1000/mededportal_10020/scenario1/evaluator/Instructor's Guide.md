**Instructor’s Guide: “Altered Mental Status in a Toddler”**

**Introduction:** This simulation was written specifically for pediatric emergency medicine fellows but could be used for any advanced trainee in pediatrics or emergency medicine. It outlines the case of a child who had cerebral sinovenous thrombosis as a complication of iron deficiency anemia and dehydration. The importance of this case is to illustrate a rare diagnosis and in doing so, achieve educational objectives: 1) recognize and stabilize an acutely ill child 2) generate a comprehensive differential diagnosis 3) perform a thorough neurologic exam including fundoscopy 4) demonstrate effective team leadership 6) communicates effectively and 7) appropriate disposition.

1. Resources
   1. Labs **Appendix A (1-3)**
      1. These lab values should be given to the trainee when asked for them specifically.
      2. After a history and physical are complete and they give an adequate differential diagnosis of the child’s condition, they may ask for labs. They should inform the parent that labs are needed and explain the need.
      3. Anemia, thrombocytosis
      4. Lumbar puncture results: normal CSF but opening pressure measured at 30
   2. Picture of optic disc **Appendix B**
      1. To be given when the trainee initiates a fundoscopic exam
      2. Reading: papilledema
   3. Chest X-Ray **Appendix C**
      1. To be given at the trainee’s request
      2. Looking for enlarged heart, mesenteric lymph nodes or pneumonia
      3. Reading: normal for toddler
   4. CT scan of the brain without contrast **Appendix D (1-4)**
      1. To be given when the trainee requests a CT and provides indications for one
      2. This does not have to be used, as long as the trainee provides evidence to wait for an MRI
      3. Read: normal brain
   5. MRI of the brain with/without contrast **Appendix E (1-3)**
      1. To be given very late in the scenario when you are confident that they have achieved all goals of the scenario or if the trainee becomes frustrated
      2. Read: There is loss of normal “flow void” within the left sigmoid sinus, concerning for sinus thrombosis. Compare to normal “flow void” in the right sigmoid sinus.
   6. MRV of the brain **Appendix E.4**
      1. Given if the trainee asks for it
      2. Read: Thrombosis of torculum, occipital sinus and both the transverse sinuses with complete occlusion of left transverse dural venous sinus and mild expansion of right is worse dural venous sinus
2. Conceptual Background
   1. This child was seen in the pediatric emergency department. It was used as a simulation case because cerebral sinus thrombosis is a rare occurrence in children but if gone unrecognized may lead to devastating morbidity or mortality.
   2. The presumed mechanism of the thrombosis in this child was: iron deficiency anemia leading to thrombocytosis. He then became ill and dehydrated which then lead to the cerebral sinus thrombosis.
   3. The length of this case gives the senior resident or fellow the ability to go through a wide differential diagnosis. It also gives them time to communicate results and next steps with the parent.
3. Educational Objectives
   1. Recognize a sick child
   2. Perform a comprehensive neurologic exam
   3. Demonstrate knowledge of causes of altered mental status in toddler
   4. Demonstrate good communication with the family
   5. Demonstrate good team management skills
   6. Demonstrate proper disposition
4. Implementation
   1. Pre-simulation event
      1. Email actors 2 days and then 1 day before the event. This will ensure reading of material and a replacement if needed.
         1. Script for the Actors **Appendix F**
         2. Roles they will play
   2. Simulation
      1. Practical Implementation
         1. Set up in emergency department if using a wireless simulator or simulation center
         2. Need 2 faculty/facilitator; one to run the simulator, one to observe and provide guidance for the actors
         3. This simulation takes about 25-30 minutes
      2. Distribute name tags **Appendix G**
      3. Speak to the actors about their roles
         1. Parent actors: gives the AMPLE history
         2. Parent actors: be persistent in asking questions
         3. Parent actors: repeat the same question until it is answered
         4. Nurse/tech actor: watch for signs from the leader in case the scenario needs to be led in another direction
         5. Nurse/tech actor: watch the instructor for hints (i.e. to help trainees remember the headache portion of the history, the instructor makes gestures to help the actor remind the trainee)
      4. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer
         1. Ask: Ask: “What do you want to do next?
         2. Ask: “What are you thinking?”
         3. Ask: “How does the child look? What are his vitals?”
         4. Ask: “What have we ruled out?”
         5. Ask: “What do you think is going on?”
5. Feedback from past implementation
   1. This simulation was successfully implemented on two separate groups of pediatric emergency medicine fellows. The first group was comprised of senior and junior fellows. The second comprised of two new fellows, one with a background in pediatrics and the other with a background in emergency medicine. Because the chief complaint is so broad, the fellow needed to be lead in the direction of a neurologic diagnosis.
   2. The fellow needs to obtain a focused history, gather their labs and imaging and circle back and restate the case. Ideally that involves the parent actor. While the fellow was restating the case to the parent, they could be lead in the right direction.
   3. If they do a complete neurologic exam, including a fundoscopic exam, they are lead to the diagnosis quickly and it runs smoothly. If they skip this step, the diagnosis takes longer to obtain.
   4. In the debriefing session, it is important to tell the trainee that this was a tough scenario and why good communication is important.
