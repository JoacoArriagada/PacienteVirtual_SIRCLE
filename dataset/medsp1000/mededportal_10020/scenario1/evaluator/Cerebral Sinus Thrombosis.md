**Simulation Interest Group Scenario**

1. Title: Altered Mental Status in 2 year old boy
2. Target Audience: Senior pediatric residents, senior emergency medicine residents and pediatric emergency medicine fellows
3. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives
   1. Primary
      1. Initiate appropriate physical exam
      2. Age and chief compliant appropriate differential diagnosis
      3. Order appropriate testing
      4. Proper communication with family
   2. Secondary- detailed technical goals and behavioral goals, didactic points
      1. Identify self and members of the family
      2. Appropriately obtain a history
      3. Place an IV while obtaining labs
      4. Check POC glucose
      5. Engage patient and family while performing a thorough physical exam
      6. Complete neurologic exam
      7. Appropriate use of consultants
      8. Proper disposition
   3. Critical Action Checklist
      1. Recognize a sick child
      2. Perform a comprehensive neurologic exam
      3. Good Communication with family
4. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up- Human patient simulator either in the pediatric emergency department or in the simulation center
   2. Mankin Set Up- Toddler Gaumard simulator with wireless control, IV set up, IO needle and bone, syringes with medication labels
   3. Props – Picture of papilledema, CT, MRI, EKG, Chest X-ray and lab results **(APPENDIX A-E**. Basic airway and code blue cart
   4. Distractors
      1. Actors for mom and grandma
5. Actors
   1. Roles
   2. Action Role
6. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)
   1. Scenario background given to participants (specify if given freely or must be asked for)
      1. Chief complaint: Tired At triage, he was minimally interactive
      2. Past medical history: Endoscopy for coin in his esophagus (Freely)
      3. Meds and allergies: None (Freely)
      4. Family/social history: Leukemia and lymphoma in family members (Freely)
   2. Scenario conditions initially
      1. History patient gives: For the last 2 weeks he has not been himself and for the last day, he has been worse. (Freely)
      2. Patient’s initial exam: Pale looking toddler, laying still on the gurney with his eyes open. Vitals: Temp: 36.6C, HR: 129, Respiratory rate: 32, blood pressure 95/49, pulse ox 99% RA. Weight 12.7 kg. Sleepy, hard to engage, fussy and not easily consolable. Awakes when poked for IV then right back to sleep

Cardiovascular: 2/6 systolic murmur heard best at left sternal border

Neurologic: When not stimulated falls back asleep. Cranial nerves II-XII are grossly intact. Patella reflexes are 2+ bilaterally. Kicks and moves arms symmetrically. Reaches for things symmetrically. No truncal ataxia when sitting up. Muscular bulk and tone are normal

* + 1. Patient’s physiology: Patient’s consumption of milk is excessive, causing iron deficiency which in turn causes microcytic anemia and thrombocytosis. Anemia and thrombocytosis along with dehydration from an illness caused a cerebral sinus thrombosis.
  1. Scenario branch points
     1. Branch point: Communication (**APPENDIX J**)
     2. Branch point: Medical management (**APPENDIX K**)

1. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)
   1. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer
2. Ask: Ask: “What do you want to do next?
3. Ask: “What are you thinking?”
4. Ask: “How does the child look? What are his vitals?”
5. Ask: “What have we ruled out?”
6. Ask: “What do you think is going on?”

   1. Tips for actors (see instructor’s guide)
      1. Distribute Script for the Actors (**APPENDIX F**)
      2. Distribute name badges (**APPENDIX G**)
8. Debriefing Plan
   1. Method of debriefing
      1. Watch Video
      2. Given the the outcome of the scenario, the instructor, team members and actors should evaluate:
         1. What would I stop doing? What things did not work?
         2. What should I start doing?
         3. What should I continue doing? What worked for me?
   2. Actual debriefing materials
      1. Video of the simulation
      2. Article: Dlamini N et al. Cerebral Venous Sinus (sinovenous) thrombosis in children. Neurosurgical Clinics of North America. July 2010;21(3):511-27
      3. Article: Corrales-Medina, F et al. Cerebral Sinovenous Thrombosis Associated with Iron Deficiency Anemia Secondary to Severe Menorrhagia: A Case Report. Journal of Child Neurology. 2014, Vol. 29(9) NP62-NP64
   3. This should be kept positive, something to strive to do in the future
   4. Evaluation of simulation (**APPENDIX I**)
9. Pilot Testing and Revisions

A. Numbers of participants: 4-6 participants

B. Performance expectations, anticipated management mistakes

1. Expected to recognize sick
2. Expected to respond to parents that have high expectations and move past it to take care of the child
3. Mistake: May go down a metabolic path
4. Mistake: May stop at CT instead of talking with neurology or ordering an MRI
5. Mistake: Prematurely admit patient without going through the full altered mental status work up
