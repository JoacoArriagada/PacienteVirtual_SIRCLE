**Instructor’s Guide: “Altered Mental Status in a Toddler”**

**Introduction:** It outlines the case of a child who had cerebral sinovenous thrombosis as a complication of iron deficiency anemia and dehydration.

1. Resources
   1. Labs **Appendix A (1-3)**
      1. These lab values should be given to the trainee when asked for them specifically.
      2. After a history and physical are complete, they may ask for labs. They should inform the parent that labs are needed and explain the need.
      3. Anemia, thrombocytosis
      4. Lumbar puncture results: normal CSF but opening pressure measured at 30
   2. Picture of optic disc **Appendix B**
      1. To be given when the trainee initiates a fundoscopic exam
      2. Reading: papilledema
   3. Chest X-Ray **Appendix C**
      1. To be given at the trainee’s request
      2. Looking for enlarged heart, mesenteric lymph nodes or pneumonia
      3. Reading: normal for toddler
   4. CT scan of the brain without contrast **Appendix D (1-4)**
      1. To be given when the trainee requests a CT and provides indications for one
      3. Read: normal brain
   5. MRI of the brain with/without contrast **Appendix E (1-3)**
      1. To be given very late in the scenario or if the trainee becomes frustrated
      2. Read: There is loss of normal “flow void” within the left sigmoid sinus, concerning for sinus thrombosis. Compare to normal “flow void” in the right sigmoid sinus.
   6. MRV of the brain **Appendix E.4**
      1. Given if the trainee asks for it
      2. Read: Thrombosis of torculum, occipital sinus and both the transverse sinuses with complete occlusion of left transverse dural venous sinus and mild expansion of right is worse dural venous sinus
4. Implementation
   1. Pre-simulation event
      1. Email actors 2 days and then 1 day before the event. This will ensure reading of material and a replacement if needed.
         1. Script for the Actors **Appendix F**
         2. Roles they will play
   2. Simulation
      1. Practical Implementation
         1. Set up in emergency department if using a wireless simulator or simulation center
         2. Need 2 faculty/facilitator; one to run the simulator, one to observe and provide guidance for the actors
         3. This simulation takes about 25-30 minutes
      2. Distribute name tags **Appendix G**
      3. Speak to the actors about their roles
         1. Parent actors: gives the AMPLE history
         2. Parent actors: be persistent in asking questions
         3. Parent actors: repeat the same question until it is answered
         4. Nurse/tech actor: watch for signs from the leader in case the scenario needs to be led in another direction
         5. Nurse/tech actor: watch the instructor for hints (i.e. to help trainees remember the headache portion of the history, the instructor makes gestures to help the actor remind the trainee)
      4. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer
         1. Ask: Ask: “What do you want to do next?
         2. Ask: “What are you thinking?”
         3. Ask: “How does the child look? What are his vitals?”
         4. Ask: “What have we ruled out?”
         5. Ask: “What do you think is going on?”
