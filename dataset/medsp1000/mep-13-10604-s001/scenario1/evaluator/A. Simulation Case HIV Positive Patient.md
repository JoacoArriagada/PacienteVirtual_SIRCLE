**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The anesthesia resident enters the preoperative room to see Ms. Jones. Her pastor, Reverend Paris accompanies her. The resident introduces himself/herself, explains why he/she is there, and put his/her name on the communication board as well as that of the attending anesthesiologist. The resident recognizes Ms. Jones’ nervousness and is empathetic towards her.

The resident asks Ms. Jones who she brought with her today, and asks her permission to discuss her medical history with her pastor in the room. When Ms. Jones indicates her preference for privacy, the resident diplomatically asks the pastor to leave.

The resident goes over Ms. Jones’ past medical history and asks if she or any of her family members has ever had a problem with anesthesia. When discussing current medications, the resident learns Ms. Jones has HIV. The resident handles this information with sensitivity and respects her wish not to reveal it to for either her mother or her pastor. At the conclusion of discussing her medical history, Ms. Jones asks that the pastor be allowed to come back in the room, and the resident is accommodating. The resident then goes over the consent for anesthesia, explaining everything in understandable terms. When Ms. Jones expresses fear about intraoperative medical errors, the resident reassures her that the team of health care professionals will take good care of her.

For the consent for anesthesia, the resident explains the following to Ms. Jones, without using medical jargon:

* She is having a hysterectomy and general anesthesia.
* The resident will be giving her some relaxing medication through her IV and taking her to the operating room where the resident will put her to sleep with medication through the IV.
* After she is asleep, the resident will place a small breathing tube to help her breathe.
* During surgery, the resident will monitor her vital signs and make sure she is doing well.
* She will be given any type of help she needs.
* At the end of surgery, the resident will wake her and remove the breathing tube.
* Before she wakes up, the resident will give her medications in the IV for pain and prevention of nausea and vomiting.
* The resident will take her to the recovery room after the surgery, so the nurses and the resident can make sure her pain is under control and monitor her for a little while after anesthesia.
* The resident will ask her if she will accept blood products, if needed.
* The risks of anesthesia should then be discussed:
  + There are minor risks to anesthesia like nausea and vomiting, pain, and a sore throat from the breathing tube.
  + There are also some major risks that are very rare like stroke, heart attack, and even death, but these are very rare and are not expected to occur.

The patient is then asked to sign the consent form (Appendix E: Anesthesia Consent Form). The resident asks if she or Pastor Paris have any questions.

Near the end of the consent process, the resident receives a phone call from an operating room nurse, who will tell the resident the operating room is ready. The nurse will warn the resident that if Ms. Jones’ case starts late, an “anesthesia delay” will be documented. When Ms. Jones and the pastor express their wish to wait and talk to her mother before surgery, the resident complies with their wish after consulting the attending anesthesiology attending.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*1. Failure to ask Ms. Jones who she brought with her today, and failure to ask her permission to discuss her medical history with her pastor in the room.* In advance of the simulation, the residents will attend a presentation on patient- and family-centered care. Additionally, the facilitators will plan to discuss this critical step in the post-simulation debriefing.

*2. Uncertainty about delaying surgery until Ms. Jones gets an opportunity to talk to her mother.*

In advance of the simulation, the residents will attend a presentation on patient- and family-centered care. Additionally, the facilitators will plan to discuss this critical step in the post-simulation debriefing.
