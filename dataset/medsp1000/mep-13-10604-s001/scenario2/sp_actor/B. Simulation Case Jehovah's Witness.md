| **Brief narrative description of case** | Ms. Brown is a 65-70 year-old female who is pre-operative, awaiting surgery for a hip replacement due to avascular necrosis of the femoral head. Her daughter, Sheri Scott, accompanies her.  An anesthesia resident joins Ms. Brown to obtain consent for anesthesia.  Ms. Brown is in excruciating pain. However, the nurse informs the resident that pain medication is being withheld, because consent for surgery has not yet been obtained by the surgeon. Further, there will be a delay in her surgery, because the surgeon is working on an emergency trauma case. Ms. Brown and her daughter urgently appeal for pain medication. If Ms. Brown receives pain medication, her pain subsides.  While obtaining consent for surgery, the resident should discuss the potential need to give blood. Ms. Brown's daughter emphatically expresses they are Jehovah's Witnesses and it is against their religious beliefs to receive a blood transfusion. Ms. Brown concurs with her daughter but with less vigor. She exhibits only mild reservations about receiving blood. In a one-on-one encounter with the resident, Ms. Brown asks the resident to administer blood if a situation arises in which she needs it to save her life. She asks that neither her daughter nor anyone else know. |
