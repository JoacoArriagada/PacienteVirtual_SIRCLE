| **Brief narrative description of case** | Ms. Brown is a 65-70 year-old female who is pre-operative, awaiting surgery for a hip replacement due to avascular necrosis of the femoral head. Her daughter, Sheri Scott, accompanies her.  An anesthesia resident joins Ms. Brown to obtain consent for anesthesia.  Ms. Brown is in excruciating pain. However, the nurse informs the resident that pain medication is being withheld, because consent for surgery has not yet been obtained by the surgeon. Further, there will be a delay in her surgery, because the surgeon is working on an emergency trauma case. Ms. Brown and her daughter urgently appeal for pain medication.  The resident may be hesitant to give pain medication until after consent for surgery is obtained. Alternatively, the resident may elect to give Percocet, which Ms. Brown has been taking at home, or consult the attending anesthesiologist, who will tell the resident to give Percocet. If Ms. Brown receives pain medication, her pain subsides.  While obtaining consent for surgery, the resident should discuss the  potential need to give blood. Ms. Brown’s daughter emphatically expresses they are Jehovah’s Witnesses and it against their religious beliefs to receive a blood transfusion. Ms. Brown concurs with her daughter but with less vigor. She exhibits only mild reservations about receiving blood. The resident may become of aware of her body language and reservations and diplomatically find a way to ask her daughter to leave the room. If that happens, in a one-on-one encounter with the resident, Ms. Brown asks the resident to administer blood if a situation arises in which she needs it to save her life. She asks that neither her daughter nor anyone else know.  The overall learner goals for the case are to (a) apply patient- and family-centered care to improve the quality of patient care and the patient’s and family’s experience and (b) apply best practices for obtaining anesthesia consent. |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | By the end of the post-simulation debriefing, the learners will be able to:   * Apply core concepts of patient- and family-centered care. * Appropriately obtain informed consent for general anesthesia. * Apply situational awareness during interactions with patients  and families. * Adhere to ethical principles during interactions with patients  and families. |
| **Critical Actions** | The resident immersed in the simulation should do the following:   * Introduce himself/herself, explain why he/she is there, and put his/her name on the communication board as well as that of the attending anesthesiologist. * Either order Percocet for pain relief or consult the attending anesthesiology physician. * Explain what happens before, during, and after surgery in an understandable manner. * Recognize Ms. Brown’s body language and reservations about not  receiving blood, if she needs it to save her life, prompting the resident to ask the daughter to leave the room to allow for a one-on-one dialog with Ms. Brown. |
| **Learner Preparation** | Prior to participating in the simulation, the residents attend a brief presentation about patient- and family- centered care (Appendix I: Patient- and family-centered care slides).  Immediately prior to the simulation, both the resident who is going to be immersed in the simulation and the other resident who will observing via live camera feed are given the following information:  Ms. Brown is a 65-70 year-old female who is pre-operative, awaiting surgery for a hip replacement due to avascular necrosis of the femoral head. She has had groin pain that has progressively worsened over the last several weeks.  Her medications are: prednisone; albuterol inhaler; Percocet; multivitamin.  There will a delay in in the start of Ms. Brown’s surgery, because the surgeon is working on an emergency trauma case.  Obtain informed consent for undergoing a general anesthetic and discuss the possibility of blood transfusion.  In addition to the above information, the resident observing via a live camera feed will receive a checklist (Appendix F: Checklist for faculty, standardized patients and observing residents to use to while observing the simulation and as an aid to debriefing) to complete while observing the simulation. The resident will use the completed checklist as a guide when participating in the post-simulation debriefing. |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The anesthesia resident enters the preoperative room to see Ms. Brown. Her daughter, Sheri Scott, accompanies Ms. Brown. The resident introduces himself/herself, explains why he/she is there, and puts his/her name on the communication board as well as that of the attending anesthesiologist. The resident recognizes Ms. Brown’s pain and is empathetic towards her. The resident immediately addresses Ms. Brown’s concern by calling the attending anesthesiology physician, who directs the resident to administer Percocet.

The resident goes over Ms. Brown’s past medical history and asks if she or any of her family members has ever had a problem with anesthesia. For the consent for anesthesia, the resident explains the following to Ms. Brown, without using medical jargon:

* She is having a hip replacement and general anesthesia.
* The resident will be giving her some relaxing medication through her IV and taking her to the operating room where the resident will put her to sleep with medication through the IV.
* After she is asleep, the resident will place a small breathing tube to help her breathe.
* During surgery, the resident will monitor her vital signs and make sure she is doing well.
* The health care team will give her any type of help she needs.
* At the end of surgery, the resident will wake her and remove the breathing tube.
* Before she wakes up, the resident will give her medications in the IV for pain and prevention of nausea and vomiting.
* The resident will take her to the recovery room after the surgery, so the nurses and the resident can make sure her pain is under control and monitor her for a little while after anesthesia.
* The resident asks her if she will accept blood products, if needed. In response to learning she is a Jehovah Witness, the resident discusses alternative fluid products. Though she is a Jehovah’s Witness and her daughter is adamant she can’t receive blood, the resident senses Ms. Brown’s reservation about not receiving blood. The resident diplomatically finds a way to ask Ms. Brown’s daughter to leave the room. The resident then discusses privately with Virginia Brown her wishes about receiving blood if it is needed.
* The resident discusses risks of anesthesia:
  + There are minor risks to anesthesia like nausea and vomiting, pain, and a sore throat from the breathing tube.
  + There are also some major risks that are very rare like stroke, heart attack, and even death, but these are very rare and are not expected to occur.

The resident asks the patient to sign the consent form (Appendix E: Anesthesia Consent Form). Then, the resident asks Ms. Brown and her daughter if they have any questions.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*1. Failure to immediately address Ms. Brown’s pain.* In advance of the simulation, the residents will attend a presentation on patient- and family-centered care. Additionally, the facilitators will plan to discuss this critical step in the post-simulation debriefing.

*2. Failure to discern Ms. Brown’s reluctance to withhold blood, if it is needed to save her life.* In advance of the simulation, the residents will attend a presentation on patient- and family-centered care. Additionally, the facilitators will plan to discuss this critical step in the post-simulation debriefing.
