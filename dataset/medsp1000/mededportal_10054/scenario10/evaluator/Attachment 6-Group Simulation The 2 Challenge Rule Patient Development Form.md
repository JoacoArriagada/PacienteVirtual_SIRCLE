# SIMULATION CENTER

Goldberg Surgical Skills Laboratory An American College of Surgeons Accredited Education Institute Patient Development Form

Lesson Title: The 2 Challenge Rule

Clinical Service and Learner: Anesthesia / Group Simulation: CA-1 through CA-3 residents

# Goals:

To educate residents about and gain experience practicing the 2 Challenge Rule to improve team communication, patient safety and health care quality.

# Objectives:

. Describe clinical situations where the 2 Challenge Rule may be useful to improve team communication, patient safety and health care quality (Patient Care, Medical Knowledge, Practice Based Learning and Improvement, Systems Based Practice, Interpersonal and Communication Skills, Professionalism) Demonstrate ability to utilize advocacy and inquiry when employing the 2 Challenge Rule in a clear, respectful and effective manner (Patient Care, Medical Knowledge, Practice Based Learning and Improvement, Interpersonal and Communication Skills, Professionalism) List barriers to action that preclude health care professionals from successfully employing the 2 Challenge Rule and describe ways of overcoming potential barriers (Patient Care, Medical Knowledge, Practice Based Learning and Improvement, Systems Based Practice, Interpersonal and Communication Skills, Professionalism)

Environment and Scenario: Three simulation exercises will be presented in tandem in a standard perioperative care setting. All participants will be expected to employ the 2 Challenge Rule, utilizing both advocacy and inquiry, and provide suggested patient care improvement proposals. Formal debriefing will follow the last simulation exercise.

# Scenario 1: Incomplete Medical Workup / Difficult Intubation

A surgeon, running significantly behind schedule, requests expeditious case turnover for an elective right mastectomy on a 78 year old female with the below noted medical problems:

o Right breast CA, o Symptomatic Reflux,

o HTN,
o CAD,
o Valvular Heart Disease o COPD,
o Longstanding IDDM, and o Renal Insufficiency.

The patient is a poor historian and states that she had some "heart tests" in the recent past because of a PCP concern. She does not know the test results. The patient did not bring in her medication list, admits being allergic to some antibiotic ("swelling"). The only labs charted are a Hct of 42, Plt of 320K, and an EKG with SR at 90, NSSTT changes, T wave inversion inferior leads. More labs were drawn in her PCP's office although the results are not available. On physical exam the woman has a receding mandible and is a presumed difficult intubation. She does not remember being told about previous anesthesia-related problems. There are no old medical records available for review. The patient requests general anesthesia and does not want to know anything about the procedure. General anesthesia consent has been obtained, although the anesthesiologist requested to speak with the patient's PCP to obtain more detailed medical information. The PCP was busy evaluating a patient and will return the phone call in approximately 10 minutes. An anesthesia tech inform the anesthesiologist that there will be a 10 minute delay obtaining an advanced airway cart, secondary to equipment cleaning. The surgeon and circulating nurse both enter the room rushed and begin to wheel the patient back to the OR, in an effort make up for lost time. They reassure the patient that they will take great care of her during the procedure in a cheerful demeanor. The surgeon and circulating nurse will move the patient to the OR if not stopped by the anesthesiologist. The surgeon and circulating nurse will not be receptive to the anesthesiologist requesting extra time to complete the preoperative evaluation or obtain necessary advanced airway equipment. If challenged, the surgeon will tell the anesthetist, "Just put in an LMA and everything will be OK."
