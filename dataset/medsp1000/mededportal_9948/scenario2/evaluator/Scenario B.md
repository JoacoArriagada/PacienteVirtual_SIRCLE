**Scenario B**

Setting – level 2 trauma center in the community with trauma surgery, orthopedics, and neurosurgery available but not in-house; the hospital does have an intensive care unit (ICU); no pediatrics, Obstetrics/gynecology (OB/gyn), or psychiatry; there is a cardiologist on call but no cath lab

Cases – Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE), ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI)🡪Ventricular Tachycardia (V-tach), Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH)

* Resident enters pediatric room – finds 11 year old male and mom who complain about patient having knee pain
* Initial encounter should be brief as resident gets history, completes physical exam, and orders initial images **(4 min)**
* Before images come back, resident paged overhead to next room STAT for unstable patient with chest pain
* Resident finds obese male complaining of chest pain. Patient is diaphoretic and dyspneic but his mental status and blood pressure are initially normal. Electrocardiogram (ECG) shows STEMI. Almost immediately after the ECG is obtained the patient goes into pulseless V-tach. Patient has return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) after first defibrillation attempt. Patient remains unresponsive after defibrillation and needs to be intubated (THIS SHOULD BE AN EASY INTUBATION). The resident should intubate the patient, give aspirin and anticoagulation, consider therapeutic hypothermia, and page cardiology. (**12 min**)
* While the resident is doing all of these things (in the middle of the intubation procedure), page overhead for “Pediatric Medical Alert – 2 minutes”.
* Before cardiology calls back, pediatric medical alert arrives. If resident does not go to the pediatric patient, nurse states that the patient is sick and resident better get to that room right away.
* Resident finds 6 week old with mother in pediatric resuscitation room. The child has been vomiting, is hypotensive, and lethargic. Blood pressure (BP) does not respond to intravenous fluids (IVF). Resident should do appropriate infectious workup and labs, undress patient, diagnose CAH by examination and labs, and give hydrocortisone. If not, blood pressure continues to deteriorate. After appropriate treatment resident should call pediatric endocrinology and arrange to have patient transferred to pediatric hospital. Pediatrics is initially reluctant to take patient but will accept if resident is adamant. If they do not complete transfer, patient will become hypotensive again and can seize and/or begin to look septic **(14 min)**
* Half way through the CAH case, nurse reports that SCFE patient is getting impatient and wants to know about images right away. If resident leaves CAH case before resuscitation and transfer, patient will deteriorate and die.
* After pediatric patient is transferred, resident should return to check on SCFE patient. They may return briefly to check on cards patient who is stable, but if they don’t return to SCFE soon, the nurse will continue to report angry messages from family until the SCFE patient and mom eventually elope.
* Resident returns to SCFE patient and reviews images. Upon diagnosing SCFE, orthopedics will respond immediately to resident page and set up urgent outpatient follow-up **(2 min)**
* After SCFE patient is discharged, cardiology calls back about the STEMI. The cardiologist states that he has privileges at a facility that has a cath lab and is 10 minutes away, but he prefers the patient just get tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) so he doesn’t need to come in. The resident must insist that the cardiologist needs to go in and must transfer the patient to the other hospital.**(3 min)**

35 minutes case time; 20 minutes debrief; 5 minutes additional changeover time and cushion
