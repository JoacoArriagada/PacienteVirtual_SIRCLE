**SCENARIO A**

Setting – level 2 trauma center in the community with trauma surgery, orthopedics, and neurosurgery available but not in-house; the hospital does have an intensive care unit (ICU); no pediatrics, obstetrics/gynecology (Ob/gyn) or psychiatry; there is a cardiologist on call but no catheterization lab

Cases – trauma transfer, snakebite, delivery of shoulder dystocia

* Resident enters trauma room – report displayed (or verbally reported by confederate) indicates patient transferred from small rural, freestanding emergency department (ED) for trauma care; was in an all-terrain vehicle (ATV) accident and reportedly has multiple injuries requiring trauma surgery and orthopedics – images but no reports are available
* Initial encounter should be brief as resident gets history, completes primary survey, and begins to review images **(4 min)**
* Before resident has chance to finish reviewing images, page overhead calls medical alert to adjoining room; if resident remains with trauma patient, the nurse insists that the patient in the next room is dying and needs to be seen right away
* Resident finds patient with snakebite in the next room; anti-venin treatment is indicated and patient will continue to get worse if not given; DO NOT START ANAPHYLACTIC REACTION IMMEDIATELY ON ADMINISTRATION OF CRO-FAB – WILL START ANAPHYLACTIC REACTION AFTER DELIVERY CASE **(6 min)**
* As soon as anti-venin is given or after 6 minutes in snakebite room, whichever comes first, overhead page calls for resident “STAT to Ambulance Bay” for pregnant patient in active labor
* As resident is leaving room, nurse tells resident that the trauma patient wants to leave and is starting to complain; if resident returns to trauma room, pregnant patient and fetus will deteriorate and eventually die
* Resident arrives in ambulance bay to find pregnant patient actively delivering. There is no OB or pediatrics in the hospital and if resident asks to move patient the nurse responds there are no open beds in the ED and that delivery is imminent. Resident must deliver the child immediately despite the difficulties with the shoulder dystocia. The longer it takes to deliver, the worse the neonate’s vital signs and Apgars will be and the longer it will take to resuscitate him. Once the resident delivers the infant and placenta, resuscitates the infant, and controls mom’s bleeding, they should page pediatrics and OB at the closest tertiary care hospital **(12 min)**
* As soon as the resident asks for these pages, or 12 minutes into the OB case, one of the nurses yells that the resident needs to get back to the snakebite room because the patient is dying. The resident must immediately return to the room, where the patient is now in the anaphylactic stage of the scenario. They must immediately recognize the reaction and give appropriate treatment or the patient will rapidly deteriorate and die. The resident must insist that the patient be admitted, or the patient will have a delayed second reaction **(7 min)**
* Upon returning to the trauma patient’s room, the resident should conclude the patient has no major injuries; the patient actually feels better and wants to leave; if the resident tries to admit the patient, the trauma surgeon will scoff and the patient will get angry **(4 min)**
* As soon as the trauma patient is discharged, OB and pediatrics call the resident who must explain the situation and set up transfer. Once this is done the scenario is finished **(2 min)**

35 minutes case time; 20 minutes debrief; 5 minutes additional changeover time and cushion
