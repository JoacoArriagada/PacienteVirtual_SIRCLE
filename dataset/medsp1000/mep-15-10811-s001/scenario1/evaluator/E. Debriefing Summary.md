| The debriefing sessions were lead using “debriefing with good judgment” approach. This is a reflective practice that helps trainees identify and change clinical and behavioral issues during simulation. This method is based on the assumption that a learner’s actions are an inevitable result of their “frames” which are the “constructs” or “mental models” that inform behavior and they are comprised of such things as knowledge, assumptions and feelings. The instructor adopts a “stance of genuine curiosity” and inquires about the learner’s frames. Using a combination of advocacy and inquiry, the instructor makes objective observations of and subjective judgments about the trainee’s actions during simulation, and subsequently asks questions in the spirit of genuine curiosity to uncover the trainee’s frames.1-4  We encouraged the faculty debriefers in our stimulation to complete formal training in the “debreifing with good judgement” approach by attending a training session through Hennepin Healthcare Simulation Center lead by simulation training experts.
