**A. Simulation Case**

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **SIMULATION CASE TITLE:** Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (AIDP) with acute respiratory failure | |
| **PATIENT:** Green  **PATIENT AGE:** 35 year old  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** Generalized Weakness | |

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Learner Preparation** | **Format & Setting**  For the case, you will be on the medical/surgical floor of University Hospital; you will receive case specific details right before the case.  **Simulator Information & Limitations**  You will be working with a live actor/standardized patient today. You should examine and interact with the simulated patient just like you would any other patient. Please verbalize what you are seeing, hearing, and interpreting from your assessment. You should verbalize any procedures or interventions you would like to do on the patient, and the nurse will help you or tell you that this cannot be done on the standardized patient.  **Equipment & Environment**  If you need a crash cart or any other emergency supplies they will be located in the hall outside of the sim room, and if you would like to call for a consult there is a phone mounted on the wall of the sim room with a number posted above it. Please use this number to call anyone you need to speak to.  **Confederate Role**  You will have a nurse with you in the room; they will perform just like any other RN you would work with. |

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Initial Presentation | |
| **Overall Appearance** | Prior to starting case, tell learners: "You are on inpatient Neurology service. A new transfer from an outside hospital just hit the floor. The patient came from an ED with a chief complaint of weakness. The nurse received verbal sign-out from the outside hospital."  Nurse Report: "Nurse received report stating the patient has had 1 week of progressive weakness. Started in legs and now in the past 1-2 days has started spreading to both arms. The patient has been unable to walk for the past day, and has been dropping things."  Patient is in a gown, sitting up in a hospital bed, in no acute distress. Monitoring and general equipment is available in the room, but not set up at this time until requested by learners. |
