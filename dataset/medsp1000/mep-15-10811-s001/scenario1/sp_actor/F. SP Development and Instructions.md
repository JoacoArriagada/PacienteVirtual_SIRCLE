| Physical Exam- List exam maneuvers expected for this case and any abnormal findings that SP will simulate. | * General appearance: SP is lying in bed, appears slightly anxious, speaking in a soft and breathless voice, head is slightly dropped forward.  * Cardiovascular exam: Learners will auscultate over the chest; this exam will be normal.  * Respiratory exam: SP should appear breathless, but be careful to avoid actually hyperventilating. The SP can portray breathlessness by appearing generally uncomfortable, speaking in a soft voice, and being unable to finish a sentence without taking extra breaths. If asked to take deep breaths, the exam is normal except that it should be difficult for the SP to take a deep breath. If asked to count numbers within one exhalation ("breath counting"), the SP should only be able to count to 9-10 with one breath.  * Neurologic exam: Mental status is normal, SP is awake and alert, speech is fluent, and orientation questions are answered correctly. Eye movements and pupils are normal. Ability to raise eyebrows and shut eyelids is normal. If asked to open mouth and stick out tongue, this is normal. If asked to puff cheeks, the SP should have weakness with this. Speech is soft. Head is slightly dropped forward at rest due to neck weakness. If head or neck strength is assessed, then the SP should demonstrate weakness and give very little resistance. When upper extremity strength is assessed, the SP can lift both arms up against gravity but gives very little resistance to learner pressure. The weakness is symmetric on both sides. When lower extremity strength is assessed, the SP cannot lift legs up off the bed. If passive resistance is assessed, the SP should fully relax and tone should be flaccid. If repetitive motor movements are assessed, the movements should be slightly slow due to weakness but should not get weaker with repetition. If sensation is assessed, the SP should report not sensing stimuli over the distal limbs, but preserved sensation in proximal legs and arms; sensation over the abdomen, trunk, or face is normal. If proprioception is assessed, the SP should report "I don't know" when asked about the direction of movement. If deep tendon reflexes are checked, the SP should deliberately try to make reflexes less pronounced. Coordination is slow because of weakness but not clumsy. If asked to walk, the SP should reply "I can't walk because I'm too weak." |

**Standardized patient's evolution over the course of the simulation:**

As the simulation proceeds, the SP develops worsening respiratory status, which results in decline in mental status and hemodynamic instability.

| Obtain pertinent history | If asked, answer questions per the instructions above. If not asked, offer: "For the last week, I have had worsening weakness and tingling. It started in my feet and has spread up my legs and into both my arms. For the last couple days, the weakness was so bad that I could not walk. In fact, I can barely lift my arms or legs off the bed. I have also felt some shortness of breath, which started today. The shortness of breath is worse when I am lying flat. And I am having some problems with swallowing; earlier today I choked while drinking water. Prior to this past week, I was feeling fine and did not have any medical problems. Although, I was briefly sick about a month ago and had diarrhea at that time, but that fully resolved." |
| Obtain pertinent physical exam | If asked, verbalize and demonstrate weakness, numbness, and areflexia per the instructions above. |
| Recognize/verbalize abnormal vitals | If the team does not react, say: "I'm feeling a little short of breath and I feel like my heart is racing." |
| Perform high-yield neuromuscular exam | If asked, demonstrate neck weakness and poor breath counting. |
| Call respiratory therapist (RT) and request NIF/FVC and ABG | If this is not done, develop more labored breathing and have difficulty completing sentences. The SP should take some heavy and shallow breaths, avoid actual hyperventilation, and can verbalize "my breathing feels worse," with breath interrupting the words. Posture should be hunched and uncomfortable. |
| Start non-invasive ventilation (NIV)/BIPAP | If this is not done, say: "I feel very short of breath. Can you give me anything to help?" Voice is soft and the SP should take a breath every couple of words. |
| Recognize/verbalize patient's decline in mental status and respiratory status despite NIV | If this is not recognized, close the eyes, start grunting and snoring, and do not awaken to verbal or tactile stimulation. |
