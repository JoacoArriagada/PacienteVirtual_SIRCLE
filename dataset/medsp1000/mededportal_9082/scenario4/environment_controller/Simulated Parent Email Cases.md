# Otitis Media Case: Assume you are in the first months of your internship in pediatrics, emergency medicine, or family medicine.

Yesterday afternoon you saw Johnny, a 25 month old boy who came in with 1 day of runny nose, low grade fever and pulling on his left ear. He was otherwise healthy and fully immunized. Your exam (confirmed by your attending) showed an uncomfortable but non-toxic child with axillary temp of 38.3, clear rhinorrhea, red tonsils (not enlarged) and an immobile and slightly red left tympanic membrane. In the clinic you gave a dose of acetaminophen and 2 drops of lidocaine ear drops. In 15 minutes he was smiling.

You've told the mother that Johnny has a viral respiratory infection with a left ear infection. You've told her that this infection will most likely improve on its own in 2 or 3 days without antibiotics, and that there is excellent evidence that treating the symptoms with acetaminophen and analgesic ear drops is now the standard of care for this condition. She has a number to call day or night if Johnny's symptoms worsen, or if they persist for more than 2 days. Mother left the clinic seemingly satisfied by this approach.

This morning, you receive this email:

Dear Dr. Smith and Dr.

I am extremely disappointed in you and your clinic by your failure to treat my son's ear infection. He is still sick this morning. My older children always got antibiotics for their infections. You can call in a prescription to the Downtown Pharmacy at 555- 7654. If you don't give me the antibiotics we will take him to a doctor who will. I will check my email later this morning for your response.

John Sr.

Immediately afterward, your continuity clinic advisor (Dr. Smith) sends you an email

Dear

Please respond to this father about the recommended treatment of his son. Give him some background on why we did what we did. Then let me know what source(s) you used to reassure him, and let me know what I can do.

Dr. Smith

Please respond to this parent in a way you think would be most appropriate in box 1.
