**Facilitator’s Guide**

**Title: Simulated Pediatric Physician-Parent Email Cases**

**Purpose:** Because of this increased interest in email communication, our third year pediatric clerkship developed a curriculum on principles of email communication with patients. In our simulated emails, parents expressed concern, anger or confusion about their child’s medical care. The four cases are:

1) Parent refusing a Hepatitis B vaccination in newborn

2) Concern regarding a delay in treatment when a strep culture turned positive after negative rapid strep test results

3) Concern about radiation exposure from voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG) after 2nd urinary tract infection

4) Parent expecting antibiotics for otitis media

These simulated parent e-mails are used as a tool to aid in educating students about patient-physician communication and in evaluating students’ medical knowledge, communication skills and professionalism.

**Learning objectives:** After actively participating in both a discussion on principles of good communication and responding to two simulated email cases, the engaged student will be able to:

1) Demonstrate principles of effective email communication

2) Demonstrate professionalism in written communication with a patient

3) Discuss common pediatric medical problems that often require negotiation and information sharing with patients and parents

**Context that this was used:** These cases are used during the pediatric clerkship at the University of Michigan Medical School. Students access the “parent email” cases during week 1 via a secure website. They write a response during that week via the secure website which can be accessed by faculty members. During week 2, a faculty member meets with the group of students to discuss general principles of good communication with patients and principles specific to email communication. The discussion focuses on:

1) Communication- acknowledging parental concerns, avoiding jargon

2) Professionalism- being respectful, keeping attending physician informed

3) Medical knowledge- identifying the medical issue and using correct clinical decision making skills

After the discussion, students are handed their own email response from week 1 and the grading rubric that corresponded with the case and are asked to review their own response. If students have any concerns or questions, the faculty member is available to review the student’s response, but this is primarily a self-reflective exercise. During week 2, students have an option of responding to an additional case for faculty feedback if they want more practice answering parental emails.

During week 3 students repeat the process from week 1 with a new case. This response is graded by a faculty member using the corresponding grading rubric which contributes to a portion of the student’s final grade. Feedback is given using the grading rubric.

Although we use a secure website to administer these cases, they can also be emailed to students with the responses returned to the faculty who are responsible for feedback and grading.

**Total time:** 60 min to lead discussion on email communication, about 60 min to grade 6 student responses.
