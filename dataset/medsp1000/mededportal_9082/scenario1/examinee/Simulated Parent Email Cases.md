# Immunizations Case: Assume you are in the first months of your internship in pediatrics or family medicine.

You are on the Newborn service, seeing a family who has identified your continuity clinic practice as follow-up for the infant. When offered the vaccine for Hepatitis B, the mother refuses. A recent episode of TalkShow identified vaccinations as a possible cause for autism, and she feels uncomfortable giving vaccines until she has more information. The two of you agree to discuss this when the infant comes to her follow-up appointment with you in 3 days.

The next day you receive this email message from the mother:

Dear Dr. Smith and Dr.

I have been doing some internet research about vaccines, and I am shocked that you and other members of the medical establishment would continue to recommend them as so much evidence shows they cause autism. Several websites, including www.xxxx.com, have detailed explanations indicating that the high levels of mercury and other heavy metals found so commonly in vaccines are responsible for the epidemic of autism. I feel strongly at this point that my child should not be vaccinated for her own health. If your practice is not able to accommodate my views, I will be taking my child to a different practice.

Jane Sand

Immediately afterward, your continuity clinic advisor (Dr. Smith) sends you an email:

Dear

Please respond to this mother about her concerns about vaccines. Some background information might be helpful for her---from less inflammatory sources---and give some reassurance about immunizations and autism.

Dr. Smith

Please respond to this parent in a way you think would be most appropriate in box 1.

In box 2, communicate with the continuity clinic advisor.
