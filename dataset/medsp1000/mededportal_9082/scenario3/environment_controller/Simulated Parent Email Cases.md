# VCUG: Assume you are in the first months of your internship in pediatrics or family medicine.

You are an intern in pediatric clinic and recently diagnosed an 18 month-old boy, Titus, with his second UTI. After starting a course of cephalexin, you order a renal ultrasound and VCUG. You left a message for the parents yesterday to let them know that the renal ultrasound was normal and reminded them that the VCUG is scheduled for tomorrow.

The following morning you and your attending receive an email from Titus’ mother.

Dear Dr. Bean and Dr.

Thank you for letting us know that Titus’ kidney ultrasound was normal. Since kidneys look normal I don’t see why he needs the x-ray test that is scheduled for tomorrow. I heard on the news last night that exposure to radiation from x-rays can cause cancer. After discussing this with my husband we decided to cancel the appointment for the test tomorrow. Also, does he still need to take the antibiotic?

Thanks, Jane Brown

Immediately afterward, your continuity clinic advisor (Dr. Bean) sends you an email

Dear

Please respond to this mother about her concerns about the VCUG and why we think it is a necessary test. I know we tried to explain this in clinic, but obviously we missed the target. Please make sure you explain the risks and benefits of testing v. not testing and let me know what source(s) you used.

Thanks,

Dr. Bean

Please respond to this parent in a way you think would be most appropriate in box 1.

In box 2, communicate with the continuity clinic advisor.
