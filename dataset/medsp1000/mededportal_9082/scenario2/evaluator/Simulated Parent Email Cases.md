# Strep Throat Case: Assume you are in the first months of your internship in pediatrics or family medicine.

Two days ago, you saw Daisy, a 7 year old girl who came in with three days of sore throat, fever and headache. Your exam revealed an erythematous pharynx and an axillary temperature of 38.9 C. At that time, you performed a rapid strep screen which was negative. With the approval of your attending, you told the father that that Daisy most likely has viral pharyngitis and that no antibiotics are necessary at this time, but that you are sending a second swab for culture and will call them with the results. You encourage symptomatic treatment to keep Daisy comfortable and hydrated.

Today, Daisy’s throat culture comes back positive for Group A Strep. You call the father to start antibiotic treatment and call in a prescription to their local pharmacy. Later in the day, you receive this email message from Daisy’s mother:

Dear Dr. Smith and Dr.

My husband just told me that Daisy has strep throat. I don’t understand why this was missed during her clinic visit two days ago. I am very upset because I just read on www.website.com that strep throat can cause kidney disease and permanent heart disease. You should have treated her two days ago when you saw her if you thought there was a chance she had strep throat. If you don’t know how to treat strep throat, maybe I should find a doctor who can.

Jane Doe

Immediately afterward, your continuity clinic advisor (Dr. Smith) sends you an email

Dear Dr.

Please respond to this mother about our initial decision making and about complications of strep throat. Give her some background information about why we did what we did, then please let me know what source(s) you used to reassure the family. Let me know what I can do.

Thanks, Dr. Smith

Please respond to this parent in a way you think would be most appropriate in box 1.

In box 2, communicate with the continuity clinic advisor.
