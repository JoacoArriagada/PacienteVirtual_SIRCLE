**Initial Psychosocial Assessment**

I. Identifying Information

Pat Franklin was referred to the medical management team after presenting at the Emergency Department. The patient is a 43 year-old Caucasian individual who identifies as Roman Catholic. The patient was brought to the ED by a spouse after experiencing weakness and numbness in the right side of the body accompanied by slurred speech. The patient has no prior episodes of these symptoms.

II. Previous Level of Functioning

The patient has worked for 23 years as a factory worker and is accustomed to engaging in moderate to heavy activity during the work day. The patient has no previous history of difficulty with daily living tasks or mobility. The patient enjoys fishing and reading detective novels.

III. Support Systems

The patient and spouse, have been married for 19 years. The spouse works as an administrator at a local accounting firm. They have no children. The patient’s father died at age 47 after having a heart attack. The patient’s mother (67 years-old) lives in a neighboring community and a sister, Anne (45 years-old), is her caretaker. The Franklins are very close to their neighbors, Jane and Mark, who have visited the hospital twice since his/her admission yesterday to offer support. The Franklins report that they are active members of their church and frequently volunteer in the community food bank.

IV. Financial Status

While a formal needs assessment has not been conducted, the Franklins report that prior to this event, their budget was tight. They report that they have not used public assistance or private charity to make ends meet. They do express concerns about possible consequences of medical expenses and Pat’s ability to continue working. They report having good medical insurance through Pat’s employer and positive and supportive relationships with both their employers, but do worry that if Pat is unable to return to work, they will no longer have health insurance.

V. Relevant Past History

The patient reports a history of diabetes and hypertension.

VI. Assessment

Pat Franklin appears to be of average intelligence and is oriented to person and place. There is some confusion about day and time. There may be some impairment of cognitive functioning, but more assessment is warranted. The patient is currently withdrawn and psychologically immobile, and presents a blunted affect, however this may be an expected consequence of current health concerns. The patient has no reported history of mental health issues. The patient expresses concern about ability to return to previous level of functioning. The patient expresses concern about not being able to return to work or manage the stairs at home.

The current health concerns notwithstanding, the patient appears to be a generally healthy person who is used to providing for the family. Initial concerns about employment and stairs in the home may signal deeper fears about a return to independent functioning.
