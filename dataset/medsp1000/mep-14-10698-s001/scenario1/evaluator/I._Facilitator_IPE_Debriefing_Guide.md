Appendix I: Facilitator IPE Debriefing Guide

**Debrief Discussion Guide for Facilitators**

**IPE Simulation**

**IPE FACILITATOR DEBRIEFING GUIDE:**

**This is only a guide. You will have 25 minutes for debriefing. You will not have time to address each of these issues. Let the students talk and see where the discussion takes the group. Try to address at least one key point under each heading.**

**Getting the conversation started: How do you think it went? Address concerns. Use Advocacy/Inquiry-type of questioning: “I noticed that…..; what was going through your mind when…”**

1. What went well? Why? What did not go so well? Why?
2. What surprised you the most working with an interprofessional team?
3. Tell me about your team’s pre-session “huddle”. What were the things that you dicussed?

**Teamwork**

1. Describe how you divided up tasks?
2. Describe your specific contribution to the session today.
3. Describe the role of leadership in this scenario.
   1. Was there a team leader?
   2. If so, who was it? How was that decided?
   3. Did each of the students agree that "X" was leading? Did "X" perceive that s/he was leading?

\*OR\*

d. How did your team demonstrate “shared leadership”?

e. How might "\*each discipline\*" take a leadership role in different patient care situations?

1. Was a hierarchy apparent? Were hidden biases/stereotypes reflected? How so? What can you do to address this in the future?

**Communication**

1. Did all team members introduce themselves (and their roles) to the patient?
2. Did team members communicate in language understood by all members of the team (including the patient) and avoid discipline specific jargon?
3. Did team members encourage ideas/opinions for all members of the group?
4. How did team members add/contribute supporting pieces of information from their discipline-specific perspective regarding the case, including information that the other disciplines would not have known?

**Roles and Responsibilities**

1. How did you prioritize discipline-specific tasks? How might you have done this differently if you were going to repeat the scenario?
2. Did all team members know what each member’s role on the team was going to be, prior to entering the patient room? How might you have handled this differently?
3. Was your own role clear in the interprofessional team? Were the roles of others clear? Explain. Is there blurring of roles or role overlap? What are your thoughts on role overlap in patient care?
4. What other roles/disciplines would you have liked to have access to? Why? How could that other discipline contribute to the patient’s care?

**Values and Ethics**

1. How did team members demonstrate respect, confidence, and trust in each other?
2. How did the team take into consideration the patient’s specific concerns?

**Review learning points**

1. Describe one aspect of team-based care that you observed today.
2. How does patient-centered care figure into interprofessional practice? Why?
3. What is the most interesting (positive or negative) thing that you learned today about another profession?
4. What do you feel like you need to know more about to work effectively on an interprofessional team?

**Plan ahead**

1. Reflect: If you were to repeat this exercise, what do you think you could do better/differently next time?

**Summary**

1. These are the things that you identified as going well:
2. These are the things you told me you need to work on:
3. Your take-home points include:
4. I saw good things in these areas:
5. Suggestions for next time?

**Thank you all for participating in this event and the debriefing and reflection!**

**Medical Management of Stroke**

*Note: Medical Management of stroke is not a learning objective. Nonetheless, some facilitators may find the following resource helpful if students ask questions about the medical management of a stroke patient such as the one they encountered in the simulation.*

1. **Did this situation involve a hemorrhagic or ischemic stroke? How do you know? What risk factors does the patient have?**

1. This patient had an ischemic stroke (hemorrhagic stroke indicates bleeding into the brain- typically these symptoms develop over time rather than abruptly). With ischemic stroke, a non-contrast CT scan would show minimal evidence of infarct immediately after the event (damage would become more pronounced over subsequent 24-72 hours); whereas, a hemorrhagic stroke would be more apparent immediately following the event).
2. Approximately 80 percent of strokes are due to ischemic cerebral infarction and 20 percent to brain hemorrhage.
3. Risk factors for ischemic stroke: hypertension, diabetes, smoking, and dyslipidemia. (PATIENT HAS ALL OF THESE)
4. Important, but unmodifiable risk factors include age, ethnicity (higher for blacks), gender (higher for males), family history, and genetics
5. If the patient were bleeding (hemorrhagic stroke), the medical team would NOT have administered tPA since that is a thrombolytic drug that lyses clots. (Once tPA is given, symptoms improve slowly over time – in some cases – things will improve 100% -- which is why tPA should be given as soon as possible, ideally within 3 hours of onset of symptoms.

2. **Is the patient receiving appropriate anticoagulation?**

1. Oral administration of aspirin (initial dose is 325 mg) within 24 to 48 hours after stroke onset is recommended for treatment of most patients
2. Nearly all patients with ischemic stroke of atherosclerotic origin should be treated with an antiplatelet agent. The combination of aspirin-extended-release dipyridamole, clopidogrel alone, or aspirin alone are all acceptable options for initial therapy.
3. Long-term anticoagulation - warfarin or another anticoagulant – should be considered for patients with chronic nonvalvular atrial fibrillation who have had an ischemic stroke (NOT NECESSARY IN THIS PATIENT)

3. **Does the patient have adequate glucose management, blood pressure, and cholesterol?**

1. For previously treated patients with known hypertension who are beyond the first few days after stroke onset, resumption of antihypertensive therapy is recommended. Of note, the EKG revealed evidence of left ventricular hypertrophy in the aVL lead (amplitude of QRS in aVL being greater than 11 mm), consistent with long-standing hypertension.
   * 1. Patient was on Lisinopril/Hydrochlorothiazide 20/25mg one tablet by mouth once daily at home.
2. Recommend high-intensity statin therapy regardless of LDL cholesterol level.
   * 1. Atorvastatin 40-80mg
     2. Rosuvastatin 20-40mg
3. For patients with diabetes who have had an ischemic stroke, it is suggested that glucose should be controlled to near-normoglycemic levels and the hemoglobin A1c value should be ≤ 7 for most patients.
   * 1. The patient took metformin at home. It may be necessary to add another diabetes medication to the patient’s daily regimen.

**4. What lifestyle changes would be most appropriate to recommend to this patient?**

1. Stop smoking
2. Minimize alcohol consumption
3. Moderate to vigorous intensity physical exercise for at least 40 minutes most days of the week
4. A Mediterranean diet (increase vegetables, fruits, and whole grains)
5. To further lower blood pressure, sodium restriction (no more than 2400 mg per day)
6. Weight reduction for obese patients is potentially beneficial for improved control of blood pressure, blood glucose, and serum lipid levels.
