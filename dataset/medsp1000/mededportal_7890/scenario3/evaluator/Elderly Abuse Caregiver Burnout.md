**Elder Abuse/Caregiver Burnout**

**Facilitators please touch on all of these objectives during small group discussion\***:

* The student will be able to recognize the psychological symptoms and physical signs of vulnerable older adults
* The students will be able to understand appropriate documentation of mistreatment findings for medical record keeping
* The students will be able to know the legal requirements for reporting elder mistreatment
* The students will become aware of treatment, counseling, and community resources for victims of mistreatment and caregivers at risk for inciting mistreatment

**\* It is most effective to share real life scenarios (what you discovered and how you reacted - what you did) with the students. This will help put it into perspective for them, even those not going into Geriatrics or have yet to take care of an elder.**

**\*It is also important to note that Caregiver Burnout affects all caregivers (babysitters, parents, children of elders, parents of mentally and physically disabled children, etc.)**

**Interview Checklist for Elder Abuse/Caregiver Burnout Case:**

1. Need complete functional history: Does the patient need assistance with basic activities of daily living (bathing, dressing, toileting, continence, ambulation and feeding)? How long has the caregiver been doing these tasks for the patient? Has the patient's function continued to decline, especially recently, so the caregiver is doing more for the patient?
2. Need a complete social history including gaining information about the following which are risk factors for mistreatment.
   1. Older age
   2. Lack of access to resources
   3. Low income
   4. Social isolation
   5. Low level of education
   6. Functional decline
   7. Substance abuse (by older person or caregiver)
   8. Psychological disorders/Cognitive impairment
   9. History of family violence
   10. Caregiver burnout and frustration
3. Need to ask the following general questions screening for mistreatment.
   1. Do you feel safe at home?
   2. How do you and your daughter get along?
   3. Is your daughter taking good care of you?
   4. Do you have frequent disagreements?
   5. What happens when you disagree?
   6. Are you yelled at?
   7. Has anyone ever scolded or threatened you?
   8. Are you afraid of anyone at home?
4. Need to recognize that multiple forms of elder mistreatment usually occur in a single victim and explore for them including: physical abuse, physical neglect, psychological abuse, financial exploitation, violation of personal rights.
5. Need to look for general clues in the caregiver
   1. Poor knowledge of patient's medical problems
   2. Excessive concern about costs
   3. Attempts to dominate medical interview
   4. Verbal abuse or hostility
   5. Evidence of substance abuse
   6. Evidence of mental health problems
   7. Evidence of financial dependence on older adult
6. Need to look for general clues in the victim
   1. Fearfulness toward caregiver
   2. Flinching or reluctance to make eye contact
   3. Diagnosis of dementia with history of disruptive behavior
   4. Dementia: unexplained resistance to, or fear of, physical touch, removing clothes, toileting, bathing
   5. Depression, anxiety, insomnia

**Reporting Requirements:**

Depending on the state law requirements, a little over half of the United States follows this mandatory reporting requirement:

"Any public or private official having reasonable cause to believe that any person 65 years of age or older with whom the official comes in contact, while acting in an official capacity, has suffered abuse, or that any person with whom the official comes in contact while acting in an official capacity has abused a person 65 years of age or older shall report . . . ." ORS 124.060.

Some states, including Indiana, mandates that everyone is required to report abuse of any vulnerable adult age 18+ (Not all older people would be considered vulnerable adults)

**Community Resources for Caregiver Burnout:**

One study found that middle-aged and older women who provided care for an ill or disabled spouse were almost **six times as likely** to suffer depressive or anxious symptoms as were those who had no caregiving responsibilities.

* Early attention to symptoms of depression through exercise, a healthy diet, positive support of family and friends, or consultation with a trained health or mental health professional may help to prevent the development of a more serious depression over time.

**Family Caregiver Alliance -** [**www.caregiver.org**](http://www.caregiver.org) **-** offers online workshops, discussion groups, and advice

**Adult Day Health Care (ADHC)** - Structured community-based programs that provide health-related services in addition to social and recreational activities, meals and social work. Health services include physical and occupational therapies, nutritional counseling and personal care. Services are provided to frail older people and adults with disabilities during the day-time, in a protective setting ("center"), commonly open during business hours on weekdays. Some programs also run during the evenings and on weekends.

**Case Management and Care Coordination Services:** Professionals, usually nurses or social workers, help family members and older adults remain independent. Care managers assess, arrange and monitor long-term care supports.

**Well Spouse Association -** [**www.wellspouse.org**](http://www.wellspouse.org) **-** A national, non-profit membership organization that provides support to wives, husbands, and partners of the chronically ill and/or disabled through established support groups in communities all over the country.
