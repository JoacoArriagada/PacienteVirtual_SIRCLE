# 1. GINA BABKINS  (GERIATRICS OSCE)

**Scenario**

An elderly female patient presents to an outpatient clinic with lightheadedness and is unsteady on her feet.

**Special equipment/props/staging**

- Setting/Room: University Health Services: exam room of an ambulatory facility.
- Chart: Gina Babkins Chart distributed at the doorway.
- SP Clothing / Makeup: Gown.

**STANDARDIZED PATIENT SIMULATION GUIDELINES**

GERIATRICS OSCE – GINA BABKINS

1. Case Overview

- Gina Babkins is a 75 year old woman who presents to the outpatient office with a complaint of lightheadedness. She has an appointment in 2 weeks but asked to be seen now because she doesn’t think she can wait.
- The examinee’s task is to obtain a focused history, perform a focused physical examination and counsel her regarding her situation. Following the encounter, the examinee will then document the encounter in a patient note.

2. ENCOUNTER SETTING

- University Health Services: exam room of an ambulatory (outpatient) medical practice.

3. SP RELATIONSHIPS (Relationship between patient and student doctor)

All participants in this encounter are strangers to one another.

4. WHAT GOES ON IN THIS ENCOUNTER

A chart is provided to the examinee before the encounter, which contains information about the chief complaint and vital signs.

Guided by the examinee’s questions and actions, the patient presents with a complaint of lightheadedness and brief medical history to back it up.

A physical examination is expected.

5. PATIENT CHARACTER PRESENTATION

- Age Range: 75
- Body Language/ Symptom Presentation: Wobbly on her feet when asked to move from the chair to the exam table.
- Affect: Good eye contact, alert.
- Appearance/ Costuming: Hospital gown; otherwise well groomed.
- Starting Position: Sitting in the chair.

6. GINA BABKINS’ STORY

- Opening line: “I have an appointment in two weeks, but I didn’t think I could wait. I hope you don’t mind. I’ve just been so lightheaded lately.”
- The examinee must get the following information out of the patient by asking the right questions. Do not volunteer any of this.
- The lightheadedness started about a week ago.
- It is a “fuzzy” feeling. It is not dizziness and the room does not spin. She has not fallen, but feels unsteady. Sometimes she feels like she is going to pass out.
- She feels lightheaded a lot of the waking day.
- It has gotten worse this week.
- She feels tired and has less of an appetite.
- She is urinating less than before even though her fluid intake has not really changed. No burning or pain with urination. No incontinence. She attributes the problem to the medication not working.
- She has been a bit constipated, and prunes seem to help.
- She may have lost weight.
- The lightheadedness is worse when she stands.
- She had a similar problem once before last year, but it went away.
- If asked about medications, the patient should hand over the medication reminder card and state that she takes all medications as prescribed except that she sometimes takes an extra furosemide when she feels bloated or her shoes/clothes feel tight.
- Medical History: hypertension, high cholesterol, leg swelling for the past year; no surgeries, no illnesses, no allergies.
- Family History: father deceased at 90 from old age; mother deceased at 87 from breast cancer; brother alive at 87 with high blood pressure; older sister age 79 had a stroke a year ago and lives in assisted living.
- Home Life: lives alone; widowed 10 years; son and daughter live out of state; retired homemaker; still drives but has not been going out much lately; nephew brought her to the doctor today; does not use alcohol, tobacco, or street drugs; does not exercise; lives in a one-story ranch house; helpful nephew nearby; church friends supportive.
- Functioning if asked: can dress and toilet independently; takes medication without assistance; can drive and shop; has not felt like cooking much lately.

7. PHYSICAL EXAM SIMULATION

The examinee is expected to do the following assessments. Except where indicated, allow the body to respond normally.

- Examine the mucous membranes of the mouth for dryness. If asked, this does not mean that the mouth feels dry or that the patient is thirsty.
- Listen to the heart.
- Check the lower extremities for swelling.
- Palpate the abdomen in all 4 quadrants. When the examinee presses on the right or left upper quadrant, the patient expresses discomfort: *When you press there I feel a little uneasy, a little sore.*
- Evaluate balance, probably by making the patient stand with eyes open and closed and walk.

8. DIALOGUE

- Use the wording shown in italics, or similar wording, to respond to the examinee.
- If asked questions not addressed here, respond “No” or create a reasonable response that would not affect the substance of the dialogue or misdirect the examinee.
- Any information supplied should be consistent from examinee to examinee.
- Allow examinee to probe for information. Do not provide every fact and detail unless drawn out.

**Key Dramatic Points & Response Guides**

- Opening line response to “What brings you in today?”:
  - *I have an appointment in two weeks, but I didn’t think I could wait.*
  - *I hope you don’t mind. I’m just not feeling myself.*
  - *I’ve been feeling lightheaded and tired lately. I don’t know what’s wrong.*
- When asked when the lightheadedness started:
  - *It started about a week ago, but it's been getting worse, this week in particular.*
- When asked to describe the lightheadedness:
  - *I feel lightheaded a lot.*
  - *My head just seems fuzzy.*
- When asked if she passed out or fainted:
  - *No, but sometimes I feel like I’m going to pass out*
- When asked what makes it worse:
  - *It's worse when I stand.*
  - *When I got out of bed this morning, I got really lightheaded.*
  - *I had to hold onto the bed.*
  - *I even had it when I was getting out of my nephew’s car today.*
- When asked if the room spins:
  - *No. I don’t really feel dizzy and the room doesn't spin.*
- When asked if head movement changes it:
  - *No*
- When asked about falls:
  - *No, I haven’t fallen, but I feel unsteady and at times like I might faint.*
- When asked about balance:
  - *I feel wobbly sometimes*
- When asked about prior episodes:
  - *Yes, I had this problem once before, last year sometime, but it went away.*
- When asked about urination:
  - *I don’t think that water pill is working anymore because I’m not urinating a lot, like I was before.*
- When asked about pain or burning with urination:
  - *No*
- When asked about incontinence:
  - *No.*
- When asked about constipation:
  - *I’ve been a little constipated. Usually, I go every other day.*
  - *I’ve been taking prunes this week to keep me regular.*
- When asked about sleep:
  - *Pretty well, thank goodness.*
- When asked about appetite and daily food:
  - *Okay.*
  - *I don’t eat as much as I used to. I just don't feel hungry.*
  - *I have some toast in the morning. If I feel like eating during the day, I have a little something. TV dinner at night*
- When asked about fluid intake:
  - *I never did really drink that much.*
  - *I drink a little water when I eat my meals and to take my medicines – you know, enough to wash it down.*
- When asked about chest pain, palpitations, cough, shortness of breath, wheezing, nausea, or vomiting:
  - *No*
- When asked whether she is taking medications:
  - *Yes, I check the bottles every day to be sure I take them correctly.*
- When asked about medication changes:
  - *No, but I do take an extra water pill when I feel bloated.*
  - *Otherwise, though, I wouldn’t change my medication without my doctor telling me to do so.*
- When asked how much extra furosemide:
  - *Oh, I would only take one extra pill a day.*
- When asked whether she has taken extra recently:
  - *A couple of times recently. My shoes felt tight and I was bloated, so I took a little extra.*
- When asked about hearing or vision changes:
  - *No*
- When asked about children:
  - *I have a daughter in California and a son in Michigan.*
  - *My nephew brought me here today, but then he went to work. He’s going to pick me up during his hour break.*
- When asked whether she lives with anyone:
  - *No.*
  - *My nephew lives about 30 minutes away from my house, but I do live near his work and he visits me every week.*
  - *He picks up medications, if I need refills, but I can’t depend on him for everything.*
- When asked about functioning at home:
  - *I can get dressed and go to the bathroom myself. If I take my time, I don't get lightheaded.*
  - *I take my medications also.*
  - *I can drive and do my own shopping, but I haven’t been getting out much.*
  - *Lately, I don’t feel like cooking either because I’m only cooking for myself.*
- When asked what she thinks is causing the lightheadedness:
  - *I don't know.*

9. WHAT TO DO WHEN THE EXAMINEE LEAVES THE ROOM

- Re-establish the room to its initial set-up: exam table flat, exam stool under table, side chair by table.
- Assume the patient’s starting position.
