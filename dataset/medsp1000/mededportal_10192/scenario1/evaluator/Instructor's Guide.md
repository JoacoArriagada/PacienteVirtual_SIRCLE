**Instructor Guide for Snake Envenomation**

**Resource Files and Use of Resource Files**

The case submission contains four elements to help run the simulation. The first element is the case narrative, which includes all pertinent case information, interpretations of the case information, and anticipated case branch points. The second element is Appendix A, a PowerPoint that contains pertinent case lab information, x-ray, and EKG to be used during the case simulation. The third element is Appendix B, a PowerPoint that contains teaching points for the debriefing session. Finally, an evaluation form is included, that contains recommended care actions.

**Purpose and Education Objectives**

Acute snake envenomation is an uncommon but potentially serious envenomation causing local tissue injury, hematologic derangements, and systemic effects. The purpose of this simulation is to present an acute snake envenomation case that allows residents, fellows and other care providers to test their knowledge base and critically evaluate the management of this type of injury.

Primary learning objectives include

* 1. Demonstrate recognition of a crotalidae snake envenomation
  2. Evaluate a snake envenomation victim for signs and symptoms of toxicity, recognizing local, hematologic and systemic effects
  3. Identify severity of envenomation based on clinical signs and symptoms
  4. Demonstrate knowledge of basic supportive care measures in snake envenomation
  5. Understand that snakebites may progress in severity, requiring a minimum 8 hours of observation prior to disposition
  6. Understand that fasciotomy is rarely indicated for treatment of snakebites

Secondary learning objectives include

* 1. Recognize the importance of repeat examination of the envenomated extremity for progression of injury
  2. Know the indications for antivenom (CroFab; Crotalidae Polyvalent Immune Fab Ovine) administration
  3. Understand the elements of basic wound care in snake envenomations
  4. Recognize the utility of poison control as a useful resource in the management of snake envenomations
  5. Understand the adverse reactions (acute allergic reaction, serum sickness) that can be seen with antivenom (CroFab) administration

**Conceptual Background**

Acute snake envenomation is an infrequent but potentially serious injury that requires an adequate knowledge base and critical thinking to manage. A simulation case allows real-time, hands on training for residents and fellows to practice managing this type of injury.

**Practical Implementation**

The simulation programming is not difficult, but the instructor should be familiar with their simulator software. Approximately 30 minutes is required for this simulation, 15 minutes to do the case, and approximately 15 minutes for debriefing and discussion. The debriefing session should be conducted in a nonjudgmental manner, while focusing on identifying knowledge gaps and critically evaluating the case management.

**Deployment and Limitations**

As this is an uncommon injury, most residents and fellows are not familiar with the management of this injury. Expected discussion topics include a basic overview and evaluation of snakebite severity, general wound care, use of antivenom (CroFab), and other supportive measures. The limitation of this case is the lack of realism, a shared problem with all simulation-based instruction.
