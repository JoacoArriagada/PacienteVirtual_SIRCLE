**Case Overview**

Jacqueline Williams is a 47-year-old cisgender woman with a history of well-controlled HIV on treatment and hypertension who presents for a new patient appointment in the HIV primary care clinic. She is transferring care from another provider. She was diagnosed with HIV approximately 9 months ago after her cisgender male partner was diagnosed. Her previous provider had started her on antiretroviral medications in a single tablet. She had been adherent to the medication. Her prior provider had also started her on hydrochlorothiazide, but she had not continued this because of the side effect of frequent urination.

Her main complaint today is of progressive fatigue over the past several months. She has a hard time getting out of bed in the morning and feels tired during the day. She does not report feeling excessively cold or warm, rashes, headaches, or polyuria/polydipsia.

She is seeking a new provider because she felt that her previous provider was not taking her concerns seriously.
