**Case 4: Depression**

**Initial Visit:**

Chris is a 15yo boy presenting with his parents for sad mood and declining school performance. His parents feel like he has not been himself the past few months. Despite their attempts to help, it seems like things are getting worse. They are frustrated that his grades have been declining and he is not completing his chores, resulting in frequent arguments at home. Chris admits that he has felt more down and irritable the past few months without any clear reasons. He reports decreased interest in playing basketball and isn’t as interested in spending time with his friends. He also reports trouble sleeping, with difficulty in falling asleep and staying asleep most nights.

Chris’ parents are worried about his change in mood and behavior but are not currently concerned about his safety. They deny other mental health or medical concerns on review of systems, deny any history of persistently elevated or irritable moods, and deny any past attempts at self-harm or suicide. Patient has no significant medical history and no past psychiatric treatment. There is a maternal family history of major depressive disorder and a possible history of bipolar disorder and substance use disorder in mother’s uncle.

When interviewed alone, Chris and his parents agree to individual interview time without his parents present. Limits of confidentiality are reviewed. Chris is slow to open up, mostly giving only one-word answers at first. He denies current romantic relationships or sexual activity, denies any substance use other than one can of soda per day, and denies self-harm or past suicide attempts. Chris is open to trying medications if others think it could help. He is reluctant to talk with someone about his feelings in individual psychotherapy because he claims he has nothing to talk about.

Chris appears grossly alert and oriented, appears stated age, is casually dressed, has somewhat below-average hygiene, and is guarded and slow to warm with slightly decreased eye contact. His speech is normal in rate, rhythm, and volume but fairly monotone. His affect is constricted, dysphoric, and irritable at times.

If directly asked about suicidal thinking, Chris denies current SI/HI/AVH, reports frequent morbid ideation, and reports he has had several periods of suicidal ideation with a plan but without intent or access. He has self-deprecating thoughts.
