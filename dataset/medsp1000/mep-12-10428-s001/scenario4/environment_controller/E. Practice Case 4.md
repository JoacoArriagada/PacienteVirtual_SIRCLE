**Appendix E: Practice Case 4**

**Opening scenario**

A 67-year old female, presents to the clinic complaining of rectal bleeding.

**Vital signs**

BP 104/66 mmHg

Temp 98.6˚F (37˚C)

RR 14/min

HR 68/min

**Patient information**

HPI: 67-year female complaining of rectal bleeding for nine days. A moderate amount of bright red blood has been noticed on clothing and in the toilet. No recent hospitalization or sick contacts. ROS is positive for mild abdominal pain for 1 week – LLQ, 4/10, “achy,” episodic. She denies rectal pain, back pain, melena, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, fever, chills, and change in appetite.

PMH: Hypothyroidism, atrial fibrillation, and hypertension. Post-menopausal, G2P2. No recent hospitalizations.

PSH: Routine colonoscopy in 2002: diverticulosis and no polyps.

MEDS: Levothyroxine 100mcg daily, lisinopril 10mg daily, metoprolol 50mg BID, warfarin 5mg daily (*if asked, INR=5.2 two weeks ago, dose adjusted down*).

ALL: none

SH: Regular smoker tobacco (34 pack-years). Denies alcohol & illicit drugs. Married, lives home with husband. Not sexually active. Retired teacher. Typical American diet. No recent travel.

FH: Sister diagnosed with colon cancer (deceased).

ROS: Abdominal pain as above (LLQ, 4/10, “achy,” episodic). Otherwise, negative.

PHYSICAL EXAM

GEN: Well perfused, no jaundice.

HEENT: Conjunctiva normal.

CV: Normal perfusion, no murmur, no edema.

ABD: Mild LLQ tenderness, no rebound, normal bowel sounds.
