**Appendix B: Practice Case 1**

**Opening scenario**

A 33-year old female, presents to the clinic complaining of leg pain.

**Vital signs**

BP 124/76 mmHg

Temp 100.9˚F (38.3˚C)

RR 20/min

HR 85/min

**Patient information**

HPI: 33-year female with right leg pain for 3 days. Pain is mild (3/10), constant, located over posterior calf and knee, non-radiating, and associated with local skin redness. Symptoms began several days following minor trauma to the lower right leg – blunt injury to posterior lower leg from tree branch resulting in abrasion and contusion.

Very concerned that these symptoms represent a DVT – a close friend died from a post-operative DVT/PE while taking OCPs.

PMH: Diabetes mellitus. No history of thrombotic disease, cancers, or recent hospitalizations. LMP 3 weeks ago, regular.

PSH: none

MEDS: Metformin 1000mg BID, ethinyl/estradiol/norgestimate daily.

ALL: none

SH: Regular smoker (5 pack-years). Denies alcohol & illicit drugs. She is a pharmaceutical salesperson who routinely drives long regional distances. Lives alone.

FH: none (specifically no family history of DVT/PE or cancer)

ROS: Denies fever, chills, chest pain, dyspnea, cough, hemoptysis, rash, or edema.

PHYSICAL EXAM

GEN: Awake/alert, no distress.

CV: Normal except for 1+ edema to the right foot and ankle.

LYMPH: Shoddy right inguinal lymphadenopathy.

EXTREMITY: Well-healed 3.5 cm superficial wound with surrounding erythema,

warmth, and tenderness over the right posterior lower leg; posterior right knee/calf tenderness; 1+ edema as above; compartments soft; no joint effusions; normal ROM knee/ankle.
