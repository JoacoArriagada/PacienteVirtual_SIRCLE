**Role Assignments:**

In each scenario, the students will be acting in their usual capacity as medical students, though they will rotate roles within each scenario (team leader, airway/breathing, CPR, defibrillator operator, etc). The preceptor will have to play multiple roles – generally they will introduce the case and interact primarily in the role of nurse, but they may also portray the patient, members of the physician team, or consultants.

**Room Setup and Equipment for All Cases:**

* Simulator (SimMan or SimBaby) on stretcher or hospital bed (latter preferred)
* Simulator monitor
* Oxygen source
* CPR stool and backboard
* Ambu bag in packaging, hanging at head of bed (not set up or attached to oxygen)
* “Crash Cart” available with the following simplified contents:
  + Oral airway
  + Nasal airway
  + Nasal cannula
  + Lubricant
  + Nonrebreather mask
  + Defibrillator with paddles attached
  + Electrogel
  + 7-10 pre-filled saline syringes to be used as any medication requested by students in scenario
* For pediatric station with Sim Baby (Molly setup with IV already in place):
  + Need all of above
  + Intraosseous needles
  + Fluid setup so that can measure actual fluid volume delivered
  + Normal saline bags
  + Glucose
  + 24 and 22 gauge IVs
  + Stopcock
  + Large syringes- 50/60 cc for fluid administration push
  + Functional IV pump that will administer fluids as set

* Rules of the Game
  + You only get what you ask for: your nurse will help you however possible, but will only do what you ask and will not initiate treatment
  + You can call anyone you want at any time
  + You can request tests, and results will be made available in real time
  + You can request treatments, and these will be made available in real time as well

**Scenario 1: Airway/Breathing**

**Introduction for Learners:**

You have just arrived on the floor to check on Mrs. Anderson, a 72 year old woman who underwent hip replacement yesterday. Nursing staff called your intern because the patient was complaining of severe hip pain. The intern gave a verbal order for pain medicine, and asked you to go and check on the patient.

**The Rest of the Story:**

The patient had been receiving 2mg hydromorphone every 4-6 hours and had gotten her last dose 30 min ago. The patient reported no relief of pain, so the nurse called the intern, who ordered a one-time dose of 4mg hydromorphone. Shortly after this was administered, the patient stopped breathing.

**Case Progression:**

* Initially simulator is laying in bed, not attached to any monitors, no supplemental oxygen, unresponsive to verbal or painful stimuli.
* Ambu bag is available at the head of the bed but it remains in its packaging, and is not set up or attached to oxygen
* Initial VS: P 78, BP 159/82, R 0, SpO2 86%
* In the absence of effective management, pulse ox will decrease to 56% over 5 minutes
* Simulator will remain set to “can’t bag, can’t intubate” until effective ventilation is achieved, at which point airway will reset, pulse ox will come up to 96% over one minute, and other vital signs will remain stable
* If effective management is not completed within 5 minutes, the patient will undergo bradyasystolic arrest. Patient will not be recoverable at this point.

**Case Termination:**

Case will end when learners stabilize patient, OR after patient has been in cardiac arrest for one minute.
