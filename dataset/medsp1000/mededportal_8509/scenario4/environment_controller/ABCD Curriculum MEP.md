**Role Assignments:**

In each scenario, the students will be acting in their usual capacity as medical students, though they will rotate roles within each scenario (team leader, airway/breathing, CPR, defibrillator operator, etc). The preceptor will have to play multiple roles – generally they will introduce the case and interact primarily in the role of nurse, but they may also portray the patient, members of the physician team, or consultants.

**Room Setup and Equipment for All Cases:**

* Simulator (SimMan or SimBaby) on stretcher or hospital bed (latter preferred)
* Simulator monitor
* Oxygen source
* CPR stool and backboard
* Ambu bag in packaging, hanging at head of bed (not set up or attached to oxygen)
* “Crash Cart” available with the following simplified contents:
  + Oral airway
  + Nasal airway
  + Nasal cannula
  + Lubricant
  + Nonrebreather mask
  + Defibrillator with paddles attached
  + Electrogel
  + 7-10 pre-filled saline syringes to be used as any medication requested by students in scenario
* For pediatric station with Sim Baby (Molly setup with IV already in place):
  + Need all of above
  + Intraosseous needles
  + Fluid setup so that can measure actual fluid volume delivered
  + Normal saline bags
  + Glucose
  + 24 and 22 gauge IVs
  + Stopcock
  + Large syringes- 50/60 cc for fluid administration push
  + Functional IV pump that will administer fluids as set

* Rules of the Game
  + You only get what you ask for: your nurse will help you however possible, but will only do what you ask and will not initiate treatment
  + You can call anyone you want at any time
  + You can request tests, and results will be made available in real time
  + You can request treatments, and these will be made available in real time as well

**Case 4 – Hypovolemic Shock in a Child**

**Introduction for Learners:**

You are on your pediatric or ER rotation and are told by your resident to go see a new admission in the ER, Toby, a 6 month old brought in by his mother for 2 days of vomiting, diarrhea and fever. On your arrival, the nurse tells you that the baby has recently become more lethargic since he had been previously evaluated by the resident. Bloodwork is pending and the child has received a dose of IV antibiotic already, as there had been some concern for sepsis initially.

**The Rest of the Story:**

The patient has received IV Ceftriaxone, but has not been started yet on any IV fluids (if asked by students). The child’s oral intake for past 24 hours has been poor and he is hypoglycemic, but this is not known (D-stick not yet done, if students request it, glucose fingerstick = 40 g/dL). There is an IV in place, but when RN attempts to use it, it is nonfunctional.

**Case Progression:**

* Initially sim baby is lying in crib, not attached to any monitors, no supplemental oxygen, will cry to painful stimuli. Both nasal cannula and nonrebreather mask are available at head of bed
* If asked, child’s fontanelle is sunken and mucous membranes are dry with cap refill 4 seconds
* Initial VS: P 160, BP 80/40, R 45, SpO2 95%
* Becomes progressively tachycardic to 190s over first 2-3 minutes. Subsequently, BP decreases to 50/25 as case progresses
* Becomes progressively more lethargic so that baby is only grunting, no vocalizations.

**Case Termination:**

Case will end when the child is stabilized, OR at 7 minutes.
