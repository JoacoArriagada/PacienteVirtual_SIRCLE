**Role Assignments:**

In each scenario, the students will be acting in their usual capacity as medical students, though they will rotate roles within each scenario (team leader, airway/breathing, CPR, defibrillator operator, etc). The preceptor will have to play multiple roles – generally they will introduce the case and interact primarily in the role of nurse, but they may also portray the patient, members of the physician team, or consultants.

**Room Setup and Equipment for All Cases:**

* Simulator (SimMan or SimBaby) on stretcher or hospital bed (latter preferred)
* Simulator monitor
* Oxygen source
* CPR stool and backboard
* Ambu bag in packaging, hanging at head of bed (not set up or attached to oxygen)
* “Crash Cart” available with the following simplified contents:
  + Oral airway
  + Nasal airway
  + Nasal cannula
  + Lubricant
  + Nonrebreather mask
  + Defibrillator with paddles attached
  + Electrogel
  + 7-10 pre-filled saline syringes to be used as any medication requested by students in scenario
* For pediatric station with Sim Baby (Molly setup with IV already in place):
  + Need all of above
  + Intraosseous needles
  + Fluid setup so that can measure actual fluid volume delivered
  + Normal saline bags
  + Glucose
  + 24 and 22 gauge IVs
  + Stopcock
  + Large syringes- 50/60 cc for fluid administration push
  + Functional IV pump that will administer fluids as set

* Rules of the Game
  + You only get what you ask for: your nurse will help you however possible, but will only do what you ask and will not initiate treatment
  + You can call anyone you want at any time
  + You can request tests, and results will be made available in real time
  + You can request treatments, and these will be made available in real time as well

**Scenario 3: Altered Mental Status**

**Introduction for Learners:**

You are pre-rounding on Ms. Johnson, a 46 year old woman with a past history of diabetes and asthma. She was admitted yesterday for thrombocytopenia thought to be caused by ITP (idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura). She had no clinical evidence of bleeding at that time. When you enter her room at 7:00am, you are unable to awaken her.

**The Rest of the Story:**

She has a platelet count of 4 and has developed a subdural hematoma. She now has elevated intracranial pressure and impending herniation. She is also hypoglycemic. The patient is unable to give any history. The preceptor, playing the role of the patient’s nurse, is available to answer questions. Some facts that can be disclosed as appropriate:

* + Patient complained of headache overnight, had no improvement with Tylenol given at 3:00am, resident gave order for Oxycodone which was given at 4:00am
  + Lantus insulin 40U was given at 10:00pm – this was an increase from patient’s usual dose of 30U, which had not been adequately controlling glucose
  + On evening shift yesterday, patient had fallen in the shower, striking her head and left elbow. She did not appear to be injured and no treatment was required other than ice.

**Case Progression:**

* Initially simulator is lying in bed, not attached to any monitors, no supplemental oxygen (if possible, set pupils to be anisocoric)
* Preceptor is in the room as “nurse”, looking busy initially, but will answer questions or help as requested – will need to help verify exam findings for students to enhance fidelity
* Patient initially has no response to verbal or painful stimuli, eyes remain closed
* Initial VS: P 54, BP 184/102, R 24, SpO2 88%
* If appropriate stabilization is not completed within 5 minutes, the patient will begin to seize.
* If appropriate stabilization is completed within 5 minutes, the patient will remain stable and case will end.
* If finger stick blood glucose is checked, the nurse will report that it is 38.
* If D-50 is administered, the patient will moan and open eyes, but there will be no speech or commands; repeat finger stick blood glucose, if requested, will be 128.
* If opiate use or naloxone is discussed with the nurse, the nurse will give a 0.4mg dose “per hospital protocol”; this will have no effect.
* After glucose correction, reassessment shows GCS 8 with eyes open to pain, moans, withdraws to pain, and persistent anisocoria.

**Case Termination:**

Case will end when learners stabilize patient, OR after patient begins to seize.
