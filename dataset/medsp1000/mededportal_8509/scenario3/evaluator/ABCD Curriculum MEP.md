**Scenario 3: Altered Mental Status**

**Objectives:**

By the end of the session, each team of learners will:

* Perform CAB assessment and call for help immediately
* Request cardiac monitoring, pulse oximetry, and blood pressure
* Ensure IV access
* Correctly administer supplemental oxygen and provide noninvasive airway management with positioning
* Request finger-stick blood glucose for a patient with altered mental status
* Discuss indications and contraindications for naloxone in a patient with altered mental status
* Perform a Glasgow Coma Scale assessment in comatose patient
* Describe a basic differential diagnosis for altered mental status, with emphasis on potential life threats
* Discuss indications for noncontrast head CT in a patient with altered mental status

**Introduction for Learners:**

You are pre-rounding on Ms. Johnson, a 46 year old woman with a past history of diabetes and asthma. She was admitted yesterday for thrombocytopenia thought to be caused by ITP (idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura). She had no clinical evidence of bleeding at that time. When you enter her room at 7:00am, you are unable to awaken her.

**The Rest of the Story:**

She has a platelet count of 4 and has developed a subdural hematoma. She now has elevated intracranial pressure and impending herniation. She is also hypoglycemic. The patient is unable to give any history. The preceptor, playing the role of the patient’s nurse, is available to answer questions. Some facts that can be disclosed as appropriate:

* + Patient complained of headache overnight, had no improvement with Tylenol given at 3:00am, resident gave order for Oxycodone which was given at 4:00am
  + Lantus insulin 40U was given at 10:00pm – this was an increase from patient’s usual dose of 30U, which had not been adequately controlling glucose
  + On evening shift yesterday, patient had fallen in the shower, striking her head and left elbow. She did not appear to be injured and no treatment was required other than ice.

**Case Progression:**

* Initially simulator is lying in bed, not attached to any monitors, no supplemental oxygen (if possible, set pupils to be anisocoric)
* Preceptor is in the room as “nurse”, looking busy initially, but will answer questions or help as requested – will need to help verify exam findings for students to enhance fidelity
* Patient initially has no response to verbal or painful stimuli, eyes remain closed
* Initial VS: P 54, BP 184/102, R 24, SpO2 88%
* If ALL critical actions ARE NOT completed within 5 minutes, the patient will begin to seize
* If ALL critical actions ARE completed within 5 minutes, the patient will remain stable and case will end

**Case Termination:**

Case will end when learners EITHER complete all critical actions successfully and stabilize patient, OR after patient begins to seize.

**Critical Actions for Learners (and Consequences):**

* CAB assessment: check carotid pulse, look for respiratory effort
* Call for help – learners may request either code team or their own resident/attending
* Request cardiac monitor and pulse ox, check blood pressure
* Provide supplemental oxygen (nonrebreather will take pulse ox up to 98% over one minute, nasal cannula will have no effect)
* Assess level of consciousness and pupils (GCS = 3, anisocoric)
* Check finger stick blood glucose (nurse will report that it is 38)
* Administer D-50 (will cause patient to moan and open eyes, but no speech or commands; repeat FSBG if requested will be 128)
* Consider naloxone (defined as asking about opiate use, discussing whether it would be appropriate with nurse, etc – NOT required to “order” it, as learners would not be ordering meds at this stage – any discussion of opiates or naloxone will prompt nurse to give a 0.4mg dose “per hospital protocol” – this will have no effect)
* Reassess level of consciousness and pupils (GCS now 8 – eyes open to pain (2), moans (2), withdraws to pain (4), patient remains anisocoric)

**Teaching Points for Debriefing:**

* Assessment of responsiveness – verbal/painful stimuli
* CAB assessment
  + Check carotid pulse for <10 seconds and BEGIN CPR IF ABSENT OR IF YOU ARE UNCERTAIN
  + If pulse is present, look for breathing and begin ventilatory support if breathing is absent or agonal
  + PERFORM ABC ASSESSMENT EVERY TIME YOU ENCOUNTER AN UNRESPONSIVE OR CRITICALLY ILL PATIENT
* CALL FOR HELP IMMEDIATELY
  + IF CAB ASSESSMENT REVEALS A PROBLEM, CALL CODE TEAM
  + If patient is unresponsive but otherwise stable, it may be appropriate to call your intern/resident
  + When in doubt, call code team – better an inappropriate activation of the code team than a dead patient!
* “When you see a sick patient, say OMI” – oxygen, monitor (including pulse ox), IV access and check full vital signs…EVERY TIME
* Supplemental oxygen
  + Some form of O2 required for EVERY seriously ill patient, especially initially
  + Comatose patients often hypoxic due to partial airway obstruction – use positioning at a minimum, may need nasal airway to maintain airway patency
  + Apply nonrebreather mask for all hypoxic patients, particularly those who are comatose – mouth breathing makes nasal cannula ineffective
  + Explain difference between oxygen delivery devices
    - NC requires nose breathing, slight increase in FiO2 but very much mixed with ambient air
    - NRB provides the most oxygen that can be given to a spontaneously breathing patient, not quite 100% FiO2 but as close as possible without a sealed system
    - Ambu also provides nearly 100% FiO2 BUT IS ONLY USED FOR APNEIC OR AGONAL PATIENTS – not effective or well tolerated for spontaneously breathing patients, is not the next “step up” from NRB if patient is breathing (for persistent hypoxia on NRB, intubation is required)
* GCS Assessment
  + Eyes (4 max)
    - Open spontaneously (4)
    - Open to voice (command – 3)
    - Open to pain (sternal rub – 2)
    - Closed (1)
  + Verbal (5 max)
    - Speaking appropriately, oriented (5)
    - Speaking coherently but disoriented or inappropriate (4)
    - Saying words with little or no context (3)
    - Moaning (2)
    - Silent (1)
  + Motor (6 max)
    - Follows commands (6)
    - Localizes pain (sternal rub – 5)
    - Withdraws to pain (nailbed pressure – 4)
    - Flexor posturing (3)
    - Extensor posturing (2)
    - Flaccid (1)
* Pupillary exam
  + PERRL = normal
  + Anisocoria – think herniation (bleed, tumor, etc) – NEED URGENT CT!! Remember that anisocoria suggests impending herniation – if mass lesion continues to expand, herniation may occur, leading to irreversible neurologic damage. Act quickly!!!
  + Miosis – think opiates (other intoxications as well – any parasympathomimetic)
  + Mydriasis – think sympathomimetic
* D is for Dextrose!!
  + Hypoglycemia is a common, life-threatening, and entirely reversible cause of altered mental status
  + Testing of FSBG universally available as a rapid bedside test
  + Must be checked in EVERY patient with altered mental status
* Naloxone
  + Very effective for reversing clinical effects of opiates
  + Definitely indicated for treatment with suspected opiate intoxication and respiratory depression
  + Can be diagnostic (rather than therapeutic) with altered mental status without respiratory depression
  + Consider in all patients with altered mental status who were recently treated with opiates, especially if new medication or increased dose, or miosis on pupillary exam
  + Would be appropriate but not mandatory in this case
* Frequent reassessment required, particularly after interventions that may change neurologic status – sometimes improvement is obvious (i.e., pt opens eyes and starts talking), sometimes more subtle and requires full re-exam
* Noncontrast head CT
  + Rapid and accurate way to assess for many life-threatening causes of altered mental status
  + Good for intracranial hemorrhage, tumor, hydrocephalus
  + May miss subarachnoid hemorrhage depending on duration
  + Mandatory for patients with coagulopathy and trauma who present with altered mental status and/or headache(as in this case)
* Differential diagnosis of altered mental status
  + Hypoglycemia
  + Hypoxia or hypercarbia
  + Intoxication or overdose
  + Mass effect (bleed, tumor, hydrocephalus)
  + Metabolic disturbances (sodium, BUN/Cr, Ca)
  + Hepatic encephalopathy (NH3)
  + Infection (meningitis, encephalitis)
