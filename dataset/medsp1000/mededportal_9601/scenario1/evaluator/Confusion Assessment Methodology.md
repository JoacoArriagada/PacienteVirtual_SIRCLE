**Confusion Assessment Method (CAM)**

1. **Acute Onset/Fluctuating Course**

Is this an acute change in mental status with evidence of fluctuation in symptoms?

1. **Inattention**

Does the patient have difficulty focusing attention (e.g., easily distractible or unable to focus on the conversation)?

1. **Disorganized Speech**

Is the patient’s speech disorganized /incoherent (e.g., rambling/irrelevant conversation, illogical flow of ideas, or unpredictable switching of subjects)?

1. **Altered Level of Consciousness**

Is the patient’s level of alertness either hyperalert (e.g., overly sensitive to environmental stimuli, easily startles), or hypoalert (e.g., lethargic, stuporous, drowsy/difficult to arouse?)

**CAM Scoring:**

Patients are “CAM Positive” for delirium when they have characteristics consistent with the above numbers:

**1 + 2 plus either 3 or 4 = delirium**

Reproduced from: Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) and CAM-ICU in: Inouye SK. Delirium in hospitalized older patients. Clin Geriatr Med 1998;14:745-764.
