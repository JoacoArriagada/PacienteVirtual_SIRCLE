## **Medical College of Wisconsin**

# 1. **Geriatric Cardiology- OSCE**

# 2. **Case Development Blueprint**

**Presenting Complaint:** “I don’t feel well.”

**Gender and Age Range:** 80 year old woman

**Patient Name:** Mary Frederickson

**Brief Summary:** Mrs. Frederickson is an 80 year old woman with undiagnosed early-moderate stage dementia who lives alone in her home after her husband’s recent death. She presented to the ED with dyspnea, nausea and lethargy. Evaluation revealed elevated cardiac enzymes and an EKG consistent with a non-ST elevation MI. A cardiac catheterization is planned. Consent for the procedure must be obtained by the cardiology fellow prior to the procedure. The patient’s daughter is present in the hospital room. The patient is non-decisional due to delirium and dementia. The daughter is the POAHC for the procedure consent.

**Key “Findings” for SP patient to Portray:**

**Dementia & Delirium:**

- Not certain of where she is (place) or the date/year.
- Memory is impaired—misses items on the 3 item recall test, has difficulty with the clock draw test, misses some items on the MOCA.
- Thinking is disorganized: responds to questions with repeated phrases (e.g., “I think that I need to go home, now”, “I need to check with my husband”). The patient tries to “cover up” the fact that she does not know the answers to the questions being asked by responding with vague and imprecise answers, not using many words.
- Confusion: does not completely understand what the physician is trying to tell her about her heart and the procedure that is being discussed. She has difficulty remembering what the physician has told her, is having difficulty paying attention (is sleepy), and is not sure where she is.
- She is not able to make a choice about whether to have the procedure done or not. She keeps saying, “I’ll be fine soon. This is all fine.”
- Not very verbal, relies on (looks to) daughter to answer most questions
- Lethargic (tired) at times, occasionally drifting off to sleep during the interview session, closing eyes for brief periods of time (few seconds).

**Key “Findings” for SP daughter to Portray:**

**Background history**

**Mom’s cognition:**

- Mom’s thinking has been “pretty good for someone her age”, but she has some repetition of questions and doesn’t always remember what she was told about various things/events.
- Sometimes Mom has difficulty with understanding why things are a certain way—or how things work (e.g. trouble managing her new phone, when the light bulb burned out she called the electrician to come to the house to replace the bulb, she recently gave substantial donations to both political parties because she “thought then they would work together better”.
- Mom’s thinking has become much worse over the last several days. She is “not herself” and “she was never this bad”. “This is such a change for her. She doesn’t know where she is now, and that is new.”

**Mom’s function/social situation:**

- Some trouble remembering things (missed appointments, meds, bill paying now that Dad has died)—IADLS impaired, ADLs ok
- She had one small car accident last month where she “rear-ended” the car in front of her at a stop light.
- Things having been more difficult for the patient now that her husband has died. He was very helpful in keeping everything going. Now the daughter is needing to be more involved and she is taking additional time off of work and on the weekends to help her mom with laundry, driving, bill paying. The daughter has started to bring some meals over to her mother’s house for dinner.

- Hospital gown for patient
- Blanket for patient/Pillow for patient

- Chair next to bedside for SP daughter
- Chair in room for physician (fellow) primarily interacting with exercise
- Chair in room for physician (fellow) observing and offering insights/thoughts
- Sheet with background information, instructions for fellow
- Pen
- Paper for writing or note taking (if desired)
