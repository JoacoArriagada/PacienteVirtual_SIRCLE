Case 1:

Zane is 23 years old and comes into your clinic to establish care. Zane identifies as genderfluid and pansexual and is generally healthy.

Zane was assigned female at birth, and uses they, them, their pronouns. Zane has been sexually active with 4 partners in their lifetime—their first encounter was at age 14 with a cisgender girl. They experimented with digital intercourse. They then dated a cisgender man and a transgender man in their later teens. They are currently monogamous with a transwoman, Kim, who Zane sees as the love of their life. They have been together for the past 2 years and are thinking of getting married someday.

When referring to anatomy, Zane isn’t comfortable with using traditional terminology for their body parts; while not completely comfortable with any specific terminology at this time, they prefer “gonads” or “genitals” for what medical providers would call the ovaries, uterus, vagina and uterus.

They are sexually active with Kim. Kim is 28 years old and identifies as a lesbian transwoman (sex assigned at birth: male; preferred pronouns: she, her, hers). Kim has not taken any hormones, nor has she had any gender-affirming surgeries. Kim works as executive director of a local non-profit.

Intercourse is unprotected. With their current partner, Kim, they use “all parts” for intercourse. To Z, this means using hands, mouth, anus, and genitals for intercourse. Kim will use hands, oral, penile insertive (oral, gonads, and anal), and anal receptive intercourse. Zane is not sure about plans for pregnancy as they aren’t sure doing something so female-gendered would feel right in their body, but the idea of having children is somewhat appealing. Zane is also considering whether or not they want hormones and/or surgery to alter their body to align better their gender identity.

**Past Medical History:** None; Has regular bleeding (does not refer to them as menses or menstrual cycles) but really uncomfortable with them (both physically and psychologically). Last bleeding was 2 months ago. They never had pap testing, HIV testing, or HPV immunization. They are up to date on childhood immunizations. They have not seen a healthcare provider since age 17.

**Past Surgical History:** tonsillectomy at age 3

**Medications:** None

**Social History:** Zane smokes 1-2 cigarettes per day. Drinks 3-4 alcoholic beverages per week. No binge drinking. No drugs.

They occasionally struggle with anxiety as they have been verbally harassed in the past; they were physically assaulted once by their parents before being kicked out of the home at age 17 (their dad punched them in the stomach after they came out). Zane couch surfed with friends after this until meeting Kim. Zane finished high school and works at the local coffee shop. They are taking classes at the community college while working in the hopes of eventually getting into medical school.

What type of preventive care, evaluation, and/or counseling should you consider for Zane?

* Cervical cancer screening—Z is due for Pap testing, but this topic should be approached cautiously as it can often cause discomfort, anxiety, and dysphoria in transgender/gender non-binary individuals
* HIV screening—recommended by the CDC for all adults aged 16-64 at least once, increased frequency for those with high risk behaviors
* Screen for depression/anxiety—rates of depression and anxiety are increased in LGBT patients, predominantly due to stigma
* Immunizations—HPV immunization; Tetanus booster; annual flu vaccine are recommended
* Counseling—Discuss pregnancy plans, especially around the fact that Z could become pregnant with current intercourse patterns. With last cycle 2 months ago, recommend pregnancy testing.
* Transition-related Care—Explore goals for transition; inquire about and work to address body dysphoria
* Offer treatment for tobacco dependence
* Legal considerations—MDPOA, Living Will
