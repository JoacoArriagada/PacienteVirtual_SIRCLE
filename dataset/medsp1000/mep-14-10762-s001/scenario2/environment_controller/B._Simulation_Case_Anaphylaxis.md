| **PATIENT NAME:** Jimmy  **PATIENT AGE:** 18 months old  **PATIENT WEIGHT:** 12 kg  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** Anaphylaxis | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | Your nurse calls you into the room to assess a patient. The patient just received IM Ceftriaxone for an ear infection and started developing hives. He is in moderate respiratory distress. Anticipated interventions include primary assessment, calling for help, initiation of anaphylaxis medications. Medics will be called. After receiving IM epinephrine, patient’s respiratory status improves. Optional 2nd Act to have return of respiratory distress prompting 2nd dose of IM epi. Scenario ends when Medics arrive. |

| **HPI** | Jimmy is an 18-month-old boy. Today he was being seen in your office for a painful left ear. He recently completed treatment for an ear infection with Augmentin and today was discovered to have a recurrence. Jimmy’s mother has trouble giving him oral medications so it was decided to treat him with IM ceftriaxone. About 5 minutes after receiving the antibiotic, the nurse calls you into the room because Jimmy isn’t looking so well. He appears to be in moderate respiratory distress.  *(Of note – if IM ceftriaxone is not administered at your site, patient could have an anaphylactic reaction to any antibiotic that would be administered.)*  When asked about events leading up to the event (SAMPLE):  SAMPLE history:  Signs/sx- develops acute onset of respiratory distress  Allergies- no known drug allergies to date  Meds- Augmentin (inconsistent use)  PMH: Ex-full term, immunizations up-to-date, history of multiple ear infections  FH: multiple food allergies in Father  If asked for review of systems:  No fever. No abdominal pain, vomiting or diarrhea. No cough or swollen lips. Normal appetite, sleeping pattern and energy.  If asked about home environment/social history:  Lives with Mother and Father. Does not attend Day Care. No other caregivers. No known sick contacts. | | |
