| **PATIENT NAME:** Kyle  **PATIENT AGE:** 18 months old  **PATIENT WEIGHT:** 10 kg  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** Status Asthmaticus | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | Your nurse calls you to care for an “ill-appearing” child. The patient is initially fully clothed. The patient is in moderate respiratory distress. Anticipated interventions include primary assessment, calling for help, initiation of asthma medications. Medics will be called. The patient responds to treatment with oxygen, albuterol and available adjunct asthma medications (ipratropium, steroid, epinephrine or terbutaline). Scenario ends when Medics arrive. |

No choking episodes. He has had decreased energy in the setting of a fever, improved with Tylenol. He has been sleeping a bit more than he normally does. Overall, less appetite.  When asked about events leading up to the event (SAMPLE):  SAMPLE history:  Signs/sx- difficulty breathing  Allergies- none  Meds- albuterol PRN  PMH: FT, no complications, immunizations up to date. If asked for review of systems:  Fever, cough, difficulty breathing. No vomiting or diarrhea.  If asked about home environment/social history:  Lives with Mother and Father. Does not attend Day Care.
