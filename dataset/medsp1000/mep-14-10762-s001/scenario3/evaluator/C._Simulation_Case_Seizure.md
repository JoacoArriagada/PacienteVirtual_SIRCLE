**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*The learners enter the room to find a patient with history of fever, vomiting and diarrhea experiencing a first-time generalized tonic-clonic seizure. The team immediately assigns team roles (number of roles will depend on number of participants) including but not limited to: team leader, airway, survey, medication administrator, person who calls 911 and communicates with EMS. Place the patient on SpO2 monitor and expose the patient. If patient is put into a position that compromises the airway, deteriorates from a respiratory perspective. They do a primary survey. The patient is noted to be febrile, tachycardic, not initially in respiratory distress. They apply oxygen 100% and continually re-assess the ABC’s. They give rectal acetaminophen to treat fever. Team successfully identifies that this is a generalized tonic-clonic seizure in the setting of a fever that lasts for > 3 minutes, therefore recognize the need to give a first line antiepileptic drug (AED). They obtain a POC glucose which 120 (only if available in your clinic). They administer benzodiazepine (IN Midazolam or PR Diazepam, whichever is available in your clinic). Seizure breaks after first dose of benzodiazepine, after which patient is post-ictal with a slowed respiratory effort and stridor. The team recognizes upper airway obstruction following the administration of a benzodiazepine medication and performs appropriate airway maneuvers, using adjuct airway supplies (ie: nasal/oral airway, BMV, as available). The medics arrive and the team signs out the patient. This scenario assumes no advanced monitoring or IV and accessory laboratory and imaging capacity at the clinic site.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

***Failure to perform the following critical actions:***

***Crucial:***

1. Call medics (911) immediately upon recognizing decompensating patient.
2. Recognize respiratory distress and seizure.
3. Monitor airway and intervene with airway maneuver.
4. Apply oxygen.
5. Administer benzodiazepine medication (PR, IN) in setting of seizure >3 minutes.

***Second-line:***

1. Place patient on SpO2 monitor.
2. Rapid bedside testing for point-of-care glucose *(if available).*
