Participant Instructions Coma:

You and your colleague are the residents on duty. You don’t know anything about the patient, other than that she has been unresponsive and on a ventilator for the past 2 days. Your task is to take a history from the family member, conduct a complete general and neurological examination of the patient, review her records, determine whether she meets brain death criteria, and counsel the family member.

* Go through the determination of brain death checklist and ensure you have addressed every necessary item on the list.
* Perform a complete cranial nerve examination on the mannequin. However, do not use water for the oculovestibular reflexes (cold calorics), as the mannequin cannot get wet. You should mimic doing it without the water.
* Call a code if necessary and appropriate.

Limitations of this simulation are as follows:

* There is not a real ventilator in the room, but the patient is intubated and connected to respiratory tubing. There is also a ventilator log.
* Deep tendon reflexes are not elicitable on the mannequin.
* If you need to do an apnea test, you must call respiratory to assist you.

**Hand this to the participants playing the role of physicians and ask them to step out of the room.**

**Checklist for Determination of Brain Death**

***Prerequisites (all must be checked)***

□ Irreversible coma with a known cause

□ Brain imaging explains coma

□ Core temperature > 36°C

□ Systolic blood pressure ≥ 100 mm Hg

□ No spontaneous respirations

□ No CNS depressant drug effect

□ No residual paralytics

□ No severe electrolyte, acid-base, or endocrine abnormality

***Examination (all must be checked)***

□ No pupillary responses to bright light

□ No corneal reflexes

□ No oculocephalic reflexes

□ No oculovestibular reflexes

□ No gag reflexes

□ No motor response to noxious stimuli in all 4 limbs (spinally mediated reflexes are permissible)

**Place on clipboard #2**

***Apnea testing (all must be checked)***

□ Patient must be hemodynamically stable

□ Patient must be preoxygenated with 100% FiO2 for > 10 minutes to achieve a PaO2 > 200 mm Hg

□ Patient must be well-oxygenated with a PEEP of 5 cm of water

□ Ventilator must be adjusted to provide PaCo2 34–45 mm Hg

□ Provide oxygen via a suction catheter to the level of the carina at 6 L/min or attach T-piece with CPAP at 10 cm H2O

□ Disconnect the ventilator

□ Spontaneous respirations must be absent. (If patient takes a breath, abort the test.)

□ If patient does not take a breath, draw arterial blood gases at 8–10 minutes and reconnect the patient to the ventilator

□ If the PCO2 ≥ 60 mm Hg or there is a 20 mm Hg rise from normal baseline value, the patient is apneic

□ Apnea test must be aborted if the patient becomes unstable--systolic blood pressure decreases to <90 mm Hg or if oxygen saturation measured by pulse oximetry is <85% for >30 seconds

Ancillary testing (only 1 needs to be performed; to be ordered only if clinical examination cannot be fully performed due to patient factors, or if apnea testing inconclusive or aborted)

□ EEG

□ TCD

□ Cerebral angiogram

Family member script

Your mother is 80-years old. She seemed to be in perfect health. Although she had hypertension, she felt fine. Medications only made her sick, so she stopped taking them. Then she collapsed 2 days ago and was immediately intubated and brought to the hospital. She has been unresponsive and on a ventilator since then.

The doctors told you that she had some type of stroke and there was significant injury to the brain. You can’t remember everything they said to you, it has all happened so fast. The intern on call last night asked for your permission to pull the plug! You are your mother’s health care proxy. However, you can’t fully comprehend everything that is happening. You are angry and distraught and would like some answers.

You should ask to stay in the room and watch the examination. If you hear the ICU monitor alarms going off, ask the physicians what is happening.

**Regarding end-of-life issues, your mother is opposed to organ donation. Please ensure you communicate this to the physicians.**

Ask the doctors the following questions:

1. What happened to her?
2. Is there any chance she can recover?
3. What kind of examination are you doing and what does it mean?
4. Is she brain dead? What does that mean and how can you tell?
5. Why is her heart still beating on the monitor?

**Hand this to the person playing the role of the patient’s child**.

Disruptive physician script

You are the night float intern transferring care over to the interns on the day shift. There is a patient who has been in a coma now for two days. You feel that there is no chance for any recovery. However, you haven’t had the chance to fully assess the patient. The family members have been pestering you with annoying questions all day, every day. They have unrealistic expectations of her walking out of the hospital, saying that God will give them a miracle. Right! You try to convince your colleagues to pull the plug. You want to get rid of that family and get some sleep.

If your colleagues ask for any information, tell them that it is all in the chart or available from the family member.

Once the family member arrives, you can step away from the bed and sit with the observers. If the physicians call for the respiratory therapist, you will be needed to re-direct them. Re-enter the simulation. Inform them that the patient is breathing over the ventilator and is not apneic. She does not need an apnea test.

Summary

The patient is an 80-year old woman with untreated hypertension, who collapsed 2 days ago, and has been unresponsive since that time. She is normothermic, her systolic blood pressure is greater than 100 mm Hg, and she is taking breaths over the ventilator. The following are absent: pupillary responses; corneal reflexes; oculocephalics and oculovestibular reflexes; gag reflex; and motor response to noxious stimuli in all 4 limbs. CT scan reveals a massive left-sided intraparenchymal hemorrhage with extension into the ventricles. Prognosis is extremely poor. Because she is still breathing over the ventilator, she does not meet brain death criteria.
