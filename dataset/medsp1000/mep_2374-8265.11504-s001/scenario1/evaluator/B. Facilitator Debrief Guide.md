### NURSE Framework

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Example | Description |
| Naming | “It sounds like you were really frightened when you got that news.” | State the patient’s emotion |
| Understanding | “I understand how this decision could be very challenging.”  Active listening without interruption.  Follow-up questions to show understanding. | Empathize with/legitimize the emotion |
| Respecting | “The medical team has noted how you are at bedside day and night, caring for your loved one." | Praise the patient for strength |
| Supporting | “It sounds like we have the same primary goal in mind, which is…” | Show support for the patient and their circumstances |
| Exploring | “Is there anything else that is troubling you about this situation?” | Ask the patient to elaborate on the emotion |

## CASE 1

**Learning Objectives:**
In this case students will...

**Communication and Interpersonal Skills**

1. Practice NURSE statements and nonverbal communication strategies to understand and de-escalate a patient’s anger.
2. Avoid inappropriate use of the unqualified phrase “I understand” in patient communication.
3. Formulate a truthful, straightforward response to a patient who requests services that are not available (e.g. a meeting between consultants who are no longer on call).

**Professionalism:**

1. Not discredit members of the team who disagree with each other or with the student’s own clinical assessment.

Student Prompt:

“You are the intern taking care of a patient who presented with septic shock secondary to *C. difficile* colitis who is now post-op day #3 following total colectomy.  The patient’s hemodynamics have markedly improved.  Unfortunately, the patient’s renal function has slowly declined from baseline (sCr 1.8). You consult the nephrologist who declares the patient is in end stage renal disease and recommends tunneled dialysis catheter placement and initiation of hemodialysis during this admission. The family understands and agrees to these plans.

Today, a new nephrologist is on service who recommends holding hemodialysis for now in hopes of renal recovery. The new nephrologist tells the patient and spouse this plan. (In terms of objective data, nothing has changed from yesterday. sCr is still 4.5-4.8, potassium is stable, and the patient has low – but still present – urine output).

You walk in the room to update the patient/family about your (the primary team’s) daily plan of care.”

Debrief/Common Pitfalls:

* Identify strengths by asking those observing the interaction to identify “NURSE” statements that were used.
* Ask: “Did anyone fall for the “I understand” trap? What happened?”
  + Discuss avoiding unqualified “I understand” statements. Despite our good intentions, it is impossible for anyone but the patient to see things perfectly from their perspective. In our own experience, providers sometimes say “I understand” as a tool of active listening without considering its implications for the patient. When each person’s experience of illness is unique, implying we understand how a patient feels (when we can never fully step into their shoes) can sound patronizing and cause further frustration.
  + In contrast, using qualified “I understand” statements to demonstrate thoughtful empathy is in alignment with the “U” in “NURSE.” Examples could include “I understand how this could be a difficult choice for you” or “I understand you to say that you feel overwhelmed by this situation.” These statements provide boundaries for what we do or do not understand and invite the patient to share more about how they are feeling.
* Ask: “Did anyone agree to plan a meeting between the two nephrologists? How did that go?”
  + Discuss the false promise trap. Despite the temptation for a quick fix, making false promises breaks trust in the medical system and further exacerbates patient frustration.
* Ask: “How can you address the family’s concerns without discrediting the consulting services?”
  + Elicit examples from the group. Discuss the importance of maintaining team integrity by respecting differences in clinical decision-making, even when it is uncomfortable to communicate this to patients. Highlight the importance of redirecting with NURSE statements to address underlying concerns and worries. If you have them, give personal examples from your practice.
