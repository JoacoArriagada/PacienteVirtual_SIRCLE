# CASE 4

**Learning Objectives:**

In this case students will...

**Communication and Interpersonal Skills**

1. Practice NURSE statements and nonverbal communication strategies to de-escalate an angry family member.
2. Elicit and address underlying reasons for a family member’s anger.

**Professionalism:**

1. Empathetically communicate the reasons for avoiding unnecessary testing.
2. Avoid making false promises regarding patient care.
3. Clarify their role on the care team in a respectful, professional manner.

**Student Prompt:**

“You are the intern that admitted a 14-year-old boy with acute right lower quadrant pain. He was evaluated with abdominal ultrasound which showed some trace pelvic free fluid.  This patient was staffed by the pediatric surgeon on call; the child’s plan overnight will be to continue IV fluids and serial abdominal exams, calling the surgical team if the patient acutely worsens.  At 6:45 PM, before you check out, you get a call from the bedside nurse that the family would like to discuss the plan of care.”

| THE PATIENT’S STORY: | **Treatment Course**: My son Dean was admitted this morning for abdominal pain and is being monitored in the hospital for appendicitis. Despite this being a potentially life-threatening condition, the surgeons did not seem too worried. A young-looking person in a white coat notified me earlier in the day that the plan was to continue watching Dean overnight and giving him IV fluids, only going to the operating room if things get worse.  This did not sit well with me. I called my cousin, a pediatrician, who said I should demand a CT scan of Dean’s abdomen, STAT. Now I don't trust that the doctors here know what they are doing.  **When** **the student corrects the opening statement and self-identifies as the doctor/intern,** say, *“My cousin has been a successful pediatrician for 14 years, and he says a CT is absolutely required to rule out appendicitis. How long have you been a doctor? You look like you graduated yesterday.”*  [Allow student to respond]  **If student explains why they cannot order the CT scan,** say, *“I don’t know what you’re talking about with all that medical jargon. Can you just call my cousin to explain things?”***If they agree,** pull out a cell phone and pretend to call, then tell the student, *“He didn’t pick up – must be busy because he is such a successful doctor.”* Then resume demanding the CT.  **If the student agrees to get CT scan**, ask, *“When can Dean get his CT? Tonight? Why are you still here, then? Go order it! STAT!”* Give the student an opportunity to backtrack and work out a solution. If they do not, the simulation will conclude here.  **If the student successfully uses one or more of the NURSE statements**, possible responses include:   * *“I am just so worried about Dean. He has always been such a healthy, happy kid, and I am afraid of what will happen if things do get worse, like the surgeons said.”* * *“I should have been paying closer attention to his symptoms, but with work being so busy I am afraid I missed the warning signs. I feel like a bad parent/grandparent.”* |

| PROFESSIONALISM ISSUES OR CHALLENGES | The attending physician overseeing the patient’s care has reasonably concluded a CT scan would not add value to Dean’s care, and it will not happen. If the student falls into the trap of agreeing to get the scan, they should recognize they have made a false promise.  In this simulation students are challenged to assume their upcoming role as intern. They should clarify their role courteously and professionally, then redirect the conversation to your underlying concerns and worries. |
