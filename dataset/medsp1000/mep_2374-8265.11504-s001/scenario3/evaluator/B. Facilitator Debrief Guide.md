### NURSE Framework

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | Example | Description |
| Naming | “It sounds like you were really frightened when you got that news.” | State the patient’s emotion |
| Understanding | “I understand how this decision could be very challenging.”  Active listening without interruption.  Follow-up questions to show understanding. | Empathize with/legitimize the emotion |
| Respecting | “The medical team has noted how you are at bedside day and night, caring for your loved one." | Praise the patient for strength |
| Supporting | “It sounds like we have the same primary goal in mind, which is…” | Show support for the patient and their circumstances |
| Exploring | “Is there anything else that is troubling you about this situation?” | Ask the patient to elaborate on the emotion |

CASE 3

**Learning Objectives:**

In this case students will...

**Communication and Interpersonal Skills**

1. Practice NURSE statements and nonverbal communication strategies to de-escalate a family member’s anger.
2. Recognize their limitations in triaging nighttime cross-cover concerns and manage the resultant challenges that can arise in responding to nonurgent concerns.

**Professionalism:**

1. Maintain appropriate ownership of patient care in a situation where they have limited knowledge of the patient’s treatment course.

**Student Prompt:**

“You are the intern taking cross-cover calls for multiple teams overnight. It is 7 PM. You have been called by three nurses regarding three different patients:

1. The nurse is unable to find a palpable pulse or a “doppler-able” pulse on a patient that underwent surgery earlier in the day.
2. The nurse reports a patient is acutely short of breath with new hypoxia with SpO2 of 82%. The patient is now on 6L nasal cannula supplemental oxygen with SpO2 of 92%.
3. The nurse reports the family of an elderly woman awaiting subacute rehab placement has arrived and would like an update on the plan.

You triage the patients appropriately and see patient #3 last. Brief chart review shows that today is hospital day 17; she was admitted for a hip fracture status post open reduction and internal fixation with what appears to be a complicated postoperative course, but you don’t have time to sift through all the records because the bedside RN pages you again, stating the family is becoming impatient. You arrive to bedside 75 minutes later to meet the family of a patient that you have never met before.”

## Debrief/Common Pitfalls:

* **Ask**: “Were there any ‘pitfalls’ that you fell into that only exacerbated the situation?” Probe about:
  + **References to the day team**
    - It’s true – it is the day team’s responsibility to inform their patients and family members about plans of care. If a patient’s family is unable to come to bedside during the day and wants updates, this would be helpful information for interns to pass along at handoff. The day team might be unaware that this family member has been coming at night for updates.
  + **Triaging/prioritizing other patient concerns**
    - Going to see this family member before seeing patients with acute respiratory failure and pulse loss would have been the wrong choice. However, as students likely saw, pitting these concerns against the family member’s is counterproductive.
    - It is important to learn how to make every patient/family feel like a top priority. You don’t have to apologize for or rationalize why you made the choice to see this family member last. Instead, you should calmly redirect the conversation using NURSE statements.
  + **“Surprises” about patient history**
    - It is impossible for a single night intern to know all the details of every patient’s plan of care. For patients with prolonged hospitalizations, parts of their hospital course might not be disclosed in handoff.
    - The patient is awaiting subacute rehab placement; it can be inferred that her small bowel obstruction is not worsening, otherwise this plan for placement would not be the case. However, it is hard to give the family member more information without learning more about the patient.
    - If you are really stuck and have time to chart review, you can pause and say, “I want to make sure I have as many details as possible for our conversation. Would it be okay with you if I step out for 10 minutes and review your mother’s chart? That way I can provide you with more detailed information.”
* **Ask:** “Does anyone have examples of NURSE statements that helped them redirect the conversation?”
  + In this family member’s case, using a **respecting** statement would be helpful since they view their nighttime visits as a way of caring for their mother. Example: **“Hearing your concerns, I can tell that you are a big advocate for your mother.”**
* **Ask:** “How do you set expectations with a family wanting updates at night?”
  + The night team’s primary responsibility is maintaining patient safety, as demonstrated by nurse calls 1 and 2. However, this scenario does frequently occur despite the day team’s best efforts.
  + It can be helpful to discuss the role of the day and night teams with family members, but only after successfully de-escalating their anger. Attempting to discuss this with an actively angry family member will often make them more upset.
