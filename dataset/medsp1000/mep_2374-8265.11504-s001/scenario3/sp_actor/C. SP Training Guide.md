**Workshop Format:**

As the SP, you will play the role of the family member or patient for each case. You may use your own name, or the one suggested in the scenario. Notably, the particulars of the patient’s medical history and treatment in each case are intentionally sparse; the goal of the session is for students to address your underlying emotions rather than the medical facts of the case. For this reason, the "History of Present Illness” section is omitted.

**Scenario Length:**

Role-play for each case should not exceed 5 minutes.  While most interactions naturally take 4-5 minutes, do not stretch the scenario unnecessarily. If, before time is up, a student so effectively diffused the situation that a reasonable person would be pacified, you may de-escalate your character’s anger and terminate the case early.

**Use of Intense Language:**
SPs should gauge their anger intensity on the students’ emotional reaction. The goal is to cause some discomfort to allow for positive, growth-minded stress – not toxic stress. In doing so, please avoid cursing and flagrant language.
**Student Prompt:**

“You are the intern taking cross-cover calls for multiple teams overnight. It is 7 PM. You have been called by three nurses regarding three different patients:

1. The nurse is unable to find a palpable pulse or a “doppler-able” pulse on a patient that underwent surgery earlier in the day.
2. The nurse reports a patient is acutely short of breath with new hypoxia with SpO2 of 82%. The patient is now on 6L nasal cannula supplemental oxygen with SpO2 of 92%.
3. The nurse reports the family of an elderly woman awaiting subacute rehab placement has arrived and would like an update on the plan.

You triage the patients appropriately and see patient #3 last. Brief chart review shows that today is hospital day 17; she was admitted for a hip fracture status post open reduction and internal fixation with what appears to be a complicated postoperative course, but you don’t have time to sift through all the records because the bedside RN pages you again, stating the family is becoming impatient. You arrive to bedside 75 minutes later to meet the family of a patient that you have never met before.”

| PROFESSIONALISM ISSUES OR CHALLENGES | Students should recognize it is unwise to pit patient needs against one another, though this is a reality of triaging in the hospital. It is important to learn how to make every patient/family feel like a top priority.  **If the student responds at any point with failure to take ownership of the patient’s care** (“This is not part of my job on night team” or frequent referencing of the “primary team”), respond, *“Can you call someone from the primary team, then? What are you doing here?”*  When the student explains that they are not the ones making daily plans for the patient, ask, *“Well what are they paying you for? I can’t believe I waited around to talk to you when you’re this useless.”* |
