# Title

# Thyroid Storm Presenting as Gastroenteritis

# Target Audience

Emergency Medicine or Emergency Medicine/Pediatrics Residents

# Brief Summary

A 31 year-old female with undiagnosed Grave’s Disease progresses to full Thyroid Storm as a result of a viral gastroenteritis.

# Text to be shown on monitor screen at bedside prior to beginning of case:

Ms. Jones, a 31 year-old white female, is brought to the Emergency Department by her roommate for 24 hours of abdominal cramping, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fevers and worsening confusion. She denies any illicit drugs or ingestion of medication. In addition, she has been complaining to her roommate of feeling anxious and “jittery” for several weeks.

(Residents will obtain a full history and physical exam including obtaining or asking the nurse to obtain vital signs at the bedside. The above merely serves as an introduction and should be read by the residents prior to interaction with the patient.)

Scenario Setting: Emergency Department telemetry bed. Acting roommate and nurse are present at the bedside

# Suggest Learning Objectives

Expected Learner Interventions

Recognize thyroid storm, give beta blockers, PTU or methimazole prior to iodide, start glucocorticoids, ICU or Medicine/Endocrine consultation for admission Fluid resuscitate for tachycardia with volume expander (Normal Saline or Lactated Ringer's Solution).

Patient Outcomes with appropriate interventions

Heart rate normalizes with all appropriate interventions. Heart rate may see some improvement but not completely normalize if all interventions are not made, feel free to Ad lib. Improvement of Altered Mental Status within 3 hours (the patient will begin communicating clearly and following commands, improvement of GI symptoms in 24 hours.

Patient Outcomes with inappropriate or lack of interventions

Cardiovascular collapse: the patient will continue to become more tachycardic. Eventually blood pressure will drop and the patient will have cardiovascular failure ending in cardiac arrest and death.

Cues/Prompt desired

 Worsening mental status, rising temperature, circulatory decompensation
