**Simulation Case**

Oral-systemic manifestations of type-2-diabetes

**Case Narrative**:

Scenario Background:

Mr. Babinski is a 70-year-old African American male with chief complaint of syncope, became lightheaded, with difficulty breathing during surgical placement of dental implants. Early in the morning, Mr. Babinski ate a light breakfast at home and took all regular medications, including diabetes medications. He anticipated the procedure would take about 1 hour but it was delayed by 1 hour due to an office emergency then it took 2 hours for the dental surgical procedure as a result of intraoral complications. Mr. Babinski was premedicated with 2 grams of amoxicillin one hour prior to procedure. Four carpules of 2% lidocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine local anesthetic were administered prior to surgical intervention. Mr. Babinski started to feel lightheaded but did not want to bother the doctor and decided he would be okay to wait until the end of the procedure. His mouth was propped open using a surgical retractor and mouth prop and he was asked periodically if he was “okay” but all he could do was say “uh-huh.” One carpule of .5% bupivacaine with 1:200,000 epinephrine was administered upon completion of the surgery to manage postoperative pain.

Additional dental chart findings include: Height 6’1’’, weight 195 pounds, 82 kilograms, body mass index 23.7. Mr. Babinski was instructed to discontinue the use of pradaxa 5 days prior to surgery. The patient has stated that on occasion experiences numbness of the fingers and toes.

Chief complaint: syncope, lightheaded, difficulty breathing

Past Medical History: Type 2 diabetes, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, cardiovascular disease, total right knee replacement 18 months ago, varicose veins on both of his legs.

Past Oral history: Mr. Babinski has history of moderate to advanced periodontal disease. Mandibular molars had to be extracted due to periodontal disease. The remaining dentition were treated with periodontal therapy (scaling and root planing) in combination with antimicrobial therapy and frequent follow-up visits. A mandibular partial removable denture was fabricated for the patient however, patient complained of metallic taste, difficulty chewing and swallowing that compromised food selection. Patient also expressed pain during chewing, and difficulty with eating of certain foods (fruits, vegetables, and meat products), recommended to manage his diabetes and comorbidities.

Titanium implants were surgically placed and restored with crowns to improve chewing efficiency and enable consumption dietary recommendations. Extensive verbal and written oral hygiene instructions to self-manage oral condition were provided as well as frequent follow-up visits were scheduled to assist patient in managing oral-systemic condition. Six months post restoration of implant retained prosthesis patient developed peri-implantitis. After several regenerative therapies and antimicrobial treatment modalities, all posterior implants were removed. During this time, blood-glucose levels were elevated and HgA1C was 8.3 percent. Patient stated he was following his daily-recommended dietary intake as well as prescription medication regimen however; his daily blood sugar readings were elevated and irregular.

Medications and Allergies: Amiodarone 200 mg q.a.m. (every day before noon), pradaxa 150 b.i.d. (twice daily), lisinopril 30mg q. a.m. and triamterene/HCTZ 37.5/25 q.a.m. lantus 15 units at bedtime, novolog 5 units at each meal, metformin 500 mg b.i.d.. Allergic to CODEINE

Family/social history: Mr. Babinski lives with his sister functions independently; he spends most of his day at the casino, senior center, gulfing and occasionally volunteers at church. He has 20 pack-years smoking history with an occasional one standard shot-glass of scotch after work for most of his adult life. Father: died at 87 years of age with Medical History: of CVA (cerebral vascular accident), COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease), and CVD (cardiovascular disease). Mother: alive at age 93. Medical History: type-2-diabetes, alzheimers disease, peripheral neuropathy, and HTN (hypertension).

Scenario conditions initially:

*History patient gives*: “I lost my teeth due to gum disease. I went to the dentist to have a second set of implants the first set fell off. I became light headed, had hard-time breathing, while they drilled the implants into my jaw.”

*Emergency room initial exam*:

* Blood Pressure: 130/80
* Temperature: 98.6° PO
* Heart rate: 70 normal sinus rhythm
* Oxygen saturation: 98% on 2L of O2
* Respiratory rate: 16bpm regular clear sounds
* Blood glucose is 127 mg/dL (normal range 65-99mg/dL)

*Intraoral examination patient presents*:

* Lips: erythematous cracks/fissures, the corner of his mouth with evidence bleeding/ulcerations and crusted with dry blood/saliva residue.
* Teeth: the patient has multiple crowns with no visible evidence of dental decay or broken teeth/roots.
* Tongue: dry, coated with heavy debris on the dorsum surface, filliform papillae elongated, ventral surface lingual varicosities.
* Oral mucosa/periodontium: generalized red swollen evidence of bleeding when touched with cotton swab, moderate to advanced periodontal disease.
* Dental implants on the lower right posterior region of the oral cavity with healing abutment secured into place.
* Oral hygiene/cleanliness: food particles and calculus build-up especially on the lingual side of the teeth
* Dentures: none
* Dental pain: moderate pain only when chewing

Mr. Babinski is monitored and responding positively to initial management of acute symptoms.
