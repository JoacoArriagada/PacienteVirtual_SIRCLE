**Appendix D: ISPE Facilitation and Debriefing Guide**

Welcome and orientation:

* Faculty welcomes students and introduces themselves; all students are equally vulnerable, and no one has prepared in advance for this experience. We are here to engage with each other in a non-threatening/non-judgmental environment to gain an appreciation for what each area of expertise contributes to the plan of care.
* Students introduce themselves and identify which profession they represent.
* Overview of the environment: Each student will be observed by peers so you can gain additional information from each profession. Each student has a limited amount of time to perform a portion of the subjective exam for a patient. The facilitator will be the timekeeper.
* This experience is subjective data collection/ interviewing only. No physical assessment is to be done except for a general survey.
* This is an ambulatory care environment that mimics the clinic setting (not acute care).
* Students are informed that they will complete a survey at the end of the session.

Introduce the case:

The facilitator reads the case introduction from the case scenario as a brief introduction to the case. No other information is provided to the students about the case. Students are assigned an order in which to perform the subjective interview in advance. A suggested interview order with 5 minutes per student is nursing, medicine, pharmacy, physical therapy, occupational therapy, and social work. Interview time per student may increase if fewer professions are present to spend thirty minutes total with the interview. If two students are present from the same profession, have them split the interview time for their profession but conduct the interview separately.

Conduct the Subjective Interview:

The faculty facilitator keeps time and reminds students when their interview time is starting and ending. Students are encouraged to focus on what they believe to be the most critical questions for their profession. They are encouraged not to repeat questions but may wish to obtain deeper details about a question asked by a previous student. Students can acknowledge that they learned information from other students.
