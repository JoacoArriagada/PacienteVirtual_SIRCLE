**Answer Key and Teaching Points for Module III**

**Question 1**

“Nora”

* Poor PO intake for 2 days
* Vomiting whatever she eats for the last day
* Drinks some sips of water

**Teaching point**

*Correct answer:* “Nora”: Expected blood glucose to be normal/low AND ketosis present.

The reduced oral intake due to anorexia, nausea, or vomiting can deplete glycogen stores, leading to hypoglycemia and/or starvation ketosis without hyperglycemia.

**Question 2.a**

Results:

* BG: 65
* Ketones: Mild/Moderate
* pH: 7.4

**Teaching point**

*Correct answer:* YES, give basal insulin now, and use the same dose.

Basal insulin is required even in a fasting state.

**Question 2.b**

You are planning for the PO challenge (PB&J sandwich = 15gm of carbs).

**Teaching point**

*Correct answer:* “Nora”: No rapid-acting insulin now, because she has hypoglycemia.

Treatment options for hypoglycemia in patients with T1D are:

* Oral rapid-acting carbohydrates (preferred for conscious individuals)
* IV dextrose (D10% – 2.5 ml/kg)
* IV/IM glucagon

**Question 2.c**

**Teaching point**

*Correct answer:* “Nora”: repeat blood glucose in 15 min.

Treat with 15gm of carbs and recheck in 15 min. Repeat cycle until BG > 70.

**Question 3**

**Teaching point**

*Correct answer:* “Nora”: Discharge home with appropriate sick day management counseling.

Principles of sick day management in patients with T1D:

1. Frequent BG and ketone check every 3-4 hrs.
2. Do NOT stop basal insulin.
3. Monitor and maintain hydration.
4. Treat the underlying cause of acute illness.
