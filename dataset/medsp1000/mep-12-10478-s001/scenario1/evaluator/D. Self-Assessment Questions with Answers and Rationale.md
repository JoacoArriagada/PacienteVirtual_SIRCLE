1. What is the difference between AST and ALT?
   1. AST detects damage to all organs except the liver, while ALT is liver specific.
   2. Although, both AST and ALT are indicators of liver damage, ALT is more specific for the liver. \*
   3. AST detects primarily damage to the spleen, while ALT detects damage primarily to the liver.
   4. If the AST is high, then the patient is experiencing some form of damage to the liver and should be treated immediately.
   5. Elevated AST alone can tell you whether the liver is functioning well or not making ALT not as important of a test.

Rationale: AST gives you information not only about the liver, but also the heart and muscles. Therefore, damage to any of these can elevate the AST. ALT is more specific in terms of the liver.

1. A patient presents to the clinic with an enlarged splenic tip upon physical examination. Is an enlarged splenic tip a normal finding in an adult? What is the medical term for enlarged spleen?
   1. Yes, being able to feel the spleen is a normal finding. Splenohypertrophy.
   2. No, but it is probably acute and will go away by the next office visit. Splenohypertrophy.
   3. It may or may not be pathological. Splenomegaly.
   4. No, further investigation should be done to try and determine the cause. Splenomegaly. \*
   5. Yes, some people have bigger spleens than others. Splenomegaly.

Rationale: An enlarged spleen is not a normal finding (it can sometimes be palpated in very slender individuals and therefore, it is important to know the size of a normal spleen) and is most often pathological. Further investigation would be needed to determine the cause. Splenomegaly is the correct term.

1. Which of the following best explains the importance of a family history when trying to determine a differential diagnosis?
   1. Family history is important so that you know what types of illnesses that your patient will develop later on in life.
   2. Family history is important so that you can counsel your patient on possible illnesses/diseases that their children could develop.
   3. Family history is important because it can guide you to an accurate diagnosis based off of what is most probable. \*
   4. Family history is important only if your patient runs the risk of genetic disorders.

Rationale: So this question states, “best explains” (so pick the best option) and then asks for the answer in terms of the differential diagnosis specifically. Therefore answer choice C portrays these two things the best. Family history is not just for genetic disorders, having a past family member with Type II diabetes increases your risk for developing diabetes for example. Although B is a way in which the family history is used, it states nothing about its use in developing a differential diagnosis. A is wrong because its states “illnesses that your patient will develop”. Just because there is a family history of a particular disease doesn’t mean they will get the disease. If you have this mentally with the family history you could miss vital information presented elsewhere. Broad initially is often times better.

1. Which of the following best describes the chief complaint?
   1. The chief complaint is what you think the patient is most worried about when they come to the clinic.
   2. The chief complaint is what the patient believes to be wrong with them.
   3. The chief complaint is contained within the history of present illness.
   4. The chief complaint is in the patient’s own words and describes the main purpose or symptom for what brought them to the clinic. \*

Rationale: The chief complaint should always be in the patient’s words and is a separate entity from the history of present illness. The statement should be able to stand alone and give you a basic idea of what the patient considers to be the most important complaint, usually what caused them to come to you.

1. Which of the following is true of BMI based off of your research.
   1. BMI does not take into consideration muscle versus fat mass. \*
   2. BMI is universally used and is the same regardless of gender or race.
   3. A BMI above 20 is considered to be obese
   4. BMI is not very helpful and does not help in determining patient’s potential risk for developing disease.

Rationale: One of the drawbacks of BMI is that it just does weight and so someone who has a high muscle mass may have a BMI over 25 categorizing them as obese on paper. This is a problem because a body builder for example would be anything, but obese. Therefore, it is important to understand the limitations of BMI. BMI does not vary based on gender, but does vary based on race. Caucasians typically have higher BMI’s than patients of Asian descent (the ranges for normal are adjusted based off of this fact also).
