3.1 Scenario Title: Antidote Dosing Error in a Pediatric Acetaminophen Overdose Patient

A. An 18 month old child reportedly ingesting a toxic amount of acetaminophen is brought in to a rural hospital ED setting. The initial treating physician decides to intravenously administer N-acetylcysteine (NAC) as the child refuses to take oral NAC and vigorously resists a nasogastric tube, and IV NAC is ordered. The physician calculates and writes the dose incorrectly, such that a massive NAC overdose will result if not picked up and corrected. If the error is picked up by participants, the facilitating nurse will be resistant to correction because she knows the initial ordering physician and trusts her experience. Additional evidence-based support such as Web references, drug package inserts, or pharmacist review will be required to change the medication order. If the error is not picked up early on, the child will receive a first dose that is 4-fold the intended dose, after which the pharmacist will identify the error and report it to the treating team. No adverse outcome will result from the slip / error. Error disclosure to the family will then need to be addressed. Optional: unidentified tricyclic antidepressant overdose can complicate the case with arrhythmias, seizures, and hemodynamic instability.

time 0

The attending ED physician, Dr. Finlay, briefly assesses the child and finds him to be cooperative and playful, with no acute distress. She is called away to an emergent patient and requests the ED staff and treating nurses to start routine medical therapy.

5 minutes

Dr. Finlay returns after calling poison control and speaking with a toxicology specialist. Activated charcoal mixed with chocolate milk is offered, which the child drinks happily, and oral N-acetylcysteine is offered, which the child vigorously refuses to take. Dr. Finlay then orders intravenous N-acetylcysteine in the following regimen; the written dose is for diluent and at least an order of magnitude greater than the intended N-acetylcysteine dose:

First infusion: 3mL/kg (39mL) of Mucomyst 20% to make 200mL of dextrose 5% solution, administered over 40 minutes
Second infusion: 10mL/kg (130mL) of Mucomyst 20% to make 500mL of dextrose 5% solution, administered over 4 hours
Third infusion: 20mL/kg (260mL) of Mucomyst 20% to make 1000mL of dextrose 5% solution, administered over 16 hours

Dr. Finlay is called away to an emergent patient resuscitation and will be unavailable for the rest of the case. The facilitator nurse has the improperly prepared infusion ready and starts it immediately as ordered unless the error is caught in time.

If participants detect the error early:
- stop the NAC infusion
- keep vitals and exam unchanged
- keep poison-control consultation available
- nurse resists the change until participants provide evidence-based support

10 minutes

If the dosage error is not picked up, the first dose has been started using an IV infusion with 0.2 micron millipore filter. The hospital pharmacist calls at this point to note the significantly high weight-based NAC dose being requested for an 18 month old child.

If the error is detected after infusion has started:
- stop the NAC infusion
- keep vitals and exam unchanged
- continue poison-control consultation
- monitor for complications such as hyponatremia and seizures

15 minutes

The first Tylenol level from about 1 hour after ingestion is reported at 55 mg/L; liver function tests, coagulation studies, and all other labs including aspirin / acetaminophen levels are normal.

Optional branch:
- unidentified concurrent overdose of the mother's tricyclic antidepressants may present with rapidly progressive seizure activity, ventricular arrhythmias, and hemodynamic instability
- if used, provide seizing then post-ictal patient, progressive tachycardia with myocardial irritability / arrhythmias, hypotension, hypoxia, unresponsiveness, and pupils dilated to 6mm

B. Scenario Distracters – None

C. Trends Needed – none for acetaminophen ingestion; optional tricyclic antidepressant toxicity branch requires hemodynamic deterioration with ventricular arrhythmias and respiratory compromise
