1.1 Scenario Title: Iatrogenic patient deterioration in CT scan (IV contrast anaphylactoid reaction)

A. IV contrast anaphylactoid reaction in an outpatient radiology patient with prior unrevealed contrast reaction who is given IV contrast for an urgent abdominopelvic CT scan. Patient is groggy, so history of prior contrast reaction is available only on questioning husband after reaction starts. Proper patient assessment, treatment, and resource utilization stabilizes patient’s anaphylactoid reaction and contrast aspiration events. Inadequate treatment results in cardiac arrest and rescue by a simulated resuscitation team. Patient care needs to be transferred to, then from, radiology staff.

i. Patient name / DOB / Sex: Bethany Akers 11/10/1953 female ii. Mode of arrival: private vehicle iii. Accompanied by: husband in waiting area iv. ED medical forms: see <<Appendix D>> Prior medical records: n/a
v. Chief complaint / History of present illness: Primary care physician Dr. Baker is requesting an oral and intravenous contrast-enhanced abdominopelvic CT scan. The patient has a history of recurrent abdominal pains over past two months, worse for two days, along with nausea, vomiting, fevers, and some diarrhea. Oral contrast has been administered as per protocol. The patient has no active complaints on arrival in suite, but is groggy from leftover hydrocodone-containing analgesics (Vicodin) she had taken prior to arrival for pain. Her husband has stepped outside.
vi. Past medical history: diverticulosis hypertension
Past surgical history: appendectomy breast biopsy vii. Medications: lisinopril hydrocodone / acetaminophen
Medication allergies: none known; patient does not offer history of "minor intravenous dye allergy" due to grogginess, and if participants discover it through the husband it should be minimized as "GI upset without serious reaction"
viii. Social history: ex-smoker. no alcohol or drugs. lives with husband, two grown children Family history: non-contributory

ix. Physical examination: groggy but appropriate; one word answers only

pre-scenario

Patient placed on CT scan gantry.

time 0

Scan is initiated with routine protocol, and IV contrast is administered.

2 minutes

After injection of contrast, patient becomes nauseous and starts actively vomiting. CT scan should be interrupted at this point if not already completed.

3 minutes

Vomiting is slightly better, but patient starts complaining of itching in throat and hands, with funny feeling in back of throat. If participants contact family, the husband can provide the prior contrast-reaction history.

5 minutes

Patient develops active throat-closing sensation, wheezing, and stridor.

Updated assessment:
- anxious patient
- generalized itching
- stridor
- wheezing
- tachycardia
- heart rate: 128 / minute
- blood pressure: 116 / 70 mmHg
- respirations: 25 / minute
- oxygen saturation: 90% on room air or with oxygen
- temperature: 99.6 degrees F / 37.6 degrees C

10 minutes

If inadequately treated, the patient progresses to florid anaphylactoid reaction with hemodynamic compromise:
- unresponsiveness
- warm and flushed skin
- worse stridor and wheezing
- heart rate: 145 / minute
- blood pressure: 72 / 40 mmHg
- respirations: 6 / minute
- oxygen saturation: 77%

20 minutes

If adequately treated with airway support, vascular access, appropriate medications, and fluids:
- hemodynamic stabilization becomes adequate for transfer of care
- persistent mild hypoxia around 92% remains due to contrast aspiration
- chest exam can show right posterior rales with generalized wheezing

If inadequately treated:
- cardiopulmonary collapse progresses to ventricular fibrillation and cardiac arrest requiring routine ACLS to be taken over by 911 paramedics
- heart rate: 0 / minute
- blood pressure: no pulses
- respirations: assisted ventilations
- oxygen saturation: no tracing
