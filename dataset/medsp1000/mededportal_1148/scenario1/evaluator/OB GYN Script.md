## <u>**Educational Objectives**</u><u>:</u>

Upon completion of the activity students will be better able to:

- Acknowledge the importance of understanding, accepting, and appreciating one’s cultural diversity and understand how cultural beliefs, perspectives, and practices may enable or hinder one’s ability to have equitable access to health care services.
- Provide health care to a diverse, multicultural patient population and recognize the impact of social, cultural and spiritual beliefs and practices upon the physician and patient relationship and management of disease. Specifically, provide care for limited English proficient (LEP) women in labor.
- Articulate the complex realities of using both professional and *ad hoc* interpreters.
- Discuss the impact of a being a LEP patient on the human birth process in particular.
- Describe disparities in health care experienced by women in labor who are LEP.

**AAMC Tool for Assessing Cultural Competence Training: Domain V**

<u>**Conceptual Background:**</u>  

This activity will allow students to build on the cultural awareness, knowledge and skill that they acquired in their preclinical curriculum. The students will have the opportunity to become culturally competent in an environment where they have the ability to acknowledge their attitudes, utilize their knowledge and refine their skills. Implementation of this activity in a clinical setting is a deliberate and conscious decision that allows this assignment to be contextually relevant in terms of actual patient care.

Providing healthcare to patients who do not speak English has become a challenge nationwide.  Federal (and many state) laws and accreditation agencies now require healthcare providers to provide interpretation services for patients who are unable to speak English.  The community issues of providing medical care while using an interpreter and the associated health care disparities will be explored with the students through the Interpreters in Labor and Delivery Activity. The activity will allow the student to identify issues affecting the health care of an LEP patient and discover how the process of medical interpretation impacts the healthcare of a woman in labor. Small group case-based discussion, reflective writings, and an OSCE will be the assessment methods for this activity.

<u>**Resources:**</u>

- OSCE station:  “Fetal Death and Interpretation”

<u>**Evaluation:**</u>

- Individual written feedback from faculty member on written reflection.
- End-of-clerkship OSCE (A) station utilizing an *ad hoc* interpreter to communicate an unexpected fetal demise in the prenatal clinic (see resources)\*.

\*Authors recommend stillbirth training to prepare students for the counseling of families experiencing a stillbirth be presented prior to utilization of the OSCE (A).  Training in the breaking of ‘bad news’ greatly enhances students’ ability to successfully complete this learning experience.
