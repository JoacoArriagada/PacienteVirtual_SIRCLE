**STANDARDIZED PATIENT CASE:** ***Jane Henderson***

**Patient Name:** Jane Henderson (female patient)

**Presenting Problem:** 

- **Patient is transferring care from her previous primary care provider due to a recent change in insurance.  She is here to meet her new provider for the first time.**  

**Actual Diagnoses:** 

High blood pressure, poorly controlled

Elevated cholesterol

Coronary artery disease, h/o stent placement 2 years ago

Arthritis

**Patient Demographics:**

	**Age:**	60 yr old

	**Sex:**	Female

	**Ethnicity:** open

	**Height:**	Average

	**Weight:**	Average to overweight

**Overview of Case**

Jane Henderson is a 60-year old retired elementary school teacher who comes in to meet her new primary care doctor.  She previously received her healthcare at Kaiser, but has not seen a doctor in the past year because of stressful social circumstances.  She comes in today at the urging of her family.  The goal of the visit from the doctor’s perspective is to become acquainted with Mrs. Henderson and to acquire information about her medical conditions, all of which are chronic, and to inquire about her social situation and its impact upon her health.  Mrs. Henderson’s agenda is that she needs refills of all her medications as she has been out for the last 3 months.

During the interview the students will be expected to:

1) Open the interview (greet the patient, make introductions, inquire into patient’s purpose for the visit)

2)  Build a relationship and establish rapport

3)  Engage in open-ended inquiry, listening, summarizing

4) Get information about the following components of her medical history

History of Present Illness

Past Medical History

Medications

Allergies

Family History

Social History

Health Related Behaviors

5) Elicit the patient’s understanding of her medical illness and how her illness has affected her function on a daily basis.

6) Inquire about psychosocial stressors and their impact on her ability to manage her illness.

7)  Inquire about the impact of the patient’s illness on her family and their role in helping to manage her illness.

This is a four-part encounter.  The patient will return to clinic in two, seven, and ten months for follow-up visits.

**PRESENTING SITUATION**

**Initial Interview**

**Mrs. Henderson**

You are working in the Family Practice clinic and Mrs. Henderson comes in for her initial visit.  She is transferring care from her previous primary care provider due to a recent change in her insurance plan.  She will begin seeing your preceptor as a primary care provider and he has asked you to perform a history on the patient.

***Mrs. Henderson***

#### 2.1.1.1. **Initial Interview**

**Your Profile**

You are Mrs. Henderson, a 60-year-old retired schoolteacher who previously got her care at Kaiser in San Francisco, but has not been seen in over a year due to significant psychosocial stressors. You are originally from Nebraska, but moved to the Bay Area after getting married in 1972.  You and your husband have been here since that time and raised your three children here.

You currently live with your husband, George 63 (a retired plumber), and your grandchildren, Tiffany (age 7) and Katie (age 5) in the Sunset District in SF.  You and your husband have had custody of your grandchildren for the past year, after your youngest daughter, Kathy, was sentenced to prison on a drug-related charge.

You were reasonably happy with your healthcare at Kaiser, but due to insurance issues, are now changing healthcare plans.  It was less expensive for you to switch to another managed care plan.  You have been somewhat overwhelmed by all your responsibilities and have ignored your own healthcare needs.  You feel guilty about the fact that you haven’t been following all your previous doctor’s recommendations.  You realize the importance of your health, but feel you cannot put your own needs ahead of your family’s needs.

**Presentation/Emotional Tone**

You have felt guilty about not being more on top of your healthcare and are somewhat embarrassed because you know your health is important and understand the seriousness of your medical condition.  You are pleasant and cooperative during the interview.  You are a stoic individual who does not complain.  You have been the pillar of support for your family and continue in this role.  You realize that you need to make some lifestyle changes, but are not sure how you will be able to make the changes given the number of responsibilities that you have taken on.

**INTERVIEW CONTENT:**

**Background – Mrs. Henderson**

You previously received your healthcare at Kaiser SF, but have not seen a doctor in the past year because of stressful social circumstances.  You have been extremely busy caring for your grandchildren and your husband hasn’t been in the best of health.  You come in today at the urging of your family and are seen by a first-year medical student in the family medicine clinic.  You are looking forward to meeting your new doctor and you need refills of all your medications today as you have been out for the last 3 months.

**Medical History – Mrs. Henderson**

History of Present Illness:

You don’t have any specific complaints today.  If asked what brings you in to the clinic you reply:

*I’m here to get a new doctor because I’ve been out of my medications for the past 3 months and my old doctor said that he couldn’t refill my prescriptions anymore because I hadn’t seen him in a year.   My family has been on me to get in and I checked my blood pressure last week at Safeway and it was 170/100.  I know that’s too high.* 

If asked how you have been feeling lately:

*I feel fine.  Just really busy…I haven’t had a chance to keep up with my health like I know I should.*  

You realize the seriousness of your medical problems, but have felt overwhelmed and stressed out.  You are fairly knowledgeable about your conditions, but there are misconceptions and gaps in your knowledge.  You don’t know the specific goals for your blood pressure or cholesterol control, but you know that 170/100 is too high.

**Past Medical History – Mrs. Henderson**

When asked about your medical problems:

*I have problems with my heart and blood pressure.  I had a stent placed in my heart 2 years ago at Kaiser to fix a blockage. Before I had the stent put in, I was having chest pain every time I walked up the stairs or carried groceries.  I haven’t had any pain since they put the stent in.*

If asked about your high blood pressure:

*I’ve had high blood pressure almost all of my life.  At first, I was only on one medicine, but now I have to take three pills.  It seems like it just keeps getting worse*

If asked about any other problems:

*Well, I have bad arthritis in my knees that makes it hard for me to walk more than a couple of blocks.  Oh, and my cholesterol is high.  That’s why they think I may have had the heart trouble.  I take a pill for that too.*

Students may ask about other symptoms related to your heart/high blood pressure:

- *You haven’t had any shortness of breath*
- *You haven’t felt dizzy or palpitations (heart racing)*
- *You don’t have any chest pain, jaw pain or arm pain*
- *You never actually had a heart attack*
- *You can’t walk too far because of your arthritis*
- *No recent headaches or blurry vision*

If asked how your medical problems have affected your life on a day-to-day basis:

*Well, I guess the biggest thing is that they make me worry more about the future. I try not to think about it too much, but I wonder if I’ll still be around in 10 years and who will be around for my grandchildren if I’m not.  Also, I can’t get around as well as I used to because of my arthritis, especially on cold days.  I can’t walk more than 3-4 blocks because of the pain in my knees.*

Students may ask what you think is causing your health problems (high blood pressure or heart disease)

*I don’t know…maybe all the stress in my life is making my blood pressure so high. Maybe if I could be less stressed, I wouldn’t need to take all these medicines. Do you think that’s possible?*

**Past Surgical History:**

*The only surgery that I have had was with my 3 children. I had c-sections*.

**Past Hospitalizations:**

*I was hospitalized overnight when they put the stent in my heart and also for the birth of my children.*

**Allergies:**

*Only to cats.*

**Medications:**

*I don’t know the names of all my medicines, but I carry this list around with me.*

**Lipitor 40 mg daily (cholesterol medicine)**

**Metoprolol 50 mg twice daily (blood pressure medicine)**

**Hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily (blood pressure medicine)**

**Lotensin 20 mg daily (blood pressure medicine)**

**Aspirin (to protect heart)**

*I haven’t taken any of my medicines except for the aspirin since I ran out a few months ago.  I’m also worried about whether the cholesterol medicine might cause liver damage.  One of my friends said she stopped taking her cholesterol medicine because she heard on the news that it caused liver problems.  I don’t need any more problems than I already have.*

*I also take an occasional Tylenol for my arthritis, but I don’t want to take too many because I also heard they can be hard on the liver. At most I take 1 or 2 a day.*

Any herbs, vitamins or complementary medicine?

*I try to take calcium tablets when I remember it because I know they are good for my bones and I don’t drink much milk.*

**Family History**

*I have 2 older brothers and 1 younger sister.  (Joe -63, Bobby 65 & Joan 48 –Both my brothers have high blood pressure and high cholesterol).  (Joe) had a triple bypass last year at age 62.  My younger sister, Joan (age 48) was diagnosed with diabetes a few months ago.  My father died of a heart attack when he was in his early 50’s.  My mother died of a stroke 3 years ago. (she was 83) She had diabetes too.*

**Social History** 

Are you married?

*Yes, for 35 years.  My husband, George, wanted to come in with me today, but he isn’t in such great health and wasn’t feeling that well.  He has emphysema from all those years of smoking and he just can’t get around like he used to. .  He gets winded pretty easily especially walking up hills or stairs, so he’s definitely less active than he used to be.  His medications have helped and he’s pretty good about taking them and if he forgets, I remind him.  He’s uses 3 different inhalers that he takes several times a day.  Fortunately he stopped smoking 10 years ago.*

Do you work or are you retired?

*I’m retired.  I taught elementary school for many years, but decided to retire when George started to have his health problems and when my grandkids were little and my daughter needed my help to take care of them..  Then I got custody of my grandchildren, so it’s a good thing I retired when I did.  I didn’t really picture myself raising another family at my age, but that’s the way things go, I guess.*

Who do you live with?

*My husband, George and my grandchildren, Tiffany (age 7) and Katie (age 5).  We got custody of them a year ago when my daughter ended up in prison.*  

How do you manage financially?

*Both George and I have pension plans.  (George work for a large national plumbing outfit with benefits) We live in the same house we’ve lived in for the past 30 years and thank goodness it is paid off. (They live in the Sunset District in SF).  But, raising two grandchildren off the little amount we get each month is tough.  We’d like to do more for Tiffany and Katie – take them places, buy them new clothes, give them dance lessons – but it isn’t really possible.*

Kathy (Mrs. Henderson’s daughter, age 28) was sentenced to prison on a drug-related charge. Kathy was always troubled and rebellious.  She got her ears pierced by friends at age 11, and got her first tattoo at age 16.  She was probably sexually active around then, unbeknownst to her parents. Like her father, she never went to college and worked at Macy's as a salesperson.  She met Jim, an older divorced manager, while at Macy's.  She became pregnant by him at age 20; though she is quite reactionary against the Catholic Church, she didn't believe in abortion and had the baby.  When Jim realized she was carrying the baby to term, he dumped her.  As a single mom, Kathy depended on her parents to care for baby Tiffany during summers Jane was home and on weekends.  Kathy often escaped with her friends from work and began doing marijuana with them, and then cocaine.  One night during an especially raucous party, she became pregnant again; for the sake of the second baby, she used all her willpower to stay off of drugs, and became clean for about 3 years.  During this time her father, who dotes on his granddaughters, retired, and it became easier for Kathy to drop the kids off at mom and dad's while she worked.  Unfortunately she began partying again after work.  About a year ago, she was caught in a sting operation selling a small amount of MJ to an undercover cop, pleaded guilty, and is in the Chowchilla women's prison for two years.  The girls visit Kathy about twice a month - though it is hard on the girls, they feel extremely comfortable with George and Jane.

Mrs. Henderson feels both angry and saddened when she thinks of her daughter in prison, but tries not to dwell on it that much because she feels that she must focus on the day-to-day responsibilities of raising her grandchildren.  Although Jane didn’t think she would be raising another family at the age of 60, she feels that it is her responsibility and is very stoic about it.  She has 2 other children, Bob (age 33) and Michelle (age 31), both of whom live out of state (one in Virginia and the other in Florida).  Bob is a real estate agent.  He is married and has 2 young children (Michael age 4 and Peter age 2).  Michelle is an elementary school teacher and is single.

Mrs. Henderson’s husband, George, is a retired plumber.  He has a number of chronic health issues including emphysema and high blood pressure. He smoked for many years, but stopped 10 years ago when the doctors first told him about his emphysema. He is pretty consistent about taking his medications (3 different inhalers), but occasionally Mrs. Henderson has to remind him.  He is not on oxygen therapy and gets around okay, though he does get winded when climbing hills or stairs so has to walk more slowly.  Mrs. Henderson and her husband have a good marriage, but the situation with her daughter and grandchildren has been an ongoing source of stress in their marriage.  George loves his granddaughters, but their place is small and the girls can be pretty rambunctious. George has always been a relatively reserved person, not prone to sharing his feelings openly, even with his family.  When he is angry or frustrated, he tends to become withdrawn.  Although Mrs. Henderson feels that George is supportive of her in general, because he is emotionally unavailable this has sometimes caused tension in their relationship.  In addition they have always had fairly traditional gender roles. Mrs. Henderson has always done the cooking, cleaning and most of the childcare.  This is true now as it was when her children were growing up. When her kids were little, Mrs. Henderson did not work, but stayed home with them.  As they got older (once they were in high school) she went back to work as a teacher. Although George will occasionally watch the kids while Mrs. Henderson goes on an errand, Mrs. Henderson does most of the cooking, cleaning and childcare. Mrs. Henderson feels that George has been somewhat depressed since his retirement.  Work and his family were always at the center of his life and since retiring he seems to have lost his purpose.  His chronic health issues have not helped either. He has always been somewhat of a loner and never seemed to need or want to have a lot of friends. George spends most of his time at home, puttering around the house or watching television. Mrs. Henderson worries about him and feels that in addition to caring for the children that she must take care of him as well.

What are your main sources of emotional support?

*My family is very important to me and I am very close to 2 of my children, Bob and Michelle.  Even though they aren’t close by, they call me every week.  I try not to burden them with all my problems, though.  I also have a lot of faith in God and this is a source of strength for me.  I go to mass every Sunday.  I used to be more involved in the church, but lately I’ve been so busy, I don’t have the time.  Seems like I’ve lost touch with most of my old friends.  I’m not sure that they could really understand my situation and I don’t really want to burden them with my problems.*

Mrs. Henderson’s main supports have been her family and her church.  Although two of her children are out of state, they contact her at least weekly by phone.  She is a devout Catholic and attends mass every week.  Previously, she was very involved in a number of church groups and had many friends. She feels she can't truly talk to her friends about what is going on at home - too embarrassing, too private, and not anyone else's business.  All her friends have highly successful kids (it seems that way, anyhow).  One was even a Rhodes Scholar a while back.  Jane is proud of her own education – went to Creighton, a Catholic university in Omaha (btw, their mascot is the Blue Jay), where she majored in education.  She taught 3rd grade in the Omaha school district for 4 years, met George at her church in Omaha, got married, and then agreed to move with him to San Francisco to get away from winters.

After one miscarriage early on, they settled down to have three kids.  Her kids do fine for themselves, but it's too much for her to talk about Kathy.  She has a very good friend, Lorraine, to whom she has whispered in the highest secrecy that Kathy has moved away to get her life in order, but the imprisonment is too much for even Jane to reveal.  Because of the children, Jane has been less and less able to go to church - though she depends on her church for social support, and though no one really asks her any questions, she's starting to feel the judgments of the congregation as she struggles with her granddaughters. Due to increasing responsibilities over the past year, she hasn’t had time to participate in any outside church activities.  Although she will occasionally have coffee or lunch with one of her friends, she really doesn’t feel that she has the time for socializing and feels they can’t really understand her situation.  She does sometimes feel down, but she tries to have a positive attitude and typically these episodes are short-lived.
