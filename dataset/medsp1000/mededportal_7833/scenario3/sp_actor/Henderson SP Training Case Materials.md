***Patient Name:*** *Jane Henderson (female patient)* 

# 16. **Presenting Problem:** 

Patient is returning for a regularly scheduled follow-up visit with her primary care provider, Dr. Johnson, who she has now met twice.  Her first visit was on November 13/15th to establish care and her follow-up visit was on January 15/17th.  During her last visit, she was having increasing headaches that she attributed to stress.  She was feeling the strain of caring for her grandchildren and felt that the stress of this might be causing the headaches.  Although previously supportive, her husband had become more withdrawn, leaving her with much of the responsibility for raising her grandchildren.  Prior to her second visit with Dr. Johnson, Mrs. Henderson did not get her blood tests drawn and was also having difficulty remembering to take her medications.  At the end of the last visit, she was more motivated to take her blood pressure medications and she had made a plan with her doctor to get her blood work done.

In early April, she experienced an episode of chest pain, SOB and palpitations.  Her husband called 911 and was rushed to the ED.  In the Emergency Department, she had a full evaluation, including labs, an electrocardiogram and a chest x-ray.  She was reassured by the doctors in the ED; they did not think the episode of chest pain was her heart but thought she might have had a panic attack.  She had been under a lot of stress and recalled that she had similar symptoms in her 20s when she was diagnosed with panic attacks.  On discharge from the emergency room, she was given an appointment for a treadmill test and the cardiologist. She had the stress test and saw the cardiologist, Dr. McNulty, several weeks ago. He reassured her that her stress test was fine and that he felt this was not her heart.  Although she feels better after hearing the news about her heart, she occasionally still feels anxious about her future, and worried about future panic attacks. The episode did motivate her to try to take better care of herself, specifically trying to watch her diet and increase her exercise.  She has also been very consistent about taking her medications on a daily basis.

# 18. **Overview of Case**

Jane Henderson is returning to a regularly scheduled follow-up visit with her primary care provider, Dr. Johnson

Since her ED visit, she has been consistent about taking her medications regularly.   She has realized how much her social stressors have contributed to her headaches and her recent panic attack.  She has not had any further episodes of chest pain or panic attacks, but does worry that she may get another panic attack. The episode of chest pain did motivate her to try to prioritize her own needs and take better care of her health.  Her husband has been acting differently since the ED visit and seems to be a little more supportive about her medical needs.  He still doesn’t help as much with the grandchildren as she would like, but he has been more communicative and open to discussing some of the stressors that she is under.

She did go get her blood tests done and is a little anxious about her results.  In addition, she had her exercise stress test and saw the cardiologist several weeks before this visit (after the ED visit), and she was told that everything was normal.  She had a little knee pain during the treadmill test, but no chest pain, shortness of breath, or palpitations.

She has recently found out that her daughter may be released sooner than anticipated from prison.  She is very ambivalent about this: she is happy for Kathy and the kids, she is concerned about her daughter’s ability to take care of the children, she is hopeful that things will work out.  She has been talking to her pastor about things and feels that he has been a source of support for her.  She realizes how isolated she had been and after her last visit to the doctor, she resolved to talk to her pastor.

She does occasionally still feel overwhelmed and somewhat down, but overall, her mood has been better.  She started an exercise class for seniors that meets twice a week (while the kids are in school) and has enjoyed getting out of the house.  She has only been going for the past 2 weeks, but is committed to continuing to go.  Her appetite is fine, but she knows that she tends to overeat when she feels anxious or stressed.  She knows that she needs to work more on her diet, but is trying to eat less fried foods.  She does still wake-up at night on occasion (once or twice a month) and has difficulty getting back to sleep because she starts to worry about the future.

# 23. **Your Profile**

You are Mrs. Henderson, a 60-year-old retired schoolteacher who is being seen in follow-up for management of your blood pressure and cholesterol. You have had 3 appointments to date at this practice, one with the medical student/primary care physician for your initial history and physical exam, a second with the nurse for a blood pressure check 2 weeks after your initial appointment, and a third visit for follow-up with the primary care physician/same medical student in which you noted increased headaches and overall social stressors.  Since the last visit, you presented to the ED and were diagnosed with a panic attack.  You have a history of these in your early 20s, but this was the first time you had experienced a panic attack since then. At the time you first had a panic attack, you were in college and were under a lot of stress with a heavy classload.  You experienced about a dozen panic attacks over the course of a few months and then they went away completely.  More recently, when you developed chest discomfort you thought it might be a panic attack, but were worried and didn’t want to take any chances because of the history of heart problems.  Your husband called 911 and the paramedics took you to the emergency department for evaluation.  All the blood tests, chest x-ray and EKG were “fine” according to the doctor and he thought that the chest pain might be related to a panic attack.  Since your visit to the ED, you had a treadmill test and saw the cardiologist who told you that your heart was fine and not the cause of the chest pain. You were able to get your fasting labs for cholesterol and fasting sugar prior to this visit, and are anxious to get the results.

You present today for a follow-up appointment from your recent emergency room visit.   One of your main concerns is that you are somewhat worried that you may have another panic attack in the future.  You also realize the relationship of stress with your blood pressure management and overall health and have tried to pay closer attention to your own personal needs. However, you are still concerned overall about your ability to balance your home responsibilities with your health concerns. You have been able to take your medication regularly since your last visit.  Your relationship with your husband has improved slightly, as after the emergency room visit, he has been able to see some of the stress you are under, and he sometimes will ask about your health. Although, he still doesn’t help as much with the grandchildren as you would like, at least he has been more open to discussing some of the stressors that you are under.

You have made an effort to go to an exercise class for seniors that meets twice a week and you have made an effort to reach out to your pastor.  He has been tremendously supportive of your situation.  You are overall still hopeful about the future, but realize that you need to continue to reach out to others. That is a challenge for you as you have always been a person that others turn to for help.

Overall, the kids have been fine.  There was a stressful period early April, around the time of your ED visit, when it wasn’t clear what the kids were going to do when school let out for the summer.  You had investigated some summer activities, all of which were way too expensive, and you were beginning to become anxious that you would need to take care of the girls full-time over the summer.
