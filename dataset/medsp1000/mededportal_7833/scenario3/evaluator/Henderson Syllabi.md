**Syllabus Materials - Mrs. Henderson Case Part 3**

**objectives**

- Assess barriers to the optimal management of (chronic) illness
- Assess psychosocial stressors, current social supports and coping mechanisms  for a patient with (chronic) illness
- Identify mechanisms to screen patients with chronic disease for depression, including the use of patient self-report questionnaires
- Assess a patient’s self-efficacy (confidence) for managing his/her own (chronic) illness
- Identify potential neurological and psychiatric aspects of the patient’s presentation

**KEY WORDS**   

Chronic Illness

Screening for mental illness

Patient Self-efficacy

**session activities** 

**A.   Student Interview** 

1.    Student interviews a patient for 30 minutes.

<u>Guidelines</u>

a.	Find a balance a thorough history and a focused history; balance obtaining information with maintaining rapport. Draw on your knowledge of this patient, as well as your general knowledge of trauma, cardiac, pulmonary, renal, cancer, geriatric/homebound problems, and at least anticipate what sorts of neurologic and psychiatric problems may be present.

b.	This patient has at least one chronic illness.  Take time to explore the patient’s experience of the illness.  How does it affect her daily life?  How confident is she in managing the illness?  What are her goals for managing her illness(es)?  What changes in her behavior does she need to make to meet these goals?  What barriers, if any, have made it difficult for her to reach her goals?

c.	Inquire about any new concerns that have arisen since the patient’s last visit and check in regarding her ongoing medical issues.

d.	Having an illness can affect patient’s psychological or mental well-being as well.  How might you screen for depression or other forms of mental illness in this patient?

At http://www.depression-primarycare.org/clinicians/toolkits/full/ you can download the “Macarthur Initiative on Depression & Primary Care” Toolkit.  You may want to use this for suggestions on how you might ask these potentially sensitive questions.

e.	Additionally, the PHQ-9 is the nine-item depression scale of the Patient Health Questionnaire. The PHQ-9 is a powerful tool for assisting primary care clinicians in diagnosing depression as well as selecting and monitoring treatment. The primary care clinician and/or office staff should discuss with the patient the reasons for completing the questionnaire and how to fill it out. After the patient has completed the PHQ-9 questionnaire, it is scored by the primary care clinician or office staff.  You can access a copy of the PHQ 9 at http://www.depression-primarycare.org/clinicians/toolkits/materials/forms/phq9/

2.  	After the interview, have a volunteer give a brief summary of the patient’s story, in the standard medical history format.

3. 	Reflection and feedback on the interview:

a.	Interviewer describes: what went well and not so well, observations of the patient, and emotional reactions to the patient and the interview. How did the balance go between comprehensive history and focused history? Eliciting content and maintaining rapport?

b.	Feedback from the group: What did the interviewer do well? Content vs. process? Suggestions for improvements?

4. 	General Discussion of the Clinical Reasoning Process

Construct a general problem list for known and suspected problems. What kinds of neurologic and psychiatric problems are or might be connected to the patient’s specific problems? Compare with how your facilitators would put things together given this patient’s story.
