**Patient Background - CRITICAL for the facilitator to understand**

You are Yadier, a 40-year-old male Cuban parolee newly-arrived to the U.S., and are one of the last entrants when the program closed in January 2017. You left Cuba approximately a year ago and spent months traveling through South and Central America before arriving in the U.S. After spending a few months in Florida, you found the cost of living to be prohibitive and moved to Louisville. You are presenting to a local practice for care.

During your Refugee Health Screening, you tested positive for HIV. You were never diagnosed in Cuba and do not believe you contracted the virus there; you believe you contracted it on your way to the U.S. or in Miami. HIV status is only shared with the health care provider and staff, not with the family.

You know other HIV-positive people who are receiving successful treatment, and you feel confident about your own treatment with antiretroviral medications you have heard about. You report occasional condom use with several partners - you are not confident about the efficacy of condoms, but you want to remain sexually active. You report regular social drinking in tandem with sexual activity.

Despite considerable economic hardships, Cuba provides free universal coverage for its own population, and has achieved country health indices comparable to developed countries, but at a dramatically lower cost.
