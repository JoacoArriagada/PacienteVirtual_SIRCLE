**Patient Background - CRITICAL for SP to understand**

You are Mawadda, a 36-year-old widowed mother of two boys (Hassan-12 and Hakim-14) and newly-arrived refugee from Baghdad, Iraq. You speak Arabic and some Kurdish, but no English. Your husband was killed following the second Iraqi invasion. You lived in a refugee camp in Jordan until resettling in the United States.

You are aware of your outlook and admit you are "Dayij" (uncomfortable). Through the interpreter, you explain that you don't like the family's new apartment, don't feel safe in your new neighborhood, and haven't made any new friends. You acknowledge feeling sad, but attribute your feelings to being away from home, isolation due to the language barrier and lack of transportation, and discomfort around strangers. You also complain of headaches, backaches, and gastrointestinal disturbances, but your exam reveals no physical underlying causes for the complaints.

You are resistant to the idea of follow-up visits or accepting a mental health referral. You show the most interest in prescriptions to treat your aches and pains. Like many refugee groups unaccustomed to the U.S. health care system, Iraqi refugee patients may feel dissatisfied with the quality of care if they do not receive a tangible treatment or prescription medication. Preventive health care is not a priority; you are likely to resist physician-driven changes in diet and exercise, regular screenings, and follow-up appointments. You experience confusion about whether to go to your family physician or the ER in case of an emergency.

You are also concerned for your children, as you are their only resource for health care after your husband's death. You are interested in family health services, and offices that can provide interpreters. You have experienced Islamophobia while in the United States, and are seeking a provider that can give you the care you need, as you are.
