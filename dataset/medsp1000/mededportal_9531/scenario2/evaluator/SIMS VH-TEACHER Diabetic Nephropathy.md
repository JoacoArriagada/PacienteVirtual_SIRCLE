**Virtual Hospital Field Trip**

**SIMS - Diabetic Nephropathy** 

**Doorway Information/Brief Introduction to Patient:** David Jackson is a 43 year-old African-American male that presents with a chief complaint of not feeling well and being unable to urinate much for the last week. He is 6’0  210lbs

<u>**Sim Patients would need stats of Pulse of 90, Resp of 16 BP of 138/85**</u>

<u>**History:**</u>

Important things students should remember to ask

    1. Recent medical procedures or surgeries . Certain surgeries can cause urinary retention.
    2. Medication used both old and new as some medications can cause urinary retention.
    3. Volume status—ask the patient how much water s/he has been drinking. Look for signs of fluid overload or dehydration In our case the patient would have fluid overload due to low urine output.
    4. Urine Output history.

<u>**Clinical Signs & Symptoms:**</u>

    - Little or no urine when you urinate.
    - Swelling, especially in your legs and feet.
    - Nausea and vomiting.
    - Flank Pain; pain in the back just below the rib cage~~.~~
    - Fatigue, Lethargy
    - Foamy appearance or excessive frothing of the urine
    - General ill feeling
    - Generalized itching
    - Headache
    - Poor appetite; Not feeling like eating.
    - Unintentional weight gain (from fluid buildup)

**Extremities/Skin**<u>**: look for swelling in the legs and feet known as edema**</u> (I’m not terribly concerned with them being able to grade the edema; right now recognizing it is appropriate)

<u>**Test to Order:**</u>

\*<u>Basic Metabolic Panel</u> (BMP)—look for electrolytes imbalance;

BUN/Creatinine > 20; students should be able to get the values from the BMP and calculate this ratio

\*<u>Microabluminuria</u> (urine to measure microalbumin) This is a very specific test to measure albumin in urine when the filtering function of the kidney begins to falter.

\*<u>24-hour urine protein</u> In the normal kidney protein does not get filtered into the urine. In diabetic nephropathy protein appears in the urine .

\*<u>CBC with Differential</u> (look for possible anemia or infection)

<u>**Treatment:**</u>

Treat the underlying disorder. In this case the patient’s diabetes.

<u>**Complications**</u>

    - Anemia
    - [<u>Chronic kidney failure</u>](http://www.nlm.nih.gov/medlineplus/ency/article/000471.htm)(rapidly gets worse)
    - Dialysis complications
    - [<u>End-stage kidney disease</u>](http://www.nlm.nih.gov/medlineplus/ency/article/000500.htm)
    - [<u>Hyperkalemia</u>](http://www.nlm.nih.gov/medlineplus/ency/article/001179.htm)
    - Severe [<u>hypertension</u>](http://www.nlm.nih.gov/medlineplus/ency/article/000468.htm)
    - [<u>Hypoglycemia</u>](http://www.nlm.nih.gov/medlineplus/ency/article/000386.htm)
    - Infections
    - [<u>Kidney transplant</u>](http://www.nlm.nih.gov/medlineplus/ency/article/003005.htm)complications
    - [<u>Peritonitis</u>](http://www.nlm.nih.gov/medlineplus/ency/article/001335.htm)(if peritoneal dialysis used)

<u>**Counseling:**</u>

Counsel the patient that s/he is at risk for kidney failure if s/he does not control their diabetes which could result in any of the complications listed above. Consult (involve) dietician and social work to help manage the patient. Dietician will help with counseling on proper diet .Social worker will help connect the patient with community resources to assist in managing diabetes.
