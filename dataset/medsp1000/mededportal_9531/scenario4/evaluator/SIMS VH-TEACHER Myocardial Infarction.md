**Virtual Hospital Field Trip**

**SIMS - Myocardial Infarction** 

**Background:** 56yo male Richard Jefferson, black male, 5’9 245lbs comes to doctor presenting with crushing chest pain that radiates down his left arm, shortness of breath and nausea.

<u>**Sim lab would need to show abnormal EKG, pulse of 105 bpm, O2 Sat of 95% R:20. An abnormal chest xray would be great**</u>

**Signs and Symptoms**:

Chest pain, Shortness of breath, nausea, vomiting, palpitations, sweating, and anxiety

**Exam:** Listen to the patient heart and lungs.  Take vital signs.

**Tests**:

Electro cardiogram is a measure of the electrical activity of the heart.  During a heart attack the electrical signals in the heart will be abnormal and can be read by the ECG.

Cardiac Enzymes: as heart muscle dies it release enzymes into the blood that can be measured.  If these enzymes are high it indicates high levels of muscle death.  This would indicate a heart attack

Ex: CPK, Troponin, and myoglobin

Coronary Angiography: way to image the coronary arteries to look for a blockage causing a MI.

Echocardiography: Ultrasound of heart to image a blocked coronary artery

**Treatments**:

It is important in a heart attack to prevent arrhythmias of the heart caused by muscle death.  This is one of the most common ways a heart attack leads to a fatality.

- If Patient has an arrhythmia they are treated with Electrical Cardio conversion/defibrillation or medication to correct the arrhythmia.

Oxygen is also given to the patient.

Angioplasty and Stent placement in order to correct the blocked coronary artery and reperfuse the area.

Thrombolytic therapy: This drug will attempt to break up the clot

Should not be used for people who have had a recent strokes, blood vessel abnormalities, intra cranial hemorrhage or recent head trauma.

Students would need to do a physical exam including:

    1. Auscultate over the heart and listen for signs of heart failure like a 3rd or 4th heart sound.
    2. Auscultate over the carotids and aorta and listen for bruits.  To make sure no there is no other atherosclerotic disease at these other sites.
    3. Obtain a urine specimen to check for diabetes mellitus and renal disease

        1. Since these are associated with accelerated MI

    1. Obtain a blood specimen and check for lipids, glucose, hematocrit, and Thyroid function

        1. This also may accelerate or explain the chest pain

    1. Obtain a Chest X RAY.  May show cardiac enlargement, ventricular aneurysm,  or other signs of heart failure
    2. Obtain an ECG.  Can give you indication as to if there has been ischemic heart disease or not.
    3. Patients with abnormal ECG may need additional imaging such as arteriography, CT, or ultrasound.

**Doctors treatment orders :**

    1. Give Aspirin immediately
    2. IV line for fluids and medications
    3. Once a full diagnosis of MI has been made one treatment pathway is to complete a percutaneous coronary intervention.
        1. This is the process of angioplasty and stenting to open the coronary arteries and allow blood flow back to the heart.
    4. Medications to prescribe
        1. Anti Platelet therapy(Aspirin) : A blood thinner that will reduce the chance of blood being blocked in the coronary artery
        2. Nitric Oxide: to treat minor events of chest pain.  Will lead to venodilation and pain reduction.
        3. Beta Blockers: they reduce myocardial oxygen demand by lowering heart rate and contractility
        4. Calcium Channel blockers: vasodilator
    5. Once treatments have been administered it will be important to counsel the patient on the healthy lifestyle and exercise in order to prevent this occurring again.
        1. A lipid reducing drug may be prescribed to decrease atherosclerotic plaque
    6. If patient smokes make sure to counsel on the dangers of smoking and the benefits of quitting.
