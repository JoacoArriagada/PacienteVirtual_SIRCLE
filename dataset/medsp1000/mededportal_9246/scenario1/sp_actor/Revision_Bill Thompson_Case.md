# Weber Test

A vibrating tuning fork is placed in the middle of the forehead or on top of the head, equidistant from the ears, and patient is asked if he can hear sound equally in both ears. You indicate both ears perceive the sound equally.

# Rinne Test

A vibrating tuning fork is placed behind the ear (along mastoid process) until the patient indicates that the sound has stopped. Then, the tuning fork is immediately placed in front of the ear. Patient is asked which location was louder. You indicate the sound is louder in front of the ear on the right. When the left ear is being tested, you indicate that the sound is louder behind the left ear.

# NEXT STEPS

The following assessment and plan is communicated to you by the doctor:

“Considering the significant amount of time you were exposed to loud noises through your occupation, sometimes using ear protection but other times not, I am favoring a diagnosis called sensorineural hearing loss. This is hearing loss that occurs as the result of damage to the specialized hearing cells in the ear. Smoking can also damage hearing cells, so I am glad that you quit. Sensorineural hearing loss may worsen but usually at a slow rate. Although it cannot be reversed, avoidance of certain things like loud noise can help prevent further loss. It is often treated with hearing aides. However, there is another type of hearing loss, called conductive hearing loss, which may explain your symptoms. This can be the result of hardening or rupture of the eardrum. As I mentioned, your ear exam was normal in appearance but I was not able to evaluate the function of your eardrum. Finally, in rare instances, growths around the ear canal can also cause this problem. Because your hearing tests were inconclusive, I would recommend a consultation with an ear, nose and throat specialist who can help decide if further testing should be performed. You will likely need a formal hearing test and this can be arranged as well. What questions do you have?”

You have no further questions and indicate that you are looking forward to finding out the cause of your problem.
