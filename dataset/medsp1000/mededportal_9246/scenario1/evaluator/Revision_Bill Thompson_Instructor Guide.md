# Purpose

This standardized patient case features Bill Thompson, an older man who has been experiencing the gradual onset of hearing loss. This exercise was originally designed to provide a formative tool to assess third-year medical students' skills in gathering information and writing a progress note in a timed environment. It has since been adapted to allow participation of different levels of learners. Medical students must be proficient in the domains of communication, data gathering and documentation to succeed within the time constraints of the USMLE $\textcircled{8}$ Step 2 Clinical Skills Exam.

# How the Materials Have Been Used

The case has been used both for training and assessment. Students receive training through the simulated testing experience. Assessment of SOAP notes has occurred but in a formative manner (not for grading purposes).

# Methods Used for Training

A standardized patient was trained to interact with me in front of the group. However, he was provided with a pre-planned script that was followed fairly closely. He was not trained to provide me with any specific feedback but did share some of his experiences as a standardized patient with the group, following the encounter. Recent adaptations of this exercise have involved training of the standardized patient to provide feedback and interact with students.

Practical Implementation Advice

Medical Student Year 1

This exercise is designed for demonstration of the standardized patient encounter for a group of approximately 15 students in a classroom setting. The standardized patient and one facilitator are seated in the front of the classroom. The class does not participate in the actual standardized patient encounter. However, students observe and take notes of the interaction, which lasts 20 minutes. Afterword, they practice typing a SOAP note in a timed manner to simulate a testing experience similar to the USMLE $\textcircled{8}$ Step 2 Clinical Skills Exam. Students are instructed to focus on completing the History of Present Illness (HPI) and Physcial Examination (PE) portions of the note. They receive 15 minutes to do this. Students submit their notes via email to the instructor, preferably in real time via a wireless Internet. They later receive written feedback. The facilitator then presents an example of a SOAP note from the encounter and allows the students to compare it with their notes.

# Medical Student Year 2

This exercise allows for the participation of small groups consisting of 6-7 students in the interview and physical examination portions of the standardized patient encounter, which comprise 20 minutes combined. The students are assigned roles in the activity, i.e.: each student receives an index card indicating a different assignment, such as HPI, PMH, etc. They take notes throughout the encounter. Afterword, they practice typing a SOAP note in a timed manner to simulate a testing experience similar to the USMLE $\textcircled{8}$ Step 2 Clinical Skills Exam. Students are instructed to focus on completing the HPI, PE and assessment portions of the note, providing two to three most likely diagnoses. They receive 15 minutes to do this. Students submit their notes via e-mail to the instructor, preferably in real time via a wireless Internet. They later receive written feedback. The facilitator then presents an example of a SOAP note from the encounter and allows the students to compare it with their notes.

Medical Student Year 3

In this exercise, students interview and and perform the physical examination portions of a standardized patient encounter independently, with a time limit of 15 minutes. They take notes throughout the encounter. Afterword, they practice typing a SOAP note in a timed manner to simulate a testing experience similar to the USMLE $\textcircled{8}$ Step 2 Clinical Skills Exam. Students are instructed to focus on completing all portions of the note, including two to three most likely diagnoses and a prioritized plan. They receive 10 minutes to do this. Students submit their notes via e-mail to the instructor, preferably in real time via a wireless Internet. They later receive written feedback.

# Medical Student Year 4

This exercise transforms a fourth-year medical student from the role of a learner to that of an educator/role model. The student demonstrates a standardized patient encounter for a group of approximately 15 students in a classroom setting. The standardized patient and facilitator (fourth-year student) are seated in the front of the classroom. The class does not participate in the actual standardized patient encounter. However, students observe and take notes of the interaction, which lasts 20 minutes. Afterword, they practice typing a SOAP note in a timed manner to simulate a testing experience similar to the USMLE $\textcircled{8}$ Step 2 Clinical Skills Exam. Students are instructed to focus on completing the History of Present Illness (HPI) and Physcial Examination (PE) portions of the note. They receive 15 minutes to do this. Students submit their notes via e-mail to the instructor, preferably in real time via a wireless Internet. They later receive written feedback. The facilitator then presents an example of a SOAP note from the encounter and allows the students to compare it with their notes.

# PREPARATION NEEDS:

Printing an adequate number of Door Notes in advance (one per student). These are included in the file entitled, “Bill Thompson_Case.” Meeting with Standardized Patient to review/rehearse dialogue and plan beforehand, which is outlined in “Bill Thompson_Case.”

LENGTH OF PATIENT ENCOUNTER:

M1, M2, M4: 20 minutes [Interview/Exam], 15 minutes [SOAP Note] M3: 15 minutes [Interview/Exam], 10 minutes [SOAP Note]

EQUIPMENT NEEDED:

STUDENT: Students need to bring clipboards with notepaper, pens, stethoscopes, and laptop computers. FACILITATOR: Otoscopes, Penlights, and Tuning Forks
