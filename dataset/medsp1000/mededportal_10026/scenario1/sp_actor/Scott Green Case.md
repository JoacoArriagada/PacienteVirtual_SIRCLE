**Identifying Case Factors: Scott Green**

**Presenting complaint:** Hospital follow-up, “No energy”

**SP demographics:** Scott Green, Male, 55-80 years, any ethnicity

**Setting:** Outpatient office / clinic

**Patient presentation:** Come wearing street clothes, sitting in chair, no visible markings. You are not in any distress but are restricted in your emotions and display little joy.

**Profile:** Mr. Green is coming for follow-up for a recent hospitalization where he was hospitalized after having an acute myocardial infarction. Upon discharge to home, he was referred to cardiac rehabilitation but he has been non-complaint due to the onset of depressive symptoms.

**History of present complaint:** You are coming to the doctor to follow-up from a recent hospital stay (discharged 4 weeks ago.) You developed chest pain while doing yard work, and when it didn’t go away, you went to the Emergency Department (ED). You were diagnosed as having a heart attack and they rushed you to the lab for a cardiac catheterization (“cath”). You had 2 stents placed and you haven’t had any pain since. You do not currently have any chest discomfort, shortness of breath, palpitations, or leg swelling.

Since you were discharged, you haven’t felt like doing anything. You used to go out to breakfast with friends 2-3 days a week but you haven’t done that because you don’t want to be pitied. You are supposed to be going to cardiac rehab but you haven’t gone in over a week because you “don’t see the point.” (You are supposed to go 3 times per week for the last 2 weeks.) You feel tired all the time and don’t feel like there’s anything that can help you get your energy back.

You aren’t interested in doing anything and feel “on edge” all of the time. You are worried your friends will pity you and that they won’t understand what you are going through. You haven’t talked to anyone about how you feel.

IF ASKED: Your chest pain was a pressure or discomfort over your left chest. The pain/pressure started while planting flowers and laying mulch outside your house. You went inside to rest and when it didn’t go away after 30 minutes, you went to the ED.

Before your heart attack, you were looking forward to the summer because you love spending time in your garden and going fishing with your grandson.
