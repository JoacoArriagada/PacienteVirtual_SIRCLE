**Instructors Guide to “Treating Taylor: Multidisciplinary management of eating disorders”**

**Distribute case text "Session 4, Part B"**

Summary: Taylor is restoring weight, but is becoming more anxious and has become more obsessive.

**Discussion topics**

- Weight restoration
- OCD/anxiety symptoms
- Recovery

**Potential probe questions**

- What do you think of the re-emergence of Taylor’s OCD symptoms?
- What do you do about Taylor’s anxiety?
- What do you do about Taylor’s urges to cut?
- What is recovery?

**Key points for discussion**

*From a behavioral model, it is quite normal that Taylor’s OCD symptoms have returned.  A helpful discussion point may be to normalize Taylor’s behavior and further assess (1) whether she meets criteria for Obsessive Compulsive Disorder, (2) whether the behavior was pre-morbid before her eating disorder, or (3) the function of the OCD behavior in addition to assessing severity. If participants feel that the anxiety and OCD is pre-morbid, participants may want to refer Taylor to a therapist who can conduct cognitive behavioral therapy which is the treatment of choice for both disorders.*

*Similarly for her anxiety and renewed urges to self-injure, it may be helpful to facilitate a discussion regarding behavior substitution.  Ask the group to problem solve different skills that may be helpful for Taylor to learn instead of behaviors that are self-destructive. Participants may find the following dialectical behavior therapy workbooks helpful:*

- *Astrachan-Fletcher E, Maslar M. The Dialectical Behavior Therapy Skills Workbook for Bulimia: Using DBT to Break the Cycle and Regain Control.* 
- *McKay M, Wood JC. The Dialectical Behavior Therapy Skills Workbook: Practical DBT Exercises for Learning Mindfulness, Interpersonal Effectiveness, Emotion Regulation & Distress Tolerance.* 

*The goal of eating disorder treatment is full recovery: 100% weight restored and behavior free of eating disorder thoughts and compensatory behaviors.  It was previously thought that most patients with eating disorders would struggle for their whole lives.  It is important for parents to know that full physical, emotional, and cognitive recovery is possible.  In fact, if properly treated, an eating disorder can be short in duration and without long-term medical consequences.  Refer to the Coutier reading for additional information on the definition of recovery.  FEAST (*[*http://feast-ed.org/FEAST.aspx*](http://feast-ed.org/FEAST.aspx)*) is a helpful website for parents using Family Based Treatment and offers definitions and examples of patients who have achieved recovery.*
