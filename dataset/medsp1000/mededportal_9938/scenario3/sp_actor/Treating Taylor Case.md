**Case Resource “Treating Taylor: Multidisciplinary management of eating disorders”**

<u>**Session 1, Part A**</u>

Taylor is a 13 year old with weight loss over the past 7-8 months.  Taylor presented to her pediatrician 3 months ago with complaints of intermittent abdominal pain, but Mom was more concerned about Taylor's recent weight loss.  Taylor was referred to the outpatient eating disorder program because Mom states that Taylor picks at all food and exercises too much.  Taylor has never seen a therapist or nutritionist.

When asked about the reason for today's visit, Taylor states, “I have pain in my stomach and my mom thinks that I am too thin.” Taylor reports having abdominal pain for the past year--pain in around the belly button that could happen any time of the day; denies vomiting.  Taylor feels nauseous, especially in the morning, so never eats breakfast.

<u>**Session 1, Part B**</u>  

Notes from the ED assessment:

Medical visit: Taylor is happy with her current weight and doesn’t know what the fuss is, when pressed she does say that she is thinks she is getting thinner, but likes to be thinner.  She weighs herself 3-4 times per week.  She wishes her stomach was flatter, but otherwise she feels OK about the way she looks.  She reports that she does a lot of exercising to stay fit. She does yoga, runs 4 miles 3 times weekly. She wants to join the cross country team. She had menarche at age 12 and has not had a period for the past 4 months. She denies laxatives or diet pills.  She denies feeling cramps after drinking milk. Her ROS is notable only for feeling a little cold in the AC this summer, and feeling slightly dizzy when standing up rapidly.  She denies headaches, skin change, or other symptoms. No stress fractures. No diarrhea or other GI symptoms.  She stools 3 times per week.

Nutrition visit: Taylor reports to the dietitian that she is happy with her current weight and does not know what the big fuss is about weight.  She became a vegetarian 4 months ago to be healthier.  She cut down on junk food.  She skips meals because she does not feel well in the morning.  She admits that she rarely eats at restaurants because she worries about what is in the food. Her dietary recall for the last 24 hours: For lunch she had a plain greek yogurt and a granola bar.  For snack after school she had an apple and pretzels.  She had a veggie wrap for dinner with lettuce, tomato, cucumbers, 3 tablespoons of hummus.  She drinks water and green tea.

Mental health visit: Taylor notes that she has been stressed lately.  She is in the 8thgrade at a new school.  She moved from Florida last year because her dad got a new job.  He now travels quite a bit.  She has an 11 year old sister. She and Mom get into arguments sometimes about her eating which adds to her stress.  She misses her friends in Florida.  She seems to cry more easily now.  She has always been a good student, a perfectionist in fact, getting mostly A’s. She feels very anxious about school since the schools here are much more rigorous than the schools in Florida. Taylor is happy with her current weight and doesn’t know what the fuss is, when pressed she does say that she is thinks she is getting thinner, but likes to be thinner.  She weighs herself 3-4 times per week.  She wishes her stomach was flatter, but otherwise she feels OK about the way she looks.  She denies self-injurious behaviors or suicidality.
<u>**Session 3, Part A**</u>

After the last visit, Taylor goes to a local residential program and then an intensive outpatient program.  She is restoring weight (BMI is now 19.0) and she has eaten while at home but still with some difficulty.  She continues to self injure, but much less frequently.  The treatment facility asks you as the primary team to help set up follow up appointments.  She still has not had her period since you met her.
