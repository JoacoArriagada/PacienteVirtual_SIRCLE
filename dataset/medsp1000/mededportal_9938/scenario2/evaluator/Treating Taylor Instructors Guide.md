**Instructors Guide to “Treating Taylor: Multidisciplinary management of eating disorders”**

<u>**Instructor Summary: Session 2**</u>

Summary: Taylor is diagnosed with Anorexia Nervosa and enters outpatient treatment. At her appointment, her health care provider discovers that she has started self-injuring by cutting.

**Discussion topics**

- Assessing for safety
- Confidentiality
- Difficult conversations
- Criteria for hospitalization (medical vs. psychiatric)
- Mental health comorbidities
- Nutrition intervention

**Potential probe questions**

- *What do you do?*
- *How do you assess the situation?*
- *What are your state regulations for confidentiality?*
- *Do you tell the family about Taylor’s cutting?*
- *What are your treatment recommendations?*
- *Should you hospitalize Taylor?*

**Key points for discussion**

*It is necessary to assess the severity of Taylor’s self-injury.  Questions that are important may include: how often is she cutting, what does she use to cut, how deep or superficial are her cuts, did she ever need stitches or ER attention for her cutting?  Clinicians need to assess if this is non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI), or if the cutting is a symptom of something more serious such as suicidal ideation or passive suicidal ideation. Most individuals who self-injure are not suicidal; however, individuals with a history of self-injury are at higher risk for suicidal thoughts, gestures, and attempts and, because of this, need to be assessed for suicide risk.  It is important to respond with a matter of fact attitude, rather than with shock or panic.*

*For many professionals, assessing suicidality is difficult and anxiety provoking.  It may be helpful to conduct a role play with one of the instructors demonstrating how to do a brief risk assessment. Some questions to ask may be: Have you thought of hurting yourself or dying? Have you thought about how you would do it? Do you have access to any of these methods?  The suicide assessment script below may also be used with either learners or instructors playing the roles of therapist and Taylor.*

*If the role play is used, discuss with the learners that you have assessed both risk factors and protective factors.  Reinforce that ideation, plan, and intent must be asked about when assessing suicide risk.*

*Assessed risk factors:* 

- *Suicidal behavior, history of suicide attempts, and self-injurious behaviors.* 
- *Current and past psychiatric disorders, key symptoms (impulsivity, hopelessness, anhedonia, anxiety/panic, command hallucinations, global insomnia).*
- *Family history: of suicide attempts or completion, mental health history*
- *Precipitants/stressors/interpersonal events*
- *Change in treatment*
- *Access to firearms or weapons*

*Assessed protective factors:* 

- *Internal: ability to cope with stressors, religious beliefs, frustration tolerance*
- *External: responsibility for people, pets, family members, positive relationships, social supports*

*Suicide Inquiry:* 

- *Ideation: frequency, intensive, duration – in the last 48 hours, past month, and worse period in their life*
- *Plan: timing, location, lethality, availability, preparatory acts*
- *Intent: extent to which patient expects to carry out the plan, explore ambivalence*

*After completing this assessment, you can determine risk level. You can conclude that the function of Taylor’s self- injury is to manage her negative mood and thoughts.  Taylor’s cutting is classified as non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI) which is a form of intentional physical self-damage or self-harm that is not accompanied by suicidal intent or ideation.  For some individuals, NSSI is used to cope with negative affect and cognitions.  If Taylor continues to self-injure or have urges to self-injure as a means to regulate her affect - an appropriate referral could be to refer her to a therapist who uses dialectical behavioral therapy (DBT).  DBT is a skills-based therapy designed to teach individuals techniques to regulate emotions, cope with stress, and avoid self-injurious behaviors. Learners can access* <u>*DBT Resources: What is DBT?*</u> *(Behavioral Tech, LLC; http://behavioraltech.org/resources/whatisdbt.cfm) for more information on DBT. The group may want to discuss interventions or local therapeutic options that may help Taylor tolerate weight restoration. DBT groups, support groups, or an individual therapist may all be appropriate choices.*

*In many states adolescents may seek treatment without the permission of their parents for various concerns: treatment of sexually transmitted diseases, birth control, pregnancy, mental health issues, and substance abuse.  It is important to understand state and local laws, as well as your clinic’s policy regarding self-injury.  There should always be a strong presumption to respect confidentiality and avoid breaking confidences when at all possible.  While confidentiality is important to facilitate a climate of trust within the patient/client relationship, there are limits to confidentiality that the patient must be informed of in the beginning of the treatment.  Laws regarding privacy and confidentiality vary from state to state.  However, there are three universal situations where clinicians are mandated to break confidentiality and have a duty to inform:*

- *Clinicians have a duty to report abuse.  If the patient discloses that they are a victim of abuse, confidentiality will be broken.  Additionally if it is disclosed that a child under the age of 18 is being abused, anyone over the age of 60 is being abused, and anyone, of any age, that has a disability is being abused, confidentiality will be broken and the abuse reported.*
- *Clinicians have a duty to protect patients from committing suicide.* 
- *Clinicians have a duty to warn and protect intended victims of homicide.*

*However, because Taylor is 13 years old, many providers may break confidentiality and inform her parents of her self-injury.  It is important to tell Taylor that you will be telling her parents about her cutting.  The clearest situations in which confidentiality is broken are those in which the patient places self or another person at significant risk of serious harm. Questions to discuss may include: Is there a high likelihood of significant harm? Will informing her parents prevent future harm?  Each discipline should know their rules and state regulations for confidentiality.*

*Individuals with eating disorders may transition between levels of care throughout their course of treatment. Levels of care are reviewed in the APA Practice Guidelines for the Treatment of Patients with Eating Disorders, as well as in the AAP Clinical Report; generally treatment options are grouped into 5 levels:*

*Level 1: Outpatient*

*Level 2: Intensive Outpatient*

*Level 3: Partial Hospitalization/Full-Day Outpatient Care*

*Level 4: Residential Treatment Center*

*Level 5: Inpatient Hospitalization*

*Appropriate level of care depends on a variety of factors including presenting symptoms, degree of medical instability, and treatment response.  Decisions about level of care may also depend on availability of local specialized programs and finances/insurance.* 

*Regardless of the level of care and mental health comorbidities, a primary goal of Taylor's treatment given her low body weight will be weight gain. Therefore, nutrition intervention, including nutritional counseling by a registered dietitian (RD), is an essential component of multidisciplinary treatment. The RD will calculate and monitor energy and nutrient intake to establish expected rates of weight change and to meet health goals, will counsel individuals and other caregivers on food selection. The RD may provide a structured refeeding plan and will offer suggestions about supplement use, if necessary. As treatment progresses, the RD will guide goal setting to normalize eating patterns for nutrition rehabilitation and weight restoration or maintenance, as appropriate****,*** *and will ensure diet quality and regular eating pattern, increased amount and variety of foods consumed, and normal perceptions of hunger and satiety. Additional information about the role of the RD in eating disorder management is included in the assigned position paper.*
