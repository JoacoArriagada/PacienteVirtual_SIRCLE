**Instructors Guide to “Treating Taylor: Multidisciplinary management of eating disorders”**

<u>**Instructor Summary: Session 1**</u>

**Distribute case text "Session 1, Part A"**

Summary: Taylor is a 13 year old with weight loss over the past 7-8 months and abdominal pain.  At Taylo's evaluation, Taylor's mother reports being concerned about the recent weight loss.

**Discussion topics**

- Discipline-specific assessment questions
- Differential diagnosis
- DSM 5 criteria for eating disorders

**Potential probe questions**

- *How do you picture Taylor?*
- *What else do you want to know?* 
- *What are your next questions?*
- *Is Taylor a typical ED patient?  Who gets eating disorders?*

**Key points for discussion**

*The differential diagnosis for eating disorder symptoms is large—focusing on different organ systems such as gastrointestinal (inflammatory bowel disease, irritable bowel syndrome, celiac disease) and endocrine (hypothyroidism, cortisol deficiency) can help participants to think through the many aspects of the differential diagnosis. This should be done with psychological diagnoses, as well, such as major depression disorder and somatoform disorder.* 

*It is important to help participants understand that each discipline may have a different focus in the questions they ask but there will be some overlap. Helping learners with the phrasing of questions is important; asking in a nonjudgmental way can help to provide clinicians with an understanding of the patient’s clinical features. Asking participants to visualize Taylor before and after the discussion for part one can illustrate for participants some of their preconceived notions about who gets eating disorders.* 

*While learners might assume that Taylor is female, it will be important to highlight that eating disorder affect both males and females and individuals of all backgrounds.  The Swanson et al. paper shows, that the lifetime prevalence of eating disorders among adolescent females is 3.9% (0.3% anorexia nervosa, 1.3% bulimia nervosa, and 2.3% binge eating disorder) and among adolescent males is 1.6% (0.3% anorexia nervosa, 0.5% bulimia nervosa, and 0.8% binge eating disorder), a ratio of about 2.5:1.  Anorexia nervosa is the least common eating disorder and Hispanic youth are the most likely to bulimia nervosa and binge eating disorder. Contrary to commonly held assumptions, measures of socioeconomic status were not significantly associated with any eating disorder presentation.* 

**Distribute case text "Session 1, Part B"**

Summary: Information obtained from Taylor's multidisciplinary assessment reveals that Taylor has lost more than 20 pounds in the past year, but is happy with her current weight even though she is underweight.  She recently became a vegetarian and her dietary recall reveals that she is eating a low calorie, low fat diet. Taylor's family moved from Florida last year and she is anxious about her new school.

**Discussion topics**

- Medical complications of eating disorders
- Calculating median body weight
- Setting developmentally appropriate weight goals
- Mental health co-morbidities common among individuals with eating disorders
- Debunking myths about EDs

**Potential probe questions**

- *Why was she asked questions about skin, stomach, menses, etc?*
- *Is she at a healthy weight? (have growth charts available to allow for calculation of MBW; available at: http://www.cdc.gov/growthcharts/data/set1clinical/cj41l024.pdf)*
- *What concerns do you have about her dietary recall?*
- *What diagnoses come to mind?*
- *What medical evaluation (laboratories and studies) would you suggest?* 

**Key points for discussion**

*Having a representative from each discipline explain specifically the goal of each question they have asked can make sure that all disciplines understand the thought processes of another’s specialty. The medical provider might explain that hair loss or dry skin can be secondary features of malnutrition, while a nutrition learner can explain the importance of calcium for adolescent bone health. Vital sign changes, growth issues, and bone loss are possible medical complications.*

*Puberty is a key feature of adolescent development and it is important for all learners to understand the physical and psychological changes associated with it. Taylor’s BMI is 15.7. The 50**th* *percentile BMI-for-age on the CDC growth chart at age 13 is 18.7, which puts her at 84% median body weight (MBW). This should be demonstrated during this segment by using growth charts referenced above or an alternative tool (such as an online calculator, a Smartphone application, or a SonneWheel™). One year ago, her BMI was 20.2 which put her at the 75**th* *percentile at age 12 (the 50**th* *percentile of the CDC for girls at age 12 is 18.1).Because of her low weight, Taylor could be given a diagnosis of anorexia nervosa. The discussion of her target weight goal is complex. Is the 50**th* *percentile appropriate or the 75**th* *percentile where she was previously? Each discipline should give their opinion. Her pubertal development, menses, and growth should be considered.* 

*The initial medical work-up for an eating disorder should include electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, Bicarbonate with BUN, creatinine, glucose, calcium, magnesium, and phosphorus), which can indicate dehydration, vomiting, refeeding syndrome, or severe malnutrition. In addition, clinicians should consider screening with a celiac panel, thyroid function tests, complete blood count (CBC), and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) to rule out other illnesses that may present similarly to eating disorders.  If Taylor has a slow or rapid heart rate, an EKG should be done to check for intervals and QTc. Constipation can be a side effect of nutritional deficiency which can cause decreased bowel motility. Constipation management can include increasing fluids and fiber in the diet. Docusate, a stool softener may be used.  In addition, polyethylene glycol (PEG) can be administered daily with 8 oz of water once to twice daily.  If a patient is extremely constipated, an initial clean out may be needed and further GI evaluation may be necessary.*
