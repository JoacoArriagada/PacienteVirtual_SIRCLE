Case Title: Pyloric Stenosis

## Environment (if using as a simulation case)

1. Room Set Up – ED, trauma bay, or simulation lab
   1. Manikin Set Up – high-fidelity simulator, monitor, peripheral IV and IO
   2. Props – airway equipment with suction, code blue cart
2. Distractors – None

## Actors (optional)

1. Roles – ED resident, airway resident or respiratory therapist, nurse
2. Who may play them – any of the above

**For Examiner Only**

**CASE SUMMARY**

**SYNOPSIS OF HISTORY/ Scenario Background**

Chief Complaint: Vomiting

History of Present Illness: 6 week old male with vomiting after most feedings and very concerned parents as the vomiting has increased over the past 24 hours to be more forceful and copious. The parents report that the patient is less interactive and fussy in the past day. He has only had 1 wet diaper in the past 24 hours. The mother reports that there was a normal pregnancy and spontaneous vaginal delivery at 39 weeks gestation. At the patients two week appointment, the pediatrician mentioned her son is 10% for height and 5% for weight, compared to other children his age. The patient has had no fever, diarrhea or sick contacts. The child just finished feeding prior to you entering the room.

Past medical history: None. Pt received Hepatitis B immunization at birth.

Medications and allergies: No medications and no known drug allergies

Family and social history: Non-contributory

**SYNOPSIS OF PHYSICAL**

VS: BP 70/55, HR 188, RR 30, T 98.9, 98% RA, Weight 3.7kg

Physical Exam:

Gen: child crying, no acute distress

HEENT: sunken fontanelle, positive red reflex, EOMI, TM clear, dry oropharynx

Neck: normal

Lungs: CTAB, no wheezing or crackles

Cardiac: tachycardic, no murmur

Abdomen: soft, small mass in RUQ, epigastric area

Extremities: normal range of motion, no cyanosis

Neurologic: normal reflexes

**Scenario branch points/ PLAY OF CASE GUIDELINES**

1. **Place on monitor and obtain intraosseous access after failure to obtain venous access.**

* Once monitor is in place, the initial vital signs are unchanged.
* The nurse states: ”IV access is difficult” after 2 attempts.
* Physician or medic obtains intraosseous access (if directed by team leader).
  + If IO access is attempted, parents become upset at multiple attempts to stick child.
  + Explain necessity of intravenous/intraosseous access due to dehydration to parents in order to calm them down.
* Bolus NS at 20 cc/kg.
  + If fluids are given, vital signs: BP 88/60, HR 165, RR 30, T98.9, 98% RA
  + If fluids are not given, vital signs unchanged.

1. **Perform suction of airway after patient aspirates on vomit and becomes hypoxic.**

* The patient begins to vomit approximately 3 to 4 ounces. The vomit is projectile, non-bloody and non-bilious. The patient then begins to choke and aspirates. SpO2 decreases to 91% RA.
  + If airway is suctioned and oxygen is given, improved O2 saturation.
  + If airway is not suctioned, O2 saturations continue to decrease until patient codes.
* Optional: Chest/KUB/Abdominal Xray ordered to evaluate aspiration.

1. **Recognize pyloric stenosis, order chemistry panel, imaging and consult surgery.**

* Ultrasound is ordered with findings consistent with pyloric stenosis.
* Learner should order a Chemistry Panel (Na+, K+, Cl-, CO2, BUN, Creatinine, and Glucose). A CBC and UA are also included if the learner is concerned for infection.
* Surgery is consulted.
