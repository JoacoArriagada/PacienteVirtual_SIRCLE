**CASE 8**
Amber Benacci,

<u>**Case Summary**</u>

Amber Benacci is a 16 year old female high school student whose mother called last week for an emergency visit to see the doctor because she caught Amber smoking. She expects the Doctor to scare her into quitting, and is very upset about the whole thing. Amber quit in the past, but all her friends smoke, and they kept encouraging her to smoke. She is used to hanging out with them, and finds it hard to be around smokers and not smoke. One friend used to blow smoke in her face. They often offer her a cigarette.  Amber quit when she realized how much money she was spending, and because of the commercials that talk about how the cigarette companies manipulate smokers. She smoked ½ a pack a day, quit 3 months ago, and then restarted 2 weeks ago. She thinks that since she wasn’t able to stay quit, she can never really quit.

Since the mother found out about the smoking last week, she has grounded Amber, and made her quit the soccer team. Mother works, but calls home every hour after Amber gets home from school to make sure she doesn't leave the house. Amber and her mother have not had a very good relationship for a couple years, but this has made things much worse. Her mother talks about how she can’t trust her, and plans on keeping her grounded for months. Amber's perception is that her mother is always mad at her for things that everybody else does all the time.

There isn't a whole lot interesting happening in her life (it seems to her like her mom never lets her do anything fun) so coming to the doctor is more "exciting" than her usual afternoon. Amber has no medical complaints, but quite a number of psychosocial problems which she readily shares with the student once she is certain that what she says will be held in confidence. She has been coming to this doctor's office since she was very young, so she is very comfortable there. Today, she is seeing a medical student, whom she has never met before.

Your challenge, as the standardized patient is:

**To portray a relapsed smoker who has returned to smoking because she’s having a hard time staying quit around her friends.**

<u>**Presentation/Emotional Tone**</u>

You are dressed in a plain colored skirt and white blouse, the accepted dress code at your Catholic high school. You are wearing no makeup except lip gloss, and you have a little heart locket or small gold crucifix hanging around your neck. Your hair is long and loose; however, you wear a "scrunchie" around your wrist, which you use to pull your hair back during school hours. As soon as school is over, you take your hair down and wear the elastic ribbon around your wrist like a bracelet. You play with it repeatedly during the interview. You are bored by the office visit, but not overly hostile; at least it is more interesting than sitting around the house. You get this across by your tone of voice and demeanor. You answer in short, clipped sentences, as though it is an effort for you to exert yourself to make conversation.

When asked why you have come to the office, you reply,

**"I don't know, my mom caught me smoking and she freaked out."**

<u>**Medical History**</u>

You have never been in the hospital or had any serious illnesses.

<u>**Family Smoking History**</u>

Your mother has told you repeatedly that both her mother and father smoked, her mother died of emphysema, and her father died of bladder cancer. She has told you many times in the last week that they both died from cigarettes, they were both very uncomfortable in their last months, and she doesn't want you to die that way too. She likes to say, "I promised myself after I saw how they died, that I would never smoke, and that none of my children would smoke." Your father and his family don't smoke.

You have a younger brother, aged 14 and a younger sister, aged 11.  Neither smokes nor knows that you smoke.

<u>**Drug/Alcohol Use**</u>

**"I drink sometimes on weekends, but I don’t drink too much."**

If asked specifically, if they have said that the conversation is confidential, and only if you trust the student:

**"I have vodka with lemonade mix."**

**"I have passed out twice."**

**"I have never been caught."**

**"I tried weed a few times, but didn't really like it."**

<u>**Smoking History**</u>

You first smoked about 3 years ago, when a friend asked you if you wanted to try. She had taken a pack of cigarettes from her father, and wanted to try them. The first time you tried them, you felt sick, and didn't like it at all. You thought it made you look cool, and you and your friend eventually smoked the whole pack over the next couple of months. About two years ago, you started bumming cigarettes from your friends, and smoking every couple of days. You buy a pack every two days, smoking about 8-10 per day, and giving the rest to friends. About one year ago, you found a cigarette machine in a college dorm where it is easy to buy a pack, and started digging through your pockets to see if you had enough. You had recently noticed how short on cash you’d been because of how much you spent on cigarettes. You had also just seen a commercial about how tobacco companies manipulate people to smoke, and about all the people that die every year because of it. You decided to quit. This was about three months ago. You really wanted a cigarette, but forced yourself not to, and were proud of your willpower. The friends you hang out with were not supportive. They blew smoke in your face, offered you a cig every time they saw you, and made fun of you for declining. After staying quit for a couple of months, even when school was stressing you out, you gave in and started smoking again. It was just too hard to be around your friends when they’re smoking, and you don’t want to lose them.

<u>**Activities**</u>

Nothing is fun, and you are commonly bored. You work at McDonalds, making fries. You have no favorite classes at school, and don't participate in school sports or activities now that your mother made you quit soccer. You get As and Bs in school, and plan on going to a college out of state (to get away from your mother). Eventually you want to be a lawyer.

<u>**Patient Interview**</u>

If the student gives you information about the dangers of smoking (e.g., The leading preventable cause of death), say,

**"Yeah, I tried to quit, but I can’t.”**

If the student warns you of long-term health risks of smoking (e.g., Smoking causes heart disease, cancer, and lung disease), say,

**"It takes a long time for those things to happen. I'm not really worried.”**

If the student asks you how much you pay for cigarettes, say,

**"Tons, that’s why I quit the first time. I was spending over \$12 a week”**

If they talk about short term risks of smoking, say,

**"My boyfriend and my friends all smoke. Besides, I smoked for years without getting caught; I just chew gum before I go home. The one thing I really don’t like is the smell on my hair."**

If a student asks about why you started smoking again:

**“I was talking to a friend after a test. I thought I did really bad cause she kept telling me all the answers I got wrong. She was smoking and offered me a cigarette. I said I would just smoke one to calm me down. I could have said “no”; I wasn’t really all that stressed, but I think she would have been hurt. She was trying to help. The next day, I was back to smoking ½ of a pack.”**

If they ask about the negative consequences of smoking, say,

**“Cost.”**

**“Mom being upset/grounding me.”**

**“Having to quit the soccer team.”**

If they ask about the positive aspects of smoking,

**Say nothing, and look down or away, in response to the first or 	second time they ask you about this.**

If they ask a third time, still in a nice way, say,

**“Being with friends and hanging out.”**

**“I don’t have a choice. I’m addicted and can’t stop.”**

If they ask in a mean way, refuse to even think about it.

Only if they bring up the real down sides do you admit that there are negative consequences for you.  They are:

**“Cost.”**

**“Mom being upset/grounding me.”**

**“Having to quit the soccer team.”**
