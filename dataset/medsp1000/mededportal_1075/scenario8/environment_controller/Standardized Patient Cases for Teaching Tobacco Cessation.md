<u>**Case Summary**</u>

Lisa is a 35 year old single office worker who quit smoking three weeks ago. She is in the office for a routine gynecological exam. She has been slightly overweight all her life (about ten pounds) but sees herself as “huge.” She weighs herself every morning and has been on several fad diets.

Her father died several weeks ago and she made a promise to him as he was dying that she would quit smoking. He had lung cancer and did not want the same thing to happen to her. She feels quite strongly about holding to her commitment but is very upset that she has already gained three pounds since she quit. She gained seven pounds the last time she quit (two years ago) and never lost it. She has now decided that if she gains five pounds she will go back to smoking, no matter what she told her father.

Lisa works as a loan processor, so she sits at a desk all day. She gets no physical activity whatsoever. In fact, she drives everywhere, even to the convenience store that is three blocks from her house. She has an Egg McMuffin on the way to work every morning and does not eat another meal until dinner. She claims to be too busy at work to take a lunch break. She grew up in an Eastern European household, so she eats lots of eggs, bread, kielbasa, sausage, pork, and potatoes. She loves butter and fries most of her meats.

**Your role is to portray a recently quit smoker who is having trouble maintaining the quit because of a fear of gaining weight. She has convinced herself that smoking is the** <u>**only**</u> **plausible way to keep from gaining weight.**

<u>**Presentation/Emotional Tone**</u>

Lisa presents as somewhat desperate and confrontational. She really wants to keep her word to her father (she adored him) but can’t stand the thought of gaining even five pounds. She is insistent that the MD do something for her to keep her from gaining weight. She wants some type of medication to reduce her appetite or she will go back to smoking.

When the student asks why you have come to the office, you reply,

**"I’m sort of desperate, doctor. I just quit smoking three weeks ago. I made a promise to my dying father that I would quit but I’ve already gained three pounds. That’s one pound a week! I just know I’m going to get huge.”**

**“I decided that if I gain more than five pounds I’m going to go back to smoking. But I don’t want to break my promise to my dad.”**

**“My doctor has been telling me for years to quit. Now, I have, but see what’s happening. What are you going to do about it?”**

**“If you want me to stay quit, you better give me something to make sure I don’t gain any more weight.”**

<u>**Your Smoking History**</u>

You started smoking in high school when one of your girl friends told you that it would help keep your weight down. You have been smoking ever since, about a pack a day. You quit once two years ago when you started dating someone who did not like your smoking. You were quit for the six months that you dated and had no problem maintaining that quit. However, when you broke up, you became upset and started smoking again. You gained about seven pounds during that six month quit and did not lose it once you started smoking again.

<u>**Family Smoking History**</u>

Your father recently died at age 58 from lung cancer. Your mother never smoked. One of your siblings smokes; the other does not.

<u>**Current Quitting History**</u>

You are using the 21-milligram patch and are having no withdrawal. In fact, you feel fine. However, you have started snacking on M and M’s all day at work.  You are enrolled in the behavior modification program that comes with your patches but have not read much of the material. The only changes you have made are reading in the evening instead of watching TV and listening to talk radio in the car. These techniques help take your mind off of thinking about cigarettes. You also have two girl friends that are your support network. You are comfortable calling them when you are worried or have an urge.

<u>**Height/Weight**</u>

You are 5’4” and weigh 140 pounds.

<u>**Drug/Alcohol Use**</u>

You drink socially, but not on a weekly basis. You used marijuana occasionally in high school and college, but it’s been ten years since you have done this.

<u>**Diet**</u>

You have extremely poor eating habits. You eat Egg McMuffins for breakfast everyday and then drink coffee and cola all day long. You never have lunch**“I’m too busy.”** Dinner is usually high in fat and carbohydrates. You fry almost everything. You eat lots of cheese, potatoes, sausage, pork, and bread. You bake a lot so there are always cookies, cakes, and pies in the house. You rarely eat vegetables.

<u>**Exercise**</u>

You do absolutely no exercise or activity. You do not walk anywhere even to the corner store. You cannot see yourself in any type of formal exercise program and would never go to a gym. **“I could never get into leotards in front of a man.”**

<u>**Patient Interview**</u>

Throughout the interview you should ask the student about any and every diet/weight loss program you can think of , e.g. , Slim Fast, The Grapefruit Diet, Atkins, etc. Even ask, at some point, if he or she will recommend gastric bypass.

**“Can’t you give me some type of pill that will make me stop eating? I’m afraid that without nicotine in my body I’m not going to be able to burn off enough calories to keep from gaining weight. So I need some kind of extra help. ”**

*If the student asks* you about your quit plan, explain that you are using the patch and are having no problems. **But do indicate that you have started to snack on M and M’s all day at work during the last week. You are eating about two bags a day now**. Also explain the behavioral changes you have started (reading in the evening instead of watching TV and listening to talk radio in the car). *If the student asks*, you may also reveal that you have a strong support system (your two girl friends) and that you feel the quit is going fine except for the fact that you have started to gain weight.

If the student says something to the effect that five pounds is not a lot of weight to gain, come back with,

**“I can’t afford to gain another pound. I have a hard enough time getting a date now. If I’m a blimp, I’ll never meet anyone.”**

If the student begins to discuss healthy eating/low fat diet, etc., ask him or her where you can get information about this. Indicate that you would like to join some type of support group.

**“Do you know of any good program in the area? I need help. Should I join something now?”**

If the student suggests that you not worry about weight gain for the next few months (until you are comfortable being a nonsmoker) **reluctantly agree** but ask if there is anything you can do in the meantime:

If the student begins to discuss exercise be very negative about joining a gym or doing anything formal. **However, agree to start walking**. You work with one of your support people and she has suggested that you take a walk on your breaks. You will at least do that.

**Zyban and the gum have been shown to minimize weight gain as a result of quitting so if the student suggests that you switch or add either of these products, agree to make the change.**
