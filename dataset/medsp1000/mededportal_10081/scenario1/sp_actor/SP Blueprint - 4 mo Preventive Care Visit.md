| **Diagnosis** | This is a routine visit for preventive care of a 4 month old. |
| **Brief summary** | You are Paulette Novelle and you are here today with your baby, Jules Novelle, for his 4 month routine care. A number of concerns are scripted into this scenario.   * You have had no breaks. You're becoming burned out. * You sleep with the baby, and wonder if this is safe. * Sometimes baby Jules is inconsolable, crying for long periods of time. * Feeding and sleeping have been on an irregular schedule. * Baby is not starting to eat solid food yet. * You sometimes use honey to sweeten foods for the baby. * Still does not sleep through the night. * Your husband smokes. * Your husband had a recent job change. * You do not have rear-facing car seats.   No physical exam will be performed during this visit. |

**SP Training Notes**

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Case Name** | Paulette Novelle and baby Jules |
| **Presenting situation** | Routine 4 month preventive care visit with a 24 year old new mother with her 4 month old baby boy |
| **Psychosocial profile** | * Mother is cooperative, a little concerned * Basically a typical new mother. |
| **Outlook and Coping** | * When questioned about baby’s behavior, some frustration shows because of the occasional episodes of inconsolable crying * Mother emotes regarding her exhaustion from sleep deprivation because of the need for feedings every four hours. |
| **Opening statement** | “He’s asleep right now. “That’s good, because he’s been cranky all week”. |
| **History of present illness** | * You were married two years ago and this is your first baby. The pregnancy went well and was uncomplicated. Baby Jules (named after your husband’s grandfather pronounced and *“zhool”)* is healthy and strong. * It has now been four months. Baby is growing and developing quickly. * There are some problems, however, and you want to talk with the pediatrician about them. |
| **Past medical history** | * Over the last four months, you have been with the baby continuously with no breaks. **You're becoming burned out.** * In the evening, you retire to your bedroom with a book, and bring the baby with you. **You both fall asleep in bed**. The baby wakes up hungry several times each night. You get him a bottle (you are not breast-feeding), and you both fall asleep again after the baby feeds. Because of the sleep disruption, you're becoming fatigued. * You also wonder if **sleeping in the same bed** is safe for the baby. * Sometimes baby Jules is **irritable**, even inconsolable. He will cry and fuss for long periods of time. Eating doesn’t help. Carrying him does not help. The only thing that makes things better is going for a ride in the car (he will fall asleep in his car seat). * He still maintains an irregular schedule, napping during the day and periodically **awakening during the night**. This is disrupting your sleep, and leaving you fatigued. * Your husband had a **recent job change**. He is a printer at the local newspaper. He now sets up the printers to run through the night so that the morning edition is ready for distribution. Therefore, he is home sleeping during the day. This is disrupting your routine. * Your **husband smokes**, although he is careful not to smoke around the baby. * You have an old, **forward facing car seat** that you got from a friend. You have heard that rear facing car seats are safer. * You use **honey** as a sweetener yourself for tea, hot cereal, and other foods. Sometimes you have given some of these foods to baby Jules. |
| **Social history** | * You have been happily married for two years. * Before becoming pregnant, you worked as a legal assistant. You would like to get back to work soon. * Your parents live in Beaumont, TX. Your **in-laws** live in Port Arthur. They are **too far away to help with child care**. |
| **Family history** | * Both parents are alive and well. * You have an older sister who has two children; both healthy. * Your husband has two siblings; both healthy. |
| **Risk Behaviors** | * Tobacco: your **husband smokes**. * You do not smoke. * You drink occasionally. * You did not drink during the pregnancy. * You do not use illicit drugs. |
| **Physical exam findings** | * No physical exam will be performed today. * Wt 14 lbs * Length 24.5 inches |
| **Special instructions** | * You will be holding a lifelike doll wrapped in a baby blanket. Indicate to the student that baby is sleeping (fortunately). * Go easy on the students, as this is one of their first encounters with a standardized patient, and they will likely be nervous. * Consider adding some of the following details about nutrition   + Baby is **still bottle feeding every four hours**, which is taxing on you.   + Baby is still **not eating any solids**. (Why is that? Baby is still unable to sit without support. To start solids infants must be able to hold head steady and sit with assistance.) |
