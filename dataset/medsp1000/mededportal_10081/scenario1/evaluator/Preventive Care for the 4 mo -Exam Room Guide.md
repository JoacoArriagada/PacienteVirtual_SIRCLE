| **Context**  The 4 month visit is an important visit. The luster of new parenthood is dimming and is being replaced by the sober reality of the challenges inherent in child-raising. In addition, this is a time when the baby is rapidly growing and changing. If a problem is to emerge, the 4 month visit is probably when it will be mentioned for the first time to the clinician.  Here are the steps that we recommend for the routine care of a 4 month old. |  |
| **History**  Update the **interval history** (for this session, assume that the baby has been seen in the clinic before and is an established patient).  Identify if the baby has any **special needs** for health care.   * *"Does your baby have any special needs such as a special diet or program?"*   Review the Bright Futures questionnaire that the parent filled out in the waiting room. Identify any potential **concerns or issues** that might impact the baby’s health.   * *“Do you have any concerns that you would like to talk about today?"*   Review and update the **medication list**.   * *"Is your child taking any medications?"* | Of course, if this was a new patient to your clinic, you would take a complete history.  “Special needs” is a broad term that includes a wide range of problems. Anything that requires special attention and intervention can be considered a special need.  Bright Futures is a trademark of the American Academy of Pediatrics. This questionnaire is used with permission.  List the generic name of the medication, the dose, and the frequency of administration. Include all medications, even over the counter medications and herbal remedies.  During the interview, review the **previsit questionnaire2**.  **![](data:image/png;base64...)**  **Normal development at 4 months**  **Social and Emotional**  Smiles spontaneously, especially at people  Likes to play with people; might cry when playing stops  Copies some movements and facial expressions, like  smiling or frowning  **Language/Communication**  Begins to babble  Babbles with expression and copies sounds he hears  Cries in different ways to show hunger, pain, or being  tired  **Cognitive**  Lets you know if she is happy or sad  Responds to affection  Reaches for toy with one hand  Uses hands and eyes together, such as seeing a toy and  reaching for it  Follows moving things with eyes from side to side  Watches faces closely  Recognizes familiar people and things at a distance  **Movement/Physical Development**  Holds head steady, unsupported  Pushes down on legs when feet are on a hard surface  May be able to roll over from tummy to back  Can hold a toy and shake it and swing at dangling toys  Brings hands to mouth  When lying on stomach, pushes up to elbows |
| **Social/Family History**  **Family situation**   * *“Are you working outside the home?”*   + *“Does your spouse work outside the home?”*   + *“How many days do you work each week?”*   + *“How long are you at work each day?”* * *"Do you use child care?”*   + *“What type of child care?"* * *“Any recent changes in your family situation?”*   **Safety**  *“Do you use a rear-facing child safety seat?”*  *“Is your baby food ever prepared with honey or eggs?”*  **Development**  **Physical activity** (assessed either through questions asked to the parent or through direct observation of the baby).   * *“Does you baby push his chest up to elbows?”* * *“Does you baby have good head control?”* * *“Does your baby move the same on the left and right side of his body?”* * *“Have you seen your baby roll and reach for objects?”*   **Cognitive**   * *“How does your baby respond to affection?”* * *“Does he express pleasure and displeasure?”*   **Communication**   * *“Does he sometimes make expressive babbling sounds?”*   **Social-emotional**   * *“Does he smile when he sees a face?”* * *“Does he seek attention and social interactions?”* * *“When he is upset, can he calm himself down on his own?”* |
| **Review of systems**  **Nutrition**   * “*Are you breast-feeding?”*   + *“How many feedings each day?”*   + *“How many hours between feedings?”*   + *“How many minutes per feeding?”*   + *“How is with breast-feeding going?”* * “*Are you formula feeding?"*   + *"How many ounces per feeding?"*   + *“What is the source of water?*   + *“Are you giving vitamins and fluoride?”*   + *“How is bottle-feeding going?”* | **Concerning signs** that should be brought to the doctor’s attention:  Doesn’t watch things as they move  Doesn’t smile at people  Can’t hold head steady  Doesn’t coo or make sounds  Doesn’t bring things to mouth  Doesn’t push down with legs when feet are placed on a hard surface  Has trouble moving  one or both eyes in all directions  **Immunizations:** Review the shot record. | **Anticipatory guidance**  Based on the parents’ concerns and questions as well as any active problems that you identify during the visit, select topics from the list below to discuss with the parent.  **Nutritional Adequacy and Growth**   * Breast-feeding (vitamin D, iron supplements) * Iron fortified formula * Solid foods when and how to add * Weight gain and growth spurts * Elimination   **Family Functioning**  **Infant Development**   * Social development * Communication skills * Physical (tummy time) * Daily routines * Sleep | **Oral Health**   * Don't share utensils/pacifier * Avoid bottle in bed   **Safety**   * Car safety seats * Food risks   + Honey   + Eggs * Burns   + hot liquids   + water heaters * Falls * Walkers * Choking * Drowning * Lead poisoning | |
