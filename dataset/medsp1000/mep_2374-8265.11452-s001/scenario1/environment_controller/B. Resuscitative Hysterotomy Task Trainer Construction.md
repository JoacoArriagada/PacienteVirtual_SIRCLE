Appendix B: Construction of Resuscitative Hysterotomy Task Trainer

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 1: Two aluminum trays were stacked together to form base support |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 2: A bedsheet was folded and placed in the aluminum trays to resemble “intra-abdominal organs” |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 3: A red-colored string was attached to a “baby” doll to resemble an “umbilical cord”, while the other end of the string was attached to a crumpled kitchen cling wrap, resembling a “placenta” |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 4: A 40 cm beach ball was painted red to resemble a “uterus”. The top of the beach ball was cut to create a small opening |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 5: The “baby”, “umbilical cord”, “placenta” and a water-filled balloon (resembling amniotic fluid sac) were packed into the beach ball. The open end of the beach ball was wrapped with a rubber band. The wrapped beach ball was placed on top of the folded bedsheet. A water-filled glove was placed just below the “uterus” to resemble the “bladder” |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 6: One layer of red foam sheet was placed to resemble “abdominal muscle” |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 7: One layer of cream felt cloth was placed to resemble “subcutaneous tissue” |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 8: The entire model was wrapped with a few layers of kitchen cling wrap to resemble “skin”. Landmarks were drawn using a marker pen on the kitchen wrap to indicate subcostal margin, umbilicus and inguinal crease |
| ![](data:image/png;base64...) | Step 9: The model was assembled with human manikin (without the abdomen component) as a resuscitative hysterotomy task trainer |
