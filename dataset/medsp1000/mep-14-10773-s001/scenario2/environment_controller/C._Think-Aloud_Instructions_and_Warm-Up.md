**Think Aloud Instructions and Procedures**

**Step 1: Planning to integrate think-alouds**

When considering using think alouds be sure to schedule enough time to review the entire video. For example, if the participant’s scenario runs 20 minutes then allow for the at least the same amount of time for the think-aloud. We encourage adding an additional 3-5 minutes to allow for a smooth transition at the end of the think aloud.

**Step 2: Prepare the video for re-watching**

A private room (ensuring privacy and a comfortable space for thinking aloud ) should be set aside for re-watching the videos. Prior to re-watching, a study team member ensures the participant’s video is ready for viewing. This helps save time and minimizes disruptions while thinking aloud. In the event think alouds are being recorded, ensure the audio recorder(s) are ready for use. A small microphone can be used to help improve audio quality.

**Step 3: Read the think prompt below (read by a researcher or educator)**

*“I am going to ask you to “think aloud” as you watch your video. I want you to describe how you arrived at your diagnosis and treatment for the patient. Please say your thoughts out loud from the moment we start the video until your final diagnosis and treatment is reached. Say as much as you feel comfortable saying. Don’t try to plan or explain what you say, just act as if you were speaking to yourself. Any questions?"*

**Step 4: Begin video**

While the participant begins rewatching the video and thinking aloud the researcher or educator neither talks nor asks any questions until the participant has completed viewing the video and thinking aloud. During the think aloud the researcher or education can acknowledges participant thinking aloud by using ‘uh huh’ or ‘hmm’(as described by Ericsson and Simon[[1]](#footnote-1)). If and when the participant pauses thinking aloud for an extended period of time (approximately 3-5 seconds) the researcher or educator should nudge the participant by saying ‘think aloud.’

**Step 5: Wrapping up**

Once the participant completes rewatching the video and thinking aloud, the researcher or educator can ask follow up questions.
