**Student Objectives:**

1. Increase understanding of the role and scope of practice of different health care disciplines in multiple settings.
2. Effectively communicate with other health care team members.
3. Collaborate as a team in developing patient-centered treatment strategies and goals.
4. Effectively engage the patient as an active member of the team.
5. Increase understanding of the widespread influences of obesity on a patient’s acute treatment and long-term behavior change.

**Facilitator Role:**

1. The role of the facilitator is to guide students through the discussion of the case study – not to be content experts. We understand you each provide a unique perspective, and do not anticipate you being comfortable with all aspects of these multidisciplinary cases!
2. Facilitators will provide guidance to students before and after they interview the simulated patients (see Table 1).
3. Discipline-specific questions /answers have been compiled (see Table 2 provided) and given to the simulated patients. We have included these questions to help you facilitate discussion related to each discipline in regard to key questions and issues, particularly if they are having difficulty generating questions.

**General set-up:**

* Refer to Table 1 for details of how these small group sessions will proceed.
* Each small group will have approximately 8 students from several disciplines represented. This means some disciplines will have more than one student representative.
* All students will have received the case prior to the session. The case presents what occurred on Pat Martin’s first visit to a community based clinic. The IPE session will focus on Pat returning for a follow-up visit aimed specifically at addressing issues related to obesity. To prepare students, faculty from each discipline will have discussed the main information and issues their discipline would want to pursue during this return visit prior to the session.

**Pharmacy Questions & Answers for SP**

**Question 1: Any adverse effects from the use of the analgesics?**

*I am particularly interested in whether they have noticed any upset stomach with the use of the ibuprofen. I see from the history that they have a history of heartburn and constipation. GERD is common in obese patients so I would like to know if the patient attributed any of this to the use of the ibuprofen. Patient can say that they do suffer from heartburn but really haven’t noticed that it is associated with their pain medication. SP can say that they have never tried something for the constipation or the heartburn, particularly if asked about OTC drugs.*

**Question 2: Follow up on the alcohol use.**

*Two to three per day is reported but we would like to know if that is every night or perhaps there is binging or an underestimation of use. Excess use of alcohol can have a negative effect on weight and the combination of alcohol and acetaminophen is particularly hepatotoxic. I would want to say that the use of alcohol is probably an average of 2-3 a day with more on days off and sometimes to help get some sleep. It would be helpful to know if beer is all the patient drinks, interested in calorie consumption here. Answer would be just beer.*

**Question 3: What exactly is the dose of ibuprofen that is used?**

*Case is written as 200-400 mg every six hours and the narrative says “2-3 pills every four hours”. Let’s have the patient say that they usually take two tablets (400 mg) but on occasion will take three tablets (600 mg).*

**Question 4: Does the ibuprofen and or acetaminophen actually relieve the pain?**

*Medication apparently helps get them through the day, but it is unclear just how much relief they receive. If good relief we may wish to increase the frequency, if inadequate may want to bump the dose. It would also be good to know if one works better than another. Let’s have the SP say that they never get complete relief from either of the two but that 3 ibuprofen tablets will usually control most of the pain. Acetaminophen and lower doses of ibuprofen work less well.*

**Question 5: Do you have insurance that would help cover the cost of prescription medications?**

*This is particularly relevant if we decide to start treating her metabolic syndrome. The answer is NO.*

**Question 6: Have you ever taken over the counter or prescription medication to help you lose weight**?

*It might be appropriate to give some examples.   Alli®(pronounced ally, as in your ally in weight loss), Xenical® or phentermine.  There is also any number of “natural” concoctions they might have used.*

*They do not claim a history of any prescription drug use in the written history.  It might be easiest for them to just deny it.  Or if you want to spice it up you might have them admit to trying an herbal preparation but it didn’t seem to work and it made them feel jumpy.*
