**Gynecologic cases:**

**CASE ONE Diagnosis: Post-operative hypovolemia**

**Page: 48 year old G2P2 postoperative day #0 (POD#0) after Total Abdominal Hysterectomy/Bilateral Salpingo-oophorectomy (TAH/BSO) with decreased urine output**

Scripted info: I just emptied the Foley bag for 40 cc in last two hours**.**

*If asked can share:*

Patient is sleeping, comfortable. She is healthy with no past medical history.

Current Vitals: BP: 95/50, RR 18, HR 120

I/O:2500/1000 from OR. Urine output has been 40 cc over last two hours

Meds: IVF (LR) @ 80 cc/hour, Morphine PCA, Toradol 30 mg IV q 6 hours

Surgical EBL: 500cc.

Nursing exam: Alert, in NAD, Heart: RRR, Lungs: CTAB,

Abdomen: Soft, tender at surgical site. Dressing clean/dry/intact

**Key teaching points:** The patient’s oliguria and tachycardia illustrate the common early signs of hypovolemia. Her benign abdominal exam should provide a reassurance that she does not have a hemoperitoneum, however the student should state that s/he will come see the patient with the goal of assessing the patient in person. This case provides an opportunity to emphasize importance of adequate fluid resuscitation in the immediate post-operative period, and the difference between IV replacement versus IV maintenance rates.
