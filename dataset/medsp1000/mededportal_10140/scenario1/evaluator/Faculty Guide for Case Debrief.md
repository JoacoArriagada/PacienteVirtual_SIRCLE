**Obstetrical cases:**

**CASE ONE Diagnosis: Post-partum vulvar/vaginal hematoma**

**PAGE: 39 yo G1P0 post-operative day #0 after vaginal delivery, with pain.**

Scripted Info: NSVD, 3rd degree laceration, 8 lb 12 oz infant, delivered one hour ago. She has increasing pain since the delivery

*If asked, can share:*

Prolonged second stage of 4 hours. Did not have anesthesia for the delivery, but has received 10 mg of morphine postpartum. Pain is located on the left labia and patient is writhing in bed from pain.

Current Vitals: BP: 90/60, RR 18, HR 110, EBL 250cc

I/O: Has not voided for 6 hours

Meds: IVF (LR) @ 100 cc/ hour.

General: Alert, oriented, very uncomfortable

Abdomen: soft, non-tender, uterus firm, at level of umbilicus.

Vulvar: Significant edema of left vulva 4- 6 cm, expanding since delivery

**Key teaching points:** Unexpected, disproportional pain should be evaluated, especially in post-partum and post-operative patients. The goal of this case is to reinforce the importance of the evaluation of pain after a vaginal delivery, and the importance of acknowledging nursing colleagues when they express concern in an ordinarily routine post-partum situation. Post-partum vulvar hematomas are not common clinical occurrences, however they should be on a clinician’s differential diagnosis when a patient has increasing pain after a vaginal delivery.
