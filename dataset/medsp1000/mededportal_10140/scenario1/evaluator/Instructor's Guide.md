**Educational Goal:**

The educational goal was to create a simulated paging curriculum to prepare learners for obstetrics and gynecology residency. The curriculum consists of eight obstetrical and gynecology cases, and includes rubrics on the assessment and management of these scenarios. The cases were created by a multi-disciplinary team of obstetrics and gynecology residents, clerkship and program leadership, and Certified Nurse Midwives (CNMs). They consist of common paging scenarios such as tachycardia in the post-operative unit, and post-partum pain. Students are assessed on Medical Knowledge, Professionalism, and Interpersonal and Communication Milestone competencies. At the completion of each case, students receive immediate feedback from the CNM who has performed the paging simulation.

**Educational Objectives:**

At the completion of this curriculum, learners will be able to:

1. Generate a differential diagnosis for pain in the immediate post-partum period.
2. Recognize signs and symptoms of post-operative hypovolemia.
3. Order initial steps of IV fluid resuscitation for post-operative hypovolemia.
4. Form a differential diagnosis for post-operative tachycardia.
5. Form a differential diagnosis for post-operative abdominal pain.
6. Recognize signs of post-operative urinary retention.
7. Exhibit professional behavior by returning pages in a timely fashion.
8. Demonstrate respectful communication with nursing colleagues.

**Implementation Advice:**

This paging curriculum took place during a four-week residency preparation elective for fourth year medical students, already matched in obstetrics and gynecology programs. The curriculum was created to assess Medical Knowledge, Professionalism and Interpersonal and Communication competencies. CNMs from our department paged the students, played the role of clinical bedside nurse, assessed student performance, and provided immediate feedback. This clinical team model was chosen purposefully. Many labor and delivery units have CNM-staffed obstetrical triage units and/or have a CNM service to which the obstetrician physicians serve as consults. CNMs at our institution are also active teachers of third year medical students during the obstetrics and gynecology clerkship, and were thus accustomed to being very engaged in medical student education. The labor and delivery triage unit at our institution is staffed by CNMs 24 hours a day, and the CNMs in triage volunteered their time for this curriculum. They paged the medical students when there were lulls in patient care activities in triage. The process of paging the student, performing the case, and filling out the evaluation took approximately seven minutes per case. The CNM leadership emphasized the service and educational value of performing these pages for our medical students. Other programs have described paying nurses to perform this role in their general surgery simulated paging curriculum.9

We had a one-hour meeting with the director of the CNM triage unit, and went through the learning objectives, paging scripts, and scoring instructions for each of the cases. She then trained the 10 CNMs during a two-hour training session. Individual folders, labeled with each student’s name, were placed in the CNM workspace in triage. Each folder contained all eight cases, individually pre-labeled with the student’s name and pager number. The cases contain standardized scripts containing clinical information, with specific clinical details conveyed only if specifically requested by the student. The Medical Knowledge and Patient Care assessment utilized scoring rubrics on clinical assessment and management. Professionalism was assessed based on timeliness when answering pages. Interpersonal and Communication competencies were assessed by the CNM utilizing a 5-point Likert Scale. For reference, we provided example descriptions of ideal communication and teamwork characteristics on the last page of the assessment form. Lastly, the CNM also decided if the student was globally competent or not for each scenario (competent or not competent). The CNM completed each individual page, filled out the assessments, and provided immediate feedback to the student. The folders were left in the CNM workspace, for our goal was to create a smooth and easy workflow for the volunteer CNMs.

The paging curriculum started with a one-hour introduction to the paging curriculum session with the medical students. During this session, we discuss the learning objectives for the curriculum, and discuss the practical logistics of the curriculum. We instruct the students during this session that they could be paged 24 hours a day during the duration of the curriculum. A paging debrief session was held with the students at the completion of the curriculum to review the eight cases and to reinforce important clinical teaching points. A faculty guide with key clinical teaching points for each case is included in this curriculum. Students received individual feedback about any Professionalism concerns that arose from the paging curriculum assessments.
