**Obstetrical cases:**

**CASE THREE Diagnosis: Post-partum urinary retention**

**PAGE: 30 yo G1P0 post-operative day #1 status post Cesarean Section for Arrest of Descent, with inability to void.**

Scripted Info: Foley catheter was removed 6 hours ago. The patient has tried twice to void without success

*If asked, can share:*

Patient is eating, comfortable, breastfeeding without a problem.

Current Vitals: BP 100/50, RR 18, HR 94

I/O: Urine output: 70 cc/hour when foley in. Total I/O: 2200/950. EBL 1000cc.

Meds: IVF: Hep lock this AM, Percocet 5/325 1-2 PO q 6 hours prn, Motrin 600 mg PO q 6 hours prn.

Nursing exam: Alert, oriented, NAD.

Abdomen: Soft, non-distended, non-tender. Uterus firm, 2 fingerbreaths above umbilicus.

**Key teaching points:** Post-partum patients can have slow return of bladder function after either a vaginal delivery or cesarean section. It is important that urinary retention be recognized expediently given that over distension of the bladder can lead to further bladder dysfunction or even bladder rupture. It is important to emphasize that if a patient has not been able to void in six hours, then the nurse should perform a catheterization (or bladder scan) to assess the bladder volume. If a patient has urinary retention, then intermittent self-catheterization should be performed until there are two consecutive residual volumes less than 100cc. Alternatively, a Foley catheter can be placed and a trial of void can be performed in 24 hours, or in 3-7 days.
