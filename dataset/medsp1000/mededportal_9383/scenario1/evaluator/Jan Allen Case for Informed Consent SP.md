**“Jan Allen”**

**Authors: Elizabeth Hager, Gretchen Diemer, MD**

<u>**CASE SUMMARY**</u>

You are Jan Allen, a 45+ year old Produce Manager at Whole Foods Grocery.

You have come to the hospital for treatment of symptoms and will now be asked to sign consent forms for a blood transfusion due to being anemic. The following information is given to fill out your character, but your main concern is focused on why you have to sign consent forms.

<u>**History of Present Illness:**</u>

For the last 3 weeks you have felt an increased inability to walk ½ a block without experiencing extreme shortness of breath. Breathing is otherwise fine.  Other associated symptoms include feeling mildly lightheaded when you stand, passage of dark stools, and increased appetite for ice. No chest pain or loss of consciousness. Your most recent physical exam reveals that your vital signs are stable except for a mildly elevated heart rate and a positive heme occult test (shows a slow bleed in you GI). You have been diagnosed with symptomatic anemia (low red blood cells) from a slow bleed in your gastro-intestinal system.  It needs further workup but is not immediately life threatening.

<u>**Past Medical History**</u>**:** 

**High blood pressure**

**High cholesterol**[genetic]).

<u>Surgeries</u>: None.

<u>Hospitalizations</u>: None

<u>Medications</u>: HCTZ 25mg daily, simvastatin 40mg daily

<u>**Social History:**</u> Occupation: Produce Manager at Whole Foods.  No children.  You are recently divorced (within the last 3 years). You are currently dating and are sexually active with significant other only.  You are using birth control (if age appropriate).  You live alone.  You have no pets and do not smoke.  You usually drink with meals or when socializing with friends.  Alcohol or substance abuse is not an issue for you.

You are a high school graduate but have pursued no other education.  You can read at a basic level (understand most things in the newspaper) but do not do so for pleasure.  You can balance your checkbook.  You do not understand medical jargon and have to ask for clarification for any “technical” medical words.  You see your physician 2-3 times a year for your blood pressure and high cholesterol, but are uncomfortable in a medical setting and have some anxiety with discussions of your medical problems.

<u>**Family History:**</u> Mother has high blood pressure and controls it with medication, otherwise she is fine.  Father died of a heart attack at age 54.  Older brother had a heart attack at age 43, but is doing well now. You have a younger sister who is healthy.

<u>**Review of Symptoms:**</u> You have shortness of breath upon walking ½ block, have mild lightheadedness when standing up and are increasingly ravenous for ice and water.

**What you want from this visit:** 

To understand the indications, risks and alternatives to a blood transfusion; sign consent forms.

<u>**APPEARANCE AND BEHAVIOR**</u>

**Physical Appearance**:

You should be in a hospital gown. Socks, bra, tank tops, sweat pants allowed under gowns.

**Behavior:**

You are concerned that you are having shortness of breath issues and are feeling lightheaded when standing up. Your symptoms have increasingly worsened over the past 3 weeks. You are concerned about the blood transfusion and your manner should be open but slightly anxious.  If asked to read the consent form, you should try for a moment and then reply “I don’t understand all this medical jargon.”

If asked to explain back what the doctor has explained to you, repeat only things that were explained simply without jargon.  Otherwise reply “I’m not sure exactly.”

**Response to Physical Exam Maneuvers:**

NO Physical Exam.

NO History Exam.

<u>**CHALLENGE**</u>**:**  At 5 min. announcement, if you haven’t brought up this question already, you should find a way to ask “DO I HAVE TO SIGN THIS FORM?” or “DO I HAVE ANY OTHER CHOICES OTHER THAN TO SIGN THIS FORM?”

<u>**PROPS NEEDED:**</u>
