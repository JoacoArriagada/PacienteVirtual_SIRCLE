**Case Study 1**:

AS is a 20-year old male undergraduate student. He immigrated to the United States from Malaysia for school. He comes to your clinic for a routine check-up before the school year starts. His vitals are normal, and a CBC, CMP, LFT, Lipid Panel, and Vitamin D level are normal as well. No other blood work was done. AS tells you that homosexual activities are illegal in Malaysia, and as such he has lied to his previous doctors in Malaysia about having intercourse with other men for fear of getting arrested. He admits to having intercourse with multiple men in high school in Malaysia, but has been in a stable, monogamous, sexually active relationship for 2 years now. He has private insurance through his college, although his medication copay is high.

**Case Study 1 Follow Up Questions**:

1. Based on the information provided, does AS qualify for PrEP?

*From the information provided in this case, it sounds like AS may qualify for PrEP. We cannot make a definitive determination yet.*

1. If yes, why, if not, what other tests need to be done to check?

*AS should receive an HIV screening to ensure he is HIV-negative. Along with this test, he should also have an STD/STI screening panel. AS should also have a kidney function test to ensure his kidneys are functioning well-enough to safely take PrEP.*

1. Does it matter that he or his partner take PrEP if they’ve been monogamous for two years?

*There is more information needed here as well. AS should be asked what he means by ‘monogamous.’ Different people have different definitions for this term, and it may involve additional partners with the consent of both AS and his partner, or a similar arrangement. AS should also be asked to give further information about his use of protection with his partner or any other sexual encounters.*

1. What patient characteristics here would make you change your mind about discussing PrEP with AS?

*If AS were HIV-positive, or had another comorbidity that would make using TDF/FTC risky. Importantly, if a patient asks about PrEP and feels they are at risk for HIV, they should be considered a PrEP candidate.*

1. What resources could you provide AS to help him afford PrEP if needed?

*The high medication copay mentioned in this case makes AS a good candidate for the Gilead® copay assistance program, which is administered nationally. He would likely qualify for state-level assistance programs as well given that he is a student.*
