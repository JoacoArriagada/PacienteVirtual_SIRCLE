**Case Study 2**:

ZH presents to the health department for their third STD/STI scare of 2018. In reviewing the chart, you notice the patient was treated for gonorrhea twice, syphilis, and chlamydia within the last year. At those appointments, the patient received HIV testing, and all were negative. A rapid HIV test was performed at the current visit, and is also negative. ZH, who identifies as gender non-binary, reports having both male and female partners. In the exam room, ZH appears nervous and is hesitant to give explicit details regarding their sexual activity, but they are adamant about utilizing “safe sex practices,” although they do not elaborate. ZH has IlliniCare health insurance (a managed Medicaid product).

**Case Study 2 Follow Up Questions**:

1. Would ZH be a good candidate for PrEP?

*Based on this information, ZH would likely be a good PrEP candidate. Further testing will be required to determine kidney function and overall health, as well as confirming HIV-negative status using a blood antigen test.*

1. What additional questions would you ask ZH, and what in the history stands out here to influence your decision about a conversation about PrEP?

*Recurrent STD diagnoses are a major red flag that the patient’s present sexual protection efforts are not working, or are nonexistent. It would be beneficial to ask ZH what they mean by “safe sex,” as this is another term that can mean different things for different people. Partners of varying gender identities is not inherently a risk factor, but this does indicate ZH has multiple partners, which is a risk factor.*

1. What other counseling should this patient receive at the time of their visits?

*ZH should be counseled about sexual risk reduction, including condom use, lubricant, PrEP, and the importance of routine testing for STD/STIs while sexually active with multiple partners. If possible, ZH should be provided with condoms and lubricant at appointments, if the facility can provide these.*

1. What other resources can be offered to this patient?

*It would be beneficial to determine if there is an underlying cause to the sexual risk taking behaviors ZH is exhibiting. The patient may benefit from the services of a mental health professional. ZH may also benefit from navigation services for accessing PrEP if it is determined they are a candidate for consultation about PrEP, including insurance support and co-pay assistance. Most Medicaid policies generally cover PrEP, however the copays can vary by carrier.*
