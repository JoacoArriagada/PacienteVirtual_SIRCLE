**Case Study 5**:

TW is a 37 year-old male who presents to the employee health clinic for possible communicable disease exposure. He is a maintenance/cleaning employee at a local hospital. The patient explains that he was cleaning out the garbage yesterday in a patient room, felt a sharp pain in his right middle finger, and saw that he was bleeding through his glove. The patient was not sure if it was a blade or needle, but believed it was a sharp item inappropriately placed in the garbage instead of the sharps container. After performing a detailed history and physical, you learn that he is currently sexually active with a monogamous female partner and has two children. His partner is out of town, and he states he has not had intercourse since the exposure incident. TW is asymptomatic and his blood work is normal. This visit and any prescriptions and follow up testing are completely cost-free because the incident happened at work.

**Case Study 5 Follow Up Questions**:

1. Is TW a candidate for PrEP?

*TW is not a candidate for PrEP as he may have already been exposed to HIV.*

1. What is the difference between PrEP and PEP?

*PrEP is taken before someone is exposed to HIV as prevention, while post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is taken after someone has a suspected exposure to HIV. The rational of PEP is to give a high dose of three antiretroviral medications to prevent a new HIV infection from occurring. PEP must be prescribed within a 72-hour window after the suspected exposure and is typically prescribed for a 30-day period. Finally, PEP is a full-dose antiretroviral regimen, and is usually a combination of antiretrovirals. There are two categories of PEP: occupational PEP (oPEP) which is used for suspected HIV exposure in a workplace setting and nonoccupational PEP (nPEP) for potential exposure outside of the workplace, such as in cases of sexual assault. Prescribing guidelines for both oPEP and nPEP are available from the CDC.*

1. Who is a candidate for PEP, and what steps are necessary for determining if someone is a candidate?

*Patients are candidates for PEP if they have potentially been exposed to HIV within the past 72 hours, and are not protected with another method (eg PrEP).*

1. What follow-up testing is required in this situation?

*TW should receive HIV testing for the next year at regular intervals, and be educated about the symptoms of acute HIV infection. He should also be educated about the use of protection with his wife until it has been confirmed he is HIV-negative.*
