**Case Study 4:**

JB is a 42-year old man presenting to his primary care physician complaining of penile discharge. From the interview, you find out that he is married with two children, and he states he is sexually monogamous with his wife. JB reports that his symptoms started one day ago. You decided to do a STD/STI test panel, and start him on an empiric antibiotic course. A follow-up appointment and symptom recheck is scheduled for 1 week.

The STD panel was positive for chlamydia, and negative for other STDs and HIV. As a part of follow-up counseling, you begin a more thorough conversation about sexual history. You also recommend he have his wife come in for testing, and offer to write an antibiotic script for her if she is showing symptoms of chlamydia. JB vehemently opposes having his wife come for testing. After reassurance of confidentiality, he reveals that he does have unprotected anal sex with men, unbeknownst to his wife. They have medical insurance through his wife’s employer.

**Case Study 4 Follow Up Questions:**

1. What can the healthcare provider do to make the patient more comfortable to discuss his sexual history openly and honestly?

*This case reinforces the position that healthcare providers are not present to judge patient situations or actions, but rather to help keep them safe in whatever capacity that requires. The provider in this case can ensure that they will not share the results of the STD test with JB’s wife, and also will not speak of JB’s sexual intercourse with men with his wife. The provider may also discuss ways that JB can pay for his care without using the insurance policy he holds with his wife, as test results and charges may appear on their common insurance statement.*

1. What are you concerned about with this patient’s behavior?

*The most concerning aspect of this case is the unprotected anal intercourse. JB should be given general sexual risk reduction counseling, including condoms and lubricant if possible. Another concerning part is the secrecy of this activity, which may be due to cultural or internalized stigma about homosexual activities. This may lead to additional issues that would indicate JB could benefit from the services of a mental health professional.*

1. Is this patient a good candidate for PrEP? What additional steps, if any, must be completed before prescribing JB PrEP if he is an interested candidate?

*The HIV negative status is a good indication, and JB should also have a kidney function test. Based on his present risk profile, he is a good PrEP candidate provided he is in good health.*

1. What additional counseling/resources should JB receive?

*The secrecy of his same-sex sexual activities is a concern as it may point to a larger issue, as described. JB should also be directed to resources that will allow him to access healthcare services with discretion, including through shared insurance with his wife. In some cases, the provider can issue a letter to the insurance carrier requesting that certain services be redacted from insurance correspondence. The administrative staff of the provider can likely assist with this.*
