**Appendix E**

**Facilitator Guide**

**IPV Curriculum Role-Plays**

**Scenario 1**: **Asking a Muslim spouse to leave the room**

Physician

You are in the resident primary care clinic meeting Mrs. Hassan, a 34yo Muslim mother of two with no significant past medical history who has come to establish care with you. Mrs. Hassan presents with her husband, an auto shop repairman. You have completed the routine history and physical during which she has no acute concerns. You come to the social history and you would like to ask her husband to leave the room so that you can screen for IPV.

Patient: Mrs. Hassan

You are a 34yo Muslim woman without significant past medical history, presenting to establish care with a new primary care doctor. Your husband joins you in the exam room although you would prefer that he would not. He tends to be controlling and verbally demeaning at home.

You have just finished going through the usual history and physical, and you do not have any acute concerns. However, you do wish your husband would leave the room so you can talk to your new doctor about the stresses of raising two children without support from your husband at home. When your doctor asks him to leave the room and he does not want to, you do not feel comfortable asserting your opinion. You would never ask your husband to do something that he does not want to do.

Husband: Mr. Hassan

You are a 35yo Muslim auto shop repairman joining your wife at an appointment with her new primary care doctor. You would prefer to be in the room with her because you do not want her to be alone with the doctor. You know things have been stressful at home recently and you do not want her talking to her doctor about it. You would really prefer to stay in the room to hear the entire visit.

**Scenario 1**: **Debriefing questions**

1. How did the scenario feel as the provider?
2. How did the scenario feel as the patient?
3. How did the scenario feel as the husband?
4. Have you observed or encountered a scenario like this in real life, in which cultural factors are contributing to the complexity of asking a patient’s partner to leave the room? If so, what strategies have you observed or implemented in this type of scenario?

* Facilitator reference: Gennari M, Giuliani C, Accordini M. Muslim Immigrant Men’s and Women’s Attitudes Towards Intimate Partner Violence. *European Journal of Psychology.* 2017; 13(4): 688-707.

This article found rigid, gender-based role division in Muslim couples. Women are considered less socially competent and in need of protection. Women must stand beside their husbands and maintain family unity. Violence may be seen as a way to regulate socially dysfunctional behaviors. Distance from their family of origin may make males feel overwhelmed with family responsibilities and make women feel a lack of social support.
