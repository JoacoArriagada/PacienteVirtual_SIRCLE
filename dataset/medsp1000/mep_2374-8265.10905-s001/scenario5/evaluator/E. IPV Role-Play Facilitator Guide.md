**Scenario 5: Screening for IPV in a contemplative patient for whom you have concerns about IPV**

Physician

You are seeing Mrs. Moore in primary care follow-up. She is a 40yo stay-at-home soccer mom with chronic abdominal pain, hypertension, and obesity. You have known her for a year and have concerns about her safety at home. Her husband accompanied her to her last visit and openly expressed degrading sentiments to her about her weight. Today she says she feels more down, depressed, and stressed than usual. She mentions she is in a rush to pick her kids up at school and make dinner before her husband gets home because otherwise she will never hear the end of it. After discussing increasing her blood pressure medications, you decide to screen her for IPV. Due to your high suspicion for abuse at home, you are quite persistent about trying to discuss this topic and offer your support to Mrs. Moore.

Patient: Mrs. Moore

You are a 40yo stay at home “soccer mom” with chronic abdominal pain, hypertension, and obesity. You are following up with your primary care doctor. During today’s visit you have talked about increasing your blood pressure medicines. You are in a rush to pick your kids up at school and make dinner before your husband gets home because otherwise you will never hear the end of it. He is always angry with you no matter what you do and he is degrading about your weight. You think he has anger management problems because he yells often and even throws things at you such as last night’s dinner because it was cold. He has never hit you but some times you worry that he will. He has never been violent toward your kids and you both try to hide the conflicts from them but it is getting harder to do so.

You have never talked to any one about this abuse. When your doctor asks about things at home, you do not want to admit the challenges with your husband. You feel like your husband is entitled to be in charge at home because he works all day and makes money to support the family. You are not sure this abuse has anything to do with your health.

Initially you do not want to disclose your relationship struggles, but then you realize this stressful relationship is probably causing your depressed mood. You also want to have a plan for what to do if the violence worsens. If pressed, you decide to talk about this with your doctor and ask for resources for help.

**Scenario 5: Debriefing Questions**

1. In what stage of change is the patient? (contemplative)
2. How did the scenario feel as the provider?
3. How did the scenario feel as the patient?
4. For the observer: What did the physician do well? How might the physician improve in their strategies for addressing IPV?
5. What next steps would you pursue to address the abuse in this scenario?
   1. Are there legal reporting requirements? (no)
   2. Brainstorm a safety plan
   3. Referrals to resources
      1. Clinic social worker
      2. Local women’s shelter
      3. National hotline
   4. Assess for immediate safety
   5. Schedule close follow up
