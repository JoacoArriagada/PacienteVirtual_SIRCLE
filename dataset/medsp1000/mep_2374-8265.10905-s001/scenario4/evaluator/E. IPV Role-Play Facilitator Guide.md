**Scenario 4: Screening for IPV in a precontemplative patient for whom you have concerns about IPV**

Physician

You have seen Julie, a 19 year old with 3 kids, in your primary care clinic a number of times. You suspect she is in a physically abusive relationship, as she often has bruises on her upper arms. Today she seems nervous and has a bruise on her belly. You are worried and decide to pursue a discussion about possible abuse at home.

Patient: Julie

You are seeing your primary care doctor today after your boyfriend kicked you in the stomach when you told him you were pregnant and he was the father. This morning he apologized; he was drunk at the time and is now really sorry. He is a very loving person, but when he drinks he can be verbally and physically abusive to you. One time when your 9 year old heard him yelling and came in the room, he threw an empty beer can at him to get him to leave. He has never struck the children and says he would never do so. Money is tight right now and without his financial support you and the kids would be homeless, so for all these reasons when the doctor asks about your bruises you talk about how messy the kids are with their toys and that you constantly trip over things and often fall.

You would really rather not tell anyone about the abuse. If your boyfriend somehow found out you told he would likely lash out at you even more, and you worry one of these times he might hurt one of the kids. Besides, even if you wanted to leave, you have nowhere to go. Only if asked multiple times, you reluctantly tell the doctor the truth about the bruises.

**Scenario 4: Debriefing Questions**

1. In what stage of change is the patient? (precontemplative)
2. How did the scenario feel as the provider?
3. How did the scenario feel as the patient?
4. For the observer: What did the physician do well? How might the physician improve in their strategies for addressing IPV?
5. What next steps would you pursue to address the abuse in this scenario?
   1. Are there legal reporting requirements? (no)
   2. Referrals to resources
      1. Clinic social worker
      2. Local women’s shelter
      3. National hotline
   3. Assess for immediate safety
   4. Schedule close follow up
