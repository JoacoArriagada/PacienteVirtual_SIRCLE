**IPV Curriculum Role-Plays**

**Scenario 2**

Physician

You are meeting a new patient in your primary care clinic, Lilah, and her boyfriend, Michael, for the first time. Lilah is a 27 year old woman with polycystic ovarian syndrome, hypertension, and type 2 diabetes. Both she and her boyfriend are engineers working at Lockheed-Martin, and they appear cheerful and happy during the visit. The patient answers most of your questions, and occasionally Michael adds comments. After completing the ROS, you decide you want to have Michael step out of the room so you can screen for IPV.

Patient: Lilah

You are a 27 year old woman with polycystic ovarian syndrome, hypertension, and type 2 diabetes. Both you and your boyfriend are engineers working at Lockheed-Martin, and you get along great, though you have been dating only 3 months and Michael seems to want to move the relationship faster than you do. He has already suggested you two should live together, and some times even picks out particular outfits he encourages you to wear because they show off your curvy figure. So, he has a lot of opinions, but still, it is nice to have someone so into you and wanting to take care of you. He even wanted to come to this appointment with your new doctor to meet him or her. What a great guy!

Partner: Michael

You are just coming along with your girlfriend, Lilah, for a doctor’s appointment. You and Lilah have been dating for 3 months. You met at work at Lockheed-Martin where you are both engineers. You know you have a tendency to get jealous and protective because there are notthat many women engineers at work, and you have seen how other guys look at Lilah. You just do not want to lose her to someone else. During the visit, you kind of wonder whether this doctor is asking too many personal questions. You are starting to feel uncomfortable because you do not understand what these questions have to do with her health.
