# 1. **Mock Page – Standardized Patient Case**

# 2. <u>**Instructor’s Guide**</u>

# 3. <u>A.</u> <u>**Identifying Case Factors**</u>

**Case:** Mock Page - Infected PICC line; answering a page from a nurse.

**Presenting Complaint:** You are the nighttime intern covering floor pages and admitting patients.  You are in the Emergency Department (ED) evaluating a patient, and because of your workload, you can’t leave the ED for at least three hours. You have just been paged to a number and must return the page.

**Diagnosis:** Infected PICC line

**Length of Encounter:** 10 minutes, with 3 additional minutes for standardized nurse (SN) checklist completion

**Learning Objectives:**

1. Assess learner’s ability to communicate professionally on the phone.
2. Assess learner’s ability to assess a patient’s medical status by phone.
3. Assess the learner’s medical knowledge regarding assessment of an infected central venous catheter.

**Target Group:** Medical students. Residents.

**Learner Level:** Fourth year medical students. Interns.

**Type of Case:** Assessment, Communication.

<u>**B. Materials/Necessary Equipment for Implementation**</u>

- Two rooms: one for learner, another for the standardized nurse (SN)
- Two telephones: phones must be able to make outgoing and accept in-bound phone calls
- Pens, pencils, paper for learner to write notes
- Clipboard to hold checklists
- Timer

An optimal testing environment would include a standardized patient testing room that is set up with a telephone with speakerphone capacity. Learners are instructed to use the speakerphone. Each encounter is also recorded on video for case review afterwards. The SN is in a separate room with a telephone.

<u>**C. Examination Preparation**</u>

1. Examiners should familiarize themselves with the standardized case station, clinical scenario, checklist, and use of the rating scales prior to the examination.
2. Standardized nurse(s) should receive the case scenario and instructions prior to the OSCE with adequate time to prepare (one to two weeks).
3. Examiners should meet with the SN to review and practice the station.
4. Examiners should instruct the SN on appropriate methods for assessing and rating the learners to ensure standardization.

# 4. <u>**D. Case Summary and Standardized Nurse Training Notes**</u>

**SN demographics:** SN may be any adult age, gender, or ethnicity. The SN should have some medical training, ideally a nurse. SN should be able to be clearly understood on the telephone.

**Setting:** Inpatient

**History of Present Complaint:**  You are the nurse taking care of Kevin Smith, a 60-year-old black male who is hospitalized for the past 2 days for cellulitis. He stepped on glass and was sutured in the ED 10 days ago and sent home. He returned 3 days ago with redness around the sutures and was admitted for IV antibiotics for cellulitis. Your shift started one hour ago and when you went to assess the patient you discovered the PICC line looks infected. You have called the doctor to report your finding.

1. When the learner calls say the following “Hello, Floor 64.”
2. The learner should say something like “Hello, I am \_\_\_ with the Green Medicine Team, and I was paged.”
3. You should then say, “I am taking care of Kevin Smith who is a patient on the Green team. He is here for cellulitis. I think the PICC is infected.”
4. If the student mentions removing the PICC, you can offer to take it out.

For example, Student: “It sounds like we need to take it out.” Nurse: *“I can do it,”* or *“We are allowed to take them out.”*

**Information for Nurse to Provide If Asked:**

- Kevin Smith is a 60-year-old black male who has been hospitalized for the past 2 days for cellulitis on his left foot. He stepped on a glass and was sutured in the ED 10 days ago. He returned 3 days ago with redness around the sutures and was admitted for cellulitis requiring IV antibiotics.

- If queried by the student, the following information should be provided:

- <u>Vitals</u>:  BP 118/62, HR 100, RR 20, Temp 38.6, no pain
- <u>I/O</u>: For the past 24 hours: intake 2400cc PO and IV; urine output 2300cc
- <u>Meds</u>:  IV fluids (Normal saline 100cc/hour), IV nafcillin every 2 hours – next due in one hour. Both are being administered through the PICC.
- <u>Assessment</u>:  Patient is alert and oriented. There is large amount of yellow/greenish pus coming from around where the PICC inserts into the skin in his right arm. Skin surrounding where the PICC line inserts into the skin is red.
- I<u>V access</u>:  There are no other IVs or lines.

- You, the nurse, just came on shift and noticed the PICC issues, so you don’t know how long it has been looking like this. You received no information about this from the previous nurse. The patient feels fine and is reporting no pain.
