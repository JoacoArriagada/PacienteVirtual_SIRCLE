**Depression in the LGBT Patient:**

**A Standardized Patient Encounter**

**Guide to the History Checklist**

Benjamin H. Huang, David A. Milling, Sarah Dimiduk, Harsha K. Nair, Karen H. Zinnerstrom

This document is a guide to the History portion of the Monitor Encounter Checklist. It is used to help both standardized patients and monitors by providing examples of questions that an examinee may ask in order to elicit specific responses during the encounter. Giving standardized patients time to study this document can help to improve consistency of portrayal.

Please note that the list starts at item 4 in order to be aligned with the history section of the Monitor Encounter Checklist.

**History**

The following section of this guide provides examples of questions that an examinee might ask in order to elicit details of the history which are relevant to criterion on the monitor checklist. Items in this section are presented answer-first, followed by a list of related questions. These lists are not comprehensive, but give a broad sense of the type of question used to elicit each criterion.

1. **Things I used to enjoy no longer make me happy.**

“How is your mood?”

“What do you do for fun?”

“Do you feel sad/depressed/hopeless?”

“Have you lost interest in hobbies or activities?”

1. **I have difficulty sleeping.**

“Are you sleeping well?”

“Do you wake up feeling well rested?”

“How much sleep do you get each night?”

“What time do you get to bed?”

1. **I’m worried because my grades are slipping.**

“How are you doing in school?”

“Has anything been stressing you lately?”

“Does concentration seem to be a problem?”

“Is it difficult to focus on tasks?”

1. **I’ve been eating less, but I haven’t lost any weight.**

“Have you gained or lost any weight?”

“How is your appetite?”

“Do you find yourself skipping meals?”

“Are you eating well?”

1. **I would never kill myself.**

“Have you ever thought about suicide?”

“Do you feel cornered or desperate?”

“Are you thinking about harming yourself?”

“Would you ever want to hurt yourself or others?”

1. **I often feel cold, but that’s not new.**

“Do you feel hot or cold all the time?”

“Do other people feel temperature differently than you do?”

“Are you ever cold when everyone else feels fine?”

1. **I don’t take any prescription medication.**

“Do you take any medications?”

1. **I live at home with my parents and sister.**

“Who do you live with?”

“Do you live at home or on campus?”

“Are you staying with your parents?”

1. **I feel safe in my relationships.**

“Do you feel safe in your relationships?”

“Do you feel safe at home?”

1. **I feel like I don’t have anyone I can talk to.**

“Who would you say is your best friend?”

“Is there anyone you generally confide in?”

“Do you communicate well with your parents/sister?”

“Who do you go to when you have a problem?”

1. **My boyfriend broke up with me 2 months ago.**

“Are you currently in a relationship?”

“What happened 2 months ago?”

“Did anything happen right around when all this started?”

1. **I have had unprotected sex.**

“Do you always use condoms?”

“Do you always use protection?”

“What makes you think you might have an HIV infection?”

1. **I have never been tested for an STD.**

“Have you ever had an STD?”

“Were you ever tested for an STD?”

“Do you think you might have an STD?”
