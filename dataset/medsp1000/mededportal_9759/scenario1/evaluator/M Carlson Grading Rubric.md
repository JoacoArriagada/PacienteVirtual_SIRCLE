**Depression in the LGBT Patient:**

**A Standardized Patient Encounter**

**Grading Rubric**

Benjamin H. Huang, David A. Milling, Sarah Dimiduk, Harsha K. Nair, Karen H. Zinnerstrom

The following document is a suggested rubric for grading examinee performances during this encounter. Each section contains a number of criterion that the examinee can score and a total underneath that section. Each criterion in a section is generally worth a single point; however, note that some sections have a total that is less than the number of criterion listed. If an examinee scores higher than the total in any given section, give that examinee a number of points equal to the total (e.g. if a examinee describes 12 criterion in the History section of the Examinee Write-Up Form, the examinee then scores 10 points since the total for the History section is 10 points).

We felt that some criterion needed to be weighted more heavily than others. Examinees score 2 points rather than 1 for these criterion. These criterion are denoted by the [2 points] tag.

Some instructors may note that anemia, a common differential for fatigue, is missing. We have omitted it from this rubric because we accept only 3 differential diagnoses from examinees on our 3rd year clinical skills exam, and feel that the details of the case more strongly indicate the diagnoses of depression, acute HIV infection, or hypothyroidism. Some programs may wish, however, to include an additional section of points for the workup of anemia if they permit examinees to submit more than 3 differential diagnoses.

**Standardized Patient Post-Encounter Checklist**

**Physical Exam**

The examinee examined your thyroid by feeling from behind and asked you to swallow.

The examinee listened to your heart in four places on bare skin.

The examinee listened to your lungs on my back in six places on bare skin (comparing right and left sides).

The examinee listened to your abdomen with a stethoscope in 4 places on bare skin.

The examinee palpated your abdomen in 4 places on bare skin.

The examinee washed his/her hands without prompting.

The examinee appropriately draped you during the physical exam.

The examinee explained each part of the physical exam before/while performing it.

The examinee explained the physical exam findings to you.

The examinee considered your comfort during the physical exam.

Total Click here to enter text./5

**Communication Skills**

The examinee greeted you warmly, introduced himself/herself, and addressed you by your name.

The examinee treated you with respect and did not talk down to, or patronize, you.

The examinee assured you of the confidential nature of your conversation.

The examinee explored your personal worry that you might have an HIV infection.

The examinee did not interrupt you while you were talking.

The examinee approached sensitive subject matters, such as your sexual history and orientation, without judgment or bias, and did not preclude the possibility that you identify as LGBT.

The examinee showed interest in you as a person, and remained friendly and compassionate for the entire encounter.

The examinee encouraged you to ask questions always answered them clearly.

The examinee used plain English and clearly explained any medical jargon.

The examinee discussed the risks of sexual activity without coming off as patronizing or judgmental.

The examinee inquired about your home life and/or social support.

The examinee provided you with information regarding the possible diagnoses.

The examinee discussed management options with you.

The examinee respected your autonomy and allowed you to make your own decisions in management.
