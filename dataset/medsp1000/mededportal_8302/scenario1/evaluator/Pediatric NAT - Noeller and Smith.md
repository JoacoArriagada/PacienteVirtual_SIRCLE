# STATES

Play of the case: The child presents to the emergency department ill appearing and then regardless of action decompensates quickly. If this is recognized, the appropriate action is to intubate the child prior to pursuing further workup. The intubated states include a

ventilator and bag-valve-mask and then a stabilized state. If the airway is note secured, the child will seize. If the seizure is not controlled or the airway is not secured, the child progresses to a bradycardic pulseless arrest to asystole.

# Initial

Eyes closed throughout

# Deteriorate

# Seizure

# Recovery

HR 130 BP 100/50 RR 0 SPO2 $1 0 0 \%$ (Intubated)

# Brady PEA

HR 40 BP 0 RR 0 SPO2 unobtainable

# Stabilized

HR 120 BP 100/50 RR 0 SPO2 $1 0 0 \%$ (Intubated)

# Asystole – dead

# Transitions

Baseline to Deteriorate after approx 3 minutes   
Deteriorate to Recovery if IV, monitor, intubated/oxygenated   
Deteriorate to Brady/PEA if no atropine during intubation or no airway intervention/O2   
(5 min)   
Deteriorate to Seizure if no recognition of deterioration   
Seizure to Recovery if bezo and airway intervention   
Seizure to Brady PEA if benzo and no airway intervention   
Brady PEA to Recovery if CPR/airway   
Recovery to Stabilized to ddx considered, CT, nsurg, PICU   
Recovery to Brady/PEA to Asystole if slow or no action

Lumbar Puncture $=$ Brady PEA to Asystole

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Give an orientation brief before every high fidelity simulation which includes reminders about re-evaluating ABCs when a change is suspected or an intervention is performed. That the physical state needs to be assessed (ie, you need to listen to the lungs, I can’t tell you what they sound like), and that the simulator responds to its environment (ie, if you tube the esophagus, the patient will decompensate until you correct the airway)   
C. We included a “chubby infant” arm for IV access so they needed to obtain access during the resuscitation or IO access.   
D. We used a children’s “bumble ball” to simulate seizure activity, since the simulator doesn’t have seizure activity.
