**I. Title:** Anterior ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction with Cardiogenic Shock

## Target Audience: Internal Medicine Interns Spring

## Learning and Assessment Objectives

Participants are expected to execute the optimal management path as defined below and through the critical actions checklist as well as discuss the pathophysiologic reasoning behind a certain course of treatment. Debriefing sessions may be used to allow each participant to reflect upon the team dynamics and to identify future technical and behavioral goals.

Critical Actions Checklist:

DONE CRITICAL ACTION

􀂆 Telemetry monitoring

􀂆 Rapid patient history

􀂆 Rapid physical examination

􀂆 Supplemental O2

􀂆 IV access

􀂆 Obtain labs, imaging (CXR), ECG

􀂆 Diagnosis of ST elevation myocardial infarction and appropriate management

􀂆 Contact appropriate consultants

􀂆 Activation of cardiac catheterization laboratory

􀂆 Hemodynamic stabilization

􀂆 Interpretation of pulmonary artery catheter values and appropriate management
## Instructor Notes

1. Tips to keep scenario flowing
2. If need for further evaluation not recognized, nurse will make a suggestion for further evaluation.
3. Nurse will prompt learners to obtain baseline ECG if not requested.
4. Nurse will prompt contacting consultants if not requested.
5. Cardiac intensive care nurse will prompt learners to make management decisions based on the pulmonary artery catheter numbers.
6. Scenario programming
7. Optimal management path
   * + - O­2­/IV/monitor
       - History and physical examination
       - Requisite studies
         * Labs: BMP, CBC, cardiac markers, coagulation profile
         * Images: ECG, CXR
       - Medical management of ST elevation myocardial infarction
       - Consulting Cardiology/Interventional Cardiology
       - Management of hypoxemic respiratory failure
       - Management of cardiogenic shock
       - Initiating appropriate intervention based on pulmonary artery catheter numbers
8. Potential complications/errors path(s)
   * + - Failure to recognize ST elevation myocardial infarction on ECG
       - Failure to contact appropriate consultants
       - Failure to appropriately manage cardiogenic shock
