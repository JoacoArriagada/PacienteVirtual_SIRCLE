# Preceptor Instructions:

Prior to the start of the session, the vital signs of SimMan $\textcircled{8}$ should be programmed to; HR 34 bpm, RR 16, BP 84/42 mmHg, and $\mathsf { S a O } _ { 2 } \mathsf { 9 7 \% }$ on room air. The rhythm should be set to $3 ^ { \circ }$ AV Block (CHB). The heart sounds and lung sounds should be set as normal.

If transcutaneous pacing is performed, turn the pacing function on, set the pacing rate at 60 or 70 bpm and slowly increase the milliamperage (MA). At first, you will see pacing spikes on the simulator cardiac monitor. As the MA reaches the pacing threshold (\~ 90 MA for our simulator, may vary), you will begin to see ventricular paced beats (capture beats) at the set pacing rate. You may also recycle the blood pressure and program a more stable value after the patient is successfully paced.

For the first ${ \sim } 1 5$ minutes of the session, 3 students will be in a simulation room examining Sim Man. The clinical preceptor will be in the simulation control room functioning in the role of the patient. The basic science preceptor will be in the simulation conference room with the other 12 students. He / she will be observing the students interactions / comments as they observe the clinical encounter, taking notes if necessary. These observations can serve as a basis for a portion of the debriefing that occurs after the initial clinical encounter. After the first 15 minutes, the entire group reconvenes in the conference room to discuss the case.

# Case History:

Mr. Stevens is a 73-year-old male who presents to the emergency department after “passing out.” Earlier today, while sitting on a bus on his way into town, he felt weak, dizzy, and was noted by his wife to collapse to the floor. According to his wife, he appeared to be unconscious for less than 1 minute. She states that when he was arousable, he seemed confused for just a few moments. No seizure activity or urinary incontinence was noted during this episode.

Upon arrival at the hospital, the patient is awake, answering questions, but stills complains of feeling weak and lightheaded.

Past Medical History: HTN, hypercholesterolemia

Medications: Metoprolol, diltiazem, pravachol

Allergies: None

Pertinent Review of Symptoms: Denies chest pain, shortness of breath, nausea or vomiting

Vital signs – HR 34 bpm, RR 16, BP 84/42 mmHg, and $\mathsf { S a O } _ { 2 } 9 7 \%$ on room air

# General Appearance

He is awake, alert and able to answer your questions.

HEENT – Normal Cardiac – Regular, no murmurs

Lungs – Clear in all fields

Abdomen – Soft, non-tender Extremities – no edema

Neuro – Alert, oriented, moves all extremities

Skin – Cool and clammy
