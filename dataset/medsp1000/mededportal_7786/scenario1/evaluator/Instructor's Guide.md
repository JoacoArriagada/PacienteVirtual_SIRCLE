# Practical Implementation

Each case is designed to incorporate a high fidelity simulator (SimMan $\textcircled{8}$ ) to allow the students to perform an initial physical examination. With minor modification, you can also incorporate history taking skills in these exercises. The simulation portion of the case can also be presented as a paper case if a high fidelity simulator is not available. The faculty preceptor could provide the complete case information including all vital signs and physical examination findings.

# Case Materials

Preceptor handout includes;

1. Background information and learning objectives   
2. Preceptor instructions   
3. Explanation of electrocardiograms   
4. Complete case history   
5. Key cardiac physiology and clinical questions with explanations   
6. Appendix

Student handout includes;

1. Background information and learning objectives   
2. Student instructions   
3. Case history   
4. Key basic science and clinical questions to address

At the beginning of each session, we advise that one of the preceptors take a few minutes to orient the learner to the exercise and the simulator.

# Alternative Method of Implementing Exercises

We are planning to run one simulation at a time as opposed to two. Because of this, we will have a larger group of students (15 students: 2 faculty). We will bring the 15 students scheduled for each session into our simulation conference room. We review the student expectations that have previously been distributed and ask for 3 volunteers to go into the simulation room and perform a focused history and physical examination on SimMan $\textcircled{8}$ . The clinical faculty preceptor is in the control room and serves as the voice of the simulator. The basic science faculty remains in the simulation conference room with the remaining 12 students in an observational capacity during the initial portion of the case ${ \sim } 1 5$ minutes).

After the initial 15 minute evaluation, all students and faculty reconvene in the simulation conference room.

This year we will be presenting each case as an unknown.

# Addressing Challenges

One method to assess learner achievement for a subset of the stated learning objectives can be through a check list that can be completed by the preceptor / instructor during the first 15 minutes of the session. Brief feedback can be provided to the group of students immediately following this portion of the exercise. The check list can be easily designed to assess whether key aspects of the history and physical examination were performed by the group of students.

# Comments:

When designing the exercise and writing the learning objectives, we purposefully used the term “review” for the majority of our objectives as opposed to language such as “demonstrate an understanding of…” because of the logistic dilemma of being able to implement pre and post exercise knowledge assessments with a large number of learners.
