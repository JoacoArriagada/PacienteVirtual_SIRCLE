# Preceptor Instructions

Prior to the start of the session, the vital signs of SimMan $\textcircled{8}$ should be programmed to; HR 184 bpm, RR 26, BP 82/40 mmHg, and $\mathsf { S a O } _ { 2 } \thinspace 9 2 \%$ on room air (will increase to $9 7 \%$ if supplemental oxygen is administered via nasal cannula). The rhythm should be set to Ventricular Tachycardia. The heart sounds should be set as normal, the lung sounds to rales.

If synchronized cardioversion is performed, have the SimMan® convert to sinus rhythm after being shocked. Use a low energy level setting on the cardioverter-defibrillator (3 – 5 joules) to avoid damaging the simulator.

For the first ${ \sim } 1 5$ minutes of the session, 3 students will be in a simulation room examining Sim Man. The clinical preceptor will be in the simulation control room functioning in the role of the patient. The basic science preceptor will be in the simulation conference room with the other 12 students and will be observing the students interactions / comments as they observe the clinical encounter, taking notes if necessary. These observations can serve as a basis for a portion of the debriefing that occurs after the initial clinical encounter. After the first 15 minutes, the entire group reconvenes in the conference room to discuss the case.

# Explanation of Electrocardiograms (ECG) in Appendix (VT)

When introducing the ECG into the case, use Appendix A as the patient’s ECG.

# Case History:

A 73-year-old patient presents to the emergency department with a complaint of chest pain and dizziness after passing out. These complaints started about 30 minutes before arrival while the patient was waiting in line at the bank.

The patient recalls feeling warm and flushed and was noted by a bystander at the bank to have a brief fainting episode.

Upon arrival at the hospital, the patient is awake, answering questions, but complains of feeling weak, chest heaviness and shortness of breath.

Past Medical History: HTN, diabetes

Medications: Clonidine, diltiazem, metformin

Allergies: None

Pertinent Review of Symptoms: Denies headache, nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain

Vital signs – HR 184 bpm, RR 26, BP 82/40 mmHg, SaO2 92% (increase to $9 7 \%$ with supplemental oxygen)

General Appearance The patient is awake, alert and able to answer your questions.

HEENT – Normal

Cardiac – Regular, no murmur

Lungs – Rales

Abdomen – Soft, non-tender

Extremities – no edema

Neuro – Alert, oriented, moves all extremities

Skin – Cool and clammy
