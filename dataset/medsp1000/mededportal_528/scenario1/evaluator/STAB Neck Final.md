I. ‘Stab to neck’

II. Target Audience: Medical students, junior residents (EM or Surgery), and/or trauma team training.

# III. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives

A. Primary 1. Demonstrate understanding of general management of penetrating trauma (MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE) 2. Establish definitive airway control for neck trauma (PATIENT CARE) 3. Perform appropriate airway techniques in the setting of a difficult airway (PATIENT CARE)

B. Secondary

1. Describe the ‘zones’ of the neck (MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE) 2. Understand diagnostic options/disposition for evaluation of a penetrating neck wound (PATIENT CARE)

C. Critical actions checklist (included at end for check list use)

 Obtain large-bore IV access and give IV fluids   
 Order appropriate labs/blood products—Labs: Type and cross, hemoglobin; blood products: packed red blood cells   
 Perform complete primary and secondary survey with full exposure/skin exam and vascular exam Manage patient’s pain Establish definite airway   
 Coordinate final disposition for patient

IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – Emergency Department Critical Care/Trauma Room with necessary equipment/supplies for airway management (including cricothyrotomy kit/supplies, iLMA, Combitube, retrograde available if possible)   
B. Manikin Set Up – METI HPS (for program attached using Standard Man as patient), certainly would work well with ECS or SimMan.

Moulage: Right neck with inferior portion of zone II area stab wound. Can add towel/sheet with large amount of blood to be under patient as well. Small stab wound to left posterior chest/thorax with no active bleeding. Could place a little padding under the neck skin to simulate a small hematoma.

Clothing: Simulator in street clothes on arrival. Bloody clothing is a bonus.

C. Props:

• IV lines with mock fluid/blood • Oxygen mask, have nasal cannula available • Dressing supplies to cover wound/control bleeding • Drugs: Analgesics, induction agents, paralytics • Labs/Studies: Optional—can add trauma labs (attached), negative CXR, negative cardiac US picture. D. Distractors: optional, can add ‘friend’ of patient that is agitated in the trauma room or just outside the room—for more advanced students or CRM type course.

V. Actors

A. Roles: Nurse, Trauma Surgery consultant   
B. Who may play them: Usually actors, could be other participants   
C. Action Role – Nurse to assist with IV, monitor/vitals, and drug administration. Trauma Surgery to enter after airway is secured to discuss final disposition.

VI. Case Narrative

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants—given by Nurse on entry to room—no documentation started yet as patient presents through triage.

1. Chief complaint: ‘stabbed in neck’. 20 year old male patien   
2. Past medical history: ‘none’   
3. Meds and allergies: ‘no meds, no allergies’   
4. Family/social history: ‘admits to ETOH tonight’

B. Scenario conditions initially

1. History patient gives: ‘I was at a bar with some buddies, when this guy jumped me and stabbed me in the neck. I ran out of there, and my friends dropped me off here’ - complains of pain in the neck/stab area frequently (10/10) - if asked, states he may have been stabbed in the back as well ‘but I was running really fast’ - initially denies trouble breathing/swallowing, and is sitting up in bed (as much as the HPS bellows tolerate (about 30 degrees) - does state his right arm/hand feel ‘funny’

2. Patient’s initial exam (see additional Instructors Notes for full details)

Vitals: HR 95, BP 105/50, RR 14, Sats $9 8 \%$ , T99

Patient sitting up in bed Normal exam other than stab wounds to right neck and left back (see Moulage section), and pulse absent in right upper extremity.

C. Scenario branch points

1. If at any point in the case the awake patient has the bed flattened, he notes increasing trouble with breathing—‘Can you sit me back up?’.   
2. As time progresses in the case, patient notes some trouble swallowing and breathing and swelling in his right neck   
3. Case stabilizes after successful intubation or cricothyrotomy   
4. See attached ‘Instructors Notes’

VIII. Pilot Testing and Revisions

B. Anticipated management mistakes: lack of full exposure/exam, not finding decreased pulse in right upper extremity, not asking for/preparing for backup airway equipment (cric tray/kit/supplies).   
D. Simulation failure: sometimes airway swelling on METI HPS will still allow a decent percentage of students to intubate orally. Students that succeed in intubation typically are pulling very hard on the airway. Might be helpful to have instructor/helper in the room to verbally prompt intubator to keep correct technique. If this occurs, we just discuss back up options including cricothyrotomy in the debriefing session.
