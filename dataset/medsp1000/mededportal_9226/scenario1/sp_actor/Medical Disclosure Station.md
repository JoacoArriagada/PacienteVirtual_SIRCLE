**Title:** Disclosure of Medical Error –Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) (Authors: Antoun, Romani and Saab)

###### Case summary/ SP training notes

**Scenario:** Your name is A.K., You are 60 year old. You presented to your doctor 1 month ago as your glucose readings at home were high. The doctor adjusted your medication and added metformin 500 mg two tablets a day to your current medications. The physician asked you to measure your glucose readings at home and to follow up in 1 month.

You have good relationship with your doctor. He is caring and listens to your complaints and concerns carefully. You measured your glucose readings and kept a record of them (see attached paper#1) and you scheduled an appointment with your doctor to discuss your glucose control after the adjustment of the medications.

**Past Medical History:** Hypertension, Chronic renal failure, Diabetes Mellitus and Dyslipidemia

**Past Surgical History:** Negative

**Medications: (Your daughter has written them on paper #2)**

Enalapril 10 mg 1 tab daily

Glibenclamide 5 mg 1 tab morning and evening

Metformin 1000 mg one tab morning and evening

B aspirin 1 tab daily with food

Simvastatin 20 mg 1 tab daily

**Character and personality:** Your financial status is shaky which causes you to be irritable and angry lately.

**Social history:** You live with your wife and daughter in Beirut. Your daughter is about to get married soon and travel to the Gulf area. You work as Taxi driver. You smoke 2 packs per day for the past 30 years. You do not drink alcohol.

**Family History:** Chronic renal failure in brother, Diabetes in both parents.

**Since your last appointment:** You are really happy that the new medication has helped in the diabetes control. You feel that everything will turn out to be good after all especially after the bad news about your brother. Your brother’s physician has told him that he needs to be started on dialysis 3 times a week as his kidney function is getting worse.

**Tactics:**  When the resident tells you that he has given you the wrong medicine that can affect your kidney, you become agitated.

If the resident is empathic, apologizes and is sincere, you calm down a little bit.

If the resident is defensive and arguing, then your agitation increases more.

**During the visit:**

If asked “Why are you here”. You answer that you came as a follow up for your diabetes control. If you are asked about the glucose level at home, you give the resident paper #1.

When the resident tells you that a mistake was done, you have to become upset. You raise your voice (but do not scream) and say: “What do you mean you gave me the wrong medication?” “I could have been dead!” “Do you consider yourself a physician?”

If the resident admits the mistake and apologizes, you remain angry but state in a slightly lower voice: “Bad luck is surrounding me. Isn’t it enough that my daughter is leaving and my brother is undergoing dialysis and the work is not at its best? If I had not come today, I would be dead or on dialysis and nobody knew that it is your fault.”

If the resident reassures you that the long term damage is minimal and most probably the harm did not”, you are little bit relieved and says: “Why did it happen? Does it happens all the time” What went wrong? How could you prescribe a wrong medication?”

Whatever the resident answers of possible reasons, you answer “This system is all bad.”

If the resident remains apologizing and not argumentative, you calm down a little bit and ask” What to do now? If I go back to my previous medications, the glucose will increase again”. Also if the resident tries to give promises for change and avoidance of such an error in the future you also ask ”What to do now? If I go back to my previous medications, the glucose will increase again”.

If the resident remains defensive and tries to find excuses, you say: ”I should not have come to this clinic. It is a bad clinic.”

If the resident remains argumentative, always challenge the resident that you lost faith in the health system, you are afraid that you will become like your brother dependent on others; you do not have anyone to take care of you especially that your daughter is traveling soon.

If after 3 minutes, the resident is not calming you down and keeps escalating or s/he is completely negligent of the medical error, you say: “OK, what can we do now? What can be done to remedy what has happened”.
