**Simulation Scenario: Massive Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding**

1. **Actors:**
   1. **Roles:**
      1. Patient—played by a student or actor
2. **Case Narrative:**
   1. **Scenario Background:**
      1. Items marked with an asterisk\* must be specifically asked for by the participant(s)
      2. Hospital information: Community Memorial Hospital, a 95 bed rural hospital with an emergency department covered by one physician. The hospital is over an hour from the nearest tertiary care hospital.
      3. Patient information: Harry O’Malley a 54 year old white male
      4. Chief complaint: “Throwing up blood”
      5. Triage note: “Vomiting blood, dark stools for a few days, passed out after vomiting a lot of blood today.”
      6. \*Medic report: “Blood and beer cans all over the apartment”
      7. \*Past Medical History: Cirrhosis and alcohol abuse
      8. \*Medications & Allergies: None
      9. \*Social history: Smoking (15 pack year history), alcohol abuse (10-15 beers per day for many years)
   2. **Initial Scenario Conditions:**
      1. Patient reports: “I’ve been spitting up blood and my poop’s been black for a few days. I puked up a lot of blood today. After I passed out my wife called the ambulance and forced me to come in.”
      2. Initial exam:
         1. General: Awake and alert, slow to respond to questions but appropriate answers. Thin appearing white male
         2. HEENT: Mildly icteric sclera, otherwise normal
         3. Heart/Lungs: Tachycardic, otherwise normal
         4. Abdomen: Large protuberant abdomen, nontender, dull to percussion. Veins across the abdomen appear very prominent.
         5. Skin: Pale
         6. Extremities: Weak pulses, 1+ edema to both legs
   3. **Branch Points:**
      1. After giving some initial history information the patient becomes less responsive and more confused. He vomits additional blood and begins to have some difficulty breathing.
3. **Instructor’s Notes:**
   1. The patient-actor should be briefed on his/her role prior to the simulation, including his/her clinical story, use of the imitation blood, and clinical progression of symptoms.
