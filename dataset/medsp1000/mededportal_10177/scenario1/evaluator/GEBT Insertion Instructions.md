Balloon Tamponade Device Insertion

Blakemore Tube or Minnesota Tube

(for emergent control of esophageal variceal upper GI hemorrhage)

**Recommended Supplies:**

* Laryngoscope
* McGill forceps

**Optional Supplies:\***

* (3) three-way stopcocks
* (3) catheter/slip-tip to Luer-lock adapters
* (3) Luer lock caps
* 60 mL Luer lock syringe

\* For setup of a stopcock assembly for easier inflation & monitoring. Some of these supplies can be difficult to find, so we considered them optional.

**“Must-Have” Supplies:**

* Sengstaken-Blakemore Tube (3 port), OR
* Minnesota Tube (4 port)
* 60 mL catheter-tip syringe (Toomey)
* (4) hemostat / Kelly clamps (wrapped in tape)
* Water-base lubricant
* Roller bandage
* 500 mL—1L bag of saline
* Manometer or sphygmomanometer
* Salem sump NG tube
* Scissors/shears (for emergency deflation)

**Instructions:** *(always review manufacturer instructions as well)*

1. Secure airway (intubation) and elevate head of bed to 45 degrees.
2. Test balloons (gastric and esophageal) then fully deflate.
3. If Blakemore Tube is used: Mark salem-sump NG tube 2-3 cm above each balloon.
4. Tube ports may be occluded either by clamping (Figure 3) or with the optional stopcock assembly.
5. Lubricate the tube then insert Blakemore/Minnesota tube to 50 cm through the mouth (like an OG tube), consider using the laryngoscope and/or McGill forceps to help guide the tube.
6. Inject air into the Gastric Aspiration port while listening over the stomach for bubbling.
7. If bubbling heard, inflate the Stomach/Gastric Balloon with 50 mL of air.
8. Obtain X-ray to confirm Stomach/Gastric Balloon placement in the stomach.
9. After X-ray confirmation, inflate Stomach Balloon with an additional 200 mL of air (250 mL total).
10. Apply 0.5 to 1 kg of traction (saline bag & roller-bandage) to proximal end of tube (Figure 4).
11. Mark the tube depth at the mouth/teeth (may stretch a little but if >5cm get another CXR).
12. Evaluate for continued bleeding: Suction the Gastric Aspiration port and lavage if needed.
13. Suction the Esophagus Aspiration port (Minnesota 4-port tube) or insert salem sump tube to the mark above the stomach balloon (Blakemore 3-port Tube).
14. If bleeding continues esophagus balloon may need to be inflated.
15. Attach manometer to the pressure monitoring port (Figure 3) or Esophagus Balloon port.
16. Inflate the Esophagus Balloon to 30 mmHg. If continued bleeding inflate to 45 mmHg.
17. Obtain repeat x-ray imaging to re-confirm proper placement.

Note: The balloon tamponade device should be used only in cases of life-threatening upper GI bleeding due to esophageal varices. It is only a temporizing measure and can be associated with severe complications, including esophageal rupture. Balloon may stay in place for 24-48 hours, in close coordination with gastroenterology and/or surgery. Obtain repeat x-ray imaging if any concerns about tube positioning.
