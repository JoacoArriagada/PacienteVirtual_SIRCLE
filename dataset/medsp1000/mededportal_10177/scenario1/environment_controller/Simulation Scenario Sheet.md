**Simulation Scenario: Massive Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding**

1. **Title:** Massive upper gastrointestinal bleeding with gastroesophageal balloon tamponade
2. **Environment:**
   1. **Lab Set Up:** Emergency department
   2. **Manikin Set Up:**
      1. Volunteer/actor or manikin moulaged in imitation blood (for an example, see “Photos & Images” presentation, Images 1-2)
      2. Airway/intubation training manikin and model
      3. Lines: Availability of large-bore peripheral or central IV access
      4. Medications: Octreotide, normal saline, antibiotic (such as ceftriaxone)
   3. **Props:**
      1. X-rays showing proper and improper GEBT tube placement (see “Photos & Images” presentation, Radiographs 1-3)
      2. EKG showing sinus tachycardia
      3. Endotracheal intubation equipment
      4. GEBT tube (Blakemore or Minnesota tube) and related supplies
3. **Actors:**
   1. **Roles:**
      1. Physician—played by a resident, ideally a senior level resident
      2. Nurse(s)—played by other residents, medical or nursing students
      3. Technician—played by medical or nursing students
      4. Patient—played by a student or actor
      5. Consultant—Faculty member or a senior resident running the case may act as the voice of a consultant
4. **Case Narrative:**
   1. **Scenario Background:**
      1. Items marked with an asterisk\* must be specifically asked for by the participant(s)
      2. Hospital information: Community Memorial Hospital, a 95 bed rural hospital with an emergency department covered by one physician. The hospital is over an hour from the nearest tertiary care hospital.
      3. Patient information: Harry O’Malley a 54 year old white male
      4. Chief complaint: “Throwing up blood”
      5. Triage note: “Vomiting blood, dark stools for a few days, passed out after vomiting a lot of blood today.”
      6. \*Medic report: “Blood and beer cans all over the apartment”
      7. \*Past Medical History: Cirrhosis and alcohol abuse
      8. \*Medications & Allergies: None
      9. \*Social history: Smoking (15 pack year history), alcohol abuse (10-15 beers per day for many years)
   2. **Initial Scenario Conditions:**
      1. Patient reports: “I’ve been spitting up blood and my poop’s been black for a few days. I puked up a lot of blood today. After I passed out my wife called the ambulance and forced me to come in.”
      2. Initial exam:
         1. General: Awake and alert, slow to respond to questions but appropriate answers. Thin appearing white male
         2. HEENT: Mildly icteric sclera, otherwise normal
         3. Heart/Lungs: Tachycardic, otherwise normal
         4. Abdomen: Large protuberant abdomen, nontender, dull to percussion. Veins across the abdomen appear very prominent.
         5. Skin: Pale
         6. Extremities: Weak pulses, 1+ edema to both legs
      3. Physiology & vital Signs:
         1. Initial: Pulse—118/minute, regular; BP—92/58; Breathing—20/minute; Pulse oximetry—95% on room air; Temperature—97.6F (36.4C), oral
   3. **Branch Points:**
      1. After giving some initial history information the patient becomes less responsive and more confused. He vomits additional blood (via a balloon or JP-type drain filled with imitation blood) and begins to have some difficulty breathing.
      2. Vital signs change to: Pulse—138/minute, BP—78/44, Breathing—28/minute, Pulse oximetry—89%
      3. If appropriate resuscitative measures are taken (IV fluids and blood transfusion and airway control) the case continues.
         1. The patient continues to decline and bleeding continues.
         2. For procedures, the case is moved to the airway training manikin directly beside the moulaged actor or manikin.
         3. The case continues until consultants are called and the team verbalizes or attempts to use the GEBT tube.
         4. The team is allowed one attempt to use the GEBT tube.
      4. If resuscitative measures are NOT taken, or the GEBT tube is used incorrectly, the patient becomes pulseless, continues bleeding rapidly, and eventually expires.
      5. Consultants:
         1. Gastroenterology: The area’s only gastroenterologist on-call is doing colonoscopies 45 minutes from the hospital. He states “I’m looking into a colon as we speak, it’ll be at least 90 minutes before I can get to you”
         2. General Surgery: The only general surgeon on-call is in the operating room with a case that is expected to take another 3 hours.
         3. There are no other sub-specialty consultants available at the hospital.
         4. The hospital is over an hour away from the nearest tertiary care hospital and helicopters are not flying due to thunderstorms and low visibility.
5. **Instructor’s Notes:**
   1. During our pilot testing, use of a moulaged actor was very effective visually and to encourage interaction between participants and the “patient”.
      1. The patient-actor should be briefed on his/her role prior to the simulation, including his/her clinical story, use of the imitation blood, and clinical progression of symptoms.
   2. The initial portion of the simulation, with the patient-actor, should be allowed to progress without instructor intervention.
   3. When participants decide advanced interventions are needed (i.e. intubation), the instructor should prompt participants to perform procedures on the adjacent training models and encourage the case to continue.
   4. The simulation ends when participants consider and/or [briefly] attempt to use the GEBT tube; or do not appropriately progress in the resuscitation after minimal prompting by the instructor.
