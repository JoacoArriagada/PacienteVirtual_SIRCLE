**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The learners enter the room to find an immediately postpartum woman who feels light headed and is concerned about her heavy vaginal bleeding. They should assign team roles and then immediately assess the patient including asking for vital signs, placing the patient on monitor, performing a pelvic exam and taking a thorough history. The learner should also request that his team obtain appropriate IV access and send appropriate labs in case the patient requires blood transfusion. The learner should ask for an OB to be notified of a postpartum woman with significant hemorrhage. The learner should employ appropriate fundal pressure. Learner should also estimate blood loss. Upon completion of lab draw, pelvic exam, fundal pressure and estimation of blood loss case will end.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. *Failure to obtain IVF access or initiate resuscitative IVF* Most of our learners recognized that in that setting of childbirth and vaginal bleeding, tachycardia could represent significant maternal hemorrhage, and therefore initiated appropriate resuscitation. We found it helpful to have the tachycardia worsen and for hypotension to develop to prompt the learner to resuscitate the patient if they didn’t recognize it immediately upon learning of the vital signs.
2. *Failure to recognize postpartum hemorrhage:* some of our learners did not recognize the significance of the postpartum hemorrhage. In this case had the patient state they felt even more bleeding, and felt lightheaded. We found that this prompted all remaining listeners to perform the appropriate maneuvers to control post partem bleeding.
