**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The learners enter the room to find a gravid woman who feels the need to push. They should assign team roles and the immediately assess the patient including asking for vital signs, placing the patient on monitor, performing a pelvic exam, and assessing for nuchal cord. The learner should also request that his team obtain appropriate IV access and send appropriate labs in case the patient requires blood transfusion and resuscitative IVF. The learner should then ask for OB to be notified of an imminent delivery. After preparing for and attempting to deliver the baby the learner should recognize that the baby is failing to descend and recognize that this is a case of shoulder dystocia. Upon recognition that this case is complicated by shoulder dystocia the learner should employ appropriate maneuvers in an attempt to relieve the dystocia and deliver the baby. Upon use of the appropriate maneuvers the baby will be delivered and the case will end.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. *Failure to obtain IVF access or initiate resuscitative IVF* Most of our learners recognized that in that setting of childbirth and vaginal bleeding tachycardia could represent significant maternal hemorrhage, and therefore initiated appropriate resuscitation. We found it helpful to have the tachycardia worsen and for hypotension to develop to prompt the learner to resuscitate the patient if they didn’t recognize it immediately upon learning of the vital signs.
2. *Failure to recognize shoulder dystocia* some of our learners did not recognize that failure for the baby to descend was due to shoulder dystocia. Some thought to it was due to mechanical failure of the simulation equipment. We found it helpful to for the mother to in worried manner bring up that her previous childbirth had been complicated by shoulder dystocia as an additional prompt to the learner. If the learner was still unable to recognize that shoulder dystocia was the cause of failure to descend we had a staff member enter the scene in the role of the consultant OB who had just arrived. At this point the staff would take over the case, educate the learner about shoulder dystocia and demonstrate the appropriate maneuvers for delivery.
