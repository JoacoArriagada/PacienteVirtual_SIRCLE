**Appendix F: Case Debriefing Questions and Sample Discussion Points for Facilitators**
Case 2 Debrief Questions:

1. What are some attitudes and assumptions in this case? What was the microaggression?
2. What strategies can be employed in patient encounters in which the patient has a bias toward foreign physicians?
3. How would you discuss this patient encounter with your attending physician?

Case 2 Debrief Questions and Sample Answers:

1. What are some attitudes and assumptions in this case? What was the microaggression?

The case doesn’t explicitly say whether Dr. Patel actually has an accent. Patients sometimes make assumptions about English proficiency and country of origin based on someone’s appearance or name. Even if Dr. Patel has an accent, the patient is making an assumption that physicians who learned English as a second language are less proficient in medicine. Furthermore, patients often ask “where are you from” to gain information about the provider’s ethnic/racial background, even if the provider is from the United States. Other assumptions in this case are that foreign doctors are less qualified to give care to patients in this country, and medical schools in other countries are not as rigorous as American medical schools. Foreign doctors (and possibly foreign workers) are taking American jobs.

1. What strategies can be employed in patient encounters in which the patient has a bias toward foreign physicians?

Residents/trainees can emphasize that foreign medical graduates must complete a rigorous process to even become licensed in the United States. This includes taking all certification exams and completing residency in a given specialty, on top of all medical training in their home country. The resident and trainee can also emphasize that their attending physician is in fact their supervisor, and they are learning from his expertise.

If unsure of how to respond in these scenarios, trainees can speak about the situation with a peer, other staff member, chief resident, or attending physician.

1. What are some of the benefits of discussing this microaggression with your attending physician?

It validates that the microaggression is something worthy of discussion. It creates space for discussion about collective humanity and how to promote equitable work environments. It is an opportunity to address the microaggression as a united front. It provides the attending physician the opportunity to address the microaggression directly and returns power to the oppressed person.
