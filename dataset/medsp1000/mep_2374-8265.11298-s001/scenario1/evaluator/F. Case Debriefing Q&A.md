**Appendix F: Case Debriefing Questions and Sample Discussion Points for Facilitators**

Case 1 Debrief Questions:

2. What are some strategies that a female resident physician can utilize in order to mitigate the assumption that she is not a doctor? What strategies might she utilize to support her colleagues in nursing?
3. What are some strategies that a male bystander can utilize in this scenario?
4. Imagine that you are an upper level on an inpatient service and witnessed a similar event with one of your interns. How would you talk to your intern about it?

Case 1 Debrief Questions and Sample Answers:

1. What are some attitudes/assumptions displayed in this case? What was the microaggression in this case?

The patient is assuming that the provider is a nurse because she is female, which is a microaggression. Furthermore, the patient may have the impression that nurses are not an essential part of the medical team, with the comment “I’ll let you know when the doctor comes in and tells me what’s actually going on.”

1. What are some strategies that a female resident physician can utilize in order to mitigate the assumption that she is not a doctor? What strategies might she utilize to support her colleagues in nursing?

The resident may emphasize her qualifications and training as a physician and note that her nursing colleagues are trained differently (for example, knowledge of medication administration and operating IV pumps). All members of the healthcare team are important and essential to optimal patient care, and it is important to highlight this when addressing the patient.

1. What are some strategies that a male bystander can utilize in this scenario?

The bystander in this case scenario may refer to the female physician as “Doctor” in the presence of patients. If the female physician is his supervisor (upper level resident or attending), he may reiterate to the patient that she is senior to him (i.e. “this is my boss”). When addressing patients, use terms that they will understand such as “boss” or “supervisor.”

1. Imagine that you are an upper level on an inpatient service and witnessed a similar event with one of your interns. How would you talk to your intern about it?

Acknowledge that you noticed what the patient had said and that it was inappropriate. Use open-ended questions “What did you think about what just happened?” Take time to check in with the intern and assess their feelings about the situation. Discuss with your team about strategies to prevent misidentification of team members, such as evaluating the patient as a group and introducing all the members of the team and their roles. If sexual harassment by a patient is involved, always address the patient when it occurs.
