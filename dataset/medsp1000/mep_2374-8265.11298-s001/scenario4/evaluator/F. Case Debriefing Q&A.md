**Appendix F: Case Debriefing Questions and Sample Discussion Points for Facilitators**
Case 4 Debrief Questions:

1. What are some attitudes/assumptions displayed in this case?
2. What are some strategies that a bystander can utilize in this scenario?
3. What are the benefits of discussing this patient encounter with your colleague?

Case 4 Debrief Questions and Sample Answers:

1. What are some attitudes/assumptions displayed in this case? What are the microaggressions in this case?

The patient made an assumption about Dr. Harris that she was a member of the nutritional staff and not a physician. Although it is not a problem to assist the patient with other staff duties (removing meal trays, transporting), these requests can wear on the resident physician on top of the other job responsibilities. Additionally, male physicians are typically not expected to assist patients in the same way. The patient also asked an invasive question about Dr. Harris’ hair; this microaggression assumes that the hair of black women is not natural. Furthermore, Black people have been discriminated in the workplace due to their hairstyles. A Duke University study found that participants viewed Black hairstyles like afros, twists, or braids as less professional than White hairstyles.1 Finally, the patient implied that Dr. Harris was “an angry Black woman” by commenting on her attitude.

1. What are some strategies that the senior resident could utilize in this scenario?

The senior resident could explain to the patient that it was not Dr. Harris’ responsibility to remove his or her breakfast tray. Additionally, he or she could also remind the patient of Dr. Harris’ qualifications and her value to the healthcare team.

1. What are the benefits of discussing this patient encounter with your colleague?

By discussing the microaggression with the intern, it validates that these microaggressions are worth discussing. It creates space for discussion regarding collective humanity, how to promote equitable work environments, and gives the opportunity to address microaggressions as a medical team. Furthermore, it also gives the opportunity for your colleague to address the microaggression directly if she wishes to do so.
