**Appendix F: Case Debriefing Questions and Sample Discussion Points for Facilitators**
Case 3 Debrief Questions:

1. What are some attitudes/assumptions displayed in this case?
2. What are some strategies you can employ when a patient comments about your age or suggests that you are not old enough to be a physician?

Case 3 Debrief Questions and Sample Answers:

1. What are some attitudes/assumptions displayed in this case? What was the microaggression in this case?

The patient may be assuming that the resident is younger than he or she is and that he or she may not be old enough to have completed medical training. The patient may also be assuming that younger physicians may not provide the level of care of a more experienced physician, given that he or she has been seeing an older physician for many years.

1. What are some strategies you can employ when a patient comments about your age or suggests that you are not old enough to be a physician?

The resident could discuss that they are old enough to have completed medical training (without stating their actual age). Many patients are unfamiliar with the course of training in the medical profession, so explaining the difference between graduating medical school and completing residency training may be beneficial in this case. Furthermore, the resident could consider complimenting older physicians for their experience and point out that he or she was trained by older physicians, but that he or she is also licensed to provide medical care and is committed to providing the highest quality care for the patient.

The resident could also employ humor in this case. When the patient asks the resident about his or her age, a possible response could be “I’m old enough to be your doctor,” without disclosing his or her age.

The resident could also employ distraction techniques by saying “let’s get back to what brought you into the office today.”
