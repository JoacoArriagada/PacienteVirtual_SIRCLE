VI. Case Narrative

# a. Scenario Background

i. Chief complaint (given freely): “I was shopping in the garden center and when I reached down to pick up a plant, a snake bit me! My hand is swelling and it really hurts!” (if asked) “My friend took a picture of the snake on his phone, want to see it?”   
ii. Past Medical History (if asked): Asthma, mild intermittent   
iii. Allergies: None   
iv. Medications: albuterol rescue inhaler prn dyspnea (uses 1-2 times a month)   
v. Immunizations: “No idea”   
vi. Family and Social history: no family diseases, social alcohol (1 drink a week), no tobacco or illegal dugs   
vii. Review of systems: Mild chest tightness with dyspnea (“I think my asthma is back”)

# b. Scenario Initial State

i. Patient Habitus and Presentation: the patient gives history of being bitten by a snake. “I think it was a rattlesnake” and complains of worsening swelling and pain in the hand.   
ii. Physiology: T 37.2, HR 120bpm, BP 140/80 R 22 SPO2 98%. Narrow complex rhythm with tachycardia on monitor.   
iii. Focused exam:

1. General: Reveals a patient in moderate distress (pain) and anxious

a. Head, Ears, Eyes, Nose, Throat: No evidence of trauma. Pupils are equal, round, and reactive with intact ocular movements. No discharge.

b. Skin: Normal color and turgor, no diaphoresis or rash

c. Hand: Right hand is swollen and discolored with fang marks and edema, patient is wearing a ring and a watch.

d. Cardiovascular: Narrow complex rhythm, tachycardia. No murmurs, rubs or gallops.

e. Lungs: Tachypnea and expiratory wheezing

f. Abdomen: Non-tender

g. Neurological: Alert and oriented x 3

h. Nose and IV site: coagulopathy can be represented by adding simulated blood to area surrounding IV site and nose

# c. Scenario Branch Points

iii. Scenario C: The patient is experiencing moderate pain at the bite site, mild expiratory wheezing, and some bleeding for the nose. Appropriate treatment is initiated with pain medication administration, IVFs, albuterol nebulization administration, and antivenom administration. In this scenario, the patient will develop anaphylaxis in response to antivenom administration. The patient’s wheezing will get worse, the blood pressure will drop and the patient will complain of itching and chest tightness. The confederate nurse can say, “After I gave that first vial he developed hives”. The patient’s symptoms will improve with correctly dosed epinephrine. (Epinephrine 1:1000 0.3-0.5 ml IM)

iv. Laboratory Studies: Laboratory results are located in Appendix H

v. X-ray and EKG: Results are located in Appendix G

vi. Medications:

1. IV Normal Saline Bolus: Only minimal improvement in patients sinus tachycardia   
2. Albuterol nebulization solution: 2.5mg given in simulated nebulizer. The administration of this solution will improve the patients expiratory wheezing caused by the acute asthma exacerbation   
3. Epinephrine: 0.3-0.5 mL of a 1:1000 solution given IM will stop anaphylactic reaction to antivenom

vii. Consultation of Specialist(s): Depending on the stability of the patient and the knowledge base of the learners, we strongly suggest consultation with the Toxicology service (and/or Poison Control Center). Handoff to the Critical Care Service should be made after patient stabilization

d. Information for Actors: See Actor Prompt Sheet in Appendix B

# f. Tips to keep scenario flowing to direct actors:

i. We recommend using the confederate nurse to help guide participants if they are struggling. The confederate can also call attention to the patient if the instructor wants to add anaphylaxis management to the scenario. Communication with the confederate using an earpiece is optimal so that the instructor can help direct the scenario.   
ii. If instructor chooses to have the patient develop anaphylaxis in response to CroFab, standard anaphylaxis management (most importantly epinephrine IM) should be given and the infusion stopped. The Poison Control Center, if consulted again, will usually recommend starting a low dose epinephrine drip and continued infusion of antivenom for severe envenomation.

# Appendix K

# Simulation Central Poison Control General Snakebite Guidelines

**For simulation use only. This guideline is not meant to be a substitute for Poison Control consultation for patient care

# First Aid:

1. Clean the wound area well.   
2. Update the patient’s tetanus status.   
3. Do not apply ice on the area.   
4. Do not apply a tourniquet.   
5. Do not attempt to incise and drain or to extract venom from the bite.   
6. Prophylactic antibiotics are not recommended.   
7. Keep the extremity at the level of the heart.

# Hospital Management:

1. Obtain IV access and begin serial circumferential measurements on the affected extremity at multiple sites, starting at the bite site.

a. Mark above and below the tape, to avoid confusion regarding tape placement, at each site and label them for use of a flow sheet.   
b. Measurements should be done every 15-30 minutes initially.

2. Initial labs include: CBC with platelets, PT/INR/PTT, and fibrinogen.

a. These labs should be repeated as needed depending on presentation, but at least within 6-8 hours. Other studies should be ordered on a case-by-case basis.   
b. Type/cross any patient with obvious signs of coagulopathy.

3. Consider x-rays to assess for foreign bodies in the wound such as fangs

5. Patients with any swelling should be admitted with continued serial measurements (frequency depending on degree of swelling).

6. This guideline is not considered a substitute for consulting poison control and obtaining the most recent recommendations from the on coll toxicologist. 1-800-222-1222.

# Indication:

Rattlesnake envenomation

# Mechanism of Action:

CroFab is a venom-specific Fab fragment of immunoglobulin G (IgG). It exerts its therapeutic effect by the binding and neutralizing venom of the subfamily Crotalinae found in North America, facilitating venom inactivation, redistribution and elimination from affected body tissues.

# Administration:

1. Place patient on cardiac monitor.

2. Administer initial Control Dose; usually 4-6 vials or by toxicologist recommendation.

a. Dilute each vial with 18 mL of 0.9% saline and mix by gentle inversion. Do not shake. Vent vial to allow for air to escape.   
b. Place the total mixed vials together in 250 mL of NS. (Remove 70-100 ml of fluid from bag). Start at 25-50 mL/hr over first 10 minutes and watch for any reaction.   
c. If tolerated, give the rest over 60 minutes.   
d. Use reconstituted product within 4 hours

3. The need for additional antivenom must be based on the clinical response and the recommendations of the toxicologist.

4. A Maintenance Dose (2 vials every 6 hours X 3 doses) is recommended by the package insert and may be requested by the toxicologist once initial control has been established.

5. Admit patient to a monitored setting.

# Monitoring Parameters and Other Instructions:

1. Assess for allergies including papaya, pineapple, ovine (sheep) serum, or latex. An allergy to any of these substances is not an absolute contraindication to CroFab administration in cases of moderate to severe envenomation, but should alert personnel that the patient is at risk for an anaphylactoid or anaphylactic reaction. Consult the toxicologist for recommendations regarding how to handle this situation.

2. Determine if patient has ever received CroFab before as this may predispose the patient to an allergic reaction.

3. Obtain IV access and begin serial circumferential measurements on the affected extremity at multiple sites, starting at the bite site. Mark the width of the measuring tape at each site, and label them such that reference can be made when a flow sheet is started. In addition, if the leading edge of the swelling in the extremity is palpable, demarcate and track the progression. Initially, measurements should be done every 15-30 minutes.

4. Initial lab orders should include a CBC with platelets, PT/INR/PTT and fibrinogen. It is recommended to check a CK level to assess for rhabdomyolysis. The same lab orders should be repeated in 6-8 hours, even in patients showing no or minimal swelling since the coagulopathy may be subclinical, and more frequently if the patient is critical or showing evidence of coagulopathy. If antivenom is administered, these labs should be ordered approximately one hour after the completion of each CroFab dose.

5. Type/Screen any patient with evidence of moderate-to-severe envenomation, including coagulopathy.

6. Based on clinical trials, fifty percent of patients exhibiting a coagulopathy may have a recurrence of the coagulopathy following CroFab therapy.
