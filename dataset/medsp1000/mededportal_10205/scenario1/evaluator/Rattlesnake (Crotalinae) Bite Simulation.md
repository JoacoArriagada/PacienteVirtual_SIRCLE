# III. Learning Objectives

# a. Objectives

i. Evaluate and assess a patient with a snake bite   
ii. Prioritize diagnostic testing and therapeutic options   
iii. Synthesize clinical and laboratory data to formulate a treatment plan   
iv. Utilize appropriate resources and consultants   
v. Communicate effectively with patient, consultants and staff

# b. Critical Actions Checklist

i. Obtain accurate history including tetanus status, prior envenomation and allergies ii. Correctly identify rattlesnake iii. Perform appropriate first aid: remove jewelry, clean and wash bite, keep extremity at level of the heart iv. Update tetanus immunization v. Pain control with narcotics while being mindful of hypotension vi. Measure circumference of affected extremity on presentation and repeat every 15-30 minutes initially vii. Order CBC, chemistry, PT, PTT, fibrinogen or d-dimer, type & screen, CK viii. Consult poison control ix. Administer antivenin x. Epinephrine for anaphylaxis (optional path) xi. Once patient is stabilized, transfer/handoff to Critical Care team

# c. Critical Mistakes Checklist

i. Do not apply ice   
ii. Do not place a tourniquet   
iii. Do not attempt to incise and drain wound   
iv. Do not give prophylactic antibiotics   
v. Do not perform or recommend a fasciotomy

VI. Case Narrative

# a. Scenario Background

i. Chief complaint (given freely): “I was shopping in the garden center and when I reached down to pick up a plant, a snake bit me! My hand is swelling and it really hurts!” (if asked) “My friend took a picture of the snake on his phone, want to see it?”   
ii. Past Medical History (if asked): Asthma, mild intermittent   
iii. Allergies: None   
iv. Medications: albuterol rescue inhaler prn dyspnea (uses 1-2 times a month)   
v. Immunizations: “No idea”   
vi. Family and Social history: no family diseases, social alcohol (1 drink a week), no tobacco or illegal dugs   
vii. Review of systems: Mild chest tightness with dyspnea (“I think my asthma is back”)

# b. Scenario Initial State

i. Patient Habitus and Presentation: the patient gives history of being bitten by a snake. “I think it was a rattlesnake” and complains of worsening swelling and pain in the hand.   
ii. Physiology: T 37.2, HR 120bpm, BP 140/80 R 22 SPO2 98%. Narrow complex rhythm with tachycardia on monitor.   
iii. Focused exam:

1. General: Reveals a patient in moderate distress (pain) and anxious

a. Head, Ears, Eyes, Nose, Throat: No evidence of trauma. Pupils are equal, round, and reactive with intact ocular movements. No discharge.

b. Skin: Normal color and turgor, no diaphoresis or rash

c. Hand: Right hand is swollen and discolored with fang marks and edema, patient is wearing a ring and a watch.

d. Cardiovascular: Narrow complex rhythm, tachycardia. No murmurs, rubs or gallops.

e. Lungs: Tachypnea and expiratory wheezing

f. Abdomen: Non-tender

g. Neurological: Alert and oriented x 3

h. Nose and IV site: coagulopathy can be represented by adding simulated blood to area surrounding IV site and nose

# c. Scenario Branch Points

i. Scenario A: The patient is experiencing moderate pain at the bite site, mild expiratory wheezing, and some bleeding for the nose. Expectations for this scenario include making a timely diagnosis of the current condition (Crotalinae envenomation with possible DIC and asthma exacerbation) and then instituting a treatment plan focused on aggressive treatment of these symptoms. The patient’s heart rate will decrease with the administration of pain medication. The expiratory wheezing will resolve if albuterol nebulizer treatment(s) are given. The swelling in the arm will progress as per the confederate RN until poison control is consulted and antivenom is initiated. Labs will be consistent with DIC and the Critical Care service is called for patient handoff after stabilization.

1. Changes in Patient Condition: The patient will not deteriorate over the course of this scenario. The combination of IVFs, pain control, albuterol nebulizer, and antivenom administration will stabilize the patient   
2. Ineffective interventions: the placing of a tourniquet or incision and drainage of the wound will not result in any improvement of the underlying conditions

iv. Laboratory Studies: Laboratory results are located in Appendix H

v. X-ray and EKG: Results are located in Appendix G

vi. Medications:

1. IV Normal Saline Bolus: Only minimal improvement in patients sinus tachycardia   
2. Albuterol nebulization solution: 2.5mg given in simulated nebulizer. The administration of this solution will improve the patients expiratory wheezing caused by the acute asthma exacerbation   
3. Epinephrine: 0.3-0.5 mL of a 1:1000 solution given IM will stop anaphylactic reaction to antivenom

vii. Consultation of Specialist(s): Depending on the stability of the patient and the knowledge base of the learners, we strongly suggest consultation with the Toxicology service (and/or Poison Control Center). Handoff to the Critical Care Service should be made after patient stabilization

# Appendix K

# Simulation Central Poison Control General Snakebite Guidelines

**For simulation use only. This guideline is not meant to be a substitute for Poison Control consultation for patient care

# First Aid:

1. Clean the wound area well.   
2. Update the patient’s tetanus status.   
3. Do not apply ice on the area.   
4. Do not apply a tourniquet.   
5. Do not attempt to incise and drain or to extract venom from the bite.   
6. Prophylactic antibiotics are not recommended.   
7. Keep the extremity at the level of the heart.

# Hospital Management:

1. Obtain IV access and begin serial circumferential measurements on the affected extremity at multiple sites, starting at the bite site.

2. Initial labs include: CBC with platelets, PT/INR/PTT, and fibrinogen.

3. Consider x-rays to assess for foreign bodies in the wound such as fangs

5. Patients with any swelling should be admitted with continued serial measurements (frequency depending on degree of swelling).

6. This guideline is not considered a substitute for consulting poison control and obtaining the most recent recommendations from the on coll toxicologist. 1-800-222-1222.

# Indication:

Rattlesnake envenomation

# Administration:

1. Place patient on cardiac monitor.

2. Administer initial Control Dose; usually 4-6 vials or by toxicologist recommendation.

3. The need for additional antivenom must be based on the clinical response and the recommendations of the toxicologist.

4. A Maintenance Dose (2 vials every 6 hours X 3 doses) is recommended by the package insert and may be requested by the toxicologist once initial control has been established.

5. Admit patient to a monitored setting.
