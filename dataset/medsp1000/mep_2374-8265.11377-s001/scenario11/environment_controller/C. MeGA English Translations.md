1) What is it

Comic vignette: Doctor speaking

Doctor: “Raquel has fever. Her body temperature is higher than normal. Fever is not dangerous; it is almost always a normal reaction of the body when it is exposed to a virus or another infection. We don't always know the exact cause. You should closely watch Raquel's symptoms in the coming days. Those symptoms, such as cough, belly pain, throat pain, runny nose, or changes in her bathroom habits when she urinates or has a bowel movement [Note: In Spanish, with children it is common to use the slang terms “pipí” or “popó” with regards to bathroom habits], can give us clues about the cause of her fever.”

The diagnosis is typically made after:

Listening to the child’s symptoms and medical history   
Doing a physical exam   
Sometimes we do other tests to find out the cause of the fever, but these tests are not always recommended.

2) Cómo se trata

Comic vignette: Mother holding Raquel while listening to treatment information

Caption: We treat fever with medications that are available over-thecounter, but it is important to give the child the correct dose according to her weight.

La fiebre se trata con medicamentos de venta libre, o sea sin receta, pero es importante darle la dosis correcta según su peso.

Doctor: “We will weigh Raquel now so we can calculate the correct fever medication dose for her. At home, you should fill the small cup or syringe up to the line that indicates her dose. Do not use a spoon to measure the amount. You can give her a dose of fever medication every three hours, if needed, by alternating between two different medications: ibuprofen and acetaminophen. For example, if you give her acetaminophen at three o’clock and she still has fever at six o’clock, then you can give her a dose of ibuprofen at six o’clock.”

“Vamos a pesar a Raquel para calcular la dosis. En casa, debe medir la medicina usando el vasito o jeringa que viene con el frasco hasta llegar a la línea indicada. No use una cuchara. Puede medicarla cada 3 horas alternando entre ibuprofeno y acetaminofeno. Por ejemplo, si le da acetaminofeno a las 3 y todavía tiene fiebre a las 6, le puede dar ibuprofeno.”
