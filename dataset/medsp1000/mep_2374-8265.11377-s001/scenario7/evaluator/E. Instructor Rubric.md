**Appendix E**. Instructor Rubric for Medical Jargon Measurement and Classification

To measure and track students’ progress in meeting the learning objective of reducing use of medical jargon, instructors may use the following instructions, sample table, and flowchart (summarized from Ortega P, Cisneros R, Park YS. Spanish medical jargon: A new metric for improving patient-centered communication with Spanish-speaking patients. Patient Educ Couns. 2023;109:107644. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pec.2023.107644>)

**Part A. Steps for Medical Spanish Jargon Measurement**

1. For each transcript, count the total number of words, and cross-reference each word with a list of plain language words, such as that available in the *Diccionario para la enseñanza de la lengua española: español para extranjeros* or a similar plain language word list. The optional resource we used as a plain language word list may be accessed as follows:

Dictionary for Spanish Language Teaching: Spanish as a Foreign Language. Vox: Bibliograf. Ed. Ezquerra MA. Vox, 2000.

1. If a word **was not found** in the plain language word list, cross-reference it with an anglicism dictionary or another word list that represents the regional variety of Spanish. For United States Spanish, this may include the *Diccionario de anglicismos del español estadounidense* or a similar resource to identify potential words that are in common usage in the prevalent regional Spanish variety. In the setting of this study, we were interested in identifying words common in United States Spanish that may not have been incorporated into the plain language word list. The optional resource we used may be accessed as follows:

Moreno-Fernández F. Diccionario de anglicismos del español estadounidense. Instituto Cervantes at Harvard University; 2018. Informes del Observatorio/Observatorio Reports 037-01/2018SP. Accessed October 23, 2023. <https://cervantesobservatorio.fas.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/diccionario_anglicismos_1.pdf>

1. If a word **is** **found** either in the plain language or regional (e.g., anglicism) word lists, the instructor or another person with medical Spanish knowledge should review the words to determine whether it has a distinct medical meaning. A word has a distinct medical meaning if it has a different definition when used in a medical setting compared to when it is used in plain language. Examples in English are “lab,” “progress,” “negative,” and “positive.”
2. If a plain language/anglicism word **has a distinct medical meaning**, cross-reference it with a medical Spanish dictionary, such as the *Diccionario de términos médicos* or other similar resource. The optional resource we used for this purpose may be accessed as follows:

[Book version] Real Academia Nacional de Medicina. Diccionario de términos medicos. Real Academia Nacional de Medicina; 2012 OR [Web version] Diccionario de términos medicos: presentación. Real Academia Nacional de Medicina. Accessed October 23, 2023. <https://dtme.ranm.es/index.aspx>

Of the words with distinct medical meaning, **terms found in the medical Spanish dictionary are considered jargon.**

1. If a word **is not found** in either the plain language or anglicism word lists, cross-reference it with a Spanish medical dictionary, such as the *Diccionario de términos médicos.* Of words not found in the plain language or anglicism words list, **terms found in the medical Spanish dictionary are considered jargon.**
2. **Calculate total jargon per transcript by adding the number of words identified as jargon after Step 5 and those identified as jargon after Step 6.**
3. Terms not found in any of the word lists or in the Spanish medical dictionary should be reviewed by the instructor or another person with medical Spanish knowledge to determine whether it is a non-Spanish word. Common non-Spanish words found in medical Spanish transcripts include neologisms (made-up words) or English terms. Neologisms and English terms are sometimes used by medical Spanish learners to compensate for gaps in their Spanish vocabulary knowledge. **This step will yield the number of non-Spanish words per transcript.**

**Part B. Steps for Jargon Classification: Explained vs. Unexplained**

1. Review each jargon word (total from Part A, Step 7 above) in the context of the transcript to determine whether it is explained or unexplained. Medical Spanish instructors or individuals with medical Spanish knowledge are best suited to complete this step or guide students in peer-reviewing each other’s transcripts to determine whether a jargon term is explained or unexplained. Jargon terms are considered to be explained if they meet one or more of the following criteria:
2. The student provides a clear definition or explanation of the term.
3. The student provides a word/phrase synonym in plain language.
4. The student provides sufficient context for the term during the encounter/transcript that the meaning of the term becomes clear.
5. Count the number of jargon terms that did not meet the above criteria in each transcript. **This step will yield the number of unexplained jargon words per transcript.**

**Part C. Steps for Problem Word Classification**

1. Problem words are defined as terms that a patient is expected to have difficulty understanding. Add the number of non-Spanish words (Part A, Step 8) and the number of unexplained jargon words (Part B, Step 2). **This step will yield the total number of problem words.**
