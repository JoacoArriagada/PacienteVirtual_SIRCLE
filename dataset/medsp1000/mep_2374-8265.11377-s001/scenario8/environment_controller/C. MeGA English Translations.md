1) What is it

Comic vignette: Doctor speaking

Doctor: “This type of headache is called a migraine. A migraine usually causes pain on one side of your head, sensitivity to light and loud noise, and sometimes also nausea and vomiting. A migraine can last a few hours but sometimes it can last days. Some people experience an unusual sensation before the headache starts, such as seeing some flashes of lights or a pattern of lines their vision. Now we will focus on helping you feel better and later we will talk about how to prevent other migraines.”

1) Qué es

La doctora le explica el diagnóstico a Clara.

“Este tipo de dolor de cabeza se llama migraña o jaqueca. Una migraña típicamente causa dolor en un lado de la cabeza, sensibiliad a la luz y a los ruidos y a veces náuseas y vómito. Puede durar unas horas pero a veces días. Algunas personas tienen una sensación extraña antes del dolor de cabeza, como luces o líneas en la vista. Ahora vamos a ayudarla a sentirse mejor y luego hablaremos sobre cómo prevenir otras migrañas.”

The diagnosis is typically made after:

Listening to your symptoms and your medical history   
Doing a physical exam   
In some cases, your primary care doctor or specialist will run other tests, like magnetic resonance imaging (an MRI) of your brain, but these tests are not always recommended.

Yellow diamond: Remember! Please do not leave without asking any questions you may have. We want to help you understand and improve your health

El diagnóstico de migraña se hace después de:

2) How is it treated

Caption: Migraines are treated with medications to relieve the pain and nausea. The doctor explains the treatment plan to Clara.

Doctor: “I'm going to give you medications to relieve the migraine. First, we are going to place an IV to give you fluids and medication for the pain and nausea. We are going to turn off the lights so you can be more comfortable. Once you feel better, we will talk about a pill that you can take at home if you notice you are having the same type of headache again.”

Comic vignette: Picture of IV medications

Caption: Some migraines get better with rest and with taking a pill. Other times, migraines are more severe and may need IV treatment, especially if you are dehydrated and unable to drink fluids because of vomiting.

3) When to seek medical attention

Comic vignette: Patient asking question about follow-up and doctor providing information

Caption: Clara feels better, and she is being discharged from the hospital. The doctor explains to Clara that if she is feeling worse or notices certain concerning symptoms, she should seek medical attention immediately.

# Dialogue

Patient: “If I still feel badly tomorrow, should I return to the hospital?”

Doctor: “It is possible that tomorrow you will still feel tired or have some headache. But, there are some symptoms that would be concerning and could indicate something more serious. If you notice these symptoms, you should return to the hospital.”

Possible concerning symptoms:

Severe headache   
Headache that feels different than your usual headache   
Weakness in part of your body, difficulty speaking, or lack of balance   
Confusion

Comic vignette: Doctor and patient dialoguing about preventive health and social aspects of care

Caption: Migraines are very common, and there are some situations that can set off a migraine. These factors can be different for every person but some common examples are stress, certain foods, tobacco, and lack of sleep.

2) Cómo se trata

La migraña se trata con medicamentos para quitar el dolor y las náuseas.

La doctora le explica a Clara sobre su tratamiento:

“Le voy a dar medicamentos para aliviar la migraña. Primero, le vamos a poner una vía intravenosa para darle suero y medicina para el dolor y las náuseas. Vamos a apagar las luces para que usted esté más cómoda. Cuando se sienta mejor, vamos a hablar sobre una pastilla que puede tomar en casa si empieza a notar el mismo tipo de dolor de cabeza, otra migraña.”

Algunas migrañas se resuelven con descanso o con tomar una pastilla analgésica. Otras veces las migrañas son más fuertes y puede necesitar tratamiento intravenoso, especialmente si se encuentra deshidratada debido al vómito.
