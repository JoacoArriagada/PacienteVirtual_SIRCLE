**Medical Facts for Lumbar Puncture**

<u>**How an LP is performed:**</u>

1. The patient is positioned in the lateral decubitus position or in a seated position.  An LP is usually done in the L3-L4 interspace, to decrease the risk of injuring the spinal cord.

1. Using strict aseptic technique the skin of the lower back is cleaned with an antiseptic solution to decrease the risk of infection.

1. An area over the lower mid-portion of the back in the midline is anesthetized with local anesthetic that is introduced into the skin with a small needle.  The patient will feel a needle stick and some burning in the area as the local anesthetic is introduced.

1. A small (22-27g) needle is introduced between the vertebrae into the sac surrounding the spinal cord and some of the fluid surrounding the spinal cord is removed and sent to the laboratory for analysis.

1. The needle is removed and a Band-aide is applied to the puncture site.

<u>**Benefits of LP:**</u>

1. Appropriately diagnose meningitis

1. Ability to identify the bacteria responsible for meningitis will allow the specific selection of antibiotics appropriate for the treatment of that particular organism.

<u>**Risks for LP:**</u>

1. Development of spinal headache.  The incidence of spinal headache is directly related to the gauge of the needle used.  The incidence is approximately 25% with the use of a 22-gauge needle and 0.4% with the use of a 26-gauge needle.  Treatment includes conservative treatment with acetaminophen, NSAIDS, fluids, and caffeine.  Some cases require an epidural blood patch.  Rarely this may require further evaluation of the etiology of the headache and or surgical intervention.

1. Possible neurologic injury or problem.  This is quite uncommon and occurs in less than 1:10,000 procedures.   Sometimes this can be due to bleeding with epidural hematoma and nerve compression.  This could possibly result in the need for surgery to correct the problem.

1. Possible introduction of infection into the spinal fluid with development of meningitis.  This is very rare and occurs in less than 1:100,000 cases.

1. Possible injury to brain in the setting of elevated CNS pressure.  Performance of an LP in this setting could result in brain herniation (this should not occur if this has been previously ruled out with a head CT).

<u>**Alternatives to Lumbar Puncture:**</u>

1. Empiric antibiotics. (Disadvantage: Uncertain what is being treated and requires long-term hospital stay)
2. Supportive care or no intervention. (Disadvantage: Risk of uncontrolled infection)
