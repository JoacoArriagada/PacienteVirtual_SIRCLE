# Patient: Cee Peyare

CC: stopped breathing, blue lips

HPI: Cee Peyare is a 3 week old, former full term infant who presented after Mom noticed that he seemed to stop breathing and his lips turned blue. He was taking his bottle and started coughing like he was choking and he spit up a little. He seemed to not breathe for “a long time” but Mom doesn’t remember how long it actually was. His lips were blue, so Mom gave him 4 rescue breaths and he started breathing again. He did not have any shaking movements of his arms or legs but he did seem a little limp while she was holding him. She called EMS. When they arrived, his oxygen saturation was reportedly $93 \%$ on room air. He otherwise looked like his normal self. Mom requested EMS bring them to the emergency room for evaluation. He has not had any fevers or cold symptoms. He has been eating well, taking his normal 3-4 ounces every 3- 4 hours. There has been no difficulty breathing. He spits up with about half of his feeds and occasionally seems to choke but he has never done anything like this before. He does not sweat or seem like he has a hard time breathing when he takes his bottles. He has been growing and developing normally.

In the ED, he was noted to be afebrile, with O2 sats of $94 \%$ on RA, RR 60, HR 180, BP 75/45. Examination was remarkable only for tachycardia. Mother fed patient in ED and infant noted to become fussy during the feed with concern that he was not tolerating the feed and “arching his back.” CXR obtained showed normal lung fields and normal cardiac silhouette. Patient has been afebrile, so no infectious work-up was obtained. Patient admitted to hospital medicine further management and observation due to concerns for ALTE.

Assessment: Cee Peyare is a 3 week old, former full term infant who presents following choking episode with concerns for apnea and perioral cyanosis, concerning for ALTE. However, patient with new murmur appreciated on examination as well as poor weight gain since birth associated with a positive family history for potential congenital heart disease, raising concern for potential cardiac etiology relating to poor feeding technique in association with other symptoms. Differential for ALTE at this time includes: 1) GI – potential reflux given report of frequent spitting up and discomfort with feeds (arching of back) as well as some improvement with formula change, 2) cardiac – given new murmur, difficulty with breastfeeding, and family history of congenital heart disease as well as poor weight gain, 3) infectious – no report of fever or other infectious symptoms and therefore unlikely, 4) neurologic – no report of seizure like activity surrounding event and no family history, 5) metabolic/inborn error of metabolism – newborn screen reported to be normal and no family history, and 6) pulmonary – no appreciable family history and normal newborn screen for CF.
