IX. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

A. Multiple-patient scenario- It’s Ramadan and Intelligence has discovered the insurgency plans to commit a “Tet Offensive” style attack on all military bases simultaneously. The Division Surgeon has issued a FRAGO (fragmented order) to all Medical Units to be prepared for casualties, to conserve resources as much as possible and to obey the Geneva Convention with regards to EPW’s (Enemy Prisoner’s of War). The team was across the base getting extra supplies and when they return they discover the surgeon (the others are down at the Special Forces compound gathering more information through informal channels) has just started a laparotomy on a soldier who was hit with an AK-47 round which punched through his body armor (wasn’t wearing extra porcelain plates).

This patient arrives in a ground ambulance. Report from field medic: “This is a teenage Iraqi insurgent. He was an attacker who hurled himself at the defenses unsuccessfully. He got in close before being gunned down, and therefore we retrieved him from the open. He looks bad !”

Case progression:

1. Patient arrives with medic. Initial assessment and management interventions to be performed. Routine airway management techniques will fail, and will require surgical airway management by surgeon.

2. After surgical airway management, vital signs on reassessment: heart rate: 132 bpm blood pressure: $6 2 / 4 3 \mathsf { m m } \mathsf { H 9 }$ respiratory rate: BVM / vent oxygen saturation: $8 6 \%$ on $100 \%$ FiO2

IV’s needs to be started for 2 liter fluid bolus, then blood. The patient’s blood pressure will not respond to these interventions.

A full secondary exam needs to be completed to identify a left infrascapular GSW that is thoracoabdominal. This injury will be missed if no logroll is performed, such that the patient will decompensate into PEA and expire within 15 minutes.

3. Upon institution of airway and circulatory interventions with recognition of the thoracoabdominal GSW, the team needs to recognize the patient’s continued instability and arrange for emergency surgery at the FST.

XI. Debriefing Plan

A. Method of debriefing

1. The FST was designed to emergently treat and operate on injured soldiers. This scenario will test not only the technical, individual skills, but the ability of the team members to assume different roles. With the trauma surgeon committed to a significant operative intervention, either the nurse or the nurse anesthetist should assume the role as team leader and decide the roles for the other members. Which person becomes the team leader will vary depending on the experience, rank and personal characteristics of the two officers present.

2. See “RCT MULTI-PATIENT SIMULATION DEBRIEFING OUTLINE” file

B. Debriefing Topics

.) didactic content - trauma patient assessment $^ +$ stabilization - airway management - circulatory management - wound care - difficult airway management - thoracoabdominal wound management

2d b.) teamwork behaviors - leadership - resuscitation leadership establishment - role and responsibility assignment - collaboration - recognition and integration of team input - error recognition and correction - communication - callouts of critical information - callbacks for confirmation of information - situational awareness - continued patient reassessment - plan development and execution - task prioritization - workload assessment - team member cross-monitoring - requests for assistance - professionalism

C. Questions to facilitate the debriefing -how does the open neck injury complicate airway management? what options are available in the field? -what does the location of the GSW (infrascapular) tell you about potential injuries -discuss medical ethics of caring for injured enemy combatants in the context of the Geneva convention
