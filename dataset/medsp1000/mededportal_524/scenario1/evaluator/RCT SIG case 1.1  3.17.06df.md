XI. Debriefing Plan

A. Method of debriefing

1. The FST was designed to emergently treat and operate on injured soldiers, but a number of civilians including children have been treated in Afghanistan and Iraq. Although this is a pediatric case, there is no specific pedi equipment necessary to care for this patient, at 15 years old, so it would not be unreasonable to expect the team to be able to care for this patient. This patient is a real case from the $9 4 7 ^ { \mathrm { t h } }$ FST in Afghanistan.

2. Removing the FST surgeon from the scenario would be a good way to assess the teams’ individual and group skills. In the real world, the surgeons are often asked to leave the FST for a number of reasons. There is always somebody stationed at the FST or a direct radio link to the unit, so that a team member would be present on the arrival of all patients, but it’s conceivable a patient could come in unexpected so having the surgeon missing adds realism.

3. This scenario will test not only the technical, individual skills, but the ability of the team members to assume different roles. With the trauma surgeon missing, either the nurse or the nurse anesthetist should assume the role as team leader and decide the roles for the other members. Which person becomes the team leader will vary depending on the experience, rank and personal characteristics of the two officers present.

Debriefing Topics

a.) didactic content - trauma patient assessment $^ +$ stabilization - airway management - circulatory management - wound care - landmine injury patterns - specific concerns with adolescent trauma

b.) teamwork behaviors - leadership

- resuscitation leadership establishment

- role and responsibility assignment - collaboration

- recognition and integration of team input

- error recognition and correction - communication

- callouts of critical information

- callbacks for confirmation of information - situational awareness

- continued patient reassessment - plan development and execution - task prioritization - workload assessment - team member cross-monitoring - requests for assistance - professionalism

C. Questions to facilitate the debriefing -how is 3-hour delay in presentation going to affect patient's status? -what are the common errors in tourniquet application? -besides inadequate hemostasis, what are other significant consequences of inappropriate tourniquet application? -excluding extremity injuries, what are other injuries expected with landmine and improvised explosive devices? -discuss life-threatening complications of significant extremity injuries
