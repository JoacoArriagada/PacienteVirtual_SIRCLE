XI. Debriefing Plan

A. Method of debriefing

1. The FST was designed to emergently treat and operate on injured soldiers. Removing the surgeon from the scenario would be a good way to assess the teams’ individual and group skills. In the real world, the surgeons are often asked to leave the FST for a number of reasons. There is always somebody stationed at the FST or a direct radio link to the unit, so that a team member would be present on the arrival of all patients, but it’s conceivable a patient could come in unexpected so having the surgeon missing adds realism.

2. This scenario will test not only the technical, individual skills, but the ability of the team members to assume different roles. With the trauma surgeon missing, either the nurse or the nurse anesthetist should assume the role as team leader and decide the roles for the other members. Which person becomes the team leader will vary depending on the experience, rank and personal characteristics of the two officers present. Significant blunt multi-trauma with CNS, abdominal, and extremity injuries will require rapid resuscitation for temporary stabilization prior to disposition.

B. Debriefing Topics a.) didactic content - trauma patient assessment $^ +$ stabilization - airway management - circulatory management - wound care - MVA injury patterns - CNS injury - intraabdominal hemorrhage - liver laceration extremity trauma - humeral fracture

teamwork behaviors   
- leadership - resuscitation leadership establishment - role and responsibility assignment   
- collaboration - recognition and integration of team input - error recognition and correction   
- communication - callouts of critical information - callbacks for confirmation of information   
- situational awareness - continued patient reassessment - plan development and execution - task prioritization - workload assessment - team member cross-monitoring - requests for assistance   
- professionalism

C. Questions to facilitate the debriefing -how are military vehicle-associated injury patterns different from civilian MVAs? -how does hemorrhagic shock affect patients with intracranial injury? -discuss intubation medications in context of multi-trauma -what steps can be taken to expedite multi-trauma patient disposition?
