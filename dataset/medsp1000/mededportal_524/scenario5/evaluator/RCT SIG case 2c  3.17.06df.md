IX. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

A. Multiple-patient scenario- It’s Ramadan and Intelligence has discovered the insurgency plans to commit a “Tet Offensive” style attack on all military bases simultaneously. The Division Surgeon has issued a FRAGO (fragmented order) to all Medical Units to be prepared for casualties, to conserve resources as much as possible and to obey the Geneva Convention with regards to EPW’s (Enemy Prisoner’s of War). The team was across the base getting extra supplies and when they return they discover the surgeon (the others are down at the Special Forces compound gathering more information through informal channels) has just started a laparotomy on a soldier who was hit with an AK-47 round which punched through his body armor (he wasn’t wearing the extra porcelain plates).

This patient arrives in a ground ambulance. Report from field medic: “This is Lieutenant Colonel Brass. He was directing defensive fire and was hit just under his body armor in the right lower quadrant with shrapnel from mortar. His vital signs are BP 90/60 HR110 RR 24 O2 sat $\textcircled { 9 } \textcircled { 5 } \textcircled { 1 }$ He’s a little clammy. Secondary survey shows just one entrance wound.”

C. Flow diagram with branch points times of expected interventions and reactions from Sim Man with notes (see Appendix A checklist)

Case progression:

1. Patient arrives with medic. Initial assessment and management interventions to be performed. The patient requires immediate fluid resuscitation to prevent progression into uncompensated hemorrhagic shock. He will be a partial responder, vital signs on reassessment:

heart rate: 90 bpm blood pressure: 102 / 72 mm Hg respiratory rate: 24 oxygen saturation: $100 \%$ on $100 \%$ FiO

“I’m feeling a little better. How are the other guys doing?”

2. Later, this patient will become unstable again and require another fluid bolus and 2 units of O- blood to maintain vitals above until evacuation. “I feel like I’m gonna throw up! Man, my gut really hurts!!”

3. After administration of IV fluids and type-specific blood, the treatment team should identify the patient as needing evacuation to the CSH and a (nine line) medevac request should be given over the radio. Once medevac is called he will be stable for the rest of the scenario.

XI. Debriefing Plan

A. Method of debriefing

1. 1. The FST was designed to emergently treat and operate on injured soldiers. This scenario will test not only the technical, individual skills, but the ability of the team members to assume different roles. With the trauma surgeon committed to a significant operative intervention, either the nurse or the nurse anesthetist should assume the role as team leader and decide the roles for the other members. Which person becomes the team leader will vary depending on the experience, rank and personal characteristics of the two officers present.
