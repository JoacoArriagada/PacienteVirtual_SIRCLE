**Educational Objectives:**

Primary Objectives

1. Be able to identify/ diagnose a patient with suspected adrenal insufficiency
2. Understand and implement the correct management for a patient with adrenal insufficiency in shock

Secondary objectives

1. Be able to follow the PALS algorithm for hypotension
2. Recognize when a patient requires intubation

**Environment:**

1. Set- Up: An emergency room medical resuscitation bay. The learners may be notified that the patient has arrived and the temperature was noted to be low. The learners should identify that the patient has hypothermia and instruct the nurse and other participants of what they need, including IV access, radiographs, labs and IV fluid.
2. Mannequin Set-up: High fidelity infant simulation mannequin
3. Props: Overhead lights to use as warming lights, intubation equipment (Bag and mask, Laryngoscope, size 2.5/3.0 endotracheal tube with stylet), chest radiograph, laboratory results including a blood gas if requested.

Actors:

1. Children’s parent can be played by a case instructor, this person answers the participant’s questions about the history
2. ED nurse: typically played by one of our own pediatric ED nurses, may also be played by a case instructor. The nurse functions in a typical nursing role
3. Any consultants: the team may place a consult to any subspecialist; pediatric endocrinology, pediatric intensive care physician. To place a consult they may call on the phone in the room, or say out loud to whom they want to consult. We have a phone in the simulation room that they can call and speak to a “consultant” who is one of the case instructors. Typically the consulted team is not available to come to the bedside forcing the learners to perform the necessary procedures (in this case intubation).

Instructors Notes:

1. Scenario programming: Optimal management path – the patient is warmed using external techniques (overhead lights, warm blankets, warmed IV fluid) after about 3-5 minutes the patient’s blood pressure begins to drop and is resistant to IV fluid resuscitation. The patient should be intubated at this point, given IV steroids, given IV antibiotics, and started on a vasopressor agent.
2. Major errors:
   1. Not recognizing hypotension: Nurse may need to point out to the learners the low blood pressure
   2. Not recognizing other causes of hypotension such as adrenal insufficiency and therefore not administering IV steroids
   3. Not treating fluid resistant hypotension with vasopressor agents
   4. Not initiating antibiotics
