**Infant with adrenal insufficiency – Scenario Branch Points**

Optimal management path

* Group made aware that the patient is in the EC, they form a team and evaluate the patient
* Complete vital signs are obtained and IV is placed, pt is noted to be hypothermic
* Overhead heat lamps are placed as well as warmed blankets and warmed IVF bags
* Lab work is sent including CBC, Blood culture, ua, urine culture, CMP
* Considerations for pt being septic – antibiotics are ordered – Ampicillin and cefotaxime, consider LP is pt deemed stable
* Pt’s blood pressure begins to drop after 10 minutes of being in the ER
* IVF bolus is given with no change in the BP
* A full exam is done and the pt is noted to have ambiguous genitalia
* Team recognized that the pt may have adrenal insufficiency, a cortisol level is sent and pt is started on IV steroids – hydrocortisone
* Blood glucose should be re-checked. It will be 25. Pt will required D10 bolus (5 mL/kg)
* PICU is consulted as well as endocrine
* Pt’s BP remains low, decision is made to intubate the patient to help with the hypotension
* When the pt is intubated they will code if atropine is not given, if atropine is given then the pt will be intubated and sent to the PICU for further care
* If the patient arrests, one dose of epi will bring the patient back, if pt is intubated again without atropine arrest will occur again and it will take 3 doses of epi to bring the patient back – if pt was unable to be intubated because they coded then bagging will be easy during this time.
* Pt is taken to the PICU

Potential Complications

* Failure to form a team
* Failure to adequately treat the hypothermia – no warmer lights, no antibiotics given
* Ambiguous genitalia not recognized – failure to recognize that this patient requires steroids
* Failure to give atropine when intubating the patient
* PICU not consulted
* No final disposition
