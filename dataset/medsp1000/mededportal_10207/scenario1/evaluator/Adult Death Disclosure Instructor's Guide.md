**Instructor Guide: Adult Death Disclosure to a Surviving Spouse**

Files included in the submission

1. Learner Door\_Prompt
   1. Should be provided to the learner for review prior to entering the room.
2. SP Surviving Spouse Info
   1. Provide to SP as part of training
   2. Details information regarding setting, family dynamics, prior knowledge upon arrival in the emergency department, mood, affect, and demeanor.
3. Table of Ideal Responses\_r2
   1. Provide to SP as part of training.
   2. Scripted responses to learner
   3. Presented sequentially in order of steps in death disclosure.
   4. Contains anticipated statements by the learner
   5. Includes verbal and non-verbal cues.
4. Competency Rating Instrument
   1. Provide to faculty rater (or SP) in advance of the session.
   2. Presented sequentially in order of steps in death disclosure.
   3. For each critical action, rater should select needs improvement, meets expectations, above expectations, n/a if not observed, and add comments as necessary.
   4. The scores on this form can be verbally discussed with the learner as well.
   5. Can be used for formative or summative assessment.

Prior to the execution of this case, all SP’s were provided with both written scripts and one hour of live training. Faculty raters also received the materials, as well as guidelines on death disclosure in advance. Because of the emotional nature of the subject material, SP’s are given the opportunity to decline the role ahead of time, and those who are known to have experienced similar circumstances are not cast for the role. In addition, time is made for debriefing after the day, and for decompressing during the day. Many of the SPs have their own processes for coming out of emotionally draining characters and they are encouraged to use these. These are standard procedures within our SP program. Each SP will perform the same case about eight times in one day.

During the scenario, the residents were observed by the SP, the faculty rater, and a peer, all of whom delivered feedback. The faculty rater completed the direct observation tool which was created using language which maps to the EM milestones. The tool was reviewed for content validity by palliative care experts. The tool is organized in chronological order following the expected course of a death disclosure, though does not specficially follow the GRIEV\_ING mnemonic. We have not altered it at this time to maintain symmetry between this case and the previous submission referenced above, but it can be easily adapted as needed.

Materials needed:

* Timer/Watch
* Rater checklist and pen
* Two chairs or one chair and one examination table
* Box of tissues

Session Length: 20 minutes, divided evenly between scenario and debriefing
