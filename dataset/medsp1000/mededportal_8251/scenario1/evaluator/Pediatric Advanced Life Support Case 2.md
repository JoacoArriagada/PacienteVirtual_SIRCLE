I. Title: Pediatric Advanced Life Support Case 2: A 2 year old becomes unresponsive on the Pediatrics ward Topic: Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) Last modified: 12.2.2010

II. Target Audience: All Pediatric Advanced Life Support Providers

III. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives

A. Learning Objectives 1. Demonstrate recognition of the clinical diagnosis of ventricular fibrillation (VF) 2. Demonstrate knowledge of how to treat VF, including provision of quality CPR before and between defibrillation attempts 3. Demonstrate knowledge of how and when to use supplementary medications (e.g. epinephrine) during the treatment of VF

B. Critical Actions Checklist—a list to ensure the educational/assessment goals are met. This may include:

1. Simple checklist of critical actions—built into the case narrative that follows. For optimal performance, the student should assess the patient, offer immediate CPR (5 cycles/2 minutes), defibrillate at $2 ~ \mathrm { J } / \ k \ g$ , resume CPR (5 cycles/2 minutes), reassess the rhythm, defibrillate at $4 \ : \mathrm { J } / \ k \ g$ , resume CPR (5 cycles/2 minutes), deliver 1:10,000 epinephrine at 0.01 mg/kg/dose, defibrillate at $4 \mathrm { j } / \ k \mathsf { g }$ , resume CPR (5 cycles/2 minutes), and reassess the rhythm.

2. Optimal sequence of critical actions for this patient who suffers an inpatient arrest that was not directly witnessed—assess the patient, establish the diagnosis, initiate 5 cycles of CPR, defibrillate, resume CPR, deliver $2 ^ { \mathsf { n d } }$ defibrillation, resume CPR, deliver epinephrine, deliver $3 ^ { \mathsf { r d } }$ shock, resume CPR, reassess cardiac rhythm.

3. Maximum time interval to accomplish critical actions—built into case narrative that follows   
4. Scoring based on performance actions—built into the case narrative that follows. The scoring is designed to allow the instructor/course director to determine whether the student has earned an overall positive score $( > 0 )$ , or an overall negative score $( < 0 )$ . Overall performance scores in the negative range can arise if the student fails to resuscitate the patient or if the patient survives following commission of a single error or a series of errors that led down a “recovery” pathway. Therefore, an overall score in the negative range would reflect a need for remediation.

Weight: 12 kg T 36C HR: 0 (VF) BP: Unobtainable RR 0

Airway: Unobstructed Lungs: Absent breath sounds Heart: There is no pulse

7. Labs White blood cell count: 14,000 Electrolytes: Potassium 5, BUN 14, Creatinine 0.45, Glucose 122
