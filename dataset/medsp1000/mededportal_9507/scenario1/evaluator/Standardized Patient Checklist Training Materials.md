SP COMMENTS for Elsie Smith

Your comments are the patient’s subjective personal feelings about the interpersonal skills of the student. Write about what the student did, not about who s/he is. Never judge the person. Simply respond honestly, about how you as the patient *felt*.

## How to Write Comments:

1. Use the “sandwich technique” when writing your comments:
   1. Start and end with comments about what the student did skillfully (positive comments).
   2. The “meat of the sandwich” (or constructive criticism), should be written in the middle. (Limit your constructive criticism to 3 or 4 items at most.)
   3. If you have no constructive criticism, then just write positive comments.
2. Be specific and describe concrete student actions that affected you either positively or negatively. The format of each comment should contain this simple linkage: *What you felt* was brought on by *what the student did* :
3. *“…listened to because you repeated back things I said.”*
4. “…*belittled, because you seemed to lecture me about not meeting your expectations about how I should take my medications*
5. Write your comments as if you were talking to the student : *As Elsie, I felt…[****Elsie’s feeling****]* *because you… [****student’s behavior****].*

## What to Write Comments about:

1. Communication Checklist Comments: Be sure to write constructive comments for items where you answered “No.” Similarly, you can write about the specific, positive student behaviors for any of the items where you answered, “Yes.”
2. Comments on other Clinical Behavior Issues : You may also comment on items such as these:
3. Draping (being exposed unnecessarily)
4. Washing his/her hands (safety concerns)
5. Warning you before touching you
6. Explaining the reason performing a maneuver or asking a highly personal question
7. Explaining the results of at least one maneuver
8. Speaking in a way that makes it hard to understand what is being said(too fast or too slow)
9. Rambling or run-on sentences.
10. Behaviors that appear to demonstrate nervousness
11. Body odor, unprofessional attire, bad breath

Sample Comments:

*As Elsie Smith I felt…*

* *…connected to you by the way you shook my hand and looked directly in my eyes when you introduced yourself.*
* *…overwhelmed because you rambled on for a long time about my condition/treatment so I lost track of what you were talking about.*

* *…cared for because you retied my gown after examining my lungs*
* *…anxious as if I was taking up your time when you kept firing questions at me*
* *…really cared for when you asked if I was OK to get home by myself.*

1. Things NOT to write comments on:
2. Never comment about a student’s lack of medical knowledge: *“…distressed because you didn’t know anything about diabetes.”*
