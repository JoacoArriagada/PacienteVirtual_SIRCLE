Faculty Evaluation Guide

Ask the student to “PRESENT THE CASE HISTORY and MENTAL STATUS EXAMINATION.” They are NOT asked initially to present an assessment and plan—you will ask them about those elements later. Feel free to redirect them if overly disorganized or too slow or to prompt them for missing elements in their presentation. Overall, this part of the presentation should take **no more than 10 minutes**.

After the initial presentation, address the following topics with the student within **15 minutes**.

WHAT WAS THE MOST NOTABLE FINDING ON MENTAL STATUS EXAMINATION?

Students should respond that the patient’s mood/affect were notable, as was his thought content (suicidal ideation). Other elements that may be noted are his behavior. The patient’s judgment may be discussed as well.

NAME AT LEAST THREE DIAGNOSES ON YOUR DIFFERENTIAL FOR THIS PATIENT?

Students should be able to justify their diagnostic considerations with a discussion of DSMIV criteria. You may ask them how they would distinguish between various diagnoses if relevant, including what other information they would want to know. Students will likely discuss some of the following:

|  |
| --- |
| Major Depressive Disorder |
| Substance-Induced Mood Disorder |
| Alcohol Abuse/Dependence |
| Bereavement |
| Adjustment Disorder |
| Mood Disorder due to General Medical Condition (if students mention, should inquire about what they might be worried about) |
| Post Traumatic Stress Disorder (students should appreciate that patient denied most symptoms) |
| Bipolar Disorder (students may mention since on differential for depressed patient, but should also appreciate no endorsed symptoms of mania currently or in past) |

WHAT SHOULD BE THE NEXT STEP IN THE CARE OF THIS PATIENT?

This question could prompt a discussion of three elements: (1) further interview/workup, (2) safety/hospitalization and (3) pharmacologic/psychotherapeutic treatment. Any of these elements may be discussed with the student, but please be sure to focus on issues of safety. You should ask the students whether or not they think the patient should be hospitalized, and why they arrived at their decision. You may further prompt them to discuss the patient’s risk and protective factors.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Risk Factors | Protective Factors |
| Male | No history of suicide attempts |
| Currently depressed w/SI | Married w/daughter |
| Alcohol Use | No clear plan or intent |
| Bereaved (sister) | Seems willing to engage in care |
| Relational problems w/wife | No known family history of suicide |
| Brought in by someone else |  |
| Access to weapons |  |

Giving Feedback:

1. Clearly state that you are now going to provide the student with feedback.
2. Ask student to self-assess, beginning with the positive: “What do you think you did well?” “What do you think you either did less well or could improve?” (If you agree with their self-assessment, build off of what they say.)
3. Offer the student praise in areas in which you felt their performance was good to outstanding.
4. Offer the student constructive criticism in areas in which you felt their performance could use improvement. You may suggest specific ways they may be able to work to improve.
5. Let the student know your overall assessment: Poor, Marginal, Satisfactory or Outstanding and let them know why you think this. Let them know that this assessment is just a part of their overall clerkship evaluation.
6. Ask the student if he/she has questions for you.
7. Offer the student encouragement for the future.
