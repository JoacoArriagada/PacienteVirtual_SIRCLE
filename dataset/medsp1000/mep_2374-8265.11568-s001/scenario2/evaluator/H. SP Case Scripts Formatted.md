| THE PATIENT’S STORY: The SP will be asked to tell their symptom story and the personal and emotion impact for each of their concerns. You will want to write this in the patient’s voice. The symptom story should be able to answer this question: “Tell me more about [chief concern/additional concern], starting at the beginning and bringing me up to now.”  The personal context should be able to answer questions concerning the broader personal/psychosocial context of symptoms, especially the patient’s beliefs/attributions.  The emotional context should be able to ask how are you doing with this, how does this make you feel, how has this affected you emotionally? IMPACT: How has this affected your life? How has this been for your family? | Four days ago, I was at home and suddenly I started to feel weak and fall. I wasn’t able to move my right arm or leg (and I’m right-handed), and I couldn’t talk. My spouse called 911 and at the hospital I was diagnosed with stroke. I got a clot-busting medication in the ER. My speech improved a lot after the clot-busting medication, but I was still weak on the right side. They admitted me to the hospital. Over the next couple of days, I had several tests done, but I didn’t need surgery. My blood pressure was running high and the team has been adjusting my meds. I’m still weak in right arm and leg. I’m worried about not being able to go back to my teaching job. I had hoped to teach 1 more year before retiring. I’m also financially supporting my two children in their 20s in grad school. My memory is fine. |

| Overall course | * Your spouse called 911 and you were admitted to the hospital with the diagnosis of a stroke. A head CT was performed in the ER which showed you had a stroke from a clot. It was not the bleeding kind of stroke. * IV tPA (a clot-busting drug) was administered in the emergency department to break-up/dissolve the clot causing your stroke. Your symptoms improved after the medication. * Several tests were done during your hospital stay including studies looking at your carotid arteries (the arteries in your neck that lead to the brain). Those studies showed some plaque build-up in your arteries, but it was not severe enough to need surgery or other procedures to clear the plaque. * When you were admitted to the hospital, your blood pressure was high. The healthcare team has added another blood pressure drug and increased your cholesterol medicine. * You have no issues with your speech since receiving the tPA and are having no memory problems. * You have persistent weakness of right arm and leg. You are able to walk with a quad-cane; you can use your non-dominant (left hand) for self-care tasks and typing; you are unable to write with ease or legibility with right hand * You are discouraged and worried about not being able to go back to your teaching job. You had hoped to teach 1 more year before retiring. You are also financially supporting your two 20ish year old children in grad school. |
