# Interprofessional Education in a Pediatric Simulation: Case of an Infant with Fever

# Faculty Facilitators’ Guide

# Overview:

This is an interprofessional pediatric case simulation designed to allow students from the Colleges of Medicine, Nursing, and Pharmacy to collaborate as they work through a scenario involving an infant with fever. The simulation is interactive, consisting of a case presentation followed by three work-stations which include (1) a clinical discussion about evaluation and management for the pediatric patient, (2) an infant lumbar puncture, and (3) preparation and administration of medication. A learnercentered approach was developed to teach pediatric patient-care concepts in an interprofessional team-based system, utilizing the Core Competencies from the Interprofessional Eduational Collaborative Expert Panel (IECEP, 2011).

# Students’ Learning Objectives:

1. Apply principles of effective interpersonal communication with a standardized patient (parent), with other students, and with faculty.   
2. Demonstrate the ability to place the patient and the family at the center of the healthcare delivery system through the use of empathy, respect, and clear communication.   
3. Utilize communication tools to promote an environment of patient safety, including the I-SBAR (IHI, 2011) and Time-Out (TJC, 2012).   
4. Demonstrate the application of psychomotor skills necessary for the completion of required pediatric patient-care tasks, respective to their discipline.   
5. Formulate a problem-based, prioritized assessment for the care of an ill child.   
6. Analyze their respective roles and those of other members of the interprofessional team as they pertain to the care of an infant with a febrile illness.

# Case Presentation:

The case presentation is best completed as a Standardized Patient (SP) exercise. If multiple SPs and instructors are available, then this may be done in IP-teams. If only one SP is available, then the SP case presentation can be done in a large-group setting, with faculty facilitation. When and SP is not available, the instructors may present the history and physical exam findings to the students in an interactive session.

The SP plays the role of the parent, presenting to the emergency department (ED) with a 28 day-old female for complaints of fever, irritability, and poor feeding. As the overall simulation is designed to follow the patient-care aspects of this pediatric case from initial presentation through treatment, students are first asked to participate in the “triage” of the patient via interaction with the SP. Physical exam findings are provided by the faculty facilitators, as prompted by the students’ questions. In the triage process, the students should obtain critical information and provide a problem-based assessment. A summary of this information is presented by the students utilizing the I-SBAR communication technique. Then the students can complete a detailed clinical history from the SP. Overall, this exercise is done collaboratively, with all students having open input regarding the collection and priority of assessment data. Please refer to “Appendix 2: Patient Summary” for details of the case.

Faculty must facilitate the data gathering process, trying to involve all the students from every discipline. Discussion of the important aspects of clinical care and pediatric evaluation is facilitated, as well, during and after the SP exercise, and may include the following:

Fever as a sign or symptom, specifically in regard to the neonate or infant patient. The pediatric history and physical, specifically focused on the birth history and history of present illness (HPI) as it relates to this case. Discussion may also include Immunizations, Diet/Breastfeeding, Social History, etc.   
• Signs and symptoms of dehydration in the pediatric patient.   
• Obtaining and interpreting vital signs in the pediatric patient.   
• Obtaining and interpreting growth parameters in the pediatric patient.   
• The I-SBAR communication tool in providing hand-off of patient data.   
• Diagnostic considerations and possible interventions, including evaluation and treatment.

# Rotating Work Stations:

Once the students have completed the case presentation and general discussion, either in their IP-teams or as a larger-group, they will then rotate, in their IP-teams, through work-stations that are each facilitated by faculty members.

1. Lumbar Puncture. This station focuses on general concepts involved with clinical procedures (including informed consent, planning and preparation, “Time-Out”, and safety) and should allow for the application of these concepts in a simulated infant lumbar puncture (LP), as it relates to the case of our infant with fever. Clinical medicine (pediatrics) or nursing faculty facilitate discussion and provide coaching and assistance with the procedure as needed. As desired, or as time permits:

• Discuss the elements of informed consent (risks, benefits, alternatives) and the ethical/legal issues surrounding informed consent for medical interventions; specifically, discuss obtaining informed consent from a parent for an infant LP.   
• Discuss a general approach to planning and preparation for medical procedures.   
• Review patient and staff safety principles, including the procedural environment, positioning, restraint, monitoring, and the proper handling of equipment and specimens, specifically as it relates to the performance of a LP in an infant.   
• Perform a “Time-Out” prior to beginning the simulated procedure, to emphasize safety in verifying the proper patient and the proper procedure.   
• Review the procedural techniques for performing a LP in an infant.   
• Using sterile technique, a LP should be performed by the students, each having the opportunity to experience the different roles of the teammembers in the simulated procedure.

2. Medication Selection & Orders. This station focuses on medical management of the simulated patient. Building upon the discussions that were initiated in the case presentation, the students are asked what interventions they wish to initiate in the further evaluation and treatment of the infant. And, they must provide the orders to effect their plans. Clinical medicine (pediatrics) or pharmacy faculty facilitate discussion and oversee the order writing exercise. Please refer to “Appendix 3: Example Admission Orders”. As desired, or as time permits:

Discuss the selection of empiric antibiotics for the infant-SP, based on a review of the likely pathogens that this patient is at-risk for based on exposure history (i.e., birth).   
• Review general principles of order writing and, specifically, weight-based dosing in children.   
• Students should be allowed to collaborate, and all students should be given the opportunity to practice writing, reviewing, and verifying orders, regardless of their respective disciplines or roles on the patient-care team. Practice weight-based calculations for IV-fluids and medications.   
• Utilize medication references for assistance with recommended dosing guidelines.

3. Medication Preparation & Administration. This station focuses on medication formulation, preparation, and administration in the simulated clinical setting. Clinical nursing or pharmacy faculty facilitate discussion and provide coaching and assistance with the tasks. As desired, or as time permits:

• Discuss appropriate weight-based dosage calculation and medication administration guidelines or recommendations.   
• Students should be allowed to collaborate, and all students should be given the opportunity to prepare and verify admixtures of medications, utilizing appropriate dosage, concentration, and volume calculations.   
• Administer medications and fluids after performing appropriate safety checks.
