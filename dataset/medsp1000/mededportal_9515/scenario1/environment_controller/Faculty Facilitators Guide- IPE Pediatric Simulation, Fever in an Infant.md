# Interprofessional Education in a Pediatric Simulation: Case of an Infant with Fever

# Faculty Facilitators’ Guide

# Overview:

This is an interprofessional pediatric case simulation designed to allow students from the Colleges of Medicine, Nursing, and Pharmacy to collaborate as they work through a scenario involving an infant with fever. The simulation is interactive, consisting of a case presentation followed by three work-stations which include (1) a clinical discussion about evaluation and management for the pediatric patient, (2) an infant lumbar puncture, and (3) preparation and administration of medication.

# Case Presentation:

The case presentation is best completed as a Standardized Patient (SP) exercise. If multiple SPs and instructors are available, then this may be done in IP-teams. If only one SP is available, then the SP case presentation can be done in a large-group setting, with faculty facilitation. When and SP is not available, the instructors may present the history and physical exam findings to the students in an interactive session.

The SP plays the role of the parent, presenting to the emergency department (ED) with a 28 day-old female for complaints of fever, irritability, and poor feeding. As the overall simulation is designed to follow the patient-care aspects of this pediatric case from initial presentation through treatment, students are first asked to participate in the “triage” of the patient via interaction with the SP. Physical exam findings are provided by the faculty facilitators, as prompted by the students’ questions. Then the students can complete a detailed clinical history from the SP. Please refer to “Appendix 2: Patient Summary” for details of the case.

# Rotating Work Stations:

Once the students have completed the case presentation and general discussion, either in their IP-teams or as a larger-group, they will then rotate, in their IP-teams, through work-stations that are each facilitated by faculty members.

1. Lumbar Puncture. This station focuses on general concepts involved with clinical procedures (including informed consent, planning and preparation, “Time-Out”, and safety) and should allow for the application of these concepts in a simulated infant lumbar puncture (LP), as it relates to the case of our infant with fever.

2. Medication Selection & Orders. This station focuses on medical management of the simulated patient. Building upon the discussions that were initiated in the case presentation, the students are asked what interventions they wish to initiate in the further evaluation and treatment of the infant. And, they must provide the orders to effect their plans.

3. Medication Preparation & Administration. This station focuses on medication formulation, preparation, and administration in the simulated clinical setting.
