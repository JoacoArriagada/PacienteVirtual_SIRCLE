CHRONIC PAIN AND OPIOID ROLE PLAYS

**Case 2: Discussion of Monitoring for Risk/Safety**

It’s Friday afternoon. The patient, new to the practice, is a 32-year-old M who tells you that he has been struggling with severe lower back pain which began after a motor vehicle accident 5 years prior. When you ask about his function, the patient states that he has been unable to work and is now on disability. He states that he cannot stand or walk for more than 5-10 minutes at a time before having to sit and that is fiancée assists him with cooking and cleaning his home. When you ask about sleep he tells you that he sleeps a few hours a night, wakes up with pain, takes pain medicine and then goes back to sleep for a few more hours. The patient states that he has been taking hydrocodone 5 mg/APAP 325 mg “for years” for the pain and that it isn’t helping. He states that his previous PCP office “shut down.” When you ask what else he has tried to help with the pain he replies that he did 8 sessions of physical therapy, which might have helped a little, but his insurance would not cover any more sessions.

The participant playing the role of patient should be instructed to be anxious to get his OPM refilled and angry that the physician/provider “doesn’t believe him.”

Patient: Whatever…I am not an addict and I don’t take any other drugs or drink.

Patient: I told you I’m not an addict.

Patient: You know, I’m just so tired of people not believing I’m not in pain and not trusting me.

Patient: (pouting) Okay.
