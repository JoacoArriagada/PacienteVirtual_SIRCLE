**APPENDIX D: CHRONIC PAIN AND OPIOID ROLE PLAYS**

CHRONIC PAIN AND OPIOID ROLE PLAYS

**Case 1: Discussion of Risk/Benefits of Opioids and Goal Setting**

The patient is a 58-year-old morbidly obese woman (BMI=38) who presents with a several month-long history of bilateral knee pain R>L. She states that her pain is a “10 out of 10.” Her history and exam suggest OA which is confirmed on x-ray (moderate - severe). She has been taking ibuprofen 600 mg 1 po qid and acetaminophen 1 g po tid. She reports that she previously participated in 9 sessions of physical therapy which helped some but the pain returned to the previous state after she finished the sessions. The patient states that she took several of her husband’s hydrocodone/acetaminophen and that improved her pain some. She is requesting a prescription.

**Part 1: Goal Setting**

The participant playing the role of the physician/provider should attempt to discuss SMART goal setting with the patient regarding her goals for management of her chronic pain. The participant playing the role of a patient should be instructed to focus on the goal of being able to be more interactive with her 2 young grandchildren who she watches after school several days each week.

While multiple approaches are acceptable, below provides a framework for an effective conversation and can be used to provide the participants feedback:

Doctor: Ms. Smith I want to spend a little time talking about chronic pain with you. It’s a difficult problem and I want us to work together to help improve the pain and your quality of life. First of all, it’s important to understand that no matter what we do or what medications we use it’s very unlikely that all of your pain will go away. It’s more realistic to aim for an improvement. More important than the 1 out of 10 pain scale is setting functional and quality of life goals. What things could you do before the pain that you wish you could do now but can’t because of the pain? Think about interactions with family and friends, work, sleep, sex and spiritual activities.

Patient: Well, I watch my 2 young grandkids 2 days a week and I get frustrated because I can’t keep up with them. I spend most of the time watching them from my living room couch.

Doctor: What does being active with them look like to you?

Patient: Well, I don’t expect to run around like a young kid, but I would like to push them on the swings, maybe throw a ball back and forth, watch them on their tricycles out front….

Doctor: About how much time can you handle being active with them now?

Patient: It doesn’t seem like long….

Doctor: Would it be reasonable to set a goal spending 10 minutes being active with them each afternoon as a first step? Once you achieved that you could slowly increase the time.

Patient: I guess that would be a start.

Doctor: So when we assess if certain treatments are helping your pain that can be the measure.
