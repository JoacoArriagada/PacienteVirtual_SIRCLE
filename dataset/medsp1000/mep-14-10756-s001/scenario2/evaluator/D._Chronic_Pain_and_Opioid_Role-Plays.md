**APPENDIX D: CHRONIC PAIN AND OPIOID ROLE PLAYS**

CHRONIC PAIN AND OPIOID ROLE PLAYS

**Case 1: Discussion of Risk/Benefits of Opioids and Goal Setting**

The patient is a 58-year-old morbidly obese woman (BMI=38) who presents with a several month-long history of bilateral knee pain R>L. She states that her pain is a “10 out of 10.” Her history and exam suggest OA which is confirmed on x-ray (moderate - severe). She has been taking ibuprofen 600 mg 1 po qid and acetaminophen 1 g po tid. She reports that she previously participated in 9 sessions of physical therapy which helped some but the pain returned to the previous state after she finished the sessions. The patient states that she took several of her husband’s hydrocodone/acetaminophen and that improved her pain some. She is requesting a prescription.

**Opioid Risk/Benefit Discussion**

Ms. Smith returns 9 months later. She continues to take ibuprofen and acetaminophen. She tried an intraarticular hyaluronic acid injection in her right knee and is taking chondroitin supplementation but has not met her goal of being able to be more interactive with her grandchildren. She again requests a trial of opioids for her chronic pain.

The participant playing the role of physician/provider should be instructed to discuss the potential benefit (possible pain relief) of opioid pain medication (OPM) and review all risks. The conversation about risks of OPM should include unintentional overdose and death, risk of addiction, constipation, cognitive impairment, sexual dysfunction and osteoporosis. Below is a model of an effective discussion:

Doctor: Ms. Smith, I want to discuss the benefits and risks of opioid pain medications. Like many medications they have some benefit and unfortunately a significant amount of risk. The potential benefit is that they can improve your pain and function, however it’s important to know that they only improve pain at most in about 50% of people who try them, and we don’t know if that effect lasts long-term. The most serious risk is unintentional overdose. By that I mean if a person takes more than they are prescribed or takes these medications with other drugs like other opioids, tranquilizers, or alcohol they can fall asleep and not wake up.

Patient: I did hear something about that actor overdosing…

Doctor: Right…there is a risk of developing addiction to these medications which means someone starts to crave the drug and lose control over taking the pills. We don’t know how many people will get addicted to the medications, but it may be as high as 10%, and it can happen to anyone, even well-meaning people like me and you. If you try these pills for your pain, we will have to monitor together if you develop any of these symptoms. Other problems include constipation, nausea, drowsiness, and they can lower your sex drive. Another very important thing to know is that if other people get a hold of them who they are not prescribed for those people are at risk for overdose and death. So you need to keep these medications locked up and safe.

Patient: I had no idea…well, my pain is bad..I would like to try the medication.

Doctor: Okay. I would also ask that you try at least one other non-opioid treatment that you haven’t tried yet, like exercise, relaxation/meditation, massage.

Patient: Okay….
