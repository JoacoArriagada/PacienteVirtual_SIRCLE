**APPENDIX B: EFFECTIVE CHRONIC PAIN MANAGEMENT AND RESPONSIBLE OPIOID PRESCRIBING FACILITATOR/INSTRUCTIONAL GUIDE**

**Effective Chronic Pain Management and Responsible Opioid Prescribing – Facilitator/Instructional Guide**

**Overall Goals:**

The overall goals for our workshop are to review best practices in chronic pain management and principles of responsible opioid prescribing, discuss implementation strategies, and enhance communication with patients. The workshop needs one facilitator for each small group of 5.

**Workshop objectives:**

1. Identify best practices in chronic pain management including non-opioid and non-pharmacologic treatments.
2. Develop strategies to efficiently and effectively implement principles of responsible opioid use in practice.
3. Apply patient-centered communication skills when treating patients with chronic pain.

**Slide 19:** Return to Case 1. The patient has not had significant relief with multi-modal non-pharmacologic and non-opioid therapy and is requesting opioid pain medications (OPM).

**Slide 20:** Engage in discussion with participants if it is ever appropriate currently to initiate OPM for chronic pain? Participants will have different opinions and different arguments to support their decisions. The authors of this workshop believe that there will continue to be patients with chronic pain who have pain that may benefit from low dose opioid pain medications if they fail all other treatments and are at low-risk for opioid-use disorder.

**Slide 21:** Introduce the risk-benefit framework for communicating with patients about opioid therapy for chronic pain – it’s about the medication, not the patient! (Nicolaidis, C) In the risk-benefit framework of communication, conversations become about the risk and safety of OPM, not whether the patient’s pain is “real.” Opioid misuse and OUD become about the medication causing the patient more harm then benefit not a moral failure of the patient.

**Slide 22:** As we do for all medications, it is important to discuss possible benefits of OPM with patients and clearly outline the risks. Benefits include a modest decrease in chronic pain in 30-50% of patients that may not be long lasting. Risks include: unintentional overdose and death, particularly if patients take more than prescribed, take with other drugs or alcohol, or have certain medical conditions like sleep apnea, addiction (signs and symptoms should be described to patients), diversion, sedation/fatigue, constipation, sexual dysfunction and osteoporosis.

**Slide 23:** Interactive question 3; emphasizes the risk-benefit communication framework. Answer: E
