3. Learning Objectives
4. Demonstrate recognition of the clinical diagnosis of supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)
5. Demonstrate knowledge of how to differentiate SVT with adequate cardiac output from SVT with inadequate cardiac output
6. Demonstrate knowledge of how to treat a patient in SVT

12. Case Narrative
13. Scenario Background Given to Participants
14. Chief complaint, triage note, medic report—A 1 month old infant arrives in ED. Mother tells you that the baby was well until 2 days ago when she began to have periods of inconsolable fussiness and poor feeding. At times the infant’s color has been somewhat dusky during these episodes. The infant is now dusky and very fussy.
15. Past medical history—Term infant, uncomplicated pregnancy, labor, and delivery. No history of fever. The infant’s diet consists of both breast milk and commercial infant formula. Mother is on no medications.
16. Past surgical history--None
17. Meds and allergies—None
18. Family/social history—Noncontributory
19. Physical Exam:

Well developed, well nourished infant who is fussy and pale.

Wt: 4 kg T: 36.5C HR: 228 BP: 66/44 RR: 55/undistressed SpO2: 89%

Airway: Unobstructed

Lungs: Clear breath sounds

CV: Regular rapid rate; a strong pulse is present.

1. Labs:

Hematocrit: 38%

White blood cell count: 12,000

Electrolytes, BUN, Creatinine: all normal. Glucose 122

1. Initial Scenario Conditions: State: SVT1
2. History: The infant is fussy
3. Initial exam: The airway is unobstructed. Breath sounds are clear. Pulse is strong.
4. Patient’s physiology: HR: 228 (narrow complex) BP 66/44 RR: 55 SpO2: Not picking up well due to infant movement
5. What next: Treatment depends on degree of hemodynamic stability. In the stable patient you can consider an appropriate vagal maneuver such as the brief application of ice to the infant’s face, *without occluding the infant’s airway*. Vagal maneuver attempts should *not* delay preparations for chemical or electrical cardioversion. In this case, the pulse is strong and the patient is stable so if ice is ineffective, administer adenosine 0.1 mg/kg IV. Adenosine should be administered through the IV or IO that is positioned closest to the right atrium. A “fast flush” technique is recommended, in which a 3-way stopcock is connected to the IV port, and adenosine administration is followed immediately by a rapid saline IV flush.
6. Transitions:
   1. To State: svt12
      1. If adenosine dose is > 0.4 mg
         1. Points: (-20)
         2. Debrief: You gave more than the recommended 0.4 mg of adenosine
      2. If adenosine dose = 0.4 mg
         1. Points: 50
         2. Debrief: You gave an appropriate dose of adenosine (0.1 mg/kg)
   2. To State: SVT1
      1. If ice applied to face
         1. Points: (0)
         2. Debrief: Your maneuver did not change the cardiac rhythm. Do not delay in treating the rhythm.
      2. If adenosine dose < 0.4
         1. Points: (-20)
         2. Debrief: You gave less than the recommended dose (0.1 mg/kg) of adenosine. Ensure you used the correct adenosine administration technique.
   3. To State: ps1
      1. If synchronized cardioversion @ < 6 J
         1. Points: (-10)
         2. Debrief: You should have tried adenosine before cardioversion
      2. If synchronized cardioversion @ >5 J
         1. Points: (-10)
         2. Debrief: You should have tried adenosine before cardioversion and you used too much energy
   4. To State: ps2
      1. If unsynchronized shock @ > 1 J
         1. Points: (-100)
         2. Debrief: You should have tried adenosine before cardioversion and you did not synchronize
   5. To State: UNVTACH1
      1. If elapsed time > 240 seconds without intervention
         1. Points: (-100)
         2. Debrief: You should have administered adenosine (0.1 mg/kg) sooner
