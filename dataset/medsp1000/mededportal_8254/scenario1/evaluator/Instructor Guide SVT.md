1. Resource files included in the submission:

The case is written as a series of 11 physiologic states. Each state transitions to a new state when any of the predetermined transition criteria are met. The student acquires or loses points at the time of each state transition, depending on whether he or she makes a correct or incorrect treatment decision. The case ends when it transitions to a “final” state.

* 1. Word document: “Pediatric Advanced Life Support Case 10—A Fussy Baby”
     1. This file serves as a guide for programming this case into any infant or pediatric mannequin simulator (If an infant simulator is used, the age and weight can easily be modified). This file also contains text to guide student debriefing for each transition during the case simulation. Points are assigned to each state transition to allow the student or instructor to understand the relative importance of specific correct or incorrect actions.
  2. Case file for computer simulator: “Case 10 – A Fussy Baby”
     1. This file can be used with the Anesoft personal computer-based Pediatric Advanced Life Support Simulator, without modification. It provides all the text instructions required to allow a student to practice the case on the Anesoft PALS simulator, and receive an automated, instructor-free debriefing at the end of the case.

1. Case Objectives
   1. Demonstrate recognition of the clinical diagnosis of supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)
   2. Demonstrate knowledge of how to differentiate SVT with adequate cardiac output from SVT with inadequate cardiac output
   3. Demonstrate knowledge of how to treat a patient in SVT
      1. Demonstrate knowledge of when to use adenosine and the appropriate adenosine dose
      2. Demonstrate knowledge of when to cardiovert and the appropriate cardioversion dose
2. Conceptual Background
   1. Given the growing amount of evidence that Pediatric residents may not be sufficiently prepared to carry out advanced life support resuscitations in accordance with published guidelines, this case is intended to provide instructors with a case suitable for mannequin or computer screen-based simulation, that could provide pediatric health care workers with supplementary training in ventricular fibrillation management. The Anesoft Pediatric Advanced Life Support Simulator was developed to provide pediatric advanced life support providers with on-demand, instructor-free, interactive training in how to conduct pediatric resuscitations in accordance with American Heart Association guidelines. In our institution, the majority of providers who regularly undergo team-based resuscitation training in the full-scale mannequin simulator laboratory use these interactive computer scenarios to prepare for their team training.
3. Implementation requirements
   1. If this case is used with a programmable mannequin simulator:
      1. Facilitators should include at least one qualified acute/critical care or PALS instructor (content expert)
      2. Required equipment/personnel
         1. Cardioversion-capable mannequin
         2. Crash cart with mock medications (adenosine)
         3. Age-appropriate bag valve mask device
         4. IV or IO availability
         5. Cardioverter/defibrillator
         6. Cardiac monitor
         7. 4-6 trainees for team management
   2. If this case is to be used on a personal computer
      1. Required Equipment
         1. Windows-compatible personal computer
         2. Anesoft PALS Simulator software (version 7)
4. Deployment history at the University of Utah/Primary Children’s Medical Center and University of Colorado/Children’s Hospital Colorado
   1. Numbers of participants: this scenario has been simulated by over 500 PALS providers, General Surgery residents, and anesthesia residents at the University of Utah and the University of Colorado over the past 5 years.
   2. Performance expectations/anticipated management mistakes:
      1. Many providers offer an IV fluid bolus instead of adenosine or cardioversion
      2. Some providers will confuse adenosine with amiodarone or atropine
      3. Many providers fail to offer supplemental oxygen to the patient in SVT.
5. Limitations
   1. This case scenario tests cognitive skills only and does not test the providers’ ability to physically execute the “fast flush” technique for administering adenosine.
   2. This case was developed for a computer screen-based simulator or mannequin simulator. Accordingly, assessment points are attached to specific actions that are executed by the student and detected by the simulator software. If using the case in a “face-to-face” instructional format, the instructor can modify the point allocation scheme to suit particular objectives. For instance, an instructor may wish to consider scoring the recognition of SVT independently of the correct use of adenosine: 50 points might be given if learners identify SVT based on high heart rate only and 100 points might be given if they also mention other criteria (absence of history suggesting sinus tachycardia, quick onset, absence of heart rate variability with stimulation, absence of P waves, etc). Likewise, instructors using the case in a “face-to-face” instructional format may wish to assign points for adenosine administration only if students describe or demonstrate that adenosine is administered using the “fast flush” technique.
6. Other considerations
   1. The patient in this scenario expires if the student fails to successfully treat SVT AND also fails to initiate therapy for unstable VT. Some instructors may prefer to interrupt the case and offer further remedial training if unstable VT ensues, rather than allow the simulated patient to expire.
