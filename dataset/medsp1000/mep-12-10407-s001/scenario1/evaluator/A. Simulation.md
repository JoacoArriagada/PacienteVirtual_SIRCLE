# Ideal Scenario Flow

The learners are made aware that the patient is in the ED, they form a team with a clearly identified leader and evaluate the patient. The patient should be immediately connected to monitors and physical examination performed. Complete vital signs are obtained and patient is examined and noted to have premature ventricular contractions. Learner should check for pulse. Learner should obtain a history. Lab work should be sent including CBC, BMP with magnesium, calcium, and phosphorous, CBG. Learner should ask for ECG. Patient vomits and progresses to ventricular tachycardia. Learner should check for a pulse. Learner should recognize pulseless ventricular tachycardia. Learner should request defibrillation but start CPR until defibrillator arrives. Learner should defibrillate with 2 Joules/kg energy. Learner should continue to follow PALS Cardiac Arrest-Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia algorithm. Learner should ask for amiodarone or lidocaine to be prepared after the 2nd shock. Learner should ask for amiodarone or lidocaine to be administered after the 3rd shock. Learner should discuss differential diagnosis and need to treat reversible causes. If the learner gives amiodarone or lidocaine and defibrillates the patient the rhythm will convert to sinus with a pulse and adequate perfusion after the 4th shock.
