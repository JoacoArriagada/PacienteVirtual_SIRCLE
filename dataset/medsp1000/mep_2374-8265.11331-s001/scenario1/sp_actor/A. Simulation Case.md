| **Appendix A: *MedEdPORTAL* Simulation Case**  **SIMULATION CASE TITLE:** Acute Radiation Syndrome (ARS)  **AUTHORS:** Mel Ebeling, BS, Andrew Bloom, MD, Todd Peterson, MD  **LEARNER AUDIENCE:** Emergency medicine resident physicians | |
| **PATIENT NAME:** Will Rogers  **PATIENT AGE:** 47  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** “Something isn’t right with my hands”  **PHYSICAL SETTING:** Emergency Department | |

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Initial Presentation** | | | |
| **Overall Setting and Appearance** | Upon entering the simulated emergency department (ED) patient room, learners see a male manikin in a hospital gown reclining in a hospital bed. A blood pressure cuff and pulse oximeter are already in place, and intravenous access has already been obtained in the antecubital fossa in one arm. | | |
| **Standardized learners (and their roles in the room at case start**) | The embedded simulation person (ESP) will function as a nurse. | | |
| **HPI** | Unless otherwise specified, the following information may be provided only after the patient is asked by the learners:   * Onset: “I noticed it after I woke up yesterday. They were hurting enough that I called out of work, but it hasn’t gotten any better, so I decided to come in because they hurt so bad.” * Location: “Both of my hands” * Duration: “For the past day or so” * Character: “Besides being all red, they hurt really bad–like burning–and they feel kind of tight and swollen.” * Aggravating/Alleviating Factors: “I tried putting my hands on some bags of frozen peas, but that really hasn’t helped a whole lot with the pain or swelling. I can’t think of anything that’s made it worse.” * Radiation: “The pain really just seems to be around my hands and wrists” * Timing: “It seems like it’s gotten somewhat worse since I noticed it, but I came to the emergency department relatively quickly, so I’m hoping you can do something for it and it doesn’t get any worse.” * Severity: “The pain is maybe an 8/10.” * Associated Symptoms: “Well, a couple weeks ago after I came home from work, I wasn’t feeling good and threw up. My hands also felt kind of tingly and itchy and were kind of red like they are now. I thought I got dehydrated or had eaten something bad for lunch, maybe had an allergic reaction to it, but the next day or so, I was all back to normal.” * Occupational History: The patient works at a scrap yard. If asked about the event that occurred a couple weeks prior to presentation (see “Associated Symptoms”), the person voicing the patient should mention that during the workday, he was sorting through a pile of new scrap that was delivered to the yard and found a cool looking machine in the pile that he tinkered around with for about 15 minutes or so. He did not know what the machine was or did, but to him it looked like something he saw when he used to work at a place that welded pipes together. | | |
