**Appendix F: Faculty Facilitator Guide**

**Small Group Discussion and Role Play**

For the following cases, students will be divided into small groups of 3-5, each with a resident facilitator and attending facilitator to provide additional perspective. You can discuss with the resident about how you’d like to co-facilitate the session, as some residents may appreciate more opportunity to lead the session (especially discussion questions) and receive feedback on teaching.

**Cases**

For each case, students will review goals and initial intern perspective, then facilitators should encourage emotional responses and reflection on initial information provided. After this, group will move on to discussion questions, which can be facilitated jointly by resident and palliative care faculty. Resident should contribute to this discussion, especially if students are struggling. Once these initial thoughts are wrapped up, move on to role-play to allow students the opportunity to elicit the parent perspective. Resident will take on the role of the parent in this interaction. Faculty will facilitate the role-play and will guide students on opportunities to “rewind” or “pause” to obtain help from other group members or discuss a teaching point. Palliative care faculty will facilitate rotation of students “in the hot seat” every few questions. Note that the student’s guide contains cases and questions as below, though parent perspective and all other italicized portions are only included in the resident and faculty facilitator guides.

**Faculty Facilitation for Role Play**

The resident will “switch” into the role of the patient parent for the role play. Resident facilitators have limited experience acting in the parent role and may have a tendency to over- or under-act unintentionally, so faculty may need to provide specific coaching to ensure all learning objectives are met. Faculty should ask for volunteers for the role play. Each volunteer will have a chance to practice a portion of the conversation. A “time-out” should be called either when the student in the “hot seat” identifies feeling stuck or the faculty member identifies an area of opportunity. Faculty should give and elicit specific feedback on things that the learner did well from the group, and then work with the learner to identify an area of the conversation they would like to try again. Facilitators provide specific praise to each learner after their turn is over. Serial learners can either start the encounter from where the previous learner left off, or from the beginning, depending on how much conversational content is remaining, and what the student’s learning goal is.

**Case 1: Taylor**

**Intern’s Perspective***– Students to read aloud*

You are an intern getting sign out for your first inpatient general pediatrics ward rotation. One of your patients is Taylor, a 3-year-old girl who is described to you as “medically complex.” She sustained hypoxic injury at birth, and has significant neurologic impairment, and requires respiratory support at baseline with CPAP for obstructive sleep apnea. Over the last 6 months, she has been hospitalized 4 separate times (for a total of about 60 days) due to respiratory illnesses resulting in complications including pneumonia and respiratory failure. She required a PICU stay for 3 of these admissions and required intubation once. At baseline, she is nonverbal and requires assistance with all ADLs. She makes few purposeful movements but will smile in response to family members.

Taylor recently transferred to the pediatric floor from the PICU after an admission for pneumonia where she required escalation of her baseline respiratory support to BiPAP and vasopressors for resulting sepsis. One of the team members comments that she “doesn’t know why Taylor’s family puts her through all this.” When you received signout from the intern going off service, you were told that “Before Taylor goes home, our team needs to address her family’s goals of care for her.”

***Mother’s Perspective (Resident’s Role)***

*You are Taylor’s mom, Tammy. You are exhausted. Your husband works full time and is out of paid leave. You had a career as a legal assistant, but ever since Taylor was born, you’ve been home caring for her. You have 12-hours per day of skilled nursing hours through your insurance, but in reality, you only get coverage about 4 days per week—you are doing the rest. Taylor doesn’t really sleep on any kind of schedule, so you haven’t either in 3 years.*

*The last 6 months you’ve really noticed a decline in Taylor’s health. She used to have so much joy- rolling on the floor, playing with her parents, reaching for toys- it seemed like she was always smiling. You’d give anything to go back to that time- when you realized just how meaningful and fulfilling life can be with a kid with special needs. Ever since that one admission last fall, Taylor just hasn’t been herself. It seems like she’ll finally start to recover from one cold, and then the next one hits, and she’s back to ground zero. The CPAP machine was a recommendation from a few admissions ago. Taylor hates it! She always fusses when it’s on, and you both seem to be sleeping even less. You’re really hopeful that something can be done to allow her to stay out of the hospital longer this time, so that she can finally get back to where she used to be. But you’re also really scared that that might no longer be possible.*

*(This is background information to help the resident get into their role as the parent. Parent should start the conversation with the intern open to talking, but unsure why the intern is suddenly asking so many “big” questions. As they talk about these things, parent is becoming defensive and sad, responding in short statements with limited information. Their voice may start to crack as they struggle to hold back tears. It is difficult to talk about this. If the intern does a good job setting an agenda, asking for permission to talk about these things, and responding to the parent’s emotion, parent gradually reveals more of the “back story,” and begins to talk more openly.)*
