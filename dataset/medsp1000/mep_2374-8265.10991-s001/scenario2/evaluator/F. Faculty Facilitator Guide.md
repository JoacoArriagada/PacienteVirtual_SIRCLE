**Appendix F: Faculty Facilitator Guide**

**Educational Objectives**

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

1. Describe the fundamental components of pediatric palliative care.
2. Name and utilize three unique communication strategies for holding a difficult conversation or responding to a family member’s emotions.
3. Employ approaches to elicit a family’s goals and values when their child faces a serious medical condition.
4. Gain confidence in teaching communication skills.

**Small Group Discussion and Role Play**

For the following cases, students will be divided into small groups of 3-5, each with a resident facilitator and attending facilitator to provide additional perspective. You can discuss with the resident about how you’d like to co-facilitate the session, as some residents may appreciate more opportunity to lead the session (especially discussion questions) and receive feedback on teaching.

**Cases**

For each case, students will review goals and initial intern perspective, then facilitators should encourage emotional responses and reflection on initial information provided. After this, group will move on to discussion questions, which can be facilitated jointly by resident and palliative care faculty. Resident should contribute to this discussion, especially if students are struggling. Once these initial thoughts are wrapped up, move on to role-play to allow students the opportunity to elicit the parent perspective. Resident will take on the role of the parent in this interaction. Faculty will facilitate the role-play and will guide students on opportunities to “rewind” or “pause” to obtain help from other group members or discuss a teaching point. Palliative care faculty will facilitate rotation of students “in the hot seat” every few questions. Note that the student’s guide contains cases and questions as below, though parent perspective and all other italicized portions are only included in the resident and faculty facilitator guides.

**Faculty Facilitation for Role Play**

The resident will “switch” into the role of the patient parent for the role play. Resident facilitators have limited experience acting in the parent role and may have a tendency to over- or under-act unintentionally, so faculty may need to provide specific coaching to ensure all learning objectives are met. Faculty should ask for volunteers for the role play. Each volunteer will have a chance to practice a portion of the conversation. A “time-out” should be called either when the student in the “hot seat” identifies feeling stuck or the faculty member identifies an area of opportunity. Faculty should give and elicit specific feedback on things that the learner did well from the group, and then work with the learner to identify an area of the conversation they would like to try again. Facilitators provide specific praise to each learner after their turn is over. Serial learners can either start the encounter from where the previous learner left off, or from the beginning, depending on how much conversational content is remaining, and what the student’s learning goal is.

**Case 2: Omar**

**Goals** *– Have students read*

* Utilize open ended questions to gauge a family’s understanding of their child’s illness
* Practice using ask-tell-ask communication tool
* Discuss how understanding a family’s goals and values can guide in making care decisions

**Intern’s Perspective** *– Students to read aloud*

As an intern on the pediatric oncology service, you admit Omar, a 14-year-old with metastatic osteosarcoma who has not achieved remission after his first course of chemotherapy. He is admitted with significant bony pain requiring IV pain medications.

Your fellow explains that Omar’s prognosis is poor and that there are no treatment options available that would be expected to cure his disease. The family has the option of considering some palliative chemotherapy that could has the potential to prolong survival slightly and alleviate some symptoms, though it’s hard to predict how much each individual patient will benefit from this.

When you go with the fellow to meet the family for the first time, you would like to assess the family’s understanding of Omar’s disease.

**Questions for Discussion:**

1. What do you want to know from Omar’s family?

* *Again, here, don’t worry about how you will ask for this info, just dialogue about what the info is you’d like to learn.*

1. What are some potential challenges that could exist in this situation?

*Some potential challenges to discuss:*

* *Challenge of meeting a patient & family for the first time and having to discuss serious news*
* *Challenge of not fully understanding the options yourself: you are an intern, not an oncologist or palliative care doctor!*
* *Young teen’s role in understanding their illness and decision making.*

***Parent’s Perspective (Resident’s Role)***

*You are Omar’s mother or father. When Omar was diagnosed with metastatic osteosarcoma 1 year ago, you understood there was a possibility he wouldn’t go into remission after his initial treatment. However, you chose to focus on the present and were hopeful he would “beat the odds.” You’re still in some shock after finding out 1 week ago that Omar didn’t show much response to chemotherapy on his most recent imaging.*

*Dr. Lopez, Omar’s primary oncologist, has been thorough and patient when explaining the options at this point in Omar’s treatment. You understand that at this point, there are no more treatments that would be expected to cure Omar. Dr. Lopez explained the option of palliative chemotherapy. You know it won’t cure him but are wondering if “something is better than nothing” and are worried if you don’t do chemo, you’ll be giving up. You have never cared for anyone at the end of their life and have so many ideas swirling about what that might be like. You’ve heard a little about hospice care, but you feel intimidated about the idea of doing complex medical care at home. You wonder if it will give enough support to your family. You really want a clearer picture of what each option would look like before you make a decision. You do know that it’s most important to you that Omar’s pain be minimized, and he have the best quality of life possible.*

*Right now, you are most concerned about getting this horrible pain under control. The last few days have been horrible to watch, and you can’t imagine taking Omar back home like he was before you came to the ER this time.*

*Omar has always been independent and wants to be involved in these decisions. However, often all of the medical info overwhelms him, and right now, he is so tired from all of this pain you feel like he just needs a break.*

*(This is background information to help the resident get into their role as the parent. They can start the conversation exhausted, and a little suspicious of the sudden need to ask all of these questions. If the intern addresses the parent’s emotion and lays out their reasoning for asking these questions, the parent should soften up and begin to share their deeper understanding of Omar’s prognosis - that he cannot be cured. If the intern responds to the parent’s emotion, they can begin to share about how conflicted they are feeling, and how scared they are for the future.)*

**Practice Before Role Playing:**

1. How can you introduce this topic, and figure out if Omar wants to be a part of the conversation or not?

* *Have students practice a few introductory sentences to gauge where to hold the conversation.*

1. How can you assess what Omar’s family understands about his illness at this point?

*Ideas/prompts for students to practice*

* *Ask-tell-ask communication tool*
* *It’s helpful for me to understand where you are coming from. What did you take away from the lst clinic visit?*
* *What things were not clear after the last clinic visit?*
* *Have you talked about what to expect with Omar’s disease at this stage?*

1. How can you understand what factors and values Omar’s family will use to make decisions about the future?

*Ideas/prompts for students to practice/brainstorm:*

* *What is important to your family and Omar at this point?*
* *What are your hopes for Omar? What else?*
* *As you think about the future, are there things you want Omar to be able to experience?*
* *This might be a difficult question to answer. Are you willing to share with me what you are most worried about?*
* *My understanding of some of your family’s values is \_\_\_\_\_\_. Is this correct?*
