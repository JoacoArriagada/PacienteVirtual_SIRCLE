Operative Vaginal Delivery OSCE- instructions for standardized patient:

You are a 30 year old woman pregnant with your second baby.  Everything went fine with your first delivery 2 years ago.  Now, you are a few days before your due date, you are in labor, and things have been progressing well.  Earlier in labor, your baby’s heart rate started to drop during contractions.  The doctors placed a monitor inside your uterus to measure your contractions and have used this monitoring tube to add some more fluid to your uterus.  They have also put a monitor on the baby’s head to better track the baby’s heart rate. They gave you an oxygen mask and positioned you on your side. The baby’s heart rate improved with these changes.

You reached full dilation and began to push, but your epidural is pretty strong and it’s hard for you to feel what you are doing.  In the 40 minutes you have been pushing, the baby has only come down a little.  Now the doctors tell you the baby’s heart rate is starting to worry them again.

The doctor will counsel you on the options for delivery and will recommend the use of forceps to help deliver the baby.
