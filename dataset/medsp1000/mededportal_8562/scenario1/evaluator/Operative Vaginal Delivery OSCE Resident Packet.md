Operative Vaginal Delivery Simulation and OSCE

(Objective Structured Clinical Examination)

By the close of the session, the resident will be able to:

* Conduct informed consent discussion for operative vaginal delivery
* Perform forceps assisted vaginal delivery on the simulator demonstrating appropriate technique
* Write a procedure note documenting the delivery

Operative Vaginal Delivery Case: Counseling station

The patient is a 30 year old G2 P2 at 39+ weeks having had an uncomplicated pregnancy. Her prior delivery was a 7 pound healthy infant delivered vaginally at term. She has no past medical history or surgeries, she is of normal habitus, and on admission the estimated fetal weight was 7 pounds. She has had a normal labor curve. She received epidural anesthesia which has provided good analgesia.

Some deep variables were noted early in the active phase after spontaneous rupture of membranes which resolved with position changes, oxygen and amnioinfusion. She reached full dilation and began pushing 30 minutes ago with the head at +1 station. Over the past 40 minutes, the fetal head has slowly descended to +3 station. Her epidural is dense. There is no significant caput or molding and your judge her pelvis to be adequate.

During pushing, she has had variables with each contraction. These have deepened and developed a slow return to baseline. You have noted a baseline shift to the 100s/110s. You now notice absent fetal heart rate variability. You anticipate that delivery will not occur in the next 20 minutes if the current rate of progress is continued.

You are concerned that leaving the fetus in this environment for more than 20 minutes may place the baby in jeopardy and believe operative vaginal delivery is indicated. Conduct an informed consent discussion for operative vaginal delivery.
