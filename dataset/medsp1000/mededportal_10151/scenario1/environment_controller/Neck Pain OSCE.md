Neck Pain OSCE

It is very important to read the instructions, verbatim.

Read this, verbatim:  “You have 7 minutes to take a complete, but focused history on this patient with neck pain.  You should take notes, because you will be asked to recite some of the information later.  You will be given a warning when 2 minutes remain.”

Set your stopwatch for 7 minutes.  Give the examinee a warning when 2 minutes remain, and continue to the next section when the 7 minutes has elapsed.  If the examinee stops before the history interval ends, inform him or her that there is time remaining.  The examinee must continue with the history until 7 minutes has elapsed.   Don’t give the examinee any hints about what questions to ask.

![](images/7bc6c4b3815388ed62e7825d7d7204551146b48477a2708a1c3d8c7d8c21679d.jpg)

**Read this, verbatim:  “You examine the patient.  Cranial nerve examination is within normal limits.  There is significant loss of range of motion of the cervical spine.  Motor examination reveals increased tone and 4/5 weakness in all 4 extremities.  Reflexes are hyperactive, with a few beats of clonus at both ankles.  Babinski signs are present bilaterally.  You cannot determine a precise sensory level, but pinprick and light touch are normal on the face and decreased below the neck.**  

![](images/d1ad7b5872d4b50358527a2875525a41b1e68dd38c0f887d5c6e1dc1cc1cfeee.png)

**Set your watch for 3 minutes.  This entire page should be completed within that time period.  Read this to the examinee, “The patient’s lesion localizes to which of the following:  brain, brainstem, cervical spinal cord, thoracic spinal cord, nerve root, peripheral nerve?” The examinee must respond immediately**

**State, “You have localized the lesion to a level of the nervous system.  Now, please list 4 possible etiologies, from most concerning to least concerning.”**  

**Read this, verbatim:  “The patient already had cervical spine x-rays done and results are pending.  You are at Baptist Hospital and have the time and resources available to get any study you need.  What test would you order next to make the diagnosis?”**  

**The examinee must respond immediately.  If the examinee hesitates, prompt him or her for a response.**

If the examinee just asks for a CT or MRI, ask what part of the nervous system.

If the examinee asks for 2 different imaging studies (brain and c-spine), ask examinee to choose one.

**Regardless of whether the examinee requests the correct study, hand examinee the imaging study on the next page.**  

**Tear out this page and hand it to the examinee.**

![](images/17ec6188d80edb997d5d4a3905030774bdd82393f2f3db75705ae1dbf646c221.png)

**Read this.  “Review the image presented.  How would you interpret it?”**  

**The examinee must respond immediately.**

![](images/8b7094b9e22f8258e923784b3295c6d67681e03fb651aa6742a825010470f247.jpg)

**The MRI is a T-2 weighted sagittal section of the cervical spine.  One can determine it is T2-weighted, because the CSF is bright.  The spinal cord can be seen (gray structure within the spinal canal) surrounded by bright CSF.  Some of the vertebral bodies are labeled and the disks are indicated by blue arrows.  There is a destructive lesion within the C5 vertebral body and the normal rectangular shape is obliterated. There is narrowing of the spinal canal and compression and kinking of the spinal cord, maximal at C5. There is some bright signal within the normally gray spinal cord (red arrows).**

(Image by D. Segui, modified by D. Barratt)

**Regardless of whether the examinee answers correctly, read this.  “The radiologist read the study as a lesion in the C5 vertebral body, with compression of the spinal cord.”**

**Read this to the examinee, verbatim:  “You may take a moment to think about the most likely diagnosis, including the pertinent positives.  As soon as you are ready, please state the final diagnosis, and list the pertinent positives.  Do not list any pertinent negatives.  Also state how the history, physical examination, and diagnostic tests support your diagnosis.”**  

**Set your stopwatch for 3 minutes.  Encourage the examinee to keep listing pertinent positives, but end the session after 3 minutes.  You can give hints regarding the pertinent positives.  For example, “Are there any other clinical features that support the diagnosis?”  Or, “Are there any diagnostic evaluations that support the diagnosis?”**
