**Teaching Points: Providing Medication Instruction to an Older Adult Caregiver**

Older Adult Health Literacy

* + - * Health literacy refers to an individual’s ability to comprehend medical information and make decisions regarding their health.
* It encompasses the ability to listen, follow directions, communicate with healthcare professionals, understand medical terminology, and understand cultural norms.
* Older adults have the lowest health literacy skills among all adult age groups.
  + Of adults age 65+, 71% had difficulty understanding facts written in prose form, 80% had difficulty understanding information in graphs and tables, and 68% had difficulty understanding quantitative tasks.
* This is due to visual and hearing impairments and cognitive changes.

Tips for Effective Communication with Older Adult Caregivers:

* Limit background noise. *Always turn off the TV* if there is one in the room.
* Speak slowly and clearly, but do not shout. Instead, try lowering your voice, because age-related hearing loss is associated with loss of ability to hear higher frequencies.
* Seat yourself across from the older adult and maintain eye contact.
* Let the light fall on your face. This will allow any older adults who read lips a better view of your face.
* Rephrase sentences into shorter statements, if necessary.
* Have the older adult repeat back instructions to make sure they understand.
* Provide literature that is “easy to read,” with large font **(Ariel, 14 point),** adequate margins, has sharp contrast between text and background colors, and uses plain language.
* Repeat information if the older adult does not understand. You may need to repeat the information several times for older adults, who may have decreased cognitive skills.
* Personalize the information when possible, and highlight short-term benefits of following instructions. Older adults are much more likely to follow your orders when they can visualize an attainable, concrete, positive endpoint.
* Give instructions in the positive sense (“You must do this,” instead of “You should not do that.”) Older adults are more likely to interpret familiarity as truthfulness.
* Talk about important topics face-to-face. While sending caregivers home with literature is also important, older adults are more likely to remember instructions that you discuss in person.
* Follow up! An older adult caregiver may leave your office understanding what they need to do, but that does not mean they will be able to successfully complete their tasks at home. Emotional and environmental influences at home may cause confusion and non-adherence.

Proper Use of Asthma Medications and Avoidance of Triggers

* Children may not be adherent to asthma medications due to patient and/or caretaker misunderstanding.
* It is important to stress that inhaled corticosteroids must be taken even in the absence of symptoms.
  + Up to 23% of parents of children with persistent asthma misunderstood the purpose of inhaled corticosteroids.
  + Up to 40% of children with persistent asthma do not use a long-acting controller medication on a regular basis.
* All patients and their caregivers should have an asthma education session with a healthcare provider. Topics to be discussed should include the purpose of each medication and proper use of each medication, including how to use a spacer. Possible barriers to proper medication usage should also be discussed. The patient, caregivers, and healthcare provider should together form an asthma management plan. This is associated with greater patient compliance with long-term controller medications.
* When seeing asthma patients, it is important to ascertain whether or not the patient is taking his/her medications properly. Suggestions on how this may be accomplished are listed below.
  + Asking the patient or caregiver to describe their daily schedule in relation to asthma symptoms and medication usage.
  + Asking the patient or caregiver to estimate the number of times the patient uses his/her inhaler(s) per week.
  + Asking the patient or caregiver how often his/her inhaler medications must be refilled.
* A spacer should be used with the inhaler. The inhaler should be shaken before each use.
* Common asthma triggers include allergens, infections, irritants, exercise, and emotional stress.
