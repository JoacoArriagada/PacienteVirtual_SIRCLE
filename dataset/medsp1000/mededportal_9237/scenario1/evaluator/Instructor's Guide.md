**Instructor’s Guide**

Type of Resource:Simulation Case

Case Title: Providing Medication Instruction to an Older Adult Caregiver

**Files Included with this Resource:**

* 1. Case\_Outline.doc
  2. Critical\_Actions\_Checklist.doc
  3. Learner\_Instructions.doc
  4. Learner\_Evaluation\_Form.doc
  5. Script.doc
  6. Teaching\_Points.doc

The case outline, “Case\_Outline.doc” provides a comprehensive overview of the resource. It contains all information necessary to prepare for the simulation, including directions for lab set-up, background information regarding the case, how to present the case to learners, and how to execute the simulation. It also contains much of the information contained in the separate documents, “Learner\_Instructions.doc”, “Script.doc”, “Critial\_Actions\_Checklist.doc”, and “Teaching\_Points.doc”. The information is provided in these separate documents as well to provide an easy source of reference. The document “Learner\_Instructions.doc” provides background information and explicit instructions for the learner participating in the case. The document “Script.doc” provides instructions and suggested answers to questions that the learner may ask the standardized historian. This information is included in this separate document so that it can be given directly to the standardized historian to prepare for the case. The document “Critical\_Actions\_Checklist.doc” contains a checklist of actions that should be completed by the learner. It can be completed by an observer watching the case in real-time, or while reviewing a video recording of the case. The results of the critical action checklist should be discussed with the learner during the debriefing session. The file “Teaching\_Points.doc” contains information regarding older adult caregivers, older adult health literacy, asthma medication compliance, and asthma triggers. It should be used by the individual facilitating the debriefing session. The file “Learner\_Evaluation\_Form.doc” contains an evaluation form for the learner to complete regarding the usefulness of the case. It should be completed by the learner following the debriefing session.

**Learning Objectives:**

1. Primary
   1. Improved skills in providing education and instructions regarding asthma medication.
   2. Effective history taking and communication with an older adult caregiver.
2. Secondary
   1. Recognition of misunderstanding regarding proper administration of asthma medications.
   2. Increased awareness of grandparents and older adults as primary caregivers of pediatric patients.

**Conceptual Background**

This case was designed in response to two issues commonly encountered at the authors’ institution: 1) older adult caregivers as the primary caretaker of pediatric patients, and 2) poor adherence to asthma medication regimens. The growing number of older adults as primary caretakers of children is documented in the literature. The 2000 U.S. Census identified 2.4 million grandparent caregivers. Pediatricians should feel confident in their ability to effectively communication with this unique population. Incorporating a second theme of asthma education and medication compliance was chosen because not only are asthma medications commonly misused by caregivers of all ages, but also because medication adherence is affected by health literacy skills. Older adults have the lowest health literacy skills when compared to adults of different age groups. It was decided to incorporate these learning objectives into a simulation case because simulation is a well-accepted mode of education at the authors’ institution.

**Practical Implementation**

It is recommended that the case be completed in a simulation center with the use of a simulation mannequin as the patient. Because the case scenario takes place in a primary care clinic and physical examination is not a necessary component of the case, the simulation mannequin will not need to be connected to any monitors. It would be beneficial for someone to still man the voice of the mannequin, to make the simulation more realistic. An actor or volunteer should be recruited to play the role of the older adult caregiver. The learner should be provided a blank patient chart or triage sheet, whichever is reflective of standard materials at the institution’s primary care clinic. The simulation case should be completed in approximately 15 minutes. Approximately 20-30 minutes should be allotted for the debriefing session.

**Pilot Testing**

At the time of case submission, this case has been successfully deployed six times at the authors’ home institution. Each time, a volunteer(s) has been recruited to play the part of the older adult caregiver(s).

While there has not been a recurring theme of any specific management mistake, a few individual management mistakes have been witnessed. One learner changed the medication doses for the current medication regimen. A few learners left the exam room without ensuring that the standardized historian understood the medication regimen. During the debriefing sessions, they admitted that they were not confident that the standardized historian understood how to give the patient his medications.

Pilot testing also revealed that the success of the case is dependent on the actor/volunteer playing the older adult caregiver. Allow sufficient time to discuss the case and script with the standardized historian prior to starting the case.

During pilot testing, learners were asked to complete a confidential survey regarding the usefulness of the case. Six surveys were collected, and one hundred percent of those surveyed thought the simulation and debriefing sessions were valuable learning experiences, and would recommend the case to others.

**Best Simulation Practices**

Having deployed the simulation case several times, the authors offer the following “Best Simulation Practices” to institutions interested in using this simulation case:

* If possible, use an actor, or someone who has previously played a standardized patient, for the standardized historian. If this is not possible, allow adequate time to discuss the case with the volunteer who will be the standardized historian. If a volunteer is used as the standardized historian, try to schedule the simulation case so that the same volunteer is able to participate each time the simulation case is deployed.
* Give the learner explicit instructions regarding his objectives for this case. Re-iterate that a physical exam is not necessary, and that the patient will remain stable throughout the case.
* Allow the learner 15 minutes to interview the standardized historian.
* The instructor should complete the Critical Actions Checklist while the simulation case is taking place, ideally from a separate room, using real-time video. If this is not possible, the instructor can observe the learner and complete the checklist from an unobtrusive location in the simulation room, though the instructor’s obvious presence may affect the learner’s performance.
* Debriefing should take place immediately following the simulation. Allow 20-30 minutes for debriefing. The debriefing session should begin with open-ended questions suggested in the “Questions to Facilitate Debriefing” section of the Case Outline. The instructor should incorporate the Teaching Points and feedback regarding the Critical Actions Checklist into the debriefing discussion.
