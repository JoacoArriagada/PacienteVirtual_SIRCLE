**Case 5 (Slide #9) - 15 minutes: The obese child who gets bullied at school and has poor self-esteem. Slide content: “Too afraid to go outside”:** *8 year old overweight male stays at home all day, watches lots of TV and plays lots of video games. Mom states, “It is too dangerous for him to play outside”. His grades are suffering because of his ADHD. He gets bullied often but doesn’t fight. His self-esteem is poor. What are this child’s risk and protective factors for community violence victimization or perpetration? How would you advise this family?*

**Objectives for case #5:**

The resident will be able to:

1. Recognize that bullying, unrecognized or undertreated psychiatric disease, impulsive behavior and low self-esteem are risk factors for community violence victimization
2. Discuss strategies to effectively manage bullying
3. Recognize that bullies are often living with intimate partner violence in the home setting

**Notes to Instructor:**

*After presenting the case, ask one resident to discuss their approach to the situation. Have the resident formulate questions and talking points to the Mother as if it were a real patient encounter. Allow the resident to speak uninterrupted. Then allow the other resident to add in additional points.*

*Questions that you may use to stimulate discussion include:*

*“How might the bullying influence this child’s risk for future violent victimization?”*

*“How do you think the poor self-esteem is related?”*

*“What can this mother do to protect her child from bullies at school?”*

*“What do you think the child can do to protect himself from bullies?”*

*“Finally, you may contribute additional pearls from your clinical or academic experience that were not previously mentioned by the residents.”*

*The key points to be covered in this conversation include:*

* *Risk Factors:*[*24*](#_ENREF_24)

1. *Overweight*. Help the mother to find a local recreation center or other safe place for child to exercise. Should also consider “exergaming” (see below).[28](#_ENREF_28)
2. *Decreased self-esteem*. This child should consider being evaluated by a mental health professional as this low self-esteem may be a manifestation of depression. Depressed children have higher rates of violent injury as adolescents.[29](#_ENREF_29), [30](#_ENREF_30)
3. *Video games.* Residents should inquire about the content rating of the video games.[31](#_ENREF_31) Many children are playing rated “M” (mature) or “T” (Adolescent) games with too much violence. You should encourage the mother to purchase rated “E” (Everyone) games. Also, they should consider exergaming devices such as the Wii or the Kinect instead of traditional gaming consoles as they offer the added benefit of expending calories.[28](#_ENREF_28)
4. *Poor grades.* These poor grades may be related to a learning disability, thus an evaluation and possibly an IEP should be in place.
5. *ADHD therapy needs to be maximized* so that symptoms do not preclude good academic achievement. Also, ADHD is a cause of impulsiveness that can make one more susceptible to fighting.[32](#_ENREF_32)
6. *Bullying.*
   1. *School’s that promote a culture of transparency with regards to bullying have less bullying.*[*33*](#_ENREF_33) Bullies behave in such a manner because they believe that their actions are anonymous. If bullies knew that there were repercussions for their behavior they might not engage in them. Encourage child to not keep bullying a secret, but to tell parents or teachers.
   2. *Teach the child to be strong and firm when faced with a bully.* Have him/her practice saying something like, “I don’t want to fight you. I’m not happy with what you said, but I will not fight.” Then walk away. Practicing in front of a mirror will give child confidence and will discourage acting out of impulse.

* *Protective Factors*

1. *Child stays indoors or at home all day*. Is kept safe from his violent neighborhood. While physically safe, it interferes with his ability to develop social skills. This child needs to engage in safe social activities, such as afterschool programming that is supervised by a responsible adult.
2. *Does not fight back when bullied.* This child clearly has good conflict resolution skills however he cannot keep the bullying a secret.
