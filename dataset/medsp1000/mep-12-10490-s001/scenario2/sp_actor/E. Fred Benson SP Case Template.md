| THE PATIENT STORY: The SP will be asked to tell their symptom story and the personal and emotion impact for each of their concerns. You will want to write this is the patient voice. The symptom story should be able to answer this question:“Tell me more about [chief concern/additional concern], starting at the beginning and bringing me up to now.” The personal context should be able to answer questions concerning the broader personal/psychosocial context of symptoms, especially the patient beliefs/attributions. The emotional context should be able to ask how are you doing with this, how does this make you feel, how has this affected you emotionally? IMPACT: How has this affected your life? How has this been for your family? | Back pain: I can’t walk more than a couple of steps before my back starts hurting. It’s been going on forever - I kind of have to stoop over, and the back’s better a little. I think it’s because I’m getting old - a couple of the guys at the bar also have a hard time getting around. | Physical Exam- List exam maneuvers expected for this case and any abnormal findings that SP will simulate. (tenderness, hyper-hypo reflex, rebound, weakness etc. ) **Expected Maneuvers:** **Mouth, lungs, chest, heart, abdomen:** no abnormal findings. Spine: The trainee might push on your spine, ask you to do a forward bend, or have you move your legs in different ways in order to assess the nature of your back pain. You should be able to do everything they ask with only mild discomfort/stiffness. If the trainee pushes on your spine, no single spot should hurt more than any others. Your back pain should be present and “achy” regardless of whether or where the trainee is pushing at your back. Gait: The trainee should ask you to walk, and you should do so slowly but without wobbling or fatigue. Depression and Alcohol Screening: The trainee might ask you a few questions about depression and alcohol screen, including the following: 1. *Do you ever feel you should cut back on drinking?* No. It’s fine. Besides, Hafferty’s Tavern and the bartender there are my only real social connections. 2. *Do you get annoyed when other people ask about your drinking or say you should cut back?* No one tells you this (but I would get annoyed if someone did). 3. *Do you ever feel guilty about your drinking habits?* No, why would I? What else am I going to do with my time? Besides, I like going to the tavern and no one really cares about my health anyway. 4. *Do you ever have an eye opener to help you get past a hang over?* No. I mean, I’ve had hangovers sometimes, but I just go back to sleep until I feel better. 5. *Do you still enjoy the things that you once did?* I like going to the Tavern and doing crosswords. But it’s hard to do a lot of the things I once did because of mobility issues and sometimes I forget that I was supposed to do something. I think I would enjoy these things if I did them, though. 6. *In the past month, have you been feeling down or hopeless?* Wouldn’t you, if you were old and lonely? There isn’t much anyone can do for my problems. Old people don’t get better - they just get sicker until they die, right? 7. *Change in energy?* I have less energy than I used to. Tired all the time. 8. *Change in concentration?* Maybe my concentration has been getting worse (but might not be comfortable admitting this). 9. *Any suicidal thoughts?* No, I was raised Catholic - we can’t think about a mortal sin. But sometimes I do just wish that something would happen so I wouldn’t have to go on living anymore. 10. *Change in appetite or weight?* I’m not very hungry much anymore, but I also can’t get around the way I used to, so it’s not like I’m building up an appetite. 11. *Change in sleep habits?* I take a lot more naps than I used to, but that’s just part of getting older, right?
