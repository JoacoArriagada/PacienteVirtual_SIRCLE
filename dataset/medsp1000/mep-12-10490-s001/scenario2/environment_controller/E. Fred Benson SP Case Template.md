| THE PATIENT STORY | Back pain: I can’t walk more than a couple of steps before my back starts hurting. It’s been going on forever - I kind of have to stoop over, and the back’s better a little. I think it’s because I’m getting old - a couple of the guys at the bar also have a hard time getting around. Hey, can you get me a scooter so I can get around easier? Blood pressure: The last doctor said my blood pressure is high, but I don’t really feel that. Over the last few months, I’m tired all the time and just not feeling right. After Agnes died, I used to check my blood pressure but it’s probably been a couple of months. It was ... I don’t know, maybe 150? I don’t really like taking those pills, anyway. I know my blood pressure led to my heart problems. I used to eat healthy, but then Agnes died, and I don’t do that much any more. Everything seems to be going wrong with my life - my son died, my wife died, and now my health is going. Cognitive Decline: I don’t feel quite as sharp as I used to. Since Agnes died, I’m not really that good about taking my medicines, but I do remember sometimes. I try to keep doing the New York Times crossword puzzle to keep my brain working, though. All my friends are gone. I don’t really have much of anything to do - it’s like I don’t know what to do with myself, so you mostly stay at the bar. Yeah, my apartment gets a little messy - it’s just too much to keep on top of cleaning for yourself. Emotional Context: I’m afraid that I’ll become completely disabled/unable to walk. I’m also terrified of being placed in a long-term care facility against your will. I know I’m not eating that well, but feel like there’s no point in caring or paying attention to such things at this point in time. I’m tired, lonely, and discouraged, and it’s like I’m just waiting for the end, which seems to never come. |

| Physical Exam- List exam maneuvers expected for this case and any abnormal findings that SP will simulate. | Mouth, lungs, chest, heart, abdomen: no abnormal findings. Spine: The trainee might push on your spine, ask you to do a forward bend, or have you move your legs in different ways in order to assess the nature of your back pain. You should be able to do everything they ask with only mild discomfort/stiffness. If the trainee pushes on your spine, no single spot should hurt more than any others. Your back pain should be present and “achy” regardless of whether or where the trainee is pushing at your back. Gait: The trainee should ask you to walk, and you should do so slowly but without wobbling or fatigue. Depression and Alcohol Screening: The trainee might ask you a few questions about depression and alcohol screen, including the following: 1. *Do you ever feel you should cut back on drinking?* No. It’s fine. Besides, Hafferty’s Tavern and the bartender there are my only real social connections. 2. *Do you get annoyed when other people ask about your drinking or say you should cut back?* No one tells you this (but I would get annoyed if someone did). 3. *Do you ever feel guilty about your drinking habits?* No, why would I? What else am I going to do with my time? Besides, I like going to the tavern & no one really cares about my health anyway. 4. *Do you ever have an eye opener to help you get past a hang over?* No. I mean, I’ve had hangovers sometimes, but I just go back to sleep until I feel better. 5. *Do you still enjoy the things that you once did?* I like going to the Tavern and doing crosswords. But it’s hard to do a lot of the things I once did because of mobility issues and sometimes I forget that I was supposed to do something. I think I would enjoy these things if I did them, though. 6. *In the past month, have you been feeling down or hopeless?* Wouldn’t you, if you were old and lonely? There isn’t much anyone can do for my problems. Old people don’t get better - they just get sicker until they die, right? 7. *Change in energy?* I have less energy than I used to. Tired all the time. 8. *Change in concentration?* Maybe my concentration has been getting worse (but might not be comfortable admitting this). 9. *Any suicidal thoughts?* No, I was raised Catholic - we can’t think about a mortal sin. But sometimes I do just wish that something would happen so I wouldn’t have to go on living anymore. 10. *Change in appetite or weight?* I’m not very hungry much anymore, but I also can’t get around the way I used to, so it’s not like I’m building up an appetite. 11. *Change in sleep habits?* I take a lot more naps than I used to, but that’s just part of getting older, right? Mental Status: The trainee has been given a form that assesses your mental status. If they ask any of these questions, say that you completed one with a nurse earlier today, and that it is located in the drawer. |
