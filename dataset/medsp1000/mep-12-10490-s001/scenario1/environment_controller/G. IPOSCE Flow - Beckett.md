**Overview:**

The standardized interprofessional pharmacist for this case feels like this is the type of patient that is particularly challenging for them to work with because there is, quite simply, very little that pharmacy can do to improve his quality of life & make it more feasible for him to actually care about his health and adhere to his medication regimen. S/he doesn’t even know if it’s possible for the patient to be able to fully comprehend the seriousness of his conditions (or even if he is willing to try to do so), and finds it difficult to argue for medications being the most important thing to focus on when the patient is already facing so many other challenges across all aspects of his life. The key line should be delivered in a somewhat detached, despondent way. S/he feels that while pharmacy can do a lot of great work for many patients, there is little that can be done for those who are facing such challenging social issues that they are truly causing the most harm/distress to the patient.
