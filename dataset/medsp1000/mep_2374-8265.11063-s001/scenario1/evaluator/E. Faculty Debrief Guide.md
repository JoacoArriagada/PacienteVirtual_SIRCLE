**APPENDIX E: FACULTY DEBRIEF GUIDE FOR CASE:**

**Asthma exacerbation due to poor air quality from wildfire smoke**

**Goal:** To foster climate awareness in physician trainees using a case that demonstrates an asthma exacerbation triggered by poor air quality related to wildfires.

**Objectives:**

*At the end of this simulation exercise, faculty will guide learner to:*

* Recognize an acute asthma exacerbation based on history and physical exam
* Identify therapeutic strategies to treat an asthma exacerbation in the context of environmental trigger(s).
* Recognize the connection between climate change and adverse health impacts
* Recognize the need for preparation and adaptation in a changing ecological landscape

*At the end of this simulation exercise, faculty will assess the learner’s*

* History taking and physical exam skills
* Written note (including assessment and plan based on training level)
* Understanding of environmental risks to health

**Background**

The World Health Organization estimates that globally “climate change will cause an additional 250,000 deaths per year from 2030 to 2050.” (1) These harmful effects include increased respiratory and cardiovascular disease, injuries and premature deaths related to extreme weather events, changes in the prevalence and geographical distribution of food- and water-borne illnesses and other infectious diseases, and threats to mental health. (2)

**Wildfires and Air Quality**

Warmer temperatures and shifting weather patterns can worsen air quality. Poor air quality leads to asthma exacerbations and other respiratory and cardiovascular health effects. (3)

In the Western United States, wildfires have increased in duration and frequency. As the climate warms, there is earlier spring snowmelt and higher spring and summer temperatures causing drier soils and increased incidence of drought. These conditions increase the likelihood that wildfires will be longer and more intense than the traditional wildfire season. (4)

Individuals residing in areas impacted by wildfires inhale toxic smoke for days and weeks. Wildfire smoke has high levels of fine particle pollution which cause respiratory and cardiac disease and premature death. (5) People at greatest risk include those with heart or lung diseases, older adults and children, outdoor workers, and those in susceptible socioeconomic situations.

**General recommendations to alleviate effects of exposure to wildfire smoke (7)**

* Stay indoors- with doors, windows and fireplace dampers shut and preferably with clean air circulating through air conditioners and air cleaners.
* Check the Air Quality Index, as well as the location of fires, path of smoke plumes on the AirNow website https://www.airnow.gov/index.cfm?action=topics.smokewildfires
* Use air conditioners on the recirculation setting to keep from pulling outside air into the room.
* Air cleaning devices that have HEPA filters can provide added protection from the soot and smoke. Place damp towels under the doors and other places where outside air may leak in.
* DIrect patient to an inexpensive mask that will filter pollen and particulates. Need N-95 non-medical grade masks which are available at hardware and home improvement stores or high quality filters for home HVAC which can filter damaging fine particles Need to assure fit.

**Recommendations for *this* patient**

* Immediate treatment with short acting beta agonist (SABA)
* Administer first dose of corticosteroids
* Monitor peak flow and clinical status to determine disposition
* Consider CXR to evaluate for signs of pneumonia or infectious process
* Assess patient’s mental health in the context of anxiety over progression of her disease, isolation, dependence on others and financial limitations

**Upon discharge**

* Add course of oral steroids
* Add inhaled steroid
* Review inhaler technique to confirm correct use

**Patient Education**

* Educate on use of peak flow meter and determine an Asthma Action Plan (11)
* Discuss avoidance of exertion and reduction of outdoor activity such as gardening on high pollution days
* Monitor daily Air Quality Index, outdoor avoidance if >100 and/or AirNow website
* Investigate options to purchase air cleaner and/or use high-efficiency particulate air filter in home
* Recognize that these environmental events which have been in existence are now more intense and will be a recurrent problem
* Advise patient that although environmental conditions are more severe, they are seasonal, so outdoor activity restrictions are time bound. Ongoing monitoring of air quality will be required.
* Recognize emotional distress provoked by wildfire and climate conditions and provide a referral for social work and community services to address medication needs, grocery delivery and emotional support through behavioral health providers or support groups
* Provide contact for the national Disaster Distress Helpline, which provides counseling for people experiencing emotional distress related to human caused or natural disasters.

**Discussion points/climate and health impact takeaways**

* Recognize the impact of wildfires on the air quality and the connection to the worsening asthma?
* Recognize strategies to mitigate the environmental factors? (Masks, avoid outdoor)
* Recognize that climate conditions exacerbate wildfires and these events will continue to impact health.

**Further Discussion: Physicians as advocates**

The Lancet Commission report on Health and Climate Change states that “tackling climate change could be the greatest global health opportunity of the 21st century.” (8)

Because physicians are on the front lines of patient care, they are presently witnessing the adverse health effects that climate change has on their patients and must address these issues in the care of individual patients and the community.

“Physicians can play a substantial role in addressing climate change by advocating for climate change adaptation and mitigation policies and educating themselves about climate change and how it affects public and individual health, and the potential health threats it may pose to their community.” (9)

“Medical schools and continuing medical education providers should incorporate climate change–related coursework into curricula”. (2)
