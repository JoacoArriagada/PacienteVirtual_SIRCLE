| **DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL** | |
| **Diagnosis with support from positive and negative history and PE findings** | Asthma exacerbation due to air pollution related to wildfire- known history of intermittent asthma, and sensitivity to environmental smoke. No chest pain, no recent tobacco use, no fever or chills, and nonproductive cough. Symptoms are worse when outdoors and exposed to smoke from wildfires which have been more intense and longer lasting than in the past. Higher amounts of particulate in the atmosphere and sustained smoke in air are provoking her symptoms. She gets some relief with avoiding outside air and the use of an albuterol inhaler. Physical exam reveals wheezing and diminished peak flow from baseline. |
| **Differential with support from positive and negative history and PE findings** | * Bronchitis- remote history of tobacco but no fever or significant sputum production. * COPD remote history of tobacco but no significant sputum production. Baseline spirometry not consistent with COPD (FEV1 >80%, FEV1/FVC ratio > 70%). * Pneumonia – no fever, chills, improves indoors and with inhaler, no significant sputum production |
| **Management of Diagnostic Pain** | **Immediate Care**   * Immediate treatment with SABA * Administer first dose of corticosteroids * Monitor peak flow and clinical status to determine disposition * Consider CXR to evaluate for signs of pneumonia or infectious process   **Upon discharge**   * Add short course of oral steroids * Add inhaled steroid, duration to be determined * Review inhaler technique to confirm correct use   **Patient Education**   * Educate on use of peak flow meter and determine an Asthma Action Plan * Discuss avoidance of exertion and reduction of outdoor activity such as gardening on poor air quality days * Recognize that these environmental events which have been in existence are now more intense and will be a recurrent problem * Monitor daily Air Quality Index, outdoor avoidance if >100 * Investigate options to purchase air cleaner and/or use high-efficiency particulate air filter in home * Advise patient that although environmental conditions are more severe, they are seasonal, so outdoor activity restrictions are time bound. Ongoing monitoring of air quality will be required. |
| PROFESSIONALISM ISSUES OR CHALLENGES: | Challenges include awareness of environmental factors impacting climate change and to go beyond the isolated treatment of exacerbation of asthma to consider broader approaches to mitigation and adaptation.
