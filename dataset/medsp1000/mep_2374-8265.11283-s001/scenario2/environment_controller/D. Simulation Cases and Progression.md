| **Brief narrative description of case** | The students are in the role of interns on the Internal Medicine service. They received handoff from the night team which focuses on a new admission, Mr. Howard who is a 54-year-old male with diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and hypertension admitted for debridement of a progressive foot wound. He is scheduled to go to the OR in just a few hours however the team receives an update that the patient is not feeling well and to go investigate further. Upon entering the room, the students assess the patient through focused history, examination, and chart review that create concerns for acute symptomatic hypoglycemia. Once this concern is discussed, the students use ISBARR reporting to update the nurse to their concerns and recommend a glucose check which is found to be critically low. Following this, the team (including the nurse) works together to create a treatment plan, followed by use of ISBARR, this time to update their senior resident to the situation. |

**Case Progression & Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

*The learners enter the room and introduce themselves to the (embedded) nurse and patient. The nurse states they are glad the team is present as the patient has been feeling “off” just a few minutes ago. The team is asked to talk to the patient and investigate further. The nurse states they must leave the room to get a call light but will be back shortly to assist. Upon entering the patient room, the learner team introduces themselves to the patient and obtains information through history, physical, exam, and chart review with one student remaining at the foot of the bed in a leader/coordinator role. History taken through the provided S-A-M-P-L-E format will confirm pertinent symptoms of light-headedness, feeling cool and sweaty, and confirming the patient has diabetes and uses insulin. Physical examination identifies the patient is sweaty and tachycardic and confirms foot wound consistent with the reason for admission. Chart review confirms insulin use and that glucose levels have been low since admission. The team reviews information obtained and suggests hypoglycemia as the likely cause of the symptoms. The nurse returns to the room and asks for an update whereupon the lead learner will provide and ISBARR format that includes their concerns for hypoglycemia and recommendations to check a glucose level. The nurse confirms the requests and upon checking identifies the level is “45, which is critically low”. The team confirms this information and then discusses treatment options using their cognitive aid. They confirm the patient is NPO and then review if the patient has IV access. If IV access is not available, IM glucagon is ordered. The nurse delivers this medication. The team confirms the symptoms are improving. The senior resident (faculty) enters the room and asks for an update. The lead learner provides ISBARR handoff.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*List of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. Difficulty with bedside monitors: We found when using this case with medical students that many of our learners did not know how to properly connect EKG leads to the bedside monitor or cycle blood pressure. As this is their first course, EKG leads and blood pressure cuff are connected prior to learner group entering. Blood pressure cuff cycling is reviewed in room orientation.
2. Failure to recognize the symptoms of hypoglycemia: Some of our learners did not immediately recognize the patient symptoms may be related to hypoglycemia. The patient voice can prompt the students that symptoms are similar to his prior episodes of hypoglycemia if needed.
3. Uncertainty about treatment pathways for hypoglycemia: If learners are uncertain about initiating treatment for hypoglycemia, the embedded nurse can identify they are ready for orders to treat and prompt them to review the algorithm.
4. Inconsistent use of ISBARR: Some groups do not follow the ISBARR format to update the nurse. If the learner does not follow the desired scripting, the embedded nurse can guide them using phrasing such as “can you tell me more about the situation so I can see if there’s more I can do to help?”
