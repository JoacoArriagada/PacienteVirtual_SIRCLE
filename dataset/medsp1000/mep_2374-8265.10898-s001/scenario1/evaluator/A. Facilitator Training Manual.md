**Peer Assisted Study Sessions (P.A.S.S.)**

**Facilitator Training Manual**

Brody School of Medicine, East Carolina University

**Session Learning Objectives:**

*At the end of this session, the participants will be able to:*

1. Increase confidence in planning an organized peer assisted study session.

2. Increase confidence in facilitating an effective peer assisted study session.

3. Increase confidence in facilitating an interactive peer assisted study session.

4. Increase confidence in creating questions to facilitate active learning.

5. Increase confidence in handling challenge situations that may arise during a peer assisted study session.

## STANDARDIZED CLASSROOM ACTIVITY

Facilitator Instructions:

You and another student will be paired up to facilitate a simulated P.A.S.S. group. Your students were asked to review the information on the following page before the P.A.S.S. session. Your job is to help the group learn the material presented on the following page while dealing with any potential issues that arise. You should try to make your session interactive and involve all students if possible. You may use any strategies you would like to help teach the information. You will have 15 minutes to work with your co-facilitator to decide how you want to present the information. You will have 10 minutes in the room to cover the material.

Functions of major gastrointestinal structures14:

**Salivary glands** – secretes enzymes that break down food

**Esophagus** – muscle that allows passage of food into the stomach

**Stomach** – storage place for food, secretes acid that helps break down food

**Small intestine** – reabsorbs nutrients into the bloodstream

* Three segments – duodenum, jejunum, ileum

**Pancreas** – secretes enzymes into the duodenum that break down food

* Secretes insulin into the blood stream to help metabolize sugar

**Liver** – processes nutrients absorbed from the small intestine, breaks down drugs

**Gall bladder** – stores bile, which is released into the duodenum to break down fat

**Large intestine** – reabsorbs mostly water and some nutrients into the bloodstream

* Four segments – ascending, transverse, descending, and sigmoid colon
