Student 1 “The Know-It-All Student”

You are a student who wants to answer every question. You answer immediately before other students have the opportunity to answer. You answer whether you know the correct answer or not, but you are confident either way. If the facilitator says something like, “Let’s give someone else a turn to answer”, you stop answering questions. At this point, you should only answer the question if there is a pause and no one else is answering.

Student 2 “The Inattentive Student”

You are a student who is not paying attention to what the facilitator is teaching. You may be doodling, texting on your phone, or looking around the room. If the facilitator asks you a question, you should say “Sorry, I wasn’t listening. What did you say?”. If they do try to engage you specifically, you should start paying better attention.

Student 3 “The Unprepared Student”

You are a student who did not prepare for the session and does not understand what the facilitator is teaching. You should say, “Sorry, I haven’t even looked at this material at all. Can we go over what we were supposed to have read?” If the facilitator tells you they don’t have time to go over that, you look disappointed but then say you will just read over it while he or she continues with the topic.

Student 4 “The Distracting Student”

You are a student who continuously changes the subject to something unrelated to the topic. You may talk about what you are planning to do over the weekend, make jokes, or tell stories. You may also whisper and laugh with the student next to you. If the facilitator redirects the class to the topic at hand by saying something like “Okay, let’s get back to what we were talking about”, you stop being distracting.

Student 5 “The Shy Student”

You are a student who does not say anything unless directly called upon. If the facilitator asks you a question, you should say very hesitantly, “I’m not sure. Maybe the answer is \_\_\_\_\_?” If the facilitator praises/encourages you, you should smile and be more confident with additional answers.

Functions of major gastrointestinal structures14:

**Salivary glands** – secretes enzymes that break down food

**Esophagus** – muscle that allows passage of food into the stomach

**Stomach** – storage place for food, secretes acid that helps break down food

**Small intestine** – reabsorbs nutrients into the bloodstream

* Three segments – duodenum, jejunum, ileum

**Pancreas** – secretes enzymes into the duodenum that break down food

* Secretes insulin into the blood stream to help metabolize sugar

**Liver** – processes nutrients absorbed from the small intestine, breaks down drugs

**Gall bladder** – stores bile, which is released into the duodenum to break down fat

**Large intestine** – reabsorbs mostly water and some nutrients into the bloodstream

* Four segments – ascending, transverse, descending, and sigmoid colon
