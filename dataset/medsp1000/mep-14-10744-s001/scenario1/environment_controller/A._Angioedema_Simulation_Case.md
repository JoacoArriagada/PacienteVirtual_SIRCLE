| **Actors and roles in the room at case start** | **ICU fellow/primary learner:** Lead Fellow who will be responsible for directing the care of the patient. **Procedure/support fellow:** Optional Support Fellow to assist the lead fellow as needed. **The patient (manikin):** Represented by a manikin with voice portrayed by a simulation staff member playing through speakers into the room. The staff member portraying the voice of the patient will simulate stridorous breathing sounds and answer questions in muffled one-word sentences. **ICU intern (confederate):** A faculty or simulation staff member should perform the role of intern and provide additional patient history and can page staff if requested by the fellow. The ICU intern will intermittently leave the room to page staff as requested. If asked to page otolaryngology, the intern will report after one minute that the surgeons are in the operative room but will come as soon as possible. If asked to obtain a bronchoscope, the intern will exit the room and return after 30 seconds saying someone will bring the bronchoscope in about 10 minutes. **ICU nurse (confederate):** The ICU nurse should be performed by a critical care nurse. The nurse has recently admitted the patient from the floor and will call the fellow to evaluation the patient. The nurse will administer any medications requested by the physicians. When the patient’s eyes close, the nurse will inform the team that the patient appears sleepy. **ICU respiratory therapist (confederate):** A respiratory therapist should perform this role and assist with respiratory care needs. **Observer (confederate):** A faculty member who will observe in the room and end the scenario. | | |
| **HPI** | The patient is a 52 year-old man newly admitted from the Emergency Department (ED). He presented with several hours of lip and tongue swelling. He was treated with methylprednisolone 80 mg IV and diphenhydramine in the ED. Since arriving to the ICU, his voice has become progressively hoarse, he has developed stridor and difficulty breathing, and he can now only speak in one-word sentences. **Additional information available if requested (provided by the ICU intern):** Labs from the emergency department: Complete blood count: within normal limits. Basic metabolic profile: within normal limits. Liver function tests: within normal limit. Coagulation studies: within normal limits. | | |

| **Intervention / Time point** | **Change in Case** | **Additional Information** |
| **ACT 1:**  **Stridor**  Beginning of the scenario | Possible Interventions: * Epinephrine, H2 blocker, or steroids given: no change in scenario * Anesthesia requested: if paged, the intern will report they are busy in the OR and respiratory therapy will ask the Procedure Fellow if they can manage the airway * General Surgery consult: if paged, the intern will report they are in the OR and will come as soon as case is finished * ENT consult: if paged, the intern will report they will come as soon as possible, but it will take them about 10 minutes to arrive * Nasal cannula requested: respiratory therapy will place the cannula and take off face mask unless the Leader asks for face mask and nasal cannula to be used simultaneously * Topical lidocaine and/or nasal oxymetazoline requested: respiratory therapist applies the nebulizer and nurse provides the lidocaine topical and oxymetazoline * Bronchoscope requested: the intern will report they will go try to find the equipment, and report back in 2 minutes that the bronchoscope is 10 minutes away * Non-invasive ventilation requested: respiratory therapy will question its use in the setting of airway swelling * Decision made to intubate or place supraglottic airway: transition to Act 2 | * Vital sign monitoring can be adjusted per leader’s request * The patient can answer questions one word at a time with a hoarse voice, and becomes progressively panicked over the course of Act 1 |
| **ACT 2:**  **Cannot Intubate, Cannot Ventilate**  Transition 3 minutes into the case or decision made to intubate in Act 1 | Vitals: T 37.5°C, BP 180/110 mm Hg, HR 130/min, RR 30/min, SpO2 95%. Exam: Eyes closed. No longer responds to questions. Initially making respiratory effort but becomes apneic. Over 5 min the SpO2 will gradually decrease to 80% and the respiratory rate will gradually decrease until the patient is apneic. | * Monitoring: As in Act 1, unless changed * Weight (if asked): 70 kg |
| Possible Interventions: * Epinephrine, H2 blocker, or steroids given: no change in scenario * Sedation: ketamine, fentanyl, or midazolam will not change scenario, but propofol will transition to Act 3 * Neuromuscular blockade: any agent (rocuronium, vecuronium, cisatracurium) will transition to Act 3 * Bag-valve-mask (with or without oral airway): no chest rise, no change in vital signs * Supraglottic airway: no chest rise, no change in vital signs * Bronchoscope requested: the intern will report they will go try to find the equipment, and report back in 2 minutes that the bronchoscope is 10 minutes away * Intubation attempt: unsuccessful due to airway edema * If the decision is made to perform a surgical airway (cricothyrotomy or tracheostomy) then the ICU intern will announce that ENT has arrived, and transition to Act 3 | |
| **ACT 3:**  **Deterioration**  Transition 8 minutes into the case or if the following occur in Act 2: * Propofol or neuro-muscular blockage given * Decision made to perform surgical airway | Vitals: T 37.5°C, BP 64/35 mm Hg, HR 140/min, RR 0/min, SpO2 60%. Exam: the patient is unarousable to any stimulation. | * Monitoring: As in Act 1, unless changed * Weight (if asked): 70 kg |
| Possible Interventions: * Epinephrine, H2 blocker, or steroids given: no change in scenario * Bag-valve-mask (with or without oral airway): no chest rise, no change in vital signs * Supraglottic airway: no chest rise, no change in vital signs * Intubation attempt: unsuccessful due to airway edema * Surgical airway performed: transition to Act 4 | |
| **ACT 4: Conclusion**  Transition after 10 minutes into the case or surgical airway performed in Act 3 | Observer announces that a surgeon has arrived to perform cricothyrotomy and instructs the Lead Fellow to begin team debrief in the room. | |
