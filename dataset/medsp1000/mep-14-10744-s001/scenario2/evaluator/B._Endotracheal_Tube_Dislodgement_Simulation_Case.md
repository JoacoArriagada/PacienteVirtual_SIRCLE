**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The Lead Fellow and Support Fellow enter the room. The ICU nurse provides a brief patient history, noting worsening hypoxemia. The Support Fellow is asked to examine the patient to ensure bilateral breath sounds and troubleshoot the ventilator with the respiratory therapist while increasing the FiO2 to 1.0. The Lead Fellows voices concern for acutely worsening hypoxemia in a ventilated patient and reviews a brief differential. The respiratory therapist and Support Fellow recognize leak in the ventilator volume scalar and concern for tube dislodgement is raised. The team recognizes the endotracheal tube is out of position compared to the morning documentation. A bronchoscope is requested and in the meantime attempts are made to pass a suction catheter into the endotracheal tube. When the patient becomes apneic, the decision is made to remove the endotracheal tube over a Bougie with plans to reintubate, recognizing the airway may be difficult. Anesthesiologist support is requested, but will be delayed. After extubation, attempts are made to mask ventilate the patient, and when unsuccessful reintubation is attempted after sedation and neuromuscular blockade are given. When unsuccessful, additional salvage maneuvers are attempted including laryngeal mask airway, glidescope, or Bougie guidance. After these are unsuccessful, the decision is made to pursue surgical airway.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Uncertainty about doses of medications: Some learners may be uncomfortable with dosing of intubation medications. One option is to have the intern ask if they should call the pharmacist for recommendations, leave the room, and return shortly thereafter with the appropriate dosing information.
2. Failure to articulate a full plan for intubation: Learners may fail to articulate that the final back-up plan for intubation is a surgical airway. They may fail to clearly inform the team at what point (e.g. after how many intubation attempts or at what level of SpO2) they will transition to a surgical airway attempt. They may fail to ascertain who in the simulated team is willing to attempt surgical airway if the surgeons do not arrive in time.
3. Failure to recognize that the endotracheal tube (ETT) has been dislodged: Learners may not recognize the ventilator findings of volume leak and auto-cycling as signs that the ETT may be dislodged. Learners may fail to ask about the position of the ETT. If concern about ETT dislodgement has not been raised by Act 2, the respiratory therapist should suggest it and explain why he/she thinks this is the case.

**Debriefing Materials: Endotracheal Tube Dislodgement**

Airway Management – The Very Basics

Pre-assessment:

* Is it appropriate/safe for me to attempt right now?
* Consider potential anatomic or physiologic difficulty
  + Anatomic (potentially difficult mask ventilation) - MOANS
    - **Man** (facial hair)
    - **Obesity**
    - **Age**
    - **No teeth**
    - **Sleep apnea, Stiff lungs**
  + Anatomic (potentially difficult laryngoscopy) - LEMON
    - **Look externally**
    - **Evaluate**: 3-3-2 rule
    - **Mallampati**
    - **Obesity/obstruction**
    - **Neck mobility**
  + Physiologic Challenges- HOP
    - **Hemodynamics:** preload dependence, obstructive shock, RV failure
    - **Oxygenation/Obstructive lung disease**
    - **pH/acidemia:** avoid intubation or match/exceed minute ventilation

Preparation (SOAPME)

* **Suction**
* **Oxygen** (2 sources with delivery devices)
* **Airway** (7.0-8.0 ETT, Mac 3/4 for most adults) **&**
* **Adjuncts** (video laryngoscope, oral/nasal airways, smaller ETT, bougie, LMA, cric kit/scalpel)
* **Peripheral** (or central) **access** (free flowing with IV fluid hanging)
* **Medications** (induction, neuromuscular blockade, consider vasoactives & post-intubation sedation) **& Monitors** (pulse ox, cardiac, EtCO2, arterial line?)
* **End-tidal CO2 detector**

Plan A, B, C, D: Varies by patient and circumstance. Always consider whether or not you have appropriate backup equipment, personnel, and skills.

* Example:
  + Plan A: Direct laryngoscopy with Mac 3 +/- bougie
  + Plan B: Video laryngoscopy with Glidescope
  + Plan C: LMA
  + Plan D: Cricothyroidotomy

Positioning:

* Head elevated or reverse Trendelenberg
* Beware c-spine
* Ear-to-sternal notch
* Consider aggressive ramp, especially if obese

Preoxygenation/Denitrogenation:

* *Preoxygenation*: Goal SpO2 >95%
  + If hypoxemic, HFNC, BVM + PEEP valve, Anesthesia bag, consider NIPPV
  + Consider apneic oxygenation
  + Consider whether positive pressure ventilation before intubation is safe/warranted
* *Denitrogenation*: Usually 8 vital capacity breaths or 3 mins on 15L/min NRB

Practice Epiglottoscopy

*The epiglottis is the critical anatomical landmark.*

* *Direct*:
  + Tools
    - *Macintosh (Mac) blade:* Curved blade. Size 3 or 4. Device of choice for most.
    - *Miller blade:* Straight blade. Main challenge is tongue/soft tissue control.
  + Midline approach is favored with slow, progressive identification of the following structures: lips, teeth, tongue, base of tongue, uvula, tip of epiglottis, epiglottis, posterior arytenoids, and finally vocal cords and glottis
* *Video* is a different skill. Devices may have hyperangulated blades (e.g., Glidescope) or normal geometry (e.g., C-MAC or McGrath MAC). They require practice with the specific device. Note that while glottic view may be enhanced, tube delivery is often a challenge.

Place endotracheal tube

* Direct: Pass tube via right side of mouth into posterior pharynx (gently). Elevate tip of tube from below the arytenoids superiorly to the glottis in order to preserve view.
* Video: As above, tube delivery may be challenging and different with each device.
* Unable to place the tube
  + If the tube does not pass (but it is positioned well), attempt a gentle 90 degree right turn
  + If there is difficulty with tube placement (actual or anticipated) or limited glottic visualization, consider bougie/Eschmann

Prove proper placement

* *Waveform capnography* is the gold standard (if possible/available)
* *Colorimetric capnometry* is an alternative (Yellow=Mellow)
* *Auscultation* of chest and stomach
* Estimated *ETT depth* (average-sized adult): men 23 cm, women 21 cm

Post-intubation management

* Anticipate hypotension
  + Have fluids running and consider ordering appropriate vasoactive infusion *before* intubation for any potentially unstable patient
  + Push-dose vasopressors
    - Epinephrine (dilute 1 ml of 1:10,000 [code ampule] with 9 mL of saline to give 1:100,000 or 10 mcg/mL): Give 0.5-2mL (5-20 mcg) q2-5min
    - Phenylephrine (pre-filled 1mg/10 mL syringe): Give 0.5-2 mL (50-200 mcg) aliquots q 2-5 min prn hypotension
* Order analgesia and sedation *before* intubation and initiate as soon as it’s safe
  + Caution especially if long-acting neuromuscular blockade given
* Manage the ventilator: Ensure minute ventilation is appropriate for clinical context, wean FiO2 immediately based on pulse oximeter, double-check tidal volume, PEEP, discuss alarms/anticipate concerns, check AutoPEEP and Pplat
* Other: HOB elevated, nasogastric or orogastric tube, oral decontamination, chest radiograph
