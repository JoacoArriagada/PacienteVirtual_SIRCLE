**Critical Actions Checklist**

* Requests additional history (e.g., prior Emergency Department course and exam).
* Performs or delegates ventilator troubleshooting and endotracheal tube inspection.
* Identifies significant air leak in the ventilator system and dislodged tube.
* Requests anesthesiologist assistance and bronchoscope (which will be delayed).
* When apneic, the patient is extubated (potentially over a Bougie).
* When unable to mask ventilate, reintubation via various maneuvers.
* If unable to intubate, surgical airway is pursued.

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The Lead Fellow and Support Fellow enter the room. The ICU nurse provides a brief patient history and more detail is sought from the ICU intern The Support Fellow is asked to examine the patient to ensure bilateral breath sounds and troubleshoot the ventilator with the respiratory therapist while increasing the FiO2 to 1.0. The Lead Fellow notes acutely elevated pressures on the ventilator. After investigation of the ventilator, the Lead Fellow voices concern for an airway resistance problem. Bronchodilators are requested, and attempts are made to pass a suction catheter or bougie through the endotracheal tube. When unsuccessful, the bronchoscope is requested for closer inspection but will not arrive quickly. After further patient deterioration and inability to ventilate, the patient is extubated with plans made for urgent reintubation with a backup plan of laryngeal mask airway and ultimately a surgical airway, if necessary. After extubation, the Lead Fellow continues with the plan for re-intubation and negotiates conflict when the nurse and respiratory therapist when they disagree with the management plan. Ultimately, due to the tenuous nature of the patient’s respiratory status, the Lead Fellow proceeds with re-intubation while acknowledging dissenting opinions and offering to re-evaluate once the patient has been better stabilized.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Uncertainty about doses of medications: Some learners may be uncomfortable with dosing of intubation medications. One option is to have the intern ask if they should call the pharmacist for recommendations, leave the room, and return shortly thereafter with the appropriate dosing information.
2. Difficulty navigating conflict: There is planned conflict as part of this scenario, with which some learners may be uncomfortable. One option is to have a brief didactic session prior to the scenario equipping the learners with tools to navigate conflict in high-stakes clinical situations and achieve consensus management plans.
3. Failure to recognize need for reintubation: After extubation, the nurse and respiratory therapist will strongly encourage the fellow to pursue alternative interventions rather than reintubation. Some fellows may not recognize that reintubation is most appropriate given the uncontrolled nature of the extubation in a relatively high-risk patient. If a decision other than reintubation is pursued, then the nurse or respiratory therapist should quickly alert the fellow to signs of decompensation, including tachycardia and hypoxemia, once entering Act 4. If there is continued delay, the nurse or respiratory therapist should strongly suggest reintubation since the patient is failing extubation.

**Debriefing Materials: Endotracheal Tube Occlusion**

*Common indications for endotracheal tube exchange:*

* Change size of endotracheal tube
* Endotracheal tube malfunction
  + Cuff rupture
  + Pilot balloon tear
* Secretions/mucus plugging
* Partial dislodgement

*Cautions:*

* Severe hypoxemia requiring high PEEP
* Known or suspected difficult airway
* Severe acidosis
* Full stomach or high aspiration risk
* Hemoptysis/hematemesis

*Approaches:*

* Extubation and reintubation
  + Known easy airway
  + Not hypoxemic or hypercarbic
  + Tube already possibly dislodged
* Airway exchange catheter or bougie (blind)
  + Confident endotracheal tube is in the airway
  + Risk is undetected dislodgement
  + Re-confirm placement with end-tidal CO2
* Airway exchange catheter over bronchoscope
  + Catheter over [small] bronchoscope
  + Visualize carina
  + Feed catheter into airway under direct visualization
  + Feed endotracheal tube over catheter, then confirm placement via direct bronchoscopic visualization
  + Can “sacrifice” the disposable bronchoscope by unplugging scope and cutting proximally and feeding ET tube over it
* Hybrid approach
  + Endotracheal tube or bougie exchange with video laryngoscopy
