# Case: Shock - Anaphylaxis (Shock Case #3)

1. Goals of Session Assessment and management of a patient with anaphylactic shock (GI Bleed).

2. Learning Objectives (indicate those that apply: PC - Patient Care, MK - Medical Knowledge, PL - Practice-Based Learning, IC - Interpersonal Communications, PR - Professionalism, SB - Systems-Based Practice

a. Cognitive - The student will:

1. Take a focused history and physical (MK, PC, PL)
2. Recognize a presentation of anaphylaxis. (PC,MK)
3. Outline the proper management of a patient in shock. (PC, MK, SB)

b. Psychomotor - The student will: 1. Demonstrate the correct management of shock. (MK, PC, PL, SB)

1. Communicate and transfer care effectively to consultant. (IC, PR)

# 3. Patient Scenario

a. Baseline patient information

Age: 25 Sex: M LOC: AAOx3

b. Description of Scenario

A 25 year old male, no past medical history, presenting with coughing and anxiety after ingestion chocolate with peanuts 30 minutes ago. After eating the snack, he complained of itching, abdominal pain, and vomited. He was noted to be coughing and anxious and was brought to the ED. He is now complaining he has "something weird in his throat".

c. Triage notes a. BP 95/50, HR 110, RR 24 (stridor), T $3 5 . 8 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , SpO2 $9 8 \%$ RA,
d. Prehospital treatment None.
e. Moulage and props needed (Obvious physical findings on first sight)

# 5. If Problem Recognized and critical actions taken: (outline chain of events as patient improves)

$\circ$ Epinephrine IM (0.3 mL 1:1000) $\circ$ NS IV bolus $\circ$ Diphenhydramine IV $\circ$ Ranitidine 23 mg IV $\circ$ Albuterol nebulizer o Methylprednisolone IV

After Appropriate Treatment: Vital Signs Temperature 36.0 (Oral) HR 70 (Sinus) BP 95/50 RR 18 $\mathrm { S p O 2 9 7 \% }$ ra $9 9 \%$ on oxygen)

After 6hr Observation: Vital Signs Temperature 36.0 (Oral) HR 65 (Sinus) BP 100/52 RR 18 $\mathrm { S p O 2 9 8 \% }$ ra $9 9 \%$ on oxygen)

6. If Problem Not Recognized or critical actions not taken: (outline chain of events as patient worsens)
o If Epinephrine is not given initially, patient will get more short of breath, and have change in voice (evidence of airway edema)
o If Epinephrine is given via IV rather than IM/SC, Patient will become significantly tachycardic (HR 200s), but have no adverse event.

# 7. Teaching Points:

• Appropriate dosing and route of Epinephrine in setting of Anaphylaxis
• Timing, Half Life, and Pharmacology behind the drugs given in this case
• Appropriate time of observation, disposition (when do you admit versus send home) for patients with Anaphylaxis Appropriate patient instructions, teaching on use of Epipen if patient is to be discharged.
