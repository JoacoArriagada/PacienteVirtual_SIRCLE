Definitions/Teaching Points:

SIRS (systemic inflammatory response syndrome) requires the presence of two of the four factors:

• Temperature less than $3 6 . 0 \mathrm { C }$ or greater than $3 8 . 0 \mathrm { C }$ • Heart rate greater than 90 BPM • Respiratory rate $> 2 4$ breaths per minute or $\mathrm { P a C O } 2 < 3 2$ WBC less than 4,000 or greater than 12,000; or Bandemia ${ > } 1 0 \%$

Sepsis is SIRS plus the presence of infection.

Severe Sepsis is sepsis plus evidence of organ failure or lactate $> 4 . 0$ mmol/dL. Organ failure may be exhibited by:

• CNS (Delirium)
• Pulmonary (ALI/ARDS)
• Thrombocytopenia
• Liver (Hyperbilirubinemia)
• Acute renal failure
• Lactate more than 4.0 mmol/dL

Septic Shock is sepsis plus hypotension that is unresponsive to two fluid boluses of $2 0 { - } 3 0 \mathrm { c c / k g }$ . Hypotension is defined as a systolic blood pressure less than $9 0 \mathrm { m m H g }$ or $4 0 \mathrm { m m H g }$ below baseline blood pressures. Electronic medical records have facilitated assessment of baseline blood pressures.

Early Goal Directed Therapy (EGDT). This was the landmark study that revolutionized the care of the septic patient. Patients with severe sepsis and septic shock were randomized to receive either standard therapy or early goal directed therapy (standard therapy plus optimization of central venous oxygen saturation $( \mathrm { S c v } 0 2 > 7 0 \mathrm { m m } \mathrm { H g } )$ ). The treatment group showed an absolute risk reduction (ARR) of $1 6 \%$ , which is one life save per 6 patients treated! (Number needed to treat, NNT)

Rapid identification of the patient.
Lactate within 10 minutes of arrival.
Rapid central venous access (within 2 hours).
Preload: rapid, early fluid boluses to achieve a Central Venous Pressure (CVP) between $8 { \cdot } 1 2 \mathrm { m m }$ Hg.
Afterload: titrate vasopressors to achieve Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) between 65 to $9 0 \mathrm { m m }$ Hg. (Norepinephrine is preferred, but conclusive evidence is lacking to guide this decision.) Maintain urine output of $0 . 5 \mathrm { c c } / \mathrm { k g } /$ /hour in adults
Central Venous Oxygen Saturation (ScvO2) Conceptually, think of this as either significant oxygen debt or poor oxygen delivery. Attempt to keep $\mathrm { S c V O 2 } { > } 7 0 \%$ , $\mathrm { H g b } > 1 0 \mathrm { g / d L }$ , If ScvO2 still ${ < } 7 0 \%$ after fluids, consider pressors.
Reassess goals. CVP, MAP, ScvO2, and lactate should show improvement.
Remeasure lactate for evidence of lactate clearance (see reference below)
