# Considerations for the Use of Long‐Acting Opioids

• Long‐acting opioids should be initiated only in patients who are already opioid tolerant and are experiencing inadequate relief of pain using short‐acting opioids.   
• Long acting opioids should not be used to treat acute, post‐operative, or intermittent pain. The street value and risk of abuse and diversion varies.  Many companies are working on “abuse resistant formulations”, but no opioid formulation, either long or short acting, should be considered safe from abuse or diversion.   
• Start low and go slow.  Start with the lowest doses and increase slowly at appropriate intervals to avoid causing sedation and overdose.  It may take days for patients to achieve full steady‐state serum levels.   
• The use of opioid conversion tables to help transition patients from short acting to long acting, or from one opioid to another opioid, is reviewed later in this module.
