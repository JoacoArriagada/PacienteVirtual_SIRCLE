1. Describe the “four As” of ongoing monitoring and care of chronic pain patients: Analgesia, Activities of Daily Living, Adverse Events, and Aberrant Drug-Taking Behaviors (pp 17–18)

Four dimensions to assess, monitor, and document; organized into “the four As” (Gourlay 2005)

Analgesia: Effectiveness of medications at decreasing pain: o Record levels and changes in levels using pain rating scales. • Activities of Daily Living: Has this treatment improved functioning? Adverse Events: Any side effects or toxicities of the medications? Aberrant Drug-Taking Behavior: Signs of abuse of the medication. Some authors have recommended the addition of a fifth “A” for Affect to emphasize the importance of monitoring for depression and other mental illness during pain treatment.

For documentation purposes, it is recommended that the following additional information be obtained periodically:

o Reassessment of diagnosis and the process that was followed to make that diagnosis $\circ$ Consideration of whether further diagnostic testing or referrals are warranted o Periodic physical examination to support diagnosis.

# 4. List potentially “aberrant drug-taking behaviors” and which are more or less likely to be associated with drug abuse (p 19).

(Adapted from Manchikanti 2008.)

Aberrant drug-taking behaviors: Behaviors that look concerning, but may in reality be more or less predictive of an SUD.   
• In this case, it is of concern that the patient is taking more short-acting medication than he is prescribed, has called in for early refills, and may be taking medications from other sources (because he is taking more than is prescribed but not getting early refills from the provider’s office).   
• Overall, however, as his medications have been increased, he has shown improvement in his pain levels and decreases in these irregularities in medication use, suggesting the behaviors may be in response to undertreated pain.

Behaviors more likely to be associated with medication abuse/addiction:

• Selling medications   
• Falsification of prescription—forgery or alteration   
• Injecting medications meant for oral use   
• Obtaining medications from nonmedical sources   
• Resistance to changing medications despite deterioration in function or significant negative effects   
• Loss of control over alcohol use   
• Use of illegal drugs or controlled substances that are not prescribed for the patient   
• Recurrent episodes of: o Prescription loss or theft o Obtaining opioids from other providers in violation of treatment agreement o Increases in dosing without provider’s instruction o Running short with medication supply and requests for early refills.

Behaviors that look aberrant but may be more a part of the process of stabilizing a patient’s pain condition and less predictive of medication abuse/addiction, such as:

Asking for, or even demanding, more medication Asking for specific medications   
• Stockpiling medications during times when pain is less severe   
• Use of the pain medications to treat other symptoms   
• Reluctance to decrease opioid dosing once stable   
• And, in the earlier stages of treatment: o Increasing medication dosing without provider instruction to do so

o Obtaining prescriptions from sources other than the primary pain treatment provider o Sharing or borrowing similar medications from friends/family.

# 5. Discuss the differential diagnosis of aberrant drug-taking behaviors in chronic pain patients managed with opioids.

(Savage 2002)

• Inadequate pain management:

o Stable condition but inadequate pain control o Progressive condition/pathology o Tolerance to opioids.

• Inability to comply with treatment: o Cognitive impairment o Psychiatric conditions.

• Self-medication of mood, anxiety, sleep, posttraumatic stress disorder, addiction, etc.

• Diversion by patient or others.

The risk is that some providers may prematurely discharge patients from pain treatment for failure to follow the treatment agreement and for the provider’s suspicion of medication abuse without considering other possible explanations for the behaviors.

For patients exhibiting these types of behaviors that could be explained by legitimate reasons other than substance abuse, it is valuable to:

o Express concern.   
$\circ$ Review the treatment agreement.   
o Increase the frequency of monitoring, especially in the early stages of treatment when the patient is first being stabilized.
