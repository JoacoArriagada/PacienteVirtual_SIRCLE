# History of present complaint:

Currently feeling ‘fine’ with no symptoms until having an unexplained fall one week ago, described as “I just tripped.” Fell over a sofa in the living room, striking with left leg: I was walking toward the TV to get the remote, and I guess I just didn’t see the couch.

# Details of the recent illness/episode:

Quality: unexplained falls.  Duration: one week.  Intensity: mild. Aggravating/Alleviating factors: none.  Associated symptoms: none. Effect on your life: “I’m OK – my spouse wanted me to get checked out because my spouse thought I was walking funny.”  Medications tried: none.

# Past medical history:

Hypertension for the last 13 years.

Medications: Enalapril $1 0 ~ \mathsf { m } \mathsf { g }$ daily; hydrochorothiazide $2 5 { \mathsf { m g } }$ daily.

# Family medical history:

 No neurological illnesses in the family. Father had hypertension, died of MI at 74. Mother is alive and well.

# Social history:

 Smoked one pack per day since late teens; quit 4 years ago. Diet: “I try to eat healthy, but I have a weakness for ice cream.”

# Physical examination findings:

Left inferior quadrantanopsia (unable to see towards the left below the horizontal midline, out of either eye).   
Mild left hemiparesis (mild weakness of left arm and leg).   
Agraphesthesia, astereognosis (inability to decipher numbers or letters traced on left palm; inability to recognize objects by feeling their shapes with the left hand).   
Visual and sensory neglect of the left side (lack of noticing visual or tactile sensation on the left side when there is a simultaneous stimulus on the right side).   
Mild left hyperreflexia (tendon reflexes are more active on the left side).   
Constructional apraxia (inability to draw).   
Left leg circumduction (left leg swings outward and around when striding on right leg).   
Left Babinski (left toe extends upward involuntarily when sole of foot is scratched).
