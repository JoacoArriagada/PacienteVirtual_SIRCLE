# Case 2 Standardized Patient Training Materials

# Identifying Case Factors

Case: R. Davis

Presenting complaint: You came to the clinic today because your spouse is concerned about your hand shaking.

Length of patient encounter: 30 minutes

# Case Summary & SP Training Notes

SP Demographics Name: R. Davis

Gender: male or female
Age: 67
Ethnicity: Any
Educational Background: High School Diploma

Setting: Neurology clinic

Case overview: You came to the clinic today because your spouse is concerned about your hand shaking. The shaking started 7 months ago, in the right hand, and it’s becoming more noticeable. It seems to be happening much of the time while awake, but your spouse states that your hand does not shake while you are sleeping. It is affecting your daily activities now, mostly noticeable in being unable to write checks legibly. The shakiness seems to increase with tiredness or being angry or stressed. Other problems you have noticed are that you have fallen 2 times in the past few months, and your feet seem to be dragging when you walk. Your last fall was a few weeks ago, but there have been no injuries from any falls (specifically you did not hit your head). You notice stiffness in your arms and legs but only bring this up when pressed. You minimize your concern but admit trouble walking if pressed.

Opening statement: “My spouse wanted me to come in because my spouse noticed my hand shaking.”

# History of present complaint:

You’ve tried to avoid caffeine and chocolate, hoping to decrease your shakiness but it hasn’t made any difference. You previously kept active by walking but now get very little exercise, just walking around the house. Your activity has decreased gradually over the past few months. Associated symptoms: If asked, report: “I’ve fallen two times in the past 3 months, and my feet seem to be dragging.” If asked, the last time was a few weeks ago, and you fell backwards into a wall when turning, slumped to floor without hitting head or serious injuries, but with bruises on right arm and hip. The first fall occurred three months ago. You tripped up the stairs. If asked, admit that spouse has complained that walking is slower recently over the last 6 months, and that sometimes it is difficult to arise for a low chair. Students may ask about ‘freezing’ – inability to get started walking- deny this. Effect on life: You no longer pay bills due to difficulty writing. You don’t go out as much, as it is hard to walk. Medications tried: None, but I stopped drinking coffee. I thought the caffeine might be making me shaky. If asked, you stopped one month ago—there was no improvement. You previously drank a few cups a day and no chocolate, if followed up with more questions.

# Details of the recent illness/episode:

Quality: Painless, noticed only intermittently. Onset: Tremor began about 7 months ago. Pattern: Occurs intermittently at rest, any time, except when I’m sleeping. (Your spouse has told you this). Not always present- on and off, for seconds or minutes at a time. At first it was noticed only occasionally, but it seems to be occurring more frequently lately.
Intensity: I can’t write very legibly anymore, especially my checks. Aggravating factors: ‘Stress’, or getting upset. You are aggravated by a squirrel that keeps getting into the bird feeder and stealing all the seeds and scaring the birds away. You also have a neighbor whose dogs bark a lot when she is at work during the day.
Alleviating factors: ‘Shaking stops when I’m doing something with my hand.’ If asked, no effect of alcohol on tremor.
If asked, you have noticed that your handwriting is smaller and less legible.
“Yes” if asked if you have any problems with your skin – “The skin on my face is rough. I gave it a good scrubbing with a puffy plastic net thing my spouse gave me.” This began three months ago.
“Yes” to increasing constipation. If asked follow up Qs, you have 2 bms a week, very hard and big, hard to push out, normal color, compacted and firm. Constipation began three months ago.

# Past medical history:

High blood pressure for the last 20 years, but controlled well with Atenolol since it was diagnosed. You try to follow a low salt diet for the blood pressure.
Medication: Atenolol, 100mg daily, for past 12 years. BP usually running 140-145/80-90 when checked.
No allergies.
Hospitalizations: Once fifteen years ago for prior surgery.
Surgery: Prior surgery fifteen years ago.
No past history of Thyroid Disease, Diabetes, Stroke, Cancer, Coronary Artery Disease/Heart Disease, Emphysema/Asthma, Seizures, Fainting spells or Blackouts, Head Injury, or Depression.

# Family medical history:

If asked, “I think my uncle had some problems with his hands shaking. If asked what you think is going on: I have no idea of the cause, but there’s an uncle on my Mom’s side who had shaking in his hand.” One brother (age appropriate) with adult-onset diabetes (controlled by pill). Parents are deceased. Your mother died at 86 years old from “old age” and your father at 76 years old from a stroke.

# Social history:

Occupation: retired.
Married with 3 sons, all in good health.
You smoked cigarettes starting age 17, 2 packs per day years ago, then cut back to 1 pack per day, then quit 5 years ago.
You occasionally drink beer.

# Physical examination findings:

You will have difficulty with some of the neurological physical exams and will be unable to perform some of them. Specific instructions are included in the core exam checklist associated with this case. Findings include:

Resting right hand tremor.
Bradykinesia (slowness of movements).
Rigidity. Shuffling gait with postural instability.

Deficit in Lower Left Quadrant of visual fields due to cataracts (you are aware you have cataracts, but are not interested in surgery at this time)—cannot see fingers on L side when student holds up both hands (right and left) and asks patient to count fingers.

Mental Status Exam: All responses correct, but delivered somewhat slowly.

Diagnosis: Parkinson’s’ Disease

# Patient presentation:

When the student enters the room, you will be seated on the end of the exam table, wearing a gown robe, underwear, and booties.
You may keep your loose, low socks on, but please remove your shoes so students will have easy access to examine pulses/circulation/edema of your feet, if they choose. If the student asks how you are when entering the room, say “I’ve been better.” Throughout the encounter intermittently you will rock your hand or roll your fingers.

# Affect & Demeanor:

Your speech will be soft (but loud enough for students to hear you). You also are not very talkative, no chatting.
You will have a flat facial expression: little facial movement, minimal facial expressions, little blinking, little eye movement--generally stare straight ahead without blinking or looking around. You will not smile or laugh.
Your hand will be shaking intermittently throughout the visit with the student. Shakiness will subside with an intentional and purposeful movement. Then it will resume shaking when your arm and hand are at rest (or when you are walking).
You do not move quickly, and are slow to respond physically to any instructions during the physical.
You will display a paucity of movement: no spontaneous hand gestures or fidgeting, move slowly.
You stand slowly and walk with short shuffling steps.

# Hand Tremor:

Keep a hand in lap or on arm rest and gently rock fingers of hand with rhythmic movement, with a slight roll of thumb over Index finger (pill rolling).

If you reach for something, your hand will stop shaking until you pick up the object, then it will begin to shake again.
You are able to touch your finger to your nose, since this is an active motion.
If asked to do a writing sample, make your writing very shaky, scrawling, with small letters; shaking can stop during this action or other purposeful actions- it is a resting tremor.
If asked to draw a spiral, make it compressed.

# Special case considerations:

Physical make-up: normal habitus. Weight: within normal limits (WNL)
