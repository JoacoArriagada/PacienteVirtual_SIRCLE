# 2- Script: STATION B Group Discussion (10 minutes)

Facilitator: “Now let’s discuss Station B”

This discussion will begin with a brief look at all the teams’ responses submitted at the conclusion of the Station B. Facilitator will project responses, one question at a time.

“Let’s look first at all the teams’ differential diagnoses. Is anyone surprised by any particular diagnosis?” This is an opportunity for groups to discuss their clinical reasoning in arriving at their top three differential diagnosis items.

“Next, let us look at the patient’s EKG.” Display page 2 from Appendix H. Use this opportunity to walk through the EKG findings and instruct how it shows atrial fibrillation. Discuss any confusion or other interpretations at this time.

“Now, let’s look at the tests that the teams ordered.” This is an opportunity to comment on similarities and differences between the tests the different groups ordered, and ask the groups to explain their reasoning for tests that are either common across groups or tests that are discordant.

“Now that we know what each group was thinking up to this point in the scenario, let’s give you the results of all of Ms. Heffernan’s labs.” Display Slide 2 from Appendix E.

“Does anyone have any different ideas now? Any new diagnoses to consider?” Discuss how the lab values are or are not as expected and how they guide further management. Use the outline directly below to guide discussion.

I. Initial laboratory workup of atrial fibrillation

a. CBC— Helps to assess an ongoing infection or presence of anemia.   
b. TSH/ T4— Evaluates for subclinical hyperthyroidism.   
c. Chem 10—Rules out electrolyte derangement, as well as assesses renal function (uremia).   
d. Troponins—Obtains if clinical evidence of ischemia

“Why are all of these labs necessary?” Use this question to initiate a discussion about reversible causes of atrial fibrillation. The outline below can guide discussion.

I. Most common reversible causes of atrial fibrillation2 a. Subclinical hyperthyroidism b. Alcohol consumption c. Post-operative (particularly thoracic surgery) d. Pericarditis (viral or otherwise)

“With this in mind, let’s think about the imaging necessary for atrial fibrillation workup.” Use the outline below to guide discussion. Advance to Slide 3 of Appendix E.

I. Initial imaging workup of atrial fibrillation

a. EKG—Can assess for arrhythmia as well as evidence of myocardial infarction.   
b. Transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE) – Can evaluate for pericarditis and cardiac causes of atrial fibrillation, as well as thrombi in the atrium, a known complication of atrial fibrillation.   
c. Chest x-ray (CXR)—Evaluates for gross structural mediastinal and thoracic abnormalities.

“Now we have a better understanding of Ms. Heffernan’s diagnosis and how we can best arrive at it. Let’s turn to the question raised by the urgent care doctor—should we cardiovert her?” Display groups’ responses to the “immediate plan” question. Ask the groups to briefly defend their stated decision. Use the outline below to guide discussion.

I. Should the patient be cardioverted?3

a. Yes, if:

i. Evidence of active ischemia   
ii. Evidence of organ hypoperfusion   
iii. Severe manifestations of CHF   
iv. Known pre-excitation syndrome (accessory pathway)

b. No, if: i. Completely asymptomatic ii. Elderly with multiple comorbidities (high risk) iii. High CHADS2 score and high bleeding risk (too risky to anticoagulate during and after cardioversion)

c. How to approach cardioversion? In most patients:

i. 1 month pretreatment with warfarin, then cardiovert, and continue at least one month on warfarin, OR   
ii. Perform TEE to identify thrombus. If none, anticoagulate with heparin or warfarin for one week, cardiovert, then continue warfarin for one month.

“So it’s clear that we should not cardiovert our patient. Should we anticoagulate her? How can we make this decision?” Discuss the CHADS2-VASc score, referring the criteria below.

I. CHADS2-VASc score

a. Congestive heart failure (1 point)   
b. Hypertension (1 point)   
c. Age >75 (1 point)   
d. Diabetes Mellitus (1 point)   
e. Stroke/TIA/TE (2 points)   
f. Vascular disease (1 point)   
g. Age ${ > } 6 5$ (1 point)   
h. Sex category—female (1 point)

“Ms. Heffernan meets has a score of five out of nine, indicating that she should be anticoagulated long term. Is there anything we want to do in the short term?” Teams should discuss among themselves for thirty seconds and decide on what to do next. Facilitator should discuss the three chief goals of atrial fibrillation management (below) and highlight rate control as the next most important step.

I. Initial goals of atrial fibrillation:4 a. Rate control b. Rhythm control c. Stroke prevention
