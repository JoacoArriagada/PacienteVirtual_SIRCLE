# 6b. Practicing Medical Interviews.

(In groups of 3 students, 90 minutes).

Purpose. The purpose of this session is to practice a “first” medical interview, in preparation for the recorded interview of a Standardized Patient. Students practice with a Standardized Patient (SP) going through opening, engagement and empathy-building aspects and gather Chief Complaint, History of the Present Illness, Family, Social and Past Medical History to the extent that time allows.

Description. Each SP has a scenario to role play which includes emotional aspects of the illness and suggestions for non-verbal behaviors. A doctor will join and interview them. Observers will take notes and use the Interview Behavior Skills Checklist to gather data for the “doctor” being observed. Both “patients” and “doctors” should use non-verbal behaviors to convey status of emotion. Each student will interview a different SP. The remaining students and faculty serve as “coaches” interrupting periodically with suggestions of different strategies to try. SPs will then go backward in the interview and begin again (rewound) so that the student can practice the suggested strategy. At the end of each interview, SPs will be rotated among exam rooms.

Materials. For this activity, you need the Interview Behavior Skills Checklist, located in both the student pocket books as well as in the Facilitator Guide, and the SP scenarios. You also need several SPs.

Discussion. Each “doctor” first assesses what strategies worked well and what he might do differently at this point. “Doctors,” in conjunction with their peer and faculty coaches, should identify areas they need to practice before Communication Lab Session 4. Next, SPs provide feedback about their reactions to the “doctor” including perceptions based on “doctors’” non-verbal behavior. Then the coach will provide comments and suggestions.

# 6c. Celebrating Successes

(individual interviews and 6 student debriefings, 120 minutes).

Purpose. The purpose of this session is to put it all together in terms of the opening, negotiating the connection, the technical subroutine of data gathering, and the basic medical interviewing structure and to practice those skills with live patients.

Description. This entire session is spent with mock patient interviews. Each medical student acts as a physician for a 5 - 7 minute videotaped interview. Feedback occurs during playback. (Allot 10 minutes per student $=$ 30 minutes).

The objective in the interview is not to diagnose and treat but simply to connect with the patient, to establish rapport and enjoy the patient’s story, to practice some of the techniques we have learned, and to really listen and hear what the patient says. Students should move through the Chief Complaint and HPI parts of the medical interview to the extent possible in 5 - 7 minutes. Feel free to stop the interview if you’ve gotten stuck and begin again or add a different skill. This session is to learn, practice, and try some new communication techniques. We realize it is always difficult to receive feedback but it truly is the best way to practice communication skills.

The Standardized Patients use the Interview Behavior Skills Checklist following the live session. These may be useful for reference during the feedback phase. The“doctors” receive the Checklists at the end of the feedback session.

Materials. For this activity, you need SP scenarios and multiple SPs, the Interview Behavior Skills Checklist, and the recording of the student interviews to playback during the debriefing session.

Discussion. (10 - 15 minutes per student $=$ 60 - 90 minutes) During feedback time, medical students first evaluate themselves by addressing the following questions:

1. What did you do well?   
2. What might you do differently?   
3. What would you say are the cares, predicaments, laments of your patient?   
4. What thoughts and emotions did you have as you interviewed the patient?

Following the student’s comments, the Standardized Patient will give feedback to the “doctor” addressing specifically engagement and empathy skills. What made them comfortable? How connected did they feel to the “doctor?” Did they feel as if they would be willing to share personal information and details with this person? Did they feel cared for?

Observing medical students also give feedback related to what they thought was done well and what they might suggest the “doctor” might have tried. Coaches address any issues that may have been left out.
