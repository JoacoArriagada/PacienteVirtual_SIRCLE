**STANDARDIZED PATIENT FACILITATION GUIDELINES**

* **WELCOME STUDENTS TO THE SP EXERCISE.**
* **ASK FOR A VOLUNTEER TO WORK WITH BOB ROBERTS.**
* **DESCRIBE THE GROUND RULES:**
  + While one student is interviewing Bob, all of the rest of you will be actively participating in the exercise by noticing and writing down the skillful things that your colleague is doing.
  + Only 2 people can call a Time-Out in the exercise, and those are the student interviewing Bob and the facilitator.
  + When you or the facilitator calls for a time-out, we will talk about how things are going and provide you with some feedback. At that point, you will resume the simulation and move forward, or the simulation will end, depending upon the situation.
* **WHEN STUDENT CALLS TIME OUT AND WILL CONTINUE SIMULATION:**
  + Ask, “How is it going, {student name}?”
  + Ask, ”What are things that are going well for you?”
  + Ask, ”Where are you feeling stuck?” or “Are you not sure in which direction to go?”
  + Ask, ”Is it okay if we ask your colleagues about what you have done well?”
  + Elicit POSITIVE feedback from group about what student has done well. Try to avoid any problem solving comments at this point, and redirect any critical or negative comments. For example: “Let’s try to focus on what went well first and then we will problem solve”.
  + After the positive feedback, ask if the group has any suggestions for student to help him/her get “unstuck”.
  + Return to student and ask them if they feel as though they have gained some information in order to proceed with the simulation.
  + The facilitator helps the student formulate a fact based question that will help move the student forward. For example, “What question might you ask here?” (This gives the SP a nugget of information to use in the simulation).
  + Return to simulation (student can continue from where left off or “rewind and re-do”)
* **WHEN STUDENT/ FACILITATOR CALLS TIME OUT & SIMULATION WILL END:**
  + If student has done well, and the simulation has run its course, the simulation can end at this point with the positive feedback from the group (skip problem solving step).
  + At this point, the student has the chance to ask the Standard Patient about his/her experience when the student did or said something specifically during the interview, to get the SP’s reaction and how they felt.
  + Only elicit feedback from the SP at the very end of the exercise, not during a mid-way time-out.
* **WHEN FACILITATOR SHOULD CALL A TIME OUT:**
* If student is stuck but either does not know it or is “going downhill fast”, facilitator can call a time-out, or if student does/says something completely inappropriate or harmful
* Ask student “How is it going?” or “Are you feeling stuck?”
* Continue with simulation as above.
* It is important to let the students get feedback and have some suggestions from their group and let them go back in and “re-do” before ending the simulation.
* Facilitator should try to call time-outs when the SP is speaking, not the student, to avoid the student from thinking that he/she did something “wrong”.
* Attempt to time out when things are going well.
* **FINAL STEP OF THE SIMULATION:**
  + Ask student, “What are you going to take away from this encounter?”
  + Discuss with group what they have learned from the experience.
