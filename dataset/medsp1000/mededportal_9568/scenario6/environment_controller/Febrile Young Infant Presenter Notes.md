These infants still require some type of evaluation because observation scales are not able to reliably detect infants 1-2 months of age with serious bacterial infection. In a study of the Yale observation score, the authors found that 22% of the infants thought be well-appearing based on the observation score had a serious bacterial infection.

Slide 30:

What type of evaluation depends on your practice environment and the area of the country you live. Several different protocols were developed to address infants > 28 days old. Refer to your handout to look at specifics (Table 1 Meehan, et al. 2010).

Boston researchers studied 504 1-3 month olds with T=/>38 who looked well and had reliable caretakers. Infants were treated with ceftriaxone and sent home if WBC <20K, UA<10WBCs, CSF <10 WBCs and negative CXR. 5% had positive cultures but all did well.

Philadelphia studied 747 1-2 month olds with T=/>38.2 and, after full sepsis work-up identified 287 as low risk. They used lower WBC and sent them home without treating. One of these had positive culture for negative predictive value of 99%, sensitivity 98% and specificity 42%.

Rochester studied 931 well-appearing babies < 60 days of age with T=/>38 and identified 437 as low risk. 5 of these latter had SBI; NPV 99%, but sensitivity 92.4%. No negative outcomes

Practice setting is very important when deciding what evaluation to do. This study of primary care doctors and their management of febrile infants < 3 months old demonstrated that they only followed published guidelines 42% of the time and they had good outcomes. However, the majority of patients had multiple visits.

For infants between the ages of 29-60 with a UTI, the rates of bacteremia were approximately 6.5%. Overall, infants did very well and were at low risk for adverse events if they were well appearing, did not have a high-risk past medical history, had a peripheral band count of <1250 cells per μL, and had a peripheral absolute neutrophil count of ≥1500 cells per μL. Therefore, brief hospitalization or outpatient management with close follow-up may be considered for infants with UTIs at very low risk of adverse events.
