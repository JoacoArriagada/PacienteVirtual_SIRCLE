What about the infant with soft tissue infection? If they are febrile, they should have a complete evaluation. What if they are afebrile?

Slide 25:

Up to 90% of febrile neonates with cellulitis may have bacteremia. Because of the high rate of systemic infection in these patients, these infants require IV antibiotics and hospitalization initially.

Slide 26:

What about the neonates with soft tissue infection who are afebrile?

There is one small retrospective cohort study that evaluated the presence of serious bacterial infection in afebrile infants 0 to 28 days with skin and soft tissue infections. None of the neonates had serious bacterial infections. Skin and soft tissue infections in the study were pustulosis, cellulitis, and abscesses. This was a small study so it is hard to use to guide practice definitively. However, it may suggest that well appearing infants without fever can be managed without a full evaluation.
