# Case Title: Neutropenic Fever/Sepsis

# Summary

This is an educational resource intended to be used for a computer-enhanced mannequin based simulator lab. The intended learning audience is fourth year medical students and internal medicine interns/residents.

This case is based on experiences from the bone marrow transplant service at UCLA medical center. The patient presents with signs of an infection that rapidly evolves to sepsis and septic shock. Learners are responsible for identifying the likelihood of sepsis and initiating proper management of neutropenic fever in a hospitalized patient. As the case progresses, learners should appropriately manage sepsis, septic shock, and eventually PEA arrest. When the patient eventually expires, the team will deliver the news to a family member.

# II. Simulation

# A. State 1 – Early Infection

1. Vital Signs: temp 37.9, HR 104, bp 132/92, rr 26, saO2 $9 8 \%$ on RA

2. Note to simulation operator: Patient is awake, alert, but slightly agitated and uncomfortable.

3. Note to Facilitator: This is a 61 yo female with severe neutropenia following high dose chemotherapy. She is in the early stages of sepsis (state 1) and will progress rapidly through the subsequent stages. In the initial assessment, learners should recognize that rigors raise the strong possibility of infection in this neutropenic patient. Even with the lack of a “true fever,” antibiotics and fever evaluation (blood cultures, chest xray, urinalysis and culture) should be initiated.

- Trigger between state 1 and state 2: 2 minute after scenario begins (during the 2 minutes, gradually increase HR from 100 to 120)

# B. State 2: Early Septic Shock:

1. Vital Signs: Heart rate gradually increases from 100 to 120 (sinus tachycardia). BP drops to 87/52. Oxygen saturation drops from $9 8 \%$ to $9 5 \%$ .

2. Note to simulation operator: Patient is now disoriented and short of breath.

3. Note to Facilitator: The learner should immediately recognize the possibility of septic shock. Initial steps would include starting normal saline “wide open” as well as quickly initiating antibiotics. Optimal choices would be broad gram negative and gram positive coverage. This patient is allergic to penicillin, so initiation of penicillin or a derivative should trigger worsening hypotension $( 6 0 ^ { 3 } \mathrm { s } / 3 0 ^ { 3 } \mathrm { s } )$ . The proper choice should be Aztreonam for gram negative coverage. Given the presence of a PICC catheter, gram positive coverage with either vancomycin or linezolid should also be added.

- Trigger between state 2 and state 3:   
If learner gives a fluid bolus and initiates appropriate antibiotics, progress to state 3 - Simulation should proceed directly from state 2 to state 4 if learner does not give fluids and start appropriate antibiotics.

# C. State 3: Temporary improvement

1. Vital Signs: Patient’s blood pressure improves to 100/60 after fluids and heart rate decreases to 100, however crackles begin to appear on lung auscultation.

- Trigger between state 3 and state 4: One minute of time

# D. State 4: Return and worsening septic shock.

1. Vital Signs: BP falls to 63/30, HR increases to 140 O2 Sat decreases to $8 2 \%$ and RR increases to 30. Patient is now unarousable.

2. Note to simulation operator: Patient is now unresponsive.

3. Note to Facilitator: Correct interventions at this time include:

a) Intubation   
b) More fluids   
c) When fluids do not help, initiate pressor support   
( norepinephrine is preferable)   
d) Activated protein C $2 4 \mathrm { m c g / k g / h r } )$ could also be considered, although with caution as the patient has a mild thrombocytopenia following her stem cell transplant.

If the above interventions are done, then BP increases to 85/40, O2 Sat increases to $8 9 \%$ HR remains elevated at 140.

- Trigger between state 4 and state 5: Two minutes of elapsed time.

E. State 5: PEA Arrest- Even if correct interventions are performed, patient enters PEA arrest.

1. Note to Facilitator: Learners should initiate ACLS protocol for PEA arrest. However, despite all efforts, patient will become asystolic. Learners should eventually terminate the code due to futility.

2. Note to Simulation Operator: After the patient expires, escort team out of room and reset room with conference room table and family member. Team will then be escorted back in to deliver the news.

- Once family discussion is done, simulation will end.
