***Case Studies (30 minute per case, 3 total cases)***

***Case Study Introduction***

Facilitator Talking Points: "The goal of this portion of the session is to give everyone the chance to work through specific case scenarios using the platform for when you are navigating with real patients. We have divided everyone into break out groups with three specific roles. Please be sure to rotate for each case so that every participant gets an opportunity to be in each role.

The roles are as follows:

* Student Navigator - This student should have the necessary training platform open and go through the call as if in a real scenario.
* Family - This student should follow the patient scenario provided for each case and act as the family member as prompted.

**Case 1**

Facilitator Talking Points: "Let's get started with case 1. If you are playing the role of the family member please open "Case 1 Family Prompt", if you are the student navigator please open your training platform."

**Case 1 Family Prompt**

Instructions: You will be asked a series of questions by your peer who is serving as the patient navigator. Please use the information provided in the below answer the questions to the best of your ability.

Not all questions that you will be asked are included in the prompt below - be prepared to form your own answers to additional questions.

**Background Information**

**Name**: Jesseka Case

**Address**: 3328 5th St SE, Washington DC 20032

**Call Information**

**Context:** You are a 35 yo mother who recently moved to DC, married with three children 3 months, 5 yrs, and 9 yrs. The two older children are in school. Your primary language is English. Your 3-month-old goes to daycare and your older children go to school. You and your children are receiving Medicaid.

You and your three children saw Dr. Bear last week. The doctor referred you to the FLiP Navigators for food access.

You're very protective, answering only the questions asked, not volunteering any additional information. You are apprehensive about accepting help, you're not sure if you can trust the caller. You and your family need emergency food and do not have enough food for this week. You are interested in learning more about SNAP and WIC but have questions about what those programs involve.
