**Pediatrics Scenario – Supraventricular Tachycardia**

Objectives:

1. Recognize supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) and distinguish SVT from sinus tachycardia.
2. Manage unstable SVT with synchronized cardioversion.

Target Audience: Pediatric and Emergency Medicine residents, PEM fellows, nurses.

Scenario: 6 year old has fallen from his bike and has an obvious left leg deformity. He has no other injuries.

Setting: ED patient room or trauma bay. Patient leg is in splint via EMS. EMS could not get an IV in the field.

Further history if asked: Patient was wearing his helmet. No loss of consciousness

Past medical history is unremarkable.

Meds: None.

No allergies.

Wt: estimated 25 kg (only if asked)

**Trauma Survey** – not injuries, abrasions or contusions noted. His left leg should be described as slight deformed with medial angulation at the mid-portion of the tibia. The skin is intact and the neurovascular exam in the left foot is intact. (Alternatively, the leg can be moulaged to appear injured – a contusion may be easier to demonstrated than deformity).

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | Scenario Flow | Action |
| **9-14 minutes** | Second Visit – Unstable SVT (coma, cool extremities, dyspneic) HR 200 | Recognize unstable SVT  Immediately provide SYNCHRONIZED CARDIOVERSION at 0.5 to 1 J/kg (13-25 joules) with proper technique  Physician clears himself and others to prevent shock.  (Scenario explicitly avoids the question of sedation of child before cardioversion – resident is told that the child is “unresponsive but breathing”) |
| **15 minutes -** | Patient returns to sinus rhythm with a stable BP | Disposition to PICU or telemetry |
