**Pediatrics Scenario – Supraventricular Tachycardia**

Scenario: 6 year old has fallen from his bike and has an obvious left leg deformity. He has no other injuries. When asked about his pain, he says, “my leg hurts some, but I feel like my heart is going to pound out of my chest”. He also complains of lightheadedness.

Setting: ED patient room or trauma bay. Patient leg is in splint via EMS. EMS could not get an IV in the field. EMS elected not to board and collar patient – he is sitting upright in bed.

Further history if asked: Patient was wearing his helmet. No loss of consciousness

Past medical history is unremarkable.

Meds: None.

No allergies.

Physical Exam: *This information is for the instructor(s) – most of the exam will be obtained from examination of the mannequin. The trauma is meant to force the trainee to decide if the heart rate is from trauma (hypovolemia or pain) or SVT.*

T 36.5 **P 192** RR 22 BP 87/60 SaO2: 92-95% on room air (Information available from mannequin via exam or monitor)

Wt: estimated 25 kg (only if asked)

**General Appearance:** Anxious – complaints as noted above.

**Airway:** Patent

**Breathing:** Clear breath sounds.

**Circulation:** tachycardic, strong rapid pulse, warm extremities

**Physical exam** - no hepatosplenomegaly, no edema, normal neurologic exam.

**Trauma Survey** – not injuries, abrasions or contusions noted. Patient’s GCS is 15. His left leg should be described as slight deformed with medial angulation at the mid-portion of the tibia. The skin is intact and the neurovascular exam in the left foot is intact. (Alternatively, the leg can be moulaged to appear injured – a contusion may be easier to demonstrated than deformity).
