**Version C specific questions:**

1. **What are the potential complications of a retrobulbar block?**

Potential complications of this local anesthetic technique include:

* Inadvertant intravascular injection and resultant local anesthetic toxicity (seizure).[1]
* Intrathecal injection into the dural sheath around the optic nerve and resultant high spinal.[2,4]
* Retrobulbar hemorrhage with potential compromise to retinal perfusion or elevated intraocular pressure.[1,3]
* Globe perforation and rupture.[1]
* Extraocular muscle injury and resultant diplopia.[1]
* Direct optic nerve trauma.[1]

1. **What is the appropriate management of retrobulbar hemorrhage from retrobulbar block?**

Retrobulbar hemorrhage from retrobulbar block can result in compromise of retinal perfusion and increased intraocular pressure, which can both require urgent surgical therapy to prevent loss of vision. [4] If neither of these complications results from the hemorrhage, no immediate action is indicated, surgery can be postponed, and the hemorrhage can be treated conservatively. Discussion with the surgeon is paramount to formulating a proper plan for management. If surgical repair is indicated, general anesthesia or deep sedation under monitored anesthesia care may be warranted to facilitate the operation.
