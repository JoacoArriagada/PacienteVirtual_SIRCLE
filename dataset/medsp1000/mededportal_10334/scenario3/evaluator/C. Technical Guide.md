| **VERSION C: Sedation for Retrobulbar Block – Resultant retrobulbar hemorrhage and elevated intraocular pressure** | | | |
| State | Patient Status | Student learning outcomes or actions desired and trigger to move to next state | |
| Phase I:  Baseline | Patient awake, arrives in OR with monitors on (EKG, NIBP, SpO2) and PIV in place.  HR 65, NSR  BP 127/83  RR 16  SpO2 99% on room air | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should assess patient, including brief assessment of airway, cardiovascular, pulmonary, and neurologic systems.  Learner should provide oxygen to the patient via mask or nasal cannula, ideally with end tidal CO2 monitoring in place.  Learner will outline a plan for sedation for the retrobulbar block and prepare to administer the sedation. | **Operator:**  Begin scenario.  **Trigger:**  Learner proceeds with sedation.  **Teaching Points:**  Sedation for block ideally will be short acting to assess effectiveness of the block. All monitors should be applied and all airway equipment immediately available. |
| Phase II:  Sedation | HR 65, NSR  BP 130/87  RR 16  SpO2 100% on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should choose sedation with a variety of agents including propofol, midazolam, fentanyl, alfentanil, or other.  Learner should administer sedation or other comfort measures.  Learner should inform the surgeons when the patient is appropriately sedated to perform the block. | **Operator:**  After administration of sedation, patient closes eyes, vitals remain stable.  **Trigger:**  Learner informs surgeon that block should be performed.  **Teaching Points:**  Importance of pre-oxygenation prior to sedation and supplemental oxygen during sedation. End tidal CO2 monitoring will make respiratory monitoring easier. |
| Phase III:  Retrobulbar block | HR 65, NSR  BP 115/75  RR 8  SpO2 93% on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should continue to monitor the patient during the block. | **Trigger:**  Shortly after the block, the patient complains of pain in the right orbit and the surgeon notes significant proptosis and suspects elevated intraocular pressure. Surgeon states that immediate repair is necessary.  **Teaching Points:**  Appropriate monitors should be in place and airway equipment at the ready for a retrobulbar block. Retrobulbar hemorrhage and elevated intraocular pressure can result, sometimes necessitating immediate repair under general anesthesia or deep sedation. |
| Phase IV:  Retrobulbar hemorrhage | HR 75, NSR  BP 150/95  RR 20  SpO2 100 on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should identify potential retrobulbar hemorrhage as a cause for proptosis and pain.  Learner should discuss the situation with the surgeon.  Learner should inform the patient of the situation and induce either general anesthesia or deep sedation for emergent retrobulbar hemorrhage repair.  Learner should develop an induction plan and begin to induce general anesthesia or deep sedation. | **Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs identifies retrobulbar hemorrhage and develops an appropriate induction plan, proceed to phase V.  **Teaching Points:**  Correct identification of retrobulbar hemorrhage from retrobulbar block as a cause of elevated intraocular pressure potentially requiring emergent repair. The surgeon decides whether emergent repair is indicated. |
| Phase V:  Induction of general anesthesia or sedation | HR 75 NSR  BP 150/95  RR 20  SpO2 100% on facemask | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should pre-oxygenate the patient with a face mask  Learner should induce general anesthesia with any of the available IV anesthetics  Learner should intubate the patient with endotracheal intubation or an LMA.  Learner should provide deep sedation with monitored anesthesia care. | **Trigger:**  If learner induces appropriate general anesthetic or sedation → *END (Debrief)* |
