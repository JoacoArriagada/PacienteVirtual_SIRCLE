1. **Case Title:** Sedation for Retrobulbar Block - Resultant intravascular injection of local by surgeon and seizure
2. **Target Audience:** Anesthesia Residents (CA-1 and CA-2 years)
3. **Learning Objectives:** At the conclusion of the simulation and debriefing session, the learner will have the opportunity to demonstrate progress in several of the anesthesia milestones as outlined by The Accreditation Council for Graduate medical Education (ACGME) and The American Board of Anesthesiology (ABA) in The Anesthesiology Milestone Project as they relate to this particular clinic topic.
   1. **Global Objectives:**
      1. Learner formulates an anesthetic plan for a patient having a retrobulbar block.
      2. The learner responds to the adverse event/crisis with appropriate treatment strategies.
      3. During debriefing, the learner recites the most likely diagnosis for each problem encountered.
      4. During debriefing, the learner explains the pathophysiological basis for each crisis encountered.
      5. During the simulation, the learner directs others during the crisis with a calm, professional demeanor.
   2. **Milestones and Specific Goals by level:**

1. **Case Narrative**
   1. **General Synopsis:** You are an anesthesiologist at a small community hospital. Your first case of the day is an elective right eye cataract repair in a 77 year old male. The surgeons plan to perform a retrobulbar block to the right eye and have asked you to provide sedation for the block as well as sedation during the surgery. He is NPO for 8 hours as instructed. He took his metoprolol yesterday evening as he normally does. You are meeting him for the first time in the pre-operative holding area before wheeling him back into the OR. He has an 20 gauge IV in the left hand.
   2. **Past Medical History**: Chronic mild hypertension treated with metoprolol. Last EKG was 11 months ago and showed normal sinus rhythm with rare pre-atrial contractions. He had a cardiac stress test 2 years ago which showed no inducible ischemia. He has bilateral total knee arthroplasties at age 69 and 70 under spinal anesthesia without incident.
   3. **Social History:** The patient is a retired teacher. He lives at home with his wife who is a chronic smoker. The patient has never smoked. He drinks alcohol on rare occasions, and has never used illegal drugs. He goes for daily walks with his wife.
   4. **Allergies and Medications:** The patient has no known drug allergies**.** He takes metoprolol 50mg qhs for his chronic hypertension.
   5. **Review of Systems:**
      1. GENERAL: Feels fit and well
      2. HEAD/NECK: severe bilateral cataracts
      3. CARDIOVASCULAR: Feels occasional “skipped beats”, denies chest pain or dyspnea
      4. PULMONARY: negative
      5. NEUROLOGIC: Poor vision in both eyes secondary to cataracts
      6. GASTROINTESTINAL: negative
      7. RENAL/HEPATIC: negative
      8. ENDOCRINE: negative
      9. HEME/COAG: negative
   6. **Physical Exam:**
      1. GENERAL: Awake, alert, appears well
      2. HEIGHT/WEIGHT: 170cm/75kg (BMI: 26)
      3. HEART: regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs, rubs or gallops
      4. LUNGS: lung sounds clear to auscultation bilaterally
      5. AIRWAY: Mallampati I, normal mouth opening, slightly limited ROM of neck, thyromental distance > 3 finger-breadths, positive subluxation of the jaw
      6. NEUROLOGIC: Alert and oriented x 3; no focal deficits
      7. GASTROINTESTINAL: abdomen non-tender to palpation, normal bowel sounds
      8. VITALS: HR 65; BP 126/83; RR 16; Temp 37.2oC; SpO2 99% on room air
   7. **Laboratory, Radiology and Other Relevant Issues:** No preoperative laboratory studies were obtained. Most recent EKG shows normal sinus rhythm and rare pre-atrial contractions. Most recent stress test shows no inducible ischemia.
2. **Instructional Notes:**
   1. **Facilitator Background and Briefing Information:**
      * In these scenarios, the learner is presented with an elderly patient who requires elective cataract repair in one eye. There are no concerning pre-operative issues with this patient.
      * For all scenarios, a confederate is required to play the role of the surgeon performing the retrobulbar block.
      * The learner should plan appropriate sedation for the retrobulbar block as well as sedation for the cataract repair.
      * Each version is designed to expose learners to a major complication of retrobulbar blocks.
        2. VERSION B:
           + Following sedation by the learner and application of the retrobulbar block by the surgeon, the patient will become somnolent and then begin to convulse in a manner typical of a general tonic clonic seizure as a result of intravascular injection of local anesthetic during the block.
