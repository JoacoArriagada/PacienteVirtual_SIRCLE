| **VERSION B: Sedation for Retrobulbar Block – Resultant intravascular injection of local by surgeon and seizure** | | | |
| State | Patient Status | Student learning outcomes or actions desired and trigger to move to next state | |
| Phase I:  Baseline | Patient awake, arrives in OR with monitors on (EKG, NIBP, SpO2) and PIV in place.  HR 65, NSR  BP 127/83  RR 16  SpO2 99% on room air | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should assess patient, including brief assessment of airway, cardiovascular, pulmonary, and neurologic systems.  Learner should provide oxygen to the patient via mask or nasal cannula, ideally with end tidal CO2 monitoring in place.  Learner will outline a plan for sedation for the retrobulbar block and prepare to administer the sedation. | **Operator:**  Begin scenario.  **Trigger:**  Learner proceeds with sedation.  **Teaching Points:**  Sedation for block ideally will be short acting to assess effectiveness of the block. All monitors should be applied and all airway equipment immediately available. |
| Phase II:  Sedation | HR 65, NSR  BP 130/87  RR 16  SpO2 100% on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should choose sedation (may include a variety of agents including propofol, midazolam, fentanyl, alfentanil, etc).  Learner should administer other comfort measures (vocal reassurance, hand holding, etc).  Learner should inform the surgeons when the patient is appropriately sedated to perform the block. | **Operator:**  After administration of sedation, patient closes eyes, vitals remain stable.  **Trigger:**  Learner informs surgeon that block should be performed.  **Teaching Points:**  Importance of pre-oxygenation prior to sedation and supplemental oxygen during sedation. End tidal CO2 monitoring will make respiratory monitoring easier. |
| Phase III:  Retrobulbar block | HR 65, NSR  BP 115/75  RR 8  SpO2 93% on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should continue to monitor the patient during the block. | **Trigger:**  Shortly after the block, the patient remains somnolent and begins to convulse in a general, tonic-clonic manner.  **Teaching Points:**  Appropriate monitors should be in place and airway equipment at the ready for a retrobulbar block. Intravascular injection and resultant seizure is a rare complication. |
| Phase IV:  Intravascular local anesthetic injection and seizure | HR 80, sinus bradycardia  BP 150/90  RR 10  SpO2 93% on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should identify convulsions as potential seizure.  Learner should identify retrobulbar block as source of intravascular injection of local anesthetic.  Learner should call for help.  Learner should treat seizure with propofol, midazolam, or alprazolam.  Learner should call for Intralipid infusion from the pharmacy.  Learner should increase oxygen delivery by application of a face mask. | **Trigger:**  If learner correctly identifies intravascular injection and seizure, and provides appropriate seizure treatment and manages the airway appropriately 🡪 *END (Debrief).*  If after 3 minutes, the learner fails to treat the seizure and manage the airway appropriately, proceed to Phase V.  **Teaching Points:**  Correct identification of intravascular injection from retrobulbar block as a source of local anesthetic toxicity causing seizure. The dose of local is usually small and toxicity is short lived, but treatment is still appropriate and Intralipid should be considered. |
| Phase V:  Cardiopulmonary  arrest | HR 32, PEA  BP 48/32  SpO2 60% | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should call for help.  Learner should ask the CPR and ACLS be started.  Learner should request crash cart brought into the OR and paddles placed on patient. Learner should increase FiO2. | **Trigger:**  If learner correctly calls for help and institutes ACLS → *END (Debrief)* |
