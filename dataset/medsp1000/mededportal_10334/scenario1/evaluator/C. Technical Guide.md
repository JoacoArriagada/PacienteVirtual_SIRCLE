| **VERSION A: Sedation for Retrobulbar Block – Resultant intrathecal injection of local by surgeon** | | | |
| State | Patient Status | Student learning outcomes or actions desired and trigger to move to next state | |
| Phase I:  Baseline | Patient awake, arrives in OR with monitors on (EKG, NIBP, SpO2) and PIV in place.  HR 65, NSR  BP 127/83  RR 16  SpO2 99% on room air | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should assess patient, including brief assessment of airway, cardiovascular, pulmonary, and neurologic systems.  Learner should provide oxygen to the patient via mask or nasal cannula, ideally with end tidal CO2 monitoring in place.  Learner should outline a plan for sedation for the retrobulbar block and prepare to administer the sedation. | **Operator:**  Begin scenario.  **Trigger:**  Learner proceeds with sedation.  **Teaching Points:**  Sedation for block ideally will be short acting to assess effectiveness of the block. All monitors should be applied and all airway equipment immediately available. |
| Phase II:  Sedation | HR 65, NSR  BP 130/87  RR 16  SpO2 100% on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should choose sedation with a variety of agents including propofol, midazolam, fentanyl, alfentanil, or other.  Learner should administer sedation or other comfort measures.  Learner should inform the surgeons when the patient is appropriately sedated to perform the block. | **Operator:**  After administration of sedation, patient closes eyes, vitals remain stable.  **Trigger:**  Learner informs surgeon that block should be performed.  **Teaching Points:**  Importance of pre-oxygenation prior to sedation and supplemental oxygen during sedation. End tidal CO2 monitoring will make respiratory monitoring easier. |
| Phase III:  Retrobulbar block | HR 65, NSR  BP 115/75  RR 8  SpO2 93% on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should continue to monitor the patient during the block. | **Trigger:**  Shortly after the block, the patient remains somnolent and becomes completely apneic.  **Teaching Points:**  Appropriate monitors should be in place and airway equipment at the ready for a retrobulbar block. Intrathecal injection is a rare complication and can result in prolonged high spinal causing apnea. |
| Phase IV:  Intrathecal local anesthetic injection | HR 55, sinus bradycardia  BP 85/60  RR 0  SpO2 80% on 2L NC | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should identify somnolence, apnea, desaturation, hypotension, and bradycardia.  Learner should identify retrobulbar block as source of high spinal.  Learner should call for help.  Learner should attempt mask ventilation of the patient.  Learner should obtain definitive airway management via either placement of an LMA or endotracheal intubation with or without paralytic.  Learner should administer alpha or beta agonists to improve hemodynamics. | **Trigger:**  If learner correctly identifies intrathecal injection and manages the airway and hemodynamics appropriately 🡪 *END (Debrief).*  If after 3 minutes, the learner fails to establish ventilation and oxygenation and treat hypotension, proceed to Phase V, cardiopulmonary arrest.  **Teaching Points:**  Correct identification of intrathecal injection from retrobulbar block as a source of high spinal causing somnolence and apnea as well as hypotension. |
| Phase V:  Cardiopulmonary  arrest | HR 32, PEA  BP 48/32  SpO2 60% | **Learner Actions:**  Learner should call for help.  Learner should ask that CPR and ACLS be started.  Learner should request crash cart brought into the OR and paddles placed on patient.  Learner should increase FiO2. | **Trigger:**  If learner correctly calls for help and institutes ACLS → *END (Debrief)* |
