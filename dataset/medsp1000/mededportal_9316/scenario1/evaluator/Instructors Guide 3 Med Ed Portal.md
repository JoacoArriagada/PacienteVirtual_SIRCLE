**Use of Resource:**

This case study is designed to be used with a team of any mix of interprofessional healthcare students and 2 standardized patients (1 patient and 1 adult child/caregiver) as a standalone activity or as a part of a larger interprofessional course. The standardized patient encounter utilizes five documents. The Case Study 3: Standardized Patient Script is presented to the standardized patient and caregiver as part of pre-encounter training. The script is reviewed with faculty to clarify any questions related to their roles. Immediately before the encounter, students receive two documents, Case Study 3: Hospital Discharge Summary and Instructions to Students; the only documents available to guide the student team assessments. It is up to the student groups to decide how to go about doing team interviews (how to divide up the time, what to cover, how to handle logistics of a team interview etc.) Fifty minutes is allotted for the team assessment. During the encounter, the standardized patient’s family member presents the medication list to the team if asked. And finally, following the SP encounter, teams receive Case Study 3: Physical Exam, Laboratory Results, and Additional Chart Information. This document provides additional information about the patient e.g., laboratory results that would be reasonable for teams to have requested as part of the assessment. Developing a team- oriented care plan is based on the five documents and the 50-minute assessment.

This case could also be used in a variety of less resource intensive formats. For example, the case could be used in a paper-based format for small or large interprofessional or single profession group discussions to illustrate various geriatric patient care issues. These groups could be self-directed or supported by a faculty facilitator.

**Purpose of Resource:**

This case study encompasses a wide range of geriatric assessment issues and requires attention to topics including but not limited to: common health conditions of older age, cultural considerations, cognitive concerns, issues related to safety and function, family concerns, ethics, and end of life considerations.

Moreover, the case study presents an opportunity for students to develop skill in geriatric assessment and interprofessional teamwork, working towards the following educational objectives. Each objective is linked to a TOSCE core objective as indicated in parentheses.

1. Actively communicate as an effective member of an interprofessional team (Communication)
2. Demonstrate emerging collaborative skills while developing a patient-centered care plan (Collaboration)
3. Describe the unique perspective, value, contributions, and responsibilities that healthcare professionals bring to the geriatric interprofessional team. (Roles and Responsibilities)
4. Demonstrate the ability to collaborate with the patient and family as well as develop an understanding of the potential barriers to communication with geriatric patients including health literacy, cultural barriers, cognitive, psychosocial, and physical impairments. (Collaborative Patient-Family Centered Approach)
5. Explore and develop an understanding of the ethical dilemmas that arise in the care of older adults and manage conflicts that may occur from different perspectives of team members. (Conflict Management/Resolution)
6. Identify and develop effective teamwork skills and strategies essential for interprofessional geriatric care. (Team Function)

**Implementation advice:**

On the day of the simulation, student teams are given 10 minutes to review the door chart and hospital discharge note. They are then given 50 minutes to interview and examine the standardized patient in the skilled nursing facility room with the family member/caregiver (another standardized patient) present. Encounter props include a bed, side chair, wheelchair and walker. In addition, the student teams should have access to standard physical assessment equipment.

Depending on student enrollment, the authors have facilitated a total of one to four teams per year. When we have had four teams, we have had 2 OSCEs running simultaneously, immediately followed by the second set of two teams. We have 2 faculty members observing each OSCE and also video record the sessions for other faculty to observe later if needed. Faculty members observe the assessment from behind one-way mirrors. If a separate observation room is not available, then faculty members may choose to observe from the corner of the room. After the encounter is over, the standardized patients give the students verbal feedback, and then the faculty members give verbal feedback as well, and time is spent debriefing the experience. The faculty members also evaluate the students using the McMaster-Ottawa Team Observed Structured Clinical Encounter (TOSCE)[[4]](#footnote-4). The TOSCE tool is used to evaluate each student and team in the areas of communication, collaboration, roles and responsibilities, collaborative patient-family centered approach, conflict management/resolution, and team functioning.

Finally, the student teams are required to write and present a comprehensive, coordinated care plan describing findings and recommendations. An example note is available as a separate document. Students are instructed to create care plans that are client-centered, and that reflect all of the team members’ expertise in an integrated manner. After the case study is concluded, students complete a team effectiveness assessment about their group.
