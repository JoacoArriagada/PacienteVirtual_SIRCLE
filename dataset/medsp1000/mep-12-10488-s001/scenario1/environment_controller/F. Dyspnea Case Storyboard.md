**Appendix F: Dyspnea Case Scenario Storyboard**

The storyboard includes a step-by-step description of what may happen as the patient’s status changes and the team responds to changes in patient status. This is used by the technician to change the telemetry monitor and Ventriloscope® settings in response to team interventions.

**Act 1: Patient Assessment by Nurse**

Initial vital signs at the start of nurse assessment are in normal range to prevent monitor alarms from going off. Option: ECG and O2Sat turned off on monitor (patient went to bathroom) and nursing required to reactivate or start with ECG tracing and O2 Sat read-out on monitor—students not required to replace leads or probe.

**Initial Patient Parameters:**

HR: 98 beats/min

BP: 138/88

RR: 18

Temp: 37OC
Sat%: 98 on RA

ECG tracing: Visible
Ventriloscope® or manikin setting: Both lungs with **loud crackles** Soft Aortic Stenosis murmur

**When nurse starts assessment, vital signs are:**

HR: 110

BP: 148/70

Sat%: 98 on O2

Resp: 32

ECG: Visible

Ventriloscope® or manikin setting:
Both lungs with **loud crackles**
Soft Aortic Stenosis murmur

Standardized Patient (SP) responses:
“Went to the bathroom but barely made it back”

“It is hard to catch my breath “

“I sat up but it didn’t help”

“Can I have more oxygen?”

Available Resources:
**Nurse has post-transfusion lab result - Hgb 10**

**Act 2: Medical Team Assessment**

**Ramp vitals when nurse finishes report to medical team:**

HR 120

BP 112/68

Sat% 92% on O2

RR 32

Ventriloscope® or manikin setting:
Lungs with **loud crackles**
Soft Aortic Stenosis murmur

**Lasix 20 or 40 mg given**
• lots of urine
(SP opens valve to urine in IV bag)

**• stable BP**

**Nitroglycerin pills given**

• BP drops to 92/48

**Morphine given**

• BP drops to104/50

**Aspirin given**

• does nothing

**If IV Fluids given for drop in BP**

• IV rate increased

**Standardized Patient:**

"It’s getting harder to breathe”

**Resources available:** Chest X-ray: Cardiomegaly; pulmonary edema

ECG – NSR; No ST-elevation

ABGs – Hypoxia, unchanging

Other labs as listed

**Standardized Patient:** “Woke up short and could catch my breath”

“A little extra pressure feeling on my chest “

“Woke up earlier a couple of times SOB but not this bad”

“Can’t really talk”

**Act 3A: Phil Improves when Lasix given. Standardized patient releases “urine” to drain into urine collection bag.**

**Vital signs improve:**

HR 90

BP 120/72

Sat% 98% on oxygen

RR 24

Ventriloscope® or manikin setting:
Crackles resolve
Soft Aortic Stenosis murmur

**Standardized Patient:** "I can breathe better”

“What happened?”

“How is my heart?”

**Act 3B: Phil worsens and murmur of aortic stenosis (AS) audible**

**No change in vitals:**

HR 120

BP 112/68

Sat% 92 % on oxygen

RR 32 labored

Ventriloscope or manikin setting: **Aortic Stenosis murmur setting.** Crackle breath sounds unchanged. Soft Aortic Stenosis murmur

Lungs with loud crackles

**Standardized Patient:**
Less talking & clutching chest.

If asked: “Difficult to breathe & slight pressure”

As scenario progresses and not responding to your struggle to breathe, exaggerate labored breathing and plead for help.

If asked: “Difficult to breathe & slight chest pressure”

**Act 4: The cross-cover intern hands Phil’s care back to the primary Intern (faculty receives hand-off).**
