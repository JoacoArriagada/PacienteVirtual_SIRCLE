**Appendix J: Dyspnea Scenario Orientation**

One faculty member should introduce the students to the simulation scenario and equipment, in some detail. Asking about team skills learned in introduction or during previous scenario(s) that they will use in this scenario may increase learning through repetition.

Then scenario orientation should cover:

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| A. | **Overview of scenario:** *“In this scenario, you’ll be caring for Phil Brown, a 72 year old man admitted earlier today with a GI bleed, who is now acutely short of breath. Phil is on a medical floor, in a standard hospital room, being prepped for a colonoscopy in the morning. It is about 2300 (11 pm) shortly after change of shift for the nurses. The medical team is the cross-cover team, who got sign-out on Mr. Brown 3 hours ago.”* |
| B. | **Overview of equipment and supplies** *“Phil is a patient-actor, in a standard hospital bed and a monitor capable of displaying simulated vitals and telemetry. This is the automated blood pressure cuff connected to the monitor. You will need to call out a request for repeat vital signs. Phil ‘s BP, pulse, respiratory rate, temp and O2 saturation will be displayed on the monitor and telemetry.”*  *“Phil should be examined using this special stethoscope, called a Ventriloscope®. Our patient-actor’s physical exam is actually normal, but with this Ventriloscope® you may detect abnormal findings. It plays the exam findings we would like you to hear and incorporate into your diagnostic thinking.* ***Say out loud*** *whether you are listening for respiratory or heart sounds and the corresponding sounds will be heard in the earpieces and through a speaker. The ear pieces must be seated correctly into right and left ears to be able to hear the findings and indicate if you do not hear any sound.”*  *“Use the toy cell phone and speak loudly to call the medical team room to come stat to evaluate patient.” Say loud enough for medical team to hear.*  *“Phil has an IV you can inject medications (and draw ‘blood’ from-optional). Medications are located on this pharmacy cart. Syringes (and phlebotomy supplies without needles, and blood tubes-optional) are here. If you are asked to draw blood or administer meds, use this IV.” “Oxygen delivery devices, including nasal cannula and a non-rebreather mask are at the head of the bed.”* |
| C. | **Diagnostic testing and results** *“Phil had repeat labs drawn just before the scenario start and the night nurse has the results.  You can call the lab or order from the monitor, any add on labs, ECG or imaging, etc. Results will appear on the monitor screen.”* |
| D. | **Other available data** *“The admit H&P and ED labs is on the computer. The cross-cover intern will have a sign-out sheet including information on Phil Brown. The night nurse will receive a handoff sheet and current lab results from the evening nurse.” Folders with ED records helpful also for review while medical/pharm waiting to be called into scenario.* |
| E. | **Medications and administration** *“Immediately available medications are here on this pharmacy cart. You will need to call the pharmacy for other requests. If you need to administer a medication IV, use this IV. If it is an oral medication, it is okay to administer it orally to Phil.”* |
| F. | ***Gloves and hand-gel are available*** *(indicate location)* |
| G. | **Questions? Remember: Time is compressed from 2300 to 0700.** |
| H. | **Assign the students to scenario roles:** *“We will have students participate in the following roles”:* \_\_\_\_ Medicine floor intern cross-covering the patient \_\_\_\_ Senior resident (R3) or PA \_\_\_\_ Night shift nurse(s), primary and secondary, working as team \_\_\_\_ Hospital pharmacists, working as a team  \_\_\_\_non-participating students will be observers and asked to fill out an observation tool and   participate in debrief **Faculty roles:** Delineated prior to starting simulation. |
| I. | **Starting the scenario:** *“The medical team, along with the night pharmacist are in the team room ‘across the hall’, where they can be called if needed. You may say: ‘Medical team, why don’t you head across the hall and we will start.’ Once the medical team is out of ear-shot, they will be given the hand-off sheet (cross-cover) and roles delineated by the medical and pharmacy faculty. The scenario will start outside of Phil’s room, with the evening nurse (nursing faculty) handing his care off to the night nurse(s). ”* |
| J. | ***Ending the scenario:*** *At 7 am, primary intern (played by medicine faculty) arrives back to receive report about Phil’s care from the cross-cover intern.* |

K. **Debrief:**

**Review Clinical Management** of Dyspnea and write differential diagnosis on white board.

Option to be done before scenario or beginning of debrief. Any faculty may do this review and
 the debriefing.
