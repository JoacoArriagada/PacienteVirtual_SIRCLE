**Appendix G: Dyspnea Case Standardized Patient (SP)**

**Phil Brown: Patient information**

During the learner orientation to the scenario environment, the SP will be concealed behind a screen, usually with the lab technician. When the orientation is over and learners have gone to their respective areas to be briefed, go to bed, slide IV catheter under dressing and sit on side of the bed.

The scenario starts with the standardized patient sitting on side of a hospital bed, learning forward, having difficulty breathing, dressed in hospital gown and bottoms, wearing ID band. The patient got up to go to the bathroom (has a foley catheter) as he felt urge to have a BM. The head of bed up 45 degrees with pillow, sheet and blanket. A telemetry monitor screen is visible. An IV pole with Y-administration set; LR 1000mL hanging and used blood bag IV in one arm at antecubital space. BP cuff attached to upper arm without IV. Foley tubing coming out from under sheet, attached to urine collection bag with 50 cc urine. Bedside tray with various medications, oxygen supply delivery options, blood drawing equipment, syringes without needles. Computer with patient ED chart: H&P, Chest x-ray, lab work results.

The SP may be asked to take a “pill” which is actually a tic-tac and you have the option to refuse to take the pill. No actual needles or fluids will be used except for the “urine” bag whose flow is controlled by the SP.

The following information is the patient you will be portraying.

***HPI:*** *Mr. Brown is a 72 y/o male with 4 hours of BRBPR (bright red bleeding per rectum). He woke this morning with the urge to defecate, rushed to the bathroom, and passed a large amount of blood. He had 4 or 5 more bloody bowel movements over the next 2 hours before coming into the ED. At the time of initial evaluation, he had had no blood for over an hour. His blood pressure was normal and his first Hgb was 8. Anoscopy in the ED was negative for blood, and NG aspirate showed bilious fluid. After 2 liters of IV fluids and 2 unit of PRBCs in ED. Hgb 9 after first unit PRBCs. Chest x-ray normal in ED. He was admitted to the medical floor for further evaluation.*

*Mr. B denies abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, chest pain, and lightheadedness. He had no loose stools before today. He has no prior history of GI bleeding or liver disease. He denies hemorrhoids. He has never had a colonoscopy or EGD. He is not anticoagulated, but does report NSAID use 3 or 4x per week. No fevers, chills, change in appetite or weight loss. No recent travel. Drinks city water, denies unusual food, unpasteurized milk or cheese, animal exposure.*

**Equipment and Supplies**

Ventriloscope® settings: Initially abnormal breath sounds and when posterior chest auscultated.

SP uses remote to switch to abnormal heart sounds if someone listens to your anterior chest towards end of scenario—after Lasix or other medication given.

Clothing: A hospital gown with scrub bottoms helps keep the SP warm. He may also wear socks and have slippers available for between scenario ambulation.

IV Fluids: Kerlix® or Coban® wrapped around right or left arm with IV catheter taped in place underneath dressing.

Urine bag and tubing system: To simulate diuresis a system of an IV bag with yellow fluid and tubing connected to a urine collection bag concealed wrapped in a blanket and under patient sheet. The flow of urine can be controlled by using a stopcock so that the SP can open valve when Lasix given IV.

Oxygen saturation probe: Placement on finger, usually index.

BP cuff: A standard adult blood pressure cuff is to be wrapped around the arm without the IV.

Hospital ID band: A standard hospital ID band with patient name, year of birth and allergies is to be worn on either wrist.
