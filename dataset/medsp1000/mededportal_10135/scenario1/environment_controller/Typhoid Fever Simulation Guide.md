# Typhoid Fever presenting as Heat Stroke

A Simulation Case

# Environment

Lab set up - This case takes place in a standard Pediatric Emergency Department room. Though not required, it is best to have a simulation airway cart, monitor, oxygen setup, and a private room large enough for about 8 people to move around.

Manikin set up - An adolescent female preferred. Moulage includes diaphoresis.

Suggested Props - Fans and simulated ice packs. Remember to keep ice packs out of plain site so that learners must truly think of and ask for them.

Distractors - Mother and Father are in the room and are very concerned.

# Actors

Action role - Physician leads the team through the simulation scenario. Nurse(s) follow direction of physician and carry out orders. Paramedic drops off patient and remains available to answer questions and/or assist with resuscitation. Parents are concerned and remain available throughout scenario for questioning. PICU attending answers call for consult and discusses case by phone at conclusion of case.

# Case Narrative

Scenario Background: The following background info is not given freely to learners; it is for the instructor and other volunteers to read. Rather, the learners should receive the Triage Form (file included) at the start of the case. Any of this information may be given to the learners if they specifically ask for it.

Chief Complaint: Syncope

Medic Report: 12 year old girl picked up at home after parents said she passed out while jogging today. Parents told paramedics she was out jogging with her dad when she became confused and then passed out for “about 2 minutes.” We have started a 20 gauge IV and have administered $1 0 0 ~ \mathsf { m L }$ of normal saline so far. She has been disoriented and difficult to control in route.

History: Obtained from parents and paramedics as patient is too altered / confused to provide any history. 12 year old girl presenting by ambulance with report of possible syncopal episode while jogging. According to parents, this is an otherwise healthy cross-country runner with no prior medical history who was out jogging with her father on a hot Columbia summer afternoon. Near the end of the run she became lightheaded and dizzy, dropped to her knees, and subsequently “passed out.” There was no reported jerking activity. She did not bite her tongue or urinate on herself. Emergency Medical Services (EMS) was called for transport and reported some confusion/disorientation in route with tachycardia and hypotension (heart rate in the 130’s and blood pressure of $8 0 ^ { \circ } { \mathsf { s } } / 5 0 ^ { \circ } { \mathsf { s } } )$ . They have started peripheral IV access and have administered $1 0 0 ~ \mathsf { m L }$ of normal saline in route. The parents can tell you that the patient has never had a syncopal episode before and does jog routinely, although less so given recent high temperatures; this is her first jog outdoors in the real heat of summer this year. She has no history of surgery, but did recently travel from India by plane a few weeks ago. She does not take oral contraceptives.

Medications: None

Allergies: NKDA, no other food or environmental allergies

Family History: No pertinent family history

Social History: She is up to date on all vaccines. She is menstruating irregularly but does not use tampons. Family offers no initial travel history.

(Note: Although parents do not offer this up initially without being asked, the patient has recently returned from India within the last few weeks. If learners do not ask by the end of the case, the parents may ask the learners whether such travel history is relevant information as a way to prompt them to consider it.)

# Patient’s initial physical exam:

Temperature (T): 105.8   
Pulse (P): 134   
Respiratory rate (RR): 30   
Blood Pressure (BP): 86/53   
Oxygen Saturation (SpO2): $98 \%$ on room air   
Weight: 40 kg

General: Patient appears well nourished, is awake and alert and does interact with examiners and parents, but does not cooperate with the exam, resisting being undressed for the exam. She whines a lot and appears confused and agitated.

Neuro: She moves all extremities equally well and her strength appears symmetric. She will not follow specific commands to move them. She knows her name only, not date or location. When she does speak the words are intelligible, but will not answer specific questions. There are no meningeal signs. Her GCS is 14.

Eyes: Her eyes move in all directions and her pupils are equally round, mid-dilated, and reactive to light. No conjunctival injection. A fundoycopic exam cannot be obtained due to patient’s agitated condition.

ENT: She has tacky mucous membranes but no oropharyngeal edema or erythema and her TM’s and auditory canals are normal.

Chest: Clear to auscultation bilaterally with normal expansion and appropriate air movement in all lung fields. Her breathing is non-labored although she is tachypneic.

Cardiac: She is tachycardic but rhythm is normal with normal S1 and S2, no murmurs, rubs or gallops are appreciated. Radial and dorsalis pedis pulses are $1 +$ bilaterally. Capillary refill is 4 seconds. There is no cyanosis or edema.

Abdomen: Soft, non-tender, non-distended, with normal active bowel sounds.

Extremities: Without any deformity or obvious tenderness. Joints are not swollen.   
Range of motion in all major joints appears intact.

Skin: Without lesions other than some minor abrasions on her palms. She is palpably warm and diaphoretic, but there is no erythema or other rash.
