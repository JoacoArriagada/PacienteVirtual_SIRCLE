# Typhoid Fever presenting as Heat Stroke

A Simulation Case

# Case Narrative

History: Obtained from parents and paramedics as patient is too altered / confused to provide any history. 12 year old girl presenting by ambulance with report of possible syncopal episode while jogging. According to parents, this is an otherwise healthy cross-country runner with no prior medical history who was out jogging with her father on a hot Columbia summer afternoon. Near the end of the run she became lightheaded and dizzy, dropped to her knees, and subsequently “passed out.” There was no reported jerking activity. She did not bite her tongue or urinate on herself. Emergency Medical Services (EMS) was called for transport and reported some confusion/disorientation in route with tachycardia and hypotension (heart rate in the 130’s and blood pressure of $8 0 ^ { \circ } { \mathsf { s } } / 5 0 ^ { \circ } { \mathsf { s } } )$ . They have started peripheral IV access and have administered $1 0 0 ~ \mathsf { m L }$ of normal saline in route. The parents can tell you that the patient has never had a syncopal episode before and does jog routinely, although less so given recent high temperatures; this is her first jog outdoors in the real heat of summer this year. She has no history of surgery, but did recently travel from India by plane a few weeks ago. She does not take oral contraceptives.

Medications: None

Allergies: NKDA, no other food or environmental allergies

Family History: No pertinent family history

Social History: She is up to date on all vaccines. She is menstruating irregularly but does not use tampons. Family offers no initial travel history.

(Note: Although parents do not offer this up initially without being asked, the patient has recently returned from India within the last few weeks. If learners do not ask by the end of the case, the parents may ask the learners whether such travel history is relevant information as a way to prompt them to consider it.)

# Patient’s initial physical exam:

General: Patient appears well nourished, is awake and alert and does interact with examiners and parents, but does not cooperate with the exam, resisting being undressed for the exam. She whines a lot and appears confused and agitated.

Neuro: She moves all extremities equally well and her strength appears symmetric. She will not follow specific commands to move them. She knows her name only, not date or location. When she does speak the words are intelligible, but will not answer specific questions. There are no meningeal signs. Her GCS is 14.
