A. Scenario narrative: The patient is a healthy adult who has sustained an isolated recurrent left shoulder dislocation from raising her arms up while stretching. There is no other trauma / secondary injury; she has moderate asthma requiring inhalers and occasional steroids, but has never been mechanically ventilated. Due to her prior experiences with shoulder reductions, she insists on procedural sedation prior to reduction attempts.

As the orthopedic consultant is ready at the bedside and the simulation sets the scenario in a busy period for the ED, the study subject has been recruited to conduct an EDPS to help reduce the shoulder. C. Scenario Flow

Subject starts study scenario B.

# Time X-> Y

Manikin (operator) interacts with subject to provide background and set the conditions for the EDPS and shoulder reduction. The subject should interview and examine the patient, review the ED paperwork props and Xray images provided, then plan and discuss the EDPS anticipated with the patient and sedation team members. Appropriate and adequate completion of these steps should result in the identification of 1.) the patient as a potential difficult sedation due to the limitation in medication options (opioid allergy) and history of asthma, and 2.) the defective pulse oximetry probe. Recognition of these issues will affect subsequent scenario progression, and the subject should initiate processes to address these identified issues (i.e., select non-opioid medications that will effect appropriate sedation; obtain a functional pulse oximetry probe).

# Time 0-5 min

Upon sedation induction with intravenous administration of subject-ordered medications by the study EDPS nurse, the manikin will enter a moderately sedated state in 1 min (based on study institution medication availabilities). At this point, the study orthopedic consultant will manipulate the manikin’s left arm / shoulder and spend 4 minutes struggling with the shoulder (the 5 minute duration is intended to monitor subject vigilance so should not be abbreviated). During orthopedic manipulation, the subject should continue to monitor the patient’s overall state, physical exam, and vital signs with verbalization of monitoring behaviors.
