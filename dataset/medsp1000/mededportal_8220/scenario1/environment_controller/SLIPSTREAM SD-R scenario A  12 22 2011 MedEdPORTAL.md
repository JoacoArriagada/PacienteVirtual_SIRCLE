There is no other trauma / secondary injury and no significant past medical history. Due to his prior experiences with shoulder reductions, he insists on procedural sedation prior to reduction attempts.

As the orthopedic consultant is ready at the bedside and the simulation sets the scenario in a busy period for the ED, the study subject has been recruited to conduct an EDPS to help reduce the shoulder. For the purposes of the study, there are no additional providers available to provide assistance for this sedation session.

C. Scenario Flow

Subject starts study scenario A.

# Time X-> Y

Manikin (operator) interacts with subject to provide background and set the conditions for the EDPS and shoulder reduction. The subject should interview and examine the patient, review the ED paperwork props and Xray images provided, then plan and discuss the EDPS anticipated with the patient and sedation team members. Appropriate and adequate completion of these steps should result in the identification of 1.) the patient as a potential difficult ventilation / difficult airway management patient, and 2.) the defective bag-valve-mask (BVM). Recognition of these issues will affect subsequent scenario progression, and the subject should initiate processes to address these identified issues (e.g., obtain an LMA for difficult airway management; obtain a functional BVM).

# Time 0-5 min

Upon sedation induction with intravenous administration of subject-ordered medications by the study EDPS nurse, the manikin will enter a moderately sedated state in 1 min (based on study institution medication availabilities). At this point, the study orthopedic consultant will manipulate the manikin’s left arm / shoulder and spend 4 minutes struggling with the shoulder (the 5 minute duration is intended to monitor subject vigilance so should not be abbreviated). During orthopedic manipulation, the subject should continue to monitor the patient’s overall state, physical exam, and vital signs with verbalization of monitoring behaviors.

# Time 5-12 min

When the shoulder reduction is complete, the manikin patient will become apneic after 1 minute (i.e., 6 min into scenario) due to reduced stimulation, then become hypoxic down to $7 3 \%$ over the next minute (7 min after sedation induction). The subject must quickly recognize the apnea / hypoxia and correct these conditions by adequately ventilating the manikin (e.g., as detected by manikin lung expansion on Laerdal SimMan) using either an LMA or ETT (since bag-valve-mask ventilation will fail due to the manikin’s mustache and beard moulage). Failure to deploy an LMA or ETT within 5 minutes of apnea onset (12 min after sedation induction) will result in patient expiration through irreversible hypoxic ventricular fibrillatory cardiac arrest. Use of long-acting paralytics to intubate (e.g., vecuronium, pancuronium) will terminate the scenario as further supportive critical care will be required beyond the scope of the EDPS scenario.

# Time 12-20 min

Given the short-acting nature of institutionally-available sedatives employed with EDPS, the patient will regain spontaneous respirations at 8 min after sedation induction (i.e., 2 min of apnea) only if receiving adequate ventilatory support. He will start resisting ventilation / pulling on the LMA / ETT, require extubation $^ +$ monitoring (unless long-acting paralytics are used), and be capable of verbal interaction at 10 min after sedation induction. With the patient having required supportive management, he should be assessed and observed for complications of sedation and airway management.

Patient will ask “Is my shoulder back in? What happened?”, to which the subject should respond with a disclosure of the adverse event and discussion of consequent clinical management issues. With radiographic confirmation of a successfully-reduced left shoulder and no adverse outcome, the patient should be observed in a monitored area for an appropriate length of time (based on sedation, resuscitation and/or reversal medications used), then discharged in accordance with ED protocol re: post-sedation / -reduction management and return instructions.
