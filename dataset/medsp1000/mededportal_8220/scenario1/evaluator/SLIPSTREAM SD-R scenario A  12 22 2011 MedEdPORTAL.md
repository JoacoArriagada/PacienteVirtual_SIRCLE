D. Method of debriefing

1. This is a simulation scenario designed to function as an EDPS educational and research tool. In order to detect and gauge participant performance of EDPS safety-ensuring behaviors, the scenario contains two specific EDPS patient safety probes, a pre-programmed EDPS adverse event, and objective metrics for pre-sedation preparation, intra-sedation monitoring and post-sedation recovery assessment. As EDPS is a substantial topic for discussion, the debriefing content presented here will be limited to select discussion of the scenario’s EDPS critical actions (from section V.C.1) and the two patient safety probes as they relate to the pilot-validated Simulation EDPS Safety Composite Score (see Appendix A).‡

‡The 10-point Simulation EDPS Safety Composite Score is a proprietary metric derived from the pilot phase simulation sessions of the Simulation Learning Initiative in Procedural Sedation Training for Routine Engagement of Anticipatory Maneuvers [SLIPSTREAM] program. In situ simulation performance of ten pilot subjects (five EDPS-inexperienced [EM intern] and five EDPS-experienced [EM attending physician] operators) was analyzed to derive an objective summary metric that detects experienced EDPS operator performance with excellent test characteristics (at a cutoff score of 7.7) when used with the two SLIPSTREAM simulation scenarios. (Pilot study protocol, methods and results are available in the Simulation in Healthcare manuscript “Pilot Phase Findings from High-fidelity In Situ Medical Simulation Investigation of Emergency Department Procedural Sedation.”) Debriefing material on the individual elements of the scoring system (reverse highlight) for scenario-specific EDPS critical actions (BLUE CAPS) and patient safety probes (HIGHLIGHTED BLUE CAPS) is as follows:

# \*RECOGNITION OF POTENTIAL FOR DIFFICULT AIRWAY MANAGEMENT

The facial hair of the patient presented in this scenario can represent a significant obstacle to basic airway management and patient safety, in that the beard and mustache may preclude an adequate BVM mask seal and necessary resuscitative ventilation $^ +$ oxygenation. Alternative airway management options, in addition to routine preparations for EDPS-related supportive / resuscitative maneuvers, must be considered upon recognition of a potentially difficult airway. Use of adjuncts such as oro-/naso-pharyngeal airway devices may be attempted, provided that their application does not delay ventilation.
