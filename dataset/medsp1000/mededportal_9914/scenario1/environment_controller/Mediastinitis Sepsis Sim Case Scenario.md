Simulation Interest Group Scenario Template

# **Title** : Sepsis secondary to mediastinitis

## Target Audience: EM residents, medical students

## Environment

1. Lab Set Up

* ED or simulation lab

1. Manikin Set Up

* Hi-fidelity simulator
  + IV
  + Medications: broad spectrum antibiotics
  + Moulage: patient is tired- and ill-appearing, chest wall crepitance, minimal left lower molar swelling

1. Props

* Basic airway and code cart equipment
* EKG – sinus tachycardia
* Portable CXR – poor quality, bibasilar infiltrates with trace effusions, ? air in the mediastinum (verbal report is adequate)
* CT scan chest showing mediastinitis (verbal report is adequate)
  + Bibasilar pulmonary infiltrates
  + Air within medistinal soft tissues
  + Uncapsulated fluid collection in the posterior mediastinum

1. Distractors – none specific

## Actors

* Paramedic
  + Provides history about scene and transport to the hospital, especially important given how sick the patient is
  + May be played by the instructor
* Nurse
  + Administers medications ordered by physician
  + May need to prompt participants to complete a full exam such as palpation of the neck and chest
  + May need to prompt participants to evaluate the patient’s airway prior to transport to the CT scanner.
  + May be played by another resident or student
* Patient’s wife
  + Not present immediately in the ED (the time of her arrival is to be determined by the instructor – please see Section VII – Instructor’s notes)
  + Confirms history and provides history of patient’s complaint of dental pain if it is not elicited earlier.
  + May be played by the instructor or another resident or student if they are briefed earlier.

1. **Case Narrative** (describes what the learner will experience)
2. Scenario Background Given to Participants

Chief complaint: 41 y.o. male with chest pain

Medic report: “We were called to his house by his wife. He has had chest pain for the past 2 days. He thinks he’s had a fever. The wife said that he’s been really lethargic and just not looking well. She called today because he’s been sleeping most of the day and said he’s having trouble breathing. She should be here in a few minutes.”

1. Past medical history : DM, HTN
2. Meds/allergies : Glyburide, HCTZ/Sulfa drugs
3. Family/social history: Lives with wife and daughter; occasional alcohol and tobacco; used IV drugs in college but not in years; works in construction
4. Scenario conditions initially
5. History patient gives:

* He has had chest pain for the past two days.
  + Constant
  + Getting worse
  + Radiates to his left neck
  + Has not improved with acetaminophen
* He started feeling short of breath yesterday.
* He is having chills and feels like he has a fever but he has not checked his temperature.
* No cough
* No calf pain
* Mild nausea
* He has had pain and hot/cold sensitivity in his left lower molar but has not seen a dentist about this yet. (this should not be offered immediately)

1. Patient’s initial exam:

* VS: T 99.8 (took acetaminophen 2 hours prior to arrival) HR 118 RR 22 BP 100/60 O2 96% on room air - 99% on 2L nasal cannula
* General: Tired-appearing, ill-appearing
* HEENT: Left lower molar tenderness/slight left lower face swelling
* Neck: Left sided tenderness and crepitus
* Chest: Lungs CTA b/l, left chest wall crepitus
* CV: Tachycardic, regular rhythm, crunching sound (Hamman’s sign)
* Abdomen: Non tender
* Extremities: No calf tenderness or swelling b/l
* Neuro: Moving all extremities, answers questions appropriately but slowly
* Skin: Damp

1. Patients physiology

* The patient has mediastinitis from a dental infection.
* His infection has caused sepsis.

1. Scenario branch points

* The patient is septic from mediastinitis from an odontogenic process.
* As soon as the patient is placed on the monitor, his HR is 122 and BP 94/58. Even with aggressive fluid adminitration (2L rapidly), his pressure continues to drop by 4/2 (SBP/DBP) and his HR continues to increase by 4 every minute until vasopressors are started.
* Once vasopressors are initiated, his BP increases by 6/2 every minute to a maximum systolic pressure of 130-132 and his HR decreases by 6 every minute to a minimum of 84-90. Though he should receive a central line and arterial line, these can be placed after his CT scan is completed.
* The patient should receive broad-spectrum antibiotics immediately. If antibiotics are not initiated by the second SBP measurement in the 90s or below, the SBP should drop by 8.
* Thoracic surgery as well as oral surgery should be consulted as part of management and disposition planning.
* The patient requires a CT scan – face, neck and chest - to confirm the diagnosis and extent of the process.
  + One of the considerations prior to transfer to the radiology suite is the stability of the patient’s airway. Airway compromise is a potential consequence of mediastinitis. If the participants do not specifically address airway assessment prior to ordering imaging, the nurse should raise the question. Participants will likely choose to forego intubating the patient (which is appropriate in this scenario) but the goal is to raise the consideration.
* If participants treat the patient’s sepsis with antibiotics and vasopressors and admit him to the medical intensive care unit(MICU) without identifying mediastinitis, the patient’s BP will drop by 10/6 every minute until an SBP low of 60s. The MICU will say that the patient is not stable enough for transport and the scenario should end.

1. **Instructors Notes** (what the instructor must do to create the experience)
2. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer

* The time that patient’s wife arrives is at the discretion of the instructor, based on how the case is unfolding.
* The instructor should have the nurse or the patient’s wife lead to the discovery of the odontogenic symptoms if they are not asked.

#### Tips to direct actors

* If the odontogenic symptoms are not discovered, the wife or nurse should ask about the patient’s jaw swelling.
* If the examination is completed and the participants have not palpated the chest and neck, the nurse should prompt them to do so.
* If, prior to transport to the radiology suite, participants do not discuss airway status, the nurse should prompt this discussion by asking about his airway.
