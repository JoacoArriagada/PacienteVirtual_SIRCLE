**Mediastinitis Sepsis Simulation Case Debrief Note**

Organized by learning objective

* **Maintaining a broad differential diagnosis for chest pain, including relatively rare but high-stakes conditions**
* Acute coronary syndrome
* Pulmonary embolism
* Pneumothorax/tension pneumothorax
* Aortic dissection
* Esophageal rupture
* Pneumonia
* Mediastinitis
  + Surgical emergency with a high-mortality rate
  + Potential causes:
    - Most commonly occurs postoperatively after CABG
    - Esophageal perforation
    - Trauma (blunt abdominal or chest trauma)
    - Descending infection from the oropharynx (this is referred to as descending necrotizing mediastinitis)
    - Spread from lung infection
    - Fibrosing mediastinitis (usually results from histoplasmosis)
    - Radiation
    - Cancer
    - Idiopathic
  + Pathophysiology:
    - Typically polymicrobial source
    - Can result in bleeding, compression of critical structures, sepsis
* **Taking a complete history to identify risk factors for specific pathological processes**
  + Identifying risk factors for particular pathology can help narrow the differential diagnosis.
    - Risk factors for mediastinitis:
      * Postoperative:
        + Emergency surgery
        + Obesity
        + Postoperative shock
        + Prolonged operative time (>5 hours)
        + Repeat operation in the immediate postoperative period (usually due to bleeding)
        + Wound dehiscence
      * Esophageal perforation
        + Recent instrumentation
        + Forceful vomiting
        + Esophageal foreign body
        + Malignancy
* Infection by direct extension
  + - * + Odontogenic infection
        + Retropharyngeal abscess
        + Pancreatitis
        + Lung infection
      * Fibrosing mediastinitis
        + Usually results from histoplasmosis
* **Performing a complete physical examination**
* Vital signs : may see fever, tachycardia and hypotension if the condition has progressed to sepsis
* Hamman sign: crunching sound heard during systole
* Crepitance of the chest or neck
* Edema of the neck
* **Recognizing mediastinitis and initiating appropriate treatment**
* Airway protection
  + - Patients with mediastinitis are at risk for airway compromise
    - Their airways should be assessed for impending compromise and protected if indicated. This is especially important to consider prior to transfer to the radiology suite.
* Antibiotics
  + Typically a polymicrobial process
  + Broad spectrum antibiotics should be administered
  + If the source is known, antibiotics may be tailored
  + Fluid resuscitation
    - Aggressive IVF may be required
    - In the setting of sepsis, vasopressors should be initiated for hypotension not responding to IVF
  + Surgical consultation
    - Mediastinitis is a surgical emergency!
    - Surgery should be consulted immediately
