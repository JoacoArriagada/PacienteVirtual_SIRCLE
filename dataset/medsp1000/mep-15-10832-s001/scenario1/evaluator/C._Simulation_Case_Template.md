**Ideal Scenario Flow**

Learners identify that the patient has preeclampsia with severe features after obtaining a history and performing a physical exam. The learner will also draw labs and may perform transabdominal imaging. Learners will start magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis and will administer antihypertensives. Seizure occurs nonetheless and learners are expected to treat seizure in accordance with critical actions checklist. After improvement in blood pressure and fetal heart rate tracing are noted, learners must determine the ultimate delivery plan which will be to proceed to cesarean.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Failure to accurately diagnose preeclampsia: Learners may not obtain a complete history. They may not perform a complete physical examination. Additionally, they may not order all necessary laboratory tests.
2. Failure to administer correct doses of antihypertensives.
3. Inability to recognize the need to call for help in the setting of eclampsia: Learners may become anxious at the time of the simulated eclamptic seizure and feel the need to stop the eclamptic seizure without calling for additional providers to assist.
4. Failure to correctly manage the seizure: We anticipate that all learners will administer magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis. However, in the setting of seizures occurring after magnesium administration, we anticipate learners will ask for anticonvulsants to be administered but will not administer the correct doses to stop the seizure. They also may not address airway and oxygenation. Additionally, they may not position the patient correctly at the time of seizure. They may not ensure the rails are up and padded. Failure to correctly manage the seizure is expected given the low incidence of eclampsia and consequently, the lack of exposure to this obstetric emergency.
5. Failure to administer the correct dose of magnesium sulfate.
