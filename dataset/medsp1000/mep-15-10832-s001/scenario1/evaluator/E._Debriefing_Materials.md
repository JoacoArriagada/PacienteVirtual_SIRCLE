Examples of questions the facilitator can ask that are focused on the critical actions checklist are as follows:

1. How is a diagnosis of preeclampsia made?

A diagnosis of preeclampsia is made based on: (1) presence of systolic blood pressure greater than 140 millimeters (mm) of Mercury (Hg) or diastolic blood pressure greater than 90 mm Hg on two occasions at least four hours apart AND (2) presence of proteinuria or severe features.

Proteinuria is defined as a urine protein/creatinine ratio of greater than or equal to 0.3. Severe features include (1) systolic blood pressure greater than 160 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure greater than 110 mm Hg on two occasions at least 4 hours apart, (2) thrombocytopenia (platelet count < 100,000), (3) impaired liver function (elevated blood levels of liver transaminases to twice the normal concentration), (4) severe persistent right upper quadrant or epigastric pain unresponsive to medication and not accounted for by alternative diagnoses, or both, (5) new development of renal insufficiency (elevated serum creatinine greater than 1.1 milligrams/deciliter, or doubling of serum creatinine in the absence of other renal disease), (6) pulmonary edema and (7) new-onset cerebral or visual disturbances. 1

1. How is emergent hypertension in pregnancy managed?

In the presence of persistence of systolic blood pressure greater than 160 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure greater than 110 mm Hg for fifteen minutes, medication should be administered. Initial antihypertensive options are labetalol, hydralazine and nifedipine. 2

3. How can eclampsia be prevented?

Administration of magnesium sulfate in the absence of contraindications. Contraindications are

myasthenia gravis, pulmonary edema and renal failure.

1. How is eclampsia managed?

Anesthesia and additional obstetric team members need to be called for. The airway needs to be protected. The patient must be secured. Rails must be placed up and the rails should be padded. The patient needs to be placed in lateral decubitus position. Maternal pulse oximetry must be performed and supplemental oxygen should be administered. Suction should be available. The following medications may be given to stop the seizure: lorazepam, diazepam, phenytoin, levetiracetam. 3

The facilitator then asks all learners how educational they felt the simulation was. The ACOG District II Eclampsia Checklist that was sent prior to the simulation is then reviewed.
