# Instructor’s Guide Palliative Care in Heart Failure

He does well until 5 years later when he develops increasing lower extremity edema, dyspnea, and renal failure. He is hospitalized multiple times within several months for HF exacerbations. He requires a continuous dobutamine infusion during this admission, but is not felt to be a ventricular assist device (VAD) candidate due to his age and renal failure. During your conversations with him in the CCU, you find that he is experiencing fatigue and anorexia as well as depressed mood and anxiety about his loss of independence. He has leg pain secondary to swelling. Mr. Ryan is started on an SSRI and is given acetaminophen for his leg pain with partial relief.

He is discharged home, but readmitted in three weeks with debilitating shortness of breath. He is found to have acute renal failure and is re-admitted to the CCU and placed on inotropic support.

5) Where in the illness trajectory does Mr. Ryan fall now? How would you approach a new palliative care consult with him?

# Answer: [discuss for 10 minutes]

He has now entered the late phase of the illness trajectory graph. Pressor dependence signifies he is end-stage and it has been determined that he is not a candidate for advanced therapies. This, his renal dysfunction, and frequent readmissions portends a poor prognosis (more predictable in this phase), and patient is reporting declining QOL. FYI: Studies show increased mortality on inotropes (arrhythmias, infection), but improved QOL (less dyspnea, functional impairment). In terms of numbers, the median survival on IV inotropes is 9 months, $\boldsymbol { l }$ -year survival was $4 7 . 6 \%$ , and 2-year survival of $3 8 . 4 \%$ based on 2015 data.

Consider the following during this phase, noting this is hopefully an iteration of prior conversations:

Determine prognosis (or phase of illness) and medical options: o Home inotropes? VAD? Transplant? Hospice?   
Assess illness understanding: o Worth re-exploring, possibly reflecting back on prior conversations. If patient is unrealistic, may need to gently share that we’ve now moved into the final stages of illness (deepen awareness)   
Revisit hopes, fears, goals and values: o “What is important to you now?” o “How do you want to spend the time you have left?”   
Make recommendations: o Specific advance care planning considerations at this phase:  Health care proxy identification (which hopefully was addressed long ago) He is at risk of sudden death, so it is worth re-visiting limitation of LSTs, and making a recommendation with cardiologist’s input. Consider DNR recommendation, deactivation of ICD/CRT/VAD if applicable, avoiding re-admission to the hospital if applicable. Financial and emotional affairs: “In case things don’t go as we both hope, what things should you do now to prepare yourself and your family?’’

 Mobilize community and family supports: palliative care, home care, hospice referral
