**#3 Obstructed Labor**

**Background:**

You are engaged in work related to your doctoral research project in a rural district in Sierra Leone. Using a community-based intervention strategy, you are partnering with a community health worker (CHW) to increase treatment of malaria, and increase polio vaccination rates amongst children in the village. You are facilitating an academic-community partnership; while engaged in clinical work, you hope to establish a trusting relationship with the community in order to provide a basis for long-term community based participatory research.

In this remote rural village, you and the CHW travel together to provide home visits. Your understanding of the context of your environment is that this is a patriarchal society where polygamy is still practiced, and where females undergo genital cutting- often with significant psychological and medical complications.

**Scene:**

While making home visits, you and the CHW enter a home where a young woman is in labor, in obvious distress. She is attended by her mother-in-law.

2 actors:

**Tenneh** -- community health worker (CHW) who also is a Traditional Birth Attendant (TBA)

**Mamakoh** – patient’s mother-in-law (ML)

Student and Tenneh knock on door and introduce themselves.

**Mamakoh** is distressed. She immediately asks for help. She explains that this is her daughter in law’s (mannequin) 2nd day in labor and that it’s not progressing well. She is so fatigued that she is not even able to talk or drink.

**Tenneh** asks where the father of the child is.

**Mamakoh** explains that she is the patient’s mother-in-law and that the husband (who is 30 years his wife’s senior) is currently away and won’t be back home for another week. In the meantime, she (**Mamakoh**) is responsible for the woman’s wellbeing.

**Tenneh** asks if she and the student can perform a physical exam. The patient turns out to be just 14 years old. They learn that the labor is obstructed (**Tenneh** explains to the student that there is obstructed labor, likely from cephalopelvic disproportion given her young age). She also points out that there is evidence of genital cutting, a common practice in this region.

**Tenneh** turns to **Mamakoh** and explains that the woman appears unable to effectively deliver a baby, and there is a high chance that both the woman and the baby may die as a consequence of obstructed labor. She asks if the woman may be transported to receive emergency obstetric care. This care, at the regional district hospital, is 3 hours away by vehicle transport.

**Mamakoh** after some consideration states that it is not possible; it is not acceptable for the young woman to travel unaccompanied by her husband, who will not be back from his travels in time. **Mamakoh** also makes it clear that she cannot afford this care. She insists that you can just help her here.

**Tenneh** says that she doesn’t have the tools or experience to do a C-section. She asks the student what they should do.
