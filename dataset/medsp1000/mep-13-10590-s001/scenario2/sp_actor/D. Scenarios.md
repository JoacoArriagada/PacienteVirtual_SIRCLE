**#2 HIV Treatment**

**Background:**

You are assisting an HIV outreach team in rural Malawi. The team’s goal is to visit the most remote villages to do rapid HIV tests on pregnant women. It is known that the majority of pregnant women in these areas do not receive prenatal care or HIV treatment. You bring along rapid HIV tests that can be done in 30 minutes. If positive would be repeated for confirmation. Those who test positive a second time are offered treatment for free, but the patient must go to the nearest clinic monthly to get check ups and their HIV medications. The nearest clinic is a 5-hour walk from home or 30 minutes on motorcycle taxi, which costs $2 dollars each way. The patients are assured that their test results will be kept confidential, as will any other treatment they receive.

**Scene:**

You’re visiting a pregnant woman at her home (Figure). Her name is Blessings. She is in her third trimester. The result of her first HIV test was positive, as was your confirmatory test. After establishing rapport and ensuring privacy, your goal is to tell her that she tested positive and inform her of treatment options. You plan to find out how she is feeling clinically and to stress how important it is that she gets treatment ASAP. This is because treatment can significantly reduce her baby’s chance of becoming HIV positive and will help prevent the patient from becoming ill.

1 actor: **Blessings** – pregnant HIV woman

**Blessings** listens to the student delivering the news that she is HIV positive, and grows increasingly upset.

She tells the student that if she begins the treatment, it will be impossible to hide it from her husband. But if her husband finds out that she’s positive, he will know that she’s had relations with other men, and will very likely hurt her. He may even kick her and the baby out of the house.

She also asks the student about how the treatment will help her baby. She wants to know if the student can guarantee that the baby will be healthy if she starts treatment. She also says the clinic is too far away, and that a motorcycle taxi for 2 dollars is how much she makes in a day.

If the student keeps trying to persuade her to start the treatment, she asks him whether he thinks it’s better for her and her baby to become homeless or to try to live with HIV. She may also say things like: “You’re telling me there’s no guarantee that the treatment will save me and the baby, but I can guarantee you that our lives will be ruined if my husband (and everyone else) finds out I have HIV and that I’ve cheated on my husband. We will end up on the street, begging for food.”

If the student is too passive and is not trying very hard to persuade her, she can say something like “I will do everything to try to save my baby, but then we will have no place to go! Can your hospital help us out? Can *you* help us financially?”
