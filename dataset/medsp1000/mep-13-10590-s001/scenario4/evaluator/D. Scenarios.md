**#4 Scope of Practice**

**Background:**

You are a clinical student on a clinical rotation at a district level hospital in rural Haiti. You have been assigned to help with rounds and patient care on the TB ward. Your first week there, rounds consisted of working with a social service resident and a nurse. However, during your second week the resident left for Port-au-Prince for a family emergency, and asked you to take care of the ward for a few days. There are no other doctors at the hospital except for an obstetrician, but he is in the OR doing a C-Section.

**Scene:**

You start rounding on the next patient with Fontil, a Haitian RN.

1 actor: **Fontil**- nurse (N)

Mannequin: with pericardial effusion.

**Fontil** “The next case is Marieflor Pierre.” Fontil explains that the young woman has shortness of breath, distant heart sounds, and low blood pressure. She was just admitted to the hospital overnight and started on antibiotics for pneumonia. Her blood pressure overnight was 80/50 and heart rate was 140. The patient is now delirious and not able to speak coherently.

**Fontil** says that the patient has been treated several years ago for pulmonary TB. She is anxious to be treated quickly because she has 5 children at home and needs to return to care for them.

**Fontil** tells the student that he/she can use the obstetric ultrasound to look at the patient’s heart in order to see whether it’s pericardial TB.

**The student** uses the ultrasound, which confirms a large pericardial effusion with signs of impending tamponade (**Fontil** can say this). Fontil offers to check the blood pressure again. The womans’s blood pressure is now 70/40 and heart rate is 150.

**Fontil** asks the student what to do. “Do you want to give IV fluids?”

**Fontil**  tells the student that the nearest referral center is 8 hours away, but their ambulance has been under repair for two weeks. She also says that she’s seen several social service residents perform pericardiocenteses on TB patients and it saved their lives. She tells the student that all he needs is a 16 gauge needle connected to a 50 ml syringe, and that she could hold the ultrasound probe if he wants her to. She urges him to do the procedure, as the woman is dying.

**Debriefing:**

* What options do you have? What do you think is the best course of action?
* Which ethical principles and concepts are relevant in this case? Do you think they’re in conflict, and can that conflict be successfully resolved?
* Who will be affected by your decision, and whose interests does your resolution prioritize?
* If you decide to perform the pericardiocentesis, how should you go about informed consent?
* If you decide not to perform it, how should you negotiate this with the nurse?
* Does the resolution resonate with your personal values? Do you feel at ease, or do you continue to feel troubled even though you believe you did the best thing under the circumstances?
* Should you ever perform a task for which you don’t feel absolutely competent? Where do you think you should draw the line?

**Student Feedback:**

The relevant ethical considerations in this case include:

* Non-maleficence
* Beneficence
* Utilitarian concerns (“Will this produce the best results for everyone concerned?”)
* Professionalism
* Autonomy (the patient is delirious – would she consent to such treatment if she had a chance to?)
