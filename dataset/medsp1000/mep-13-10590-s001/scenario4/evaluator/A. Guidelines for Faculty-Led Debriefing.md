**Appendix A: Guidelines for Faculty Led Debriefing**

The primary purpose of a debriefing session following each scenario is for the trainees to jointly reflect on the experience they shared during the scenarios.

The focus of each debriefing session is twofold:

- critical reflection: to consciously reflect on the impact various ethical issues had on the trainees’ emotional state and moral values, and

- strategy: to jointly work out what an appropriate response would be if the trainees found themselves in a similar situation.

The course of a debriefing session (15 minutes):

The guidelines below are *suggestions* on how to lead a debriefing session; if the trainees want to discuss different issues or in a different order, make sure their concerns are appropriately addressed. However, always attempt to bring into discussion the relevant issues identified below, especially if the debate seems focused on a single topic and is not progressing beyond a certain concern.

Before starting, the faculty should set a learning environment that is confidential and non-confrontational. We suggest that the debrief starts with the trainee who just completed the experience, then broaden to the larger group; this allows the trainee who just acted out the scenario to set the initial discussion.

- It is a good idea to start the debriefing session by suggesting that the trainees try to identify the ethical dilemma(s) they just witnessed. We suggest more open-ended questions at the beginning of the session.

- Prompt the trainees to discuss whether they find them easy or difficult to resolve. (“What would you do in a situation like this and why?”)

- If it seems appropriate, urge the trainees to try to identify whether the issues they struggled with were primarily emotional or ethical.

- Ask the trainees to identify ethical issues that are particularly relevant in this case. You can help them with a list of key ethical issues below and urge them to discuss which ones pertain to the scenario at hand and why.

* Informed consent
* Autonomy
* Truth-telling
* Cultural differences/ cultural relativity
* Limited resources
* Utilitarian concerns
* Value of life
* Justice
* Human rights
* Professional obligations
