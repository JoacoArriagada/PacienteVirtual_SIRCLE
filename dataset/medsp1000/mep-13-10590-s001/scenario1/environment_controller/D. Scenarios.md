**#1 Diverted Medications**

**Background:**

You are a trainee working on a medical program in rural Liberia, organized by your home academic medical center. Your institution has been partnering at this site for several years and your colleagues have a good, ongoing relationship with the Liberians who are also working on the project. The primary goal of the project is to help the district level hospital maintain a sufficient inpatient pharmacy formulary. Your institution has been providing funding to obtain the most important medications. Your academic mentor has returned to the US after accompanying you to the site. During the second week of your work helping with the pharmacy inventory, you notice several inconsistencies in the pharmacy records. One of your main duties is to help prevent stock-outs of crucial medications. You discover that the stock of Ceftriaxone IV is considerably less than at the same time last month, and will likely be stocked out in a day or two.

**Scene:**

As you’re checking the inventory and noticing further inconsistencies, you are approached by one of your colleagues, Dr. Alphia, who is a hospital doctor from Monrovia, Liberia’s capital. You are later joined by Dr. Beverly, the medical director of the hospital, who is originally from the rural area where you’re working.

2 actors:

**Dr. Alphia** (hospital doctor from Monrovia – Liberia’s capital),

**Dr. Beverly** (medical director of the hospital who is originally from the area).

Both wearing white coats.

Other characters: supervising pharmacist (Mr. Momolu)

**Dr. Alphia**: Asks the student whether he’s noticed that the medications keep on disappearing. Regardless of the student’s answer, Dr. Alphia continues – she says she’s outraged, she suspects the supervising pharmacist (she can say that she already brought up the issue with the supervising pharmacist, who was very evasive). She asks the student whether she/he thinks they should tell Dr. Beverly. Again, regardless of the student’s answer, she says she doesn’t think it would be right not to at least notify Dr. Beverly of their suspicions.

**Dr. Beverly** enters.

**Dr. Alphia** explains what she’s noticed and whom she suspects.

**Dr. Beverly** explains that the pharmacist, Mr. Momolu, is using the medications for a free clinic he’s running through his church for patients and family members who can’t afford the entrance fees at the hospital. Dr. Beverly provides a couple of examples, such as: “A 3-year-old’s life had been saved just yesterday with the antibiotics the pharmacist obtained in this way.”

**Dr. Alphia** is not satisfied, nor happy with the fact that they’re lying to and taking advantage of the U.S. institution. She asks the student: “Don’t you think you should inform your institution of what’s happening with their money?” Dr. Alphia can mention that this is what why foreigners think there’s so much corruption when working in poor countries. She expresses her frustration at the rampant corruption in her country.

If the student sides with Dr. Alphia, Dr. Beverly tries to convince him/her to change his/her mind:

**Dr. Beverly** can appeal to these concerns:

* There are many lives and limbs being saved that would not have been possible without the availability of the free medications (a child; a mother of 6; etc.).
* The program will be abandoned if the U.S. institution finds out. She can argue that no one will be any better because of that, while many will be worse off.
* People who have been getting help from the pharmacist will be upset with the hospital for firing him, which Dr. Beverly would be forced to do if the student tells his/her institution. This could even result in violent protests.

If the student sides with Dr. Beverly, Dr. Alphia tries to convince him/her to change his/her mind:

**Dr. Alphia** can say things like:

* Our American partners are trying to help us and we’re abusing their help by lying to them.
* No wonder no one wants to cooperate with us, we clearly can’t do anything without corruption.
* How can you watch your school be taken advantage of and do nothing about it?
* This is saving some lives in the short run, but in the long run we’re all losing because no one will want to help us any longer once word gets out.

If the student is too passive, this can simply be an argument between Dr. Beverly and Dr. Alphia. The scene can end with Dr. Alphia storming out, and Dr. Beverly saying something like “Do what you think is the right thing” to the student before exiting the room.
