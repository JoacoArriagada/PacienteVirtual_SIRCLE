# Wide Complex Tachycardia CASE SCRIPT

# V. CASE:

49 year old white male with history of non ischemic cardiomyopathy, multiple episodes of ventricular tachycardia status post AICD/pacemaker who presents with palpitations for ${ \sim } 1$ hour. $\mathrm { P t }$ has history of similar episodes in past with visits to outside hospital emergency department requiring multiple painful “shocks” that were horrible. Pacer has not fired today.

Pt last had similar palpitations yesterday however resolved on own in less than 1hour. Patient is anxious and concerned appearing sitting upright stating he feels dizzy and doesn’t want to die. Pt arrived to the emergency department by private car with his girlfriend.

ROS: shortness of breath, tightness in chest

PMH: Cardiomyopathy, AICD, Ventricular Tachycardia, Hypertension, Hyperlipidemia, Substance Abuse

Meds: Lisinopril, Metoprolol, Sertraline, Alprazolam (last taken this morning)

Allergies: No drug allergies

Social History: occasional etoh, $\%$ pack per day cigarettes

Family History: Father with heart attach in past

# SIM Exam:

Vital Signs: BP 118/78, P 155 VTach, T 98.6, R 20, O2 Sat $1 0 0 \%$   
General: anxious, repeatedly asks if he is going to die, sitting up gripping stretcher   
HEENT: pupils equal and round, normocephalic, atraumatic, moist mucous membranes   
CV: tachycardic, regular   
Lung: clear to auscultation bilaterally   
Abd: Soft, non tender, non distended   
Skin: warm and dry   
Neuro: alert and oriented, normal strength

# VI. CRITICAL ACTIONS:

Primary Survey: Assess airway, IV, O2 Monitor, Vital Signs   
Recognize Wide Complex Tachycardia   
Physical Exam   
Order Ancillary Data (EKG, labs, CBC, Coags, Panel 7)   
Obtain Detailed History   
Initiate Pharmacologic Treatment of WCT   
Initiate $2 ^ { \mathrm { n d } }$ Pharmacologic Treatment of WCT   
Critical Care Disposition   
Sedation Prior to Electrical Cardioversion   
Successfully deliver Cardioversion

# VII. BRANCH POINTS:

1. Treatment with appropriate antidysrhythmic $\mathord { - } >$ convert to sinus rhythm for \~1minute: Patient will report relief of symptoms while in sinus. After 1 minute patient will revert back to VTach at rate ${ \sim } 1 5 0 { \mathrm { - } } 1 6 0$ . Cueing Guideline: Nurse can suggest giving treatment, patient can verbalize what he was given last time he had similar episode of VTach   
2. If no treatment started $\mathord { - } >$ pt will become more hypotensive. Patient will require cardioversion. If no intervention have pt progress to VTach arrest. Cueing Guideline: patient can become more anxious; suggest impending doom/progression to arrest.   
3. If Cardiology not emergently consulted patient will continue to become hypotensive and will continue to revert back to ventricular tachycardia despite treatment measures. Cueing Guideline: Nurse can suggest consultation, discussion with superior   
4. If Amiodarone, Procainamide or Lidocaine infusion started pt will gradually become more hypotensive. Cueing Guideline: Patient describes being lightheaded, suggests “this is when they had to shock me last time.”   
5. If team attempts to cardiovert patient without sedating patient will begin to scream and “thrash” in bed. Cueing Guideline: Nurse suggests giving him pain medication prior to cardioversion   
6. Patient can accelerate VTach to the 180s-200s, pacer can fire when HR goes above 180. Pacer will fail to capture making participants seek alternative treatments for VTach. During debriefing discuss role of magnet inhibition as well as reasons for failure of AICD capture (ie. lead fracture, dislodgement, increased pacer threshold). Cueing Guideline: patient screams every time pacer fires.   
7. At any point in case patient can decompensate into VTach Arrest

Tips for success:

Move case forward by changing rhythm from stable to unstable VTach allowing for participants to exlore antidysrhythmics as well as cardioversion as treatment option.

Pitfalls:

Most groups failed to recognize need for sedation in cardioversion. In order to move past this see cueing guide above and encourage technician or faculty member running mannequin to respond in pain or concern/anxiety. Some groups failed to recognize hypotension associated with continuous antidysrhythmic administration and did not provide adequate volume resuscitation. Having an actor play the nurse or alternatively changing your voice as the person running the mannequin may be helpful to move case forward and suggest appropriate actions.
