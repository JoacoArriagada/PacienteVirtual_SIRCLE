Conceptual Background:

This case was created as part of our Emergency Medicine Resident Simulation Curriculum. This case is based on an actual patient seen in our emergency department and highlights specific teaching points and potential pitfalls in treatment algorithms. The simulation venue offers a unique opportunity to address team dynamics as well as provide a forum for didactic learning as it is often difficult to debrief a critical case while working in real time patient care settings.

Implementation:

Case Length: 15 minutes   
Case Debriefing: 30-45 minutes   
Case Supplies: see attached file for details of case supplies

This case was run consecutively four times in our Simulation Center using Laerdal SimMan $^ \mathrm { \textregistered }$ . Each case evaluated 4-5 emergency medicine residents per session for a total of ${ \sim } 2 0$ residents. See attached case supplies and programmed case for further case information. In our sessions, a PGY3 resident ran the simulator while a faculty member observed training from inside the simulation room. The resident, technician, or faculty member running the simulator will need some knowledge on manipulating the SimMan $^ \mathrm { \textregistered }$ mannequin. It is not necessary to have faculty run the simulator or be in the room during the session; however, having faculty assist with debriefing is helpful and beneficial to participants.

Limitations:

This case has not been used to formally evaluate residents. Information has been collected about team dynamics as well as completion of critical actions in an effort to target debriefing strategies. Perhaps in the future we can use simulation to target areas of weakness in participants and tailor education to meet their needs.

Another limitation is that the programmed case only has the initial state programmed and is intended to be run “on the fly.” This requires the faculty or technician controlling the mannequin to have knowledge in operating the mannequin. While this makes the case more difficult for the instructor, I have found that it is more beneficial for participant learning and more realistic.

# Case Pearls:

The goal is to have participants manage stable and unstable ventricular tachycardia and to prepare the team for potential complications of interventions (ie. hypotension with antidysrhythmic infusion, progression to unstable VTach, require group to perform cardioversion).

# B. Management of Wide Complex Tachycardia (WCT)

Distribute or project 2010 AHA ACLS algorithm for Tachycardia & discuss approach to wide complex tachycardia

- Unstable defined as hemodynamic instability, change in mental status, hypotension/shock. Uncommon to be unstable if HR $< 1 5 0$  Good CPR is critical for unstable VTach   
- Stable: pulses present. Start with medical therapy   
- Determine if Regular or Irregular – Irregular can signify supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)/Atrial Fibrillation (Afib) with aberrant conduction   
- Regular Rate Wide Complex tachycardia   
Amiodarone – First Line for WCT treatment   
150mg IV over 10 minutes followed by infusion of 1mg/minute for 6 hours, then   
$0 . 5 \mathrm { m g / }$ minute (max of $2 . 2 \ \mathsf { g } / 2 \angle$ hours) - Mainly Class III anti-arrhythmic, with properties of Class I, II, IV - Noncompetitive $\alpha -$ and $\beta$ -adrenergic inhibition - Recurrent ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation - Inhibits abnormal automaticity - Prolongs action potential duration - Increases the cardiac refractory period - May cause hypotension

Procainamide – First Line for WCT treatment (15 to $1 8 \mathrm { m g / k g }$ administered as slow infusion over 25-30 minutes, followed by 1-

$4 \mathrm { m g / }$ minute by continuous infusion (works for SVT and VT)

- Class IA   
- Acute therapy of many supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias   
- Decreases automaticity, increases refractory periods, and slows conduction   
- Prolongs cardiac action potential   
- Can suppress conduction over bypass tract so if AVRT or an SVT conduction over bypass tract is suspected

Lidocaine – Second Line for WCT treatment (1 to $1 . 5 \mathrm { m g / k g }$ over 2-3 minutes, may repeat doses of $0 . 5 { - } 0 . 7 5 \mathrm { m g / k g }$ in 5-10 minutes up to a total of $3 \mathrm { m g / k g } ;$ ; continuous infusion: $\boldsymbol { 1 - 4 } \ : \mathrm { m g / }$ minute)

- Class IB - Blocks both open and inactivated cardiac $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ channels (slows depolarization) $2 ^ { \mathrm { n d } }$ line (unless SVT with aberrancy suspected)   
- Unlikely to have effect on SVT with aberrancy   
Should be primary antidysrhythmic with ongoing myocardial ischemia   
- Hyperpolarize Purkinje fibers   
Decreases automaticity by reducing the slope of phase 4 and altering the threshold for excitability   
- Little effect on hemodynamic function, however has been associated with heart failure exacerbations, especially in patients with very poor left ventricular function

Sotalol – can be used for long term management

- Class III   
- Nonselective adrenergic receptor antagonist   
- Prolongs cardiac action potentials   
- Decreases automaticity, slows AV nodal conduction, and prolongs AV refractoriness   
- Ventricular tachydysrhythmias and atrial fibrillation or flutter   
- Torsades de pointes is the major toxicity with sotalol overdose

Review how to discriminate between VTach and Afib/SVT with aberrancy   
Wellens, Brugada, Griffith approaches Wellens originally described how to discriminate WCT in 1978 however sensitivity and specificity of criteria have not been determined. Brugada developed a sequential fourstep EKG analysis algorithm in 1991. Despite initially published high sensitivity and specificity of Brugada criteria subsequent studies identified a high degree of discrimination among providers applying the criteria. The sensitivity and specificity of Brugrada criteria have not been independently verified. Griffith published the most recent criteria in 1994 emphasizing VTach as the default diagnosis. There are no independent studies evaluating the sensitivity or specificity of the Griffith approach.

 Wellens:

- QRS over 0.14 seconds   
- Left axis deviation in excess of $- 3 0 ^ { \circ }$   
- Certain configurations of QRS (criteria for RBBB and for LBBB)   
- AV Dissociation

 Brugada: Fulfill any of 4 criteria $=$ VTach

- Lack of RS complex in precordial leads   
- Whether R to S interval in any precordial lead when present is greater than 100ms   
- AV Dissociation   
- Morphology criteria in leads $\mathrm { V } _ { 1 }$ and $\mathrm { V } _ { 6 }$

Griffith: SVT is diagnosed only if morphology is typical of bundle branch block - RBBB: $\mathrm { r S R ^ { \prime } }$ in $\mathrm { V } _ { 1 }$ and RS in $\mathrm { V } _ { 6 }$ with $\mathrm { R } / \mathrm { S } > 1$ - LBBB: rS or QS in $\mathrm { V } _ { 1 }$ and $\mathrm { V } _ { 2 }$ and delay to S wave nadir $< 7 0$ msec, R wave and no Q wave in $\mathrm { V } _ { 6 }$

- Bottom Line: When in doubt assume VTach (accounts for $8 0 \%$ of cases of WCT in unselected populations and ${ > } 9 5 \%$ of cases in pts with prior myocardial infarction/structural heart disease)

- Procainamide and Amiodarone are recommended as $1 ^ { \mathrm { s t } }$ line in patient with WCT of uncertain etiology; both can convert SVT even those associated with accessory pathways and VTach.   
- New 2010 AHA guidelines indicate Adenosine as safe approach to undifferentiated WCT that is monomorphic

Discuss implications of AICD in a patient with VTach and recognize potential complications of AICD (WCT in pt with AICD firing without capture, AICD fire with capture and return of WCT, AICD failure to fire etc.)

- Device should fire within first few minutes of arrhythmia   
- If AICD fires without capture consider reasons (ie. lead fracture, rise in defibrillation threshold secondary to medications, myocardial infarction, lead insulation defect)   
- Applying a magnet will inhibit defibrillation of and AICD however will not inhibit pacing function   
- If patient is stable consider interrogating pacer - can help diagnose arrhythmia, manually trigger device to provide therapy, retrieve device therapies, disable device to avoid inappropriate therapy, and adjust device parameters

Discuss the role of cardiology for pacer interrogation/overdrive pacing - Ventricular Tachycardia can sometimes be terminated by pacing the ventricle at a faster rate than the tachycardia. The reentrant circuit is interrupted by a paced impulse leaving the rhythm refractory when the reentrant wave returns thus terminating the tachycardia. - Achieved by firing short burst of 8 beats - In several observation and prospectively randomized studies, overdrive pacing terminated as many as $9 5 \%$ of episodes of spontaneous VT comparable in efficacy t low energy cardioversion

 Review indications for Electrical Cardioversion  Review methods of sedation for cardioversion

- In awake patient consider conscious sedation   
- Conscious sedation that will limit hemodynamic instability. Midazolam/Fentanyl, or Etomidate preferred over Propofol.   
- Things to consider Benzodiazepines - longer onset and offset increase risk of respiratory depression Etomidate – can lead to myoclonus
