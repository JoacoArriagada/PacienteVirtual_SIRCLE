# Title: Medicine Across The Barricade

1. Abbreviations

TMP Tactical Medical Provider

CC Chief Complaint

GSW Gunshot Wound

CAT Combat Application Tourniquet

HPI History of present illness

RLE Right Lower Extremity

PMH Past Medical History

FH Family History

ROS Review of Systems

PE Physical Exam

SWAT Special Weapons and Tactics

XABCs Exsanguinating Hemorrhage, Airway, Breathing, and Circulation

# Environment:

## Lab Setup: Room with communication equipment (phone)

## Manikin Setup: SimMan with an actively hemorrhaging extremity injury requiring tourniquet placement in order to control the hemorrhage. The manikin is dressed is any available clothing that will include long pants and a long sleeve shirt as a minimum to cover his entire body as a normal person would wear. No hat or face covering is required, but could be included if one desired.

## Distracters: Screaming from the bystander(s) out of fear on the other end of the phone.

* 1. Props: CAT/triangular bandage and stick

# Actors:

## Roles:

## Negotiator

* + - 1. This can be played by an actual negotiator if the training requires.
      2. This can be played by a layperson, as well.
      3. The basic role of the negotiator is to establish contact with the suspect, reestablish contact if communications are lost, apply verbal pressure to the medic if he/she appears to be stalled. This can come in the form of asking what is going on, asking what’s happening, asking how the victim is doing. If the medic is failing to apply a tourniquet, the negotiator may even prompt the medic toward that end offering it as a question/option.

## Bystander is an uninjured, adult hostage but referred to as a bystander for ease of nomenclature. The bystander, theoretically, could be played by a child or adolescent, which would require the medic to communicate to the required developmental level. The unique communication needs of a child, adolescent of an impaired adult were not part of our learning objectives. However, variations of the scenario could be used employing these additional learning objectives.

## Suspect (hostage taker)

## Who can play them: The negotiator should be someone familiar with the simulation case. The suspect should be someone familiar with the simulation case. The bystander can be anyone with no medical training.

# Case Narrative:

## Scenario given to participants

## The TMP will be instructed he/she is on-scene with a local SWAT team at a barricade situation with multiple hostages (number unknown). There is an injured hostage requiring medical care.

### CC: GSW

### HPI (must be requested): Hostage tried to run when bank robbers first came in and he sustained a GSW.

### PMH (must be requested): Unknown

* + 1. FH (must be requested): Unknown
    2. ROS (must be requested): Unable to attain secondary to decreased mental status

## PE

### Tachycardic, tachypneic, diaphoretic, cool clammy skin

### On the RLE there is an anterior GSW just medial to midline of the mid-thigh with a posterior GSW.

### There is active bleeding coming from the posterior GSW wound.

### Distal pulses in the RLE are decreased compared to the left.

* 1. Scenario Branch Points:
     1. Failure of TMP to introduce himself/herself properly causes the suspect to become enraged and hang up. The TMP will then have to inform the negotiator who will reestablish contact.
     2. Failure to determine the level of medical training of the bystander will result in the negotiator interrupting the TMP in order to ask that question.
     3. Failure to remain calm will cause the bystander to become hysterical.
     4. Failure to use lay-terms will result in the bystander becoming confused and angry.
     5. Failure to treat the GSW will result in the injured hostage becoming more agitated, dyspneic and pale. This will progress to death if bleeding is not stopped.
     6. Requests to move the patient out initially will be denied by the suspect.
     7. Requests to send equipment across the barricade initially will be denied.
     8. Requests to move the hostage out after the tourniquet is applied will be approved if suspect is informed that death will be imminent without proper medical treatment.

1. Instructor Notes:
   1. Tips to keep the scenario moving:
      1. Have suspect and negotiator keep applying pressure to TMP.
      2. The bystander can also ask multiple questions about next steps.
   2. Tips for actors
      1. Suspect:
         1. Hostile.
         2. Concerned about the outcome of the hostage and how it will affect the charges against him.
         3. Threatens bystander and is concerned that bystander is sabotaging the hostage’s care to cause the police to enter.
      2. Bystander
         1. Will follow commands.
         2. Does not understand medical jargon.
         3. Cannot anticipate next steps.
      3. Negotiator
         1. Calm.
         2. Will press for information if TMP does not keep him/her informed.
   3. Scenario sequence of events
      1. Introduction.
      2. Request for a bystander with medical knowledge.
      3. Introduction to bystander.
      4. Have the bystander assess the situation, including the scene and the suspect.
      5. Suspect becomes hostile about “irrelevant” information and threatens the bystander’s life.
      6. Calm the bystander.
      7. Direct a fast head-to-toe evaluation of the injured hostage, looking for all immediate life-threatening injuries.
      8. Expose GSW
      9. Assess area for possible tourniquet material.
         1. A scarf and wooden chair leg can be prepositioned in the room.
      10. Apply a tourniquet using the chair leg as a windless.
      11. Reassess the injured hostage.
          1. Tachycardic and pale.
          2. Weak and thready pulses (no pulse in affected leg).
      12. Request to move hostage out.
   4. Scenario complications
      1. If bleeding is not addressed quickly enough (The specific time to treatment can be flexed based on the desired endpoint of training; however, I set it to be approximately 5 minutes.) and the bystander is moved on to ABCs first, the injured hostage will become dyspneic and agitated.
      2. If the bleeding is still not addressed the hostage will die.
      3. If the TMP does not introduce himself/herself calmly and respectfully, the suspect will hang up. This will cause the negotiator to have to re-establish the connection.
      4. If the TMP does not speak calmly to the bystander, the bystander will become hysterical.
      5. If the TMP does not request for the hostage to be brought out following stabilization and hemorrhage control, the negotiator will request this to be done.
   5. High-Fidelity Simulator Application
      1. The need for the high-fidelity simulator comes from the fact that the bystander is ideally being played by someone with no special medical knowledge or training and no knowledge of the victim’s injury(s). Therefore, in order for the medic to assess the condition of the victim, he/she will request information which a lay person could reasonably be expected to provide such as whether the patient is breathing, has a pulse, can talk or has active external bleeding. This constellation of findings is more consistent with a high-fidelity mannequin. Furthermore, he/she may request the bystander to relay changes in these initial findings after medical therapy is initiated or after a delay in treatment occurs.
