**Simulated Application Case 1: Systematic Analysis of a Medical Error**

**Case Overview**

This case serves as an introduction to patient safety. The purpose is to bring awareness and demonstrate the impact of medical errors in healthcare practice. The simulation case specifically involves an adverse event related to a medication error. Medication error analysis requires a systematic approach including structured analysis to identify the root cause. Trainees will first participate in a brief PEA arrest code that results in death. Following the simulation, trainees will discuss the medication error by conducting a team-based written RCA with an action plan and safety recommendations to prevent future errors.

**Scenario and Tasks**

*Door note*: You will enter a simulation mock code of a patient in cardiac arrest. This is a 45-year-old male who was admitted for a right main stem pulmonary artery embolism. He has been on intravenous unfractionated heparin for the past 48 hours without dose adjustment. His aPTT has been < 20 seconds. The heparin dose was incorrectly calculated when initially ordered by the intern. In addition, nursing staff and pharmacy subsequently failed to adjust the dose of heparin for 48 hours. Your task is to follow ACLS protocol with your team. Following this, you will conduct a root cause analysis of the event. Your main task is to **identify contributing causes of the error** and create a **root cause analysis (RCA)** using the ishikawa fishbone diagram provided.

**Faculty Reference Guide- During the Case**

* Remain silent and allow trainees to run the code
* Assess if trainees:
  + give oxygen/set up intubation
  + attach defibrillator pads
  + establish IV access (patient will already have 2 large bore IVs)
  + call out leader and identify team member roles
  + recognize a non-shockable rhythm - asystole/PEA -> give adequate CPR every 2 minutes and recheck rhythm, give epinephrine every 3-5 minutes
  + ask for patient's history; if they do, faculty facilitator will read chart information to them (see below)
* After 10 minutes - faculty facilitator terminates code, states time of death

**After the code terminates**, faculty facilitator should:

* Observe if the trainees are immediately discussing potential causes of death.
* Debrief with trainees: ask questions such as: what do you think is the likely cause of death? Was there a medical error? Say, "Let's talk about the error and brainstorm contributing factors."
  + During the debriefing session, have trainees use the blank fishbone diagram to identify contributing factors to identify the root cause.
  + Advise the trainees to develop an action plan/solution for problems identified
  + Specifically ask how the process for reporting this error at your institution

**Chart information (use this information if requested during the mock code)**

"I have the chart open, here is some additional history: Patient is a 45-year-old male who was diagnosed with a new right main pulmonary artery embolism. Looks like he was on intravenous unfractionated heparin but his PTT has not been therapeutic over the past 48 hours: PTT has ranged from 20-30 when the therapeutic goal is 60-90" **Make the error obvious!**

***If trainees ask for more information***: "The patient was admitted for acute onset of shortness of breath, has no other past medical history, and his family and social history are unremarkable. The patient is a non-smoker, he is a bus driver with a sedentary lifestyle. His vital signs have been normal and routine labs, EKG, and CXR are unremarkable. Last recorded vital signs are BP 110/60, HR 86, RR 14, O2 sat 96% on room air. There is a CT angiogram from admission that shows a right main stem PE."

***If the trainees ask for any more details***: say "I don't know" or "It's not in the chart". Remember, you are only to read information from the chart.
