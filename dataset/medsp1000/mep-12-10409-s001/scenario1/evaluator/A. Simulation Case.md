| **PATIENT NAME: Albert Smith**  **PATIENT AGE: 45**  **CHIEF COMPLAINT: no complaint, patient is unresponsive** | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | This case requires high fidelity simulation mannequin. This is a 45-year-old male who was admitted for shortness of breath. He was found to have a right main stem pulmonary artery embolism. He has been on intravenous unfractionated heparin for the past 48 hours without proper dose adjustment. His aPTT has been < 20 seconds since admission. The heparin dose was incorrectly calculated when initially ordered by the intern. In addition, nursing staff and pharmacy subsequently failed to adjust the dose of heparin for 48 hours. He is now in PEA arrest. Your team is being called into a code blue/cardiac arrest. Your task is to follow ACLS protocol with your team. Following this, you will conduct a root cause analysis of the event. Your main task is to identify possible contributing causes of the error and create a root cause analysis (RCA) using the ishikawa fishbone diagram provided. Following discussion of the RCA, the group will suggest an action plan to prevent this error from occurring in the future. The overall goal for this case is for learners to understand the impact of a medical error, conduct a root cause analysis and generate an action plan to prevent future errors. | |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | This case serves as an introduction to patient safety. The purpose is to bring awareness and demonstrate the impact of medical errors in healthcare practice. The simulation case specifically involves an adverse event related to a medication error and communication breakdown. Trainees will first participate in a brief PEA arrest code that results in death. Following the simulation, trainees will discuss the medication error by conducting a team-based written RCA with an action plan and safety recommendations to prevent future errors. | |
| **Critical Actions** | Systematic Analysis of a Medical Error  During the Code   * Assessed initial vital signs * Secured airway * Appropriate ACLS protocol * Asked for patient history * Wash hands * Obtain focused PE   During RCA drawing and discussion   * Recognize medical error/adverse event * Identify contributors of the medical error * Identify strategies to decrease probability of this error * Generate a root cause analysis |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The learners enter the room to find a patient in cardiopulmonary arrest. They recognize that the patient is in PEA arrest and begin ACLS protocol. Faculty facilitator silently observes the team for team building, communication and leadership skills. Learners call out a team leader, the leader then designates a team member in charge of 1) airway, 2) CPR, 3) IV access and medication administration, 4) Time keeper. ACLS protocol for PEA arrest is initiated as soon as possible with ongoing CPR and intubation/respiratory support. During the code, the faculty facilitator reads the chart information and makes the error obvious: patient was admitted for pulmonary embolus, the heparin dose was never adjusted, and the aPTT was far below therapeutic value. The patient's respiratory and cardiac status continues to deteriorate despite all interventions initiated by the team of learners. At the 10-minute mark, the patient is pronounced dead by the faculty facilitator.

At the time of death pronunciation, learners are observed during their team debrief. Faculty facilitator debriefs with trainees: ask questions such as: what do you think is the likely cause of death? Was there a medical error? Say, "Let's talk about the error and brainstorm contributing factors." They are handed a blank fishbone diagram and are asked to draw a root cause analysis. They are expected to brainstorm potential cause of error (pharmacy, nursing, physician etc., based on their pre-reading materials). **Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Failure to call out a team leader: This scenario mimics a real life scene where many interprofessional team members walk into a code at the same time.
2. Failure to foam in and out by the physician learners.
3. Uncertainty about contributing causes for the medical error.
