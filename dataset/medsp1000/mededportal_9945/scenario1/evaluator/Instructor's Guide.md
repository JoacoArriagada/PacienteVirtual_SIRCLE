Simulation Case:

Acute Intentional Overdose in the Setting of a Signed Do Not Resuscitate Order

Intended use of case: This case is targeted towards 3rd and 4th year medical students, emergency medicine residents, emergency medicine nurses, and adjunct EM staff.

**Purpose**: The purpose of this case is to improve interdisciplinary communication skills, recognition and management an acute intentional overdose, recognition of the ethical dilemmas regarding Do Not Resuscitate Orders complicated by an acute intentional overdose, general crisis resource management skills through conflict resolution, team-based communication and patient-centered care. There is an intentional medico-ethical dichotomy built into the case to enhance its breadth of use amongst learners at various levels of training.

**Educational Objectives:**

* 1. Organize a patient care team
  2. Identify complications of intentional overdose in concert with a signed DNR order
  3. Recognize potential life-threatening complications ofa salicylateoverdose and appropriately initiate treatment
  4. Manage a critically-ill patient who decompensates using a team-based approach
  5. Acutely manage social dynamics in a crisis situation

**Conceptual Background:** This case was created as part of our Emergency Medicine Resident Simulation Curriculum in which we spend two hours per month running through simulated cases. The cases are part of our regular didactic sessions. We run through a single case each session twice: two teams (3-4 residents and students) blinded to the case and to each other’s performance on the scenario. After going through the case, we hold a learner-centered structured debriefing with the teams and the audience.

This case is based on a composite of cases seen in our emergency department regarding intentional overdoses and DNR orders with concomitant family conflicts. Moreover, this case highlights high-yield teaching points and potential pitfalls in the approach to the treatment of the poisoned patient, conflict resolution and navigating psychosocial issues we encounter in emergency medicine when on shift. This simulation folds together the reality of practicing emergency medicine by way of bona fide critical-care medicine, psychosocial complications, and medical ethics. In our experience, managing the poisoned patient involves medical management as well as the management of complex psychosocial issues.

This case can be viewed from several perspectives. For example, earlier learners (students and interns) may focus on the medical management of the aspirin overdose and its associated pathophysiological ramifications. More advanced learners may likely understand management of salicylate toxicity and thus be able to focus and learn for the ethical issues this case presents.

Detailed debriefing of this case can focus on both the medical management of the patient as well as issues regarding DNR orders, medical ethics and in particular patient autonomy, family conflicts and end-of-life issues. In our implementation of this scenario, we have observed the discussion to focus initially on medical management of the case and then evolve to the ethics issues presented. As such, debriefing provides a forum to work through the ethic dilemmas herein in detail. The overall resident and student evaluation of this case is high (4.75 on a 1 (low)- 5 scale(high)) . We have included references that further address key points of this case including current guidelines for salicylate toxicity and medical ethics.

**Implementation:**

1. The case is intended as a group simulation, with groups comprised of learners anywhere fromMS3s to PGY-4s.
2. Case Length – approximately 20 minutes.
3. 5 – 10 minute synopsis of case by faculty which includes a case summary and overview of the ethical aspects of the case.
4. Structured debriefing suggested as follows

* After scenario, move from simulation site if possible to another room. Alternatively, if you remain in the same room, move all equipment out of view of the audience.
* Ask for initial reactions from select trainees. Here, you do not have to ask all participants for their reactions; just enough reactions are needed for you to get a sense to get some emotional response to case and allow trainees to express any concerns.
* Employ both objective and subjective feedback. For example, a concrete observations (I saw, I heard, I noticed) followed by a question to assess the learner intentions. In this regard, you initiate a question and further discussion with an objective stimulus.

1. This case was run several times as part of our simulation education training using a LaerdalSimMan®. Each case evaluated 3-4 trainees lead by an upper level resident and at least 2 other residents (PGY1 or PGY2 residents) or students (MS4). Two faculty members and two senior residents observed the training scenario while a technician controlled the LaerdalSimMan®. All involved in running the case should walk through the case prior to implementation and focus on both the medical as well as the social aspects of the case. We recommend at least one faculty member have the sole role of observer with no technical responsibilities in order to indentify proper medical management and social and ethical interactions. The lead debriefer should be the faculty mentor observer who will be best equipped to identify performance gaps and gaps in team dynamics and medical knowledge.
2. For junior learners (medical students, junior residents, ED nursing), we typically emphasize the approach to salicylate poisoning. This serves as a review for our more senior residents. For the more senior residents, usually the team leaders, we emphasizethe difficult ethical aspects of the case regarding a suicide by a patient with a valid Do Not Resuscitate order. We find that during debriefing sessions we can cover both aspects of the case within 30 minutes.

**Limitations:** This case has not been used to formally evaluate residents, medical students or medical staff. Moreover, trainees may focus on the medical aspects of salicylate overdose and not recognize the complications regarding DNR orders and intentional overdose and nor the ethical implications of treating reversible conditions in the face of a DNR order. Depending on the learners involved in the SIM, different aspects can be emphasized at the instructors’ discretion. Nevertheless, EM physicians encounter these complex issues daily and recognition and familiarity of complex psychosocial issues is incumbent of our training.

**Case Pearls:**The goal is to have participants manage and stabilize a patient subsequent to an acute intentional overdose while managing complex family dynamics and dealing with the ethical dilemmas of an acute overdose and concomitant DNR orders, end-of-life issues and patient rights.
