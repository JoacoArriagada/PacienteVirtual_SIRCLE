**Simulated Patient (SP) Training: Cases Information Provided to SPs**

# Case 4: Parent/Child Scenario

You are a Hard of Hearing (HoH) parent attending a clinic visit with your 12-year-old child, who primarily communicates using ASL. Your child has been complaining of **fatigue** for the past few months, and you are worried about their **growth** and overall health. You (parent) communicate verbally and rely on seeing faces/lips for receptive communication. Your child is fluent in ASL and prefers to use an interpreter rather than voice for communication.

**Simulation Setup:**

• 1 Virtual Interpreter on the iPad is available.

**Instructions for the Patient Actors**

**Patient Demographics**

• **Name**: You may use your own name or create one

• **Age**: Child, 12. Adult 45.

• **Living Situation:** Lives with family in a suburb

**Typical Day for Child**

• Wakes up at 6:30, goes to school at 7:30. Home at 3:30 pm when sports are not in season. Used to play outside, read books, do homework, now spends more time in their room and takes naps after school.

• Used to participate in school soccer and track, is less interested now.

**Characteristics of the Child’s Condition**

**• Energy Levels:** The child has been experiencing persistent fatigue for the past few months.

• More tired than usual, even after sleeping.

• Struggling to keep up with schoolwork and daily activities.

• Not as physically active as they used to be.

• Not eating as much lately.

• **Nutrition**: Mostly vegetables, some rice and bread.

• **Other:** Child occasionally complains of wanting to chew on ice chips (sign of anemia)

**How to Act During the Simulation:**

• Both parent and child are eager for answers. The parent is visibly concerned about the child’s health.

• The child appears pale and tired. They initially give shorter answers “*I’m just tired*.” but opens up more as the conversation continues. See examples of expansion below:

*• “I don’t really know when it started… maybe a few weeks ago? I just feel tired all the time. Even when I get a full night’s sleep, I wake up feeling like I didn’t really rest. Sometimes I feel lightheaded and feel my heart pounding when I’m just sitting.”*

*• “It’s been hard to get through the day at school because I’m always sleepy, and I can’t really focus during class. I used to hang out with my friends after school or ride my bike, but now I just want to lie down when I get home.”*

*• “It’s been really frustrating. I used to love playing soccer with friends, but now I just don’t have the energy. It makes me sad that I can’t keep up with them like I used to. And I think my friends are starting to notice—like, they stopped asking me to come out and play as much. Right now I feel like I’m missing out on stuff, and it’s kind of lonely.”*

• If the provider talks too fast or looks away, you may show confusion.

• The provider should engage with both the parent and child. Attention can be redirected toward the child if most questions are being asked to the parent.

• Ask questions or express concerns when something is unclear or you need more information.

**The Parent’s Understanding**

• You understand that proper nutrition is important for your child’s growth and development.

• You believe that your child’s current diet might not be providing enough nutrients. You have tried to encourage your child to eat more but have not seen much improvement.

• You are concerned that there could be an underlying medical issue (like anemia or thyroid issues) affecting your child’s growth or appetite.

**Health Information**

**• Medical history:** None.

**• Menstrual history:** None yet.

• **Surgical history:** None.

• **Medications**: None.

**• Family history:** No growth disorders or nutritional deficiencies.

**Key Questions You Might Ask the Doctor:**

Parent

• “Is my child’s growth on track for their age?”

• “What could be causing the fatigue?”

• “Does she need tests?”

• “Are there any supplements or dietary changes you would recommend?”

• “Could their fatigue be related to nutrition or thyroid issues?”

Child

• “Will I need to get my blood drawn?”

• “I don’t really like meat. Is there anything else I could try?”

• “I used to love playing soccer with my friends but now I just don’t have the energy. What is happening to me?”
