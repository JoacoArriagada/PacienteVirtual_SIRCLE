Case Title: *Fatigue in Deaf Child Accompanied by Parent*

Case Authors: Shannon Zhou, BS; Morgan McBride, BS; Hope Pogemiller, MD, MPH

Standardized Patient Educator: Shannon Zhou, BS

Date: 03/06/2025

Name of Educational Activity: Clinical Skills Simulation: Communication and Differential Diagnosis

Chief Complaint: “I’m worried about my 12-year old child’s growth and nutrition, and they seem tired”

Main Question: How do you effectively collect a clinical history using a Video Relay Interpreter (VRI) on an iPad, while ensuring appropriate questions are directed to the Deaf child rather than their Hard-of-Hearing parent?

Case Objectives:

1. Effectively use an VRI to complete the patient visit; be cognizant of timing and placement of interpreter
2. Ask questions to the parent as well as the child to obtain a complete history
3. Create a differential regarding possible etiologies of fatigue and behavior change in a 12-year-old

Type and Level of Learner: Third and Fourth-year medical students

Ideal Scenario Flow

The learners enter the outpatient clinic and recognize that the encounter involves a Deaf child, their parent, and a virtual ASL interpreter. The VRI is already up on the iPad when students enter. Students position the device so that the child has a clear view of both the interpreter and the learners. Learners introduce themselves directly to the patient and parent, maintaining appropriate eye contact and pacing throughout the visit. They communicate in short, clear sentences, pausing for interpretation, and use plain language, drawings, or visual aids as needed. Learners balance communication between both parties, directing questions primarily to the child but inviting the parent’s input for additional context about fatigue, diet, and activity changes. As the child and parent describe tiredness, decreased activity, and pale appearance, learners ask focused questions to explore possible causes of fatigue, including nutrition, sleep, school stress, and recent illness, and begin forming a differential diagnosis that includes anemia, thyroid disease, and mood changes. Before concluding, the learner summarizes findings in plain language, explains the plan for lab testing, and answers any further questions.

Anticipated Management Mistakes

1. Remote Interpreter Disconnect: During one of the sessions, the remote interpreting technology malfunctioned, causing communication barriers. As a result, students defaulted to directing most questions to the Hard-of-Hearing parent rather than the Deaf child, limiting the child’s participation in their own visit. This highlighted the importance of having a backup communication plan (e.g. backup zoom link or laptop).
2. Of note, the parent SP in our simulation was Hard-of-Hearing but communicated verbally. This scenario can be repeated with a parent SP who is hearing.
