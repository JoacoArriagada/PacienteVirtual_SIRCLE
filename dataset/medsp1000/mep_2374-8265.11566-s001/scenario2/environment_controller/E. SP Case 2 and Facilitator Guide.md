| CASE INFORMATION | |
| Chief Concern | “I take my diabetes seriously but want to be sure I’m doing everything right.” |
| Additional Concerns | “Are my medications still the best option?” (Seen ads for Ozempic). “Could my kids get diabetes?” “Why do my feet sometimes feel numb?” |
| THE PATIENT’S STORY: | “Five years ago, my doctor told me I had type 2 diabetes. At first, I was overwhelmed, with all these new rules about food, needles, and numbers. I learned to take my metformin and insulin every morning, though sometimes I forget when life gets busy. Lately, I’ve noticed my feet tingle or feel numb, especially at night. It scares me because my father lost his toes to diabetes. I try to walk daily and eat veggies, but carbs are my weakness. Those Ozempic ads make me wonder, am I doing enough? Could my kids end up like me?”  “Some days, diabetes feels like a shadow. I’ll be reading to my kids and suddenly I think will I be here in 20 years? The fatigue makes me snap at them sometimes, and then I feel guilty. My partner helps, but they don’t get the mental load, the counting, the worrying.”  “Meal planning takes hours. Birthday cakes feel like landmines. My kids ask why I prick my finger, and I don’t want them to fear food or their genes. That’s why I’m here today to learn how to protect my family.” |

| DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL | |
| Diagnosis with support from positive and negative history and PE findings | Type 2 diabetes with early neuropathy is supported by classic symptoms (bilateral foot tingling, fatigue), poor glycemic control (missed insulin, carb struggles), and family history. |
| Differential with support from positive and negative history and PE findings | The primary concern is diabetic peripheral neuropathy, supported by bilateral foot tingling and long standing type 2 diabetes. However, the absence of foot ulcers suggests this may be in early stages. Vitamin B12 deficiency, chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy, and lumbar radiculopathy are also potential differential. |
| MANAGEMENT OR DIAGNOSTIC PLAN | Reinforce medication adherence (e.g. set phone reminders for insulin). Refer to nutritionists for portion control strategies. Add strength training 2x/week. Monitor neuropathy (annual foot exams). |
