| Overall course | The patient presents to an outpatient clinic for contraceptive counseling, expressing interest in reliable birth control due to the possibility of becoming sexually active in the near future. While she is not currently sexually active, she is planning ahead and wants to understand her options. She also shares that her menstrual periods have always been heavy and last around seven days, which she finds frustrating and inconvenient. She would prefer a method that does not require daily attention and is concerned about IUD insertion pain and the possibility of weight gain with hormonal methods.  Her pain is moderate, crampy, and occurs during menstruation but is manageable with over-the-counter medications and does not significantly impair her function. She shares that someone once told her she might have fibroids, but she has never received a formal diagnosis or treatment and is not currently seeking evaluation for it.  The focus of the encounter is on shared decision-making and contraceptive education, particularly navigating barriers to access and using adaptive communication techniques (e.g., tactile models, DeafBlind Clear Speech Interpreters) to support an informed and autonomous patient choice. |

| DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL | |
| Diagnosis with support from positive and negative history and PE findings | Encounter for birth control counseling  Stable, chronic, regular heavy bleeding with menses   * Supported by review of symptoms report that bleeding is regular and cyclical with periods * Patient is not soaking through a pad every 1-2 hours during the heaviest flow of her period, which occurs during the first two days rather than throughout the period. * Cramping does not prevent her from activities of daily living and is well-controlled with over-the-counter medications. |
| Differential with support from positive and negative history and PE findings | Note: The focus of this simulation is on communication with a DeafBlind patient with the assistance of ClearSpeech Interpreters, not necessarily differential diagnosis.  Differential includes:   * Fibroids, given family history and heavy periods with pain. Less likely without reported pelvic pressure or irregular bleeding. * Endometriosis, given cyclical pelvic cramping and heavy periods. May be less likely in the absence of intermenstrual pelvic pain. * Adenomyosis, heavy menstrual bleeding and crampy pelvic pain. Less likely without progressively worsening dysmenorrhea. * Pelvic inflammatory disease: unlikely without being sexually active and regular monthly pelvic pain. |
| MANAGEMENT OR DIAGNOSTIC PLAN | The patient is seeking long-acting reversible contraception and does not plan to have children in the near future. She prefers a method that does not require daily or somewhat frequent attention, ruling out options like the birth control pill, ring, and patch. She is open to hormonal methods but is concerned about potential weight gain, which makes the Depo-Provera injection less appealing.  Given her history of heavy menstrual bleeding, the copper IUD is not an appropriate option due to the risk of worsening flow and cramping. While hormonal IUDs are an acceptable option in theory, the patient expresses significant anxiety about insertion pain and would prefer to avoid intrauterine devices.  After a shared decision-making conversation, the etonogestrel implant (Nexplanon) emerges as the preferred option. It aligns with her desire for a low-maintenance, long-acting method, has the potential to reduce menstrual bleeding, and is not commonly associated with weight gain. Additionally, Nexplanon offers a quick and simple in-office insertion without requiring instrumentation of the uterus, addressing her concerns about pain and invasiveness. |
