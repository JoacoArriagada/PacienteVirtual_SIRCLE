**Simulated Patient (SP) Training: Cases Information Provided to SPs**

# Case 1: Appendicitis – No interpreter initially

You are a Deaf patient with severe abdominal pain at the emergency department. You feel anxious because you are in pain and the interpreter is unavailable, preventing the immediate communication of symptoms to the doctor. The student doctor must communicate, diagnose, and explain a plan using creative strategies, with the intent of confirming and clarifying the plan once an interpreter arrives.

**Simulation Setup:**

• An ASL interpreter arrives about 5 minutes into the scenario.

**Instructions for the Patient Actor**

**Patient Background:**

• **Name**: You may use your own name or create one

• **Age**: 30

• **Living Situation:** Living in a city that makes sense for your background.

**Your Condition:**

• **Pain:** Started in the mid/upper belly yesterday, now moved to lower right belly, sharp and stabbing.

• **Quality:** Sharp, stabbing pain in the lower right belly.

• **Severity:** 9/10, worst you’ve ever had.

• **Timing:** Constant, worsening since yesterday.

• **Worse with:** Movement, pressing, coughing.

• **Relieved by:** Nothing (lying still helps a little).

• **Eating & Sleep:** Poor due to pain

• **Other symptoms:** Nausea, no appetite, mild fever (checked with thermometer earlier).

• **Concerns:** Unsure what’s wrong, scared about surgery and anesthesia, curious about other treatment options.

At some point during the encounter, the medical student should indicate that you may have appendicitis. You are not sure what this means, but you understand that it might require surgery. If surgery is mentioned, you express fear and uncertainty. You want to know:

• Is there any other treatment besides surgery?

• What will happen if I wait instead of having surgery?

**How to Act While in the Absence of an Interpreter:**

• Use facial expressions, gestures, and pointing.

• Write short phrases if paper or phone are offered (e.g., “pain bad” instead of full sentences).

• React to explanations (look confused, scared, or relieved).

• Show frustration if the doctor doesn’t make an effort but engage if they try.

**How to Act During the Simulation:**

• **Pain:** Hold stomach, grimace, flinch when moving.

• **Trust:** If the student makes an effort, nod and show appreciation.

• **Frustration:** You are cooperative but in severe pain, which makes it difficult to communicate. If the student makes a real effort to communicate (e.g., using drawings, writing things down clearly) you begin to trust them and engage more.

**• Physical exam:**

* When the student presses on your lower right abdomen, tense your abdominal muscles in pain.
* If a student applies lower right abdominal pressure and releases quickly, this causes pain.
* If a student places pressure on the left lower abdomen, you point to pain in the right lower abdomen.
* If a student extends your right leg back at the hip, this causes pain.
* If a student internally rotates your right hip, this causes pain.

**Health Information**

• You have no other medical conditions or past surgeries.

• You are not taking any medications currently and have not tried anything for the pain.

**Key Questions You Might Ask the Doctor:**

• “I’m terrified of hospitals, what if I need surgery? I don’t know how anesthesia works for Deaf people like me. Will I wake up? Can they explain things clearly after? I feel alone and frustrated.”

• “Is surgery the only option? Waiting could make it worse, but I don’t know what to do.”

• “Am I going to die?”

• “Can I drink water?”

• “How long will I need to stay in the hospital?”
