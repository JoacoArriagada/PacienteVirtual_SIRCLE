* 1. **Props:**

1. **Actors:** Obstetrician
   1. **Who may play them:** other residents, other students, or simulation technician
   2. **Action Role:** The obstetrician will dissuade the learner from using anesthesia by stating “the patient won’t feel anything, it’s not necessary, etc.” If the learner still desires to induce the patient, or if the learner is uncertain of the obstetrician’s suggestion to continue without anesthesia, the obstetrician will state “if it will make you feel better, I can give them a block.” The block, however, will prove insufficient for prophylaxis against autonomic hyperreflexia.

2. **Case Narrative (VERSION A)**
   1. **General Synopsis:** You are an anesthesiologist in a private practice at a small community hospital taking in-house call. The obstetrician calls you for consultation of a patient needing a dilation and curettage (D&C). The patient, Sarah Miller, is a 20 year old female who had an unplanned pregnancy. However, she had a spontaneous incomplete miscarriage that started two weeks ago. This had been managed expectantly, but now she is presenting with continued spotting. The obstetrician tells you that a D&C needs to be done urgently because of the increased infection risk with the retained tissue in the uterus for this length of time.
   2. **Past Medical History:** Allergic rhinitis, mild asthma associated with seasonal allergies with no recent exacerbations, mild scoliosis, and paraplegia after mountain biking injury two years ago with no sensation just below her breasts.
   3. **Social History:** The patient lives alone in a dormitory at the local college, the expectant father is not present.
   4. **Allergies:** The patient has no known drug allergies.
   5. **Medications:** baclofen (10 mg, twice daily), Colace (100mg, daily), cetirizine (5mg, daily), albuterol (as needed), and fluticasone nasal spray (as needed).
