**VERSION A: Start of Scenario (Dilation & Curettage for a Patient with Retained Products of Conception)**

Phase I:  Baseline

Patient awake, in L&D OR suite with monitors on (EKG, NIBP, SpO2).  HR 75, NSR  BP 110/62  SpO2 98% on room air

Learner may assess patient, including brief assessment of airway, cardiovascular, pulmonary, and neurologic systems.  Learner may decide to let OB proceed without anesthesia present.  Learner may decide to proceed with MAC.  Learner may decide to proceed with spinal or GA.  Learner may decide to have OB/GYN perform a block (+/- MAC).

**Operator:**  Begin scenario.

**Trigger:**  Learner communicates decision to OB/GYN regarding whether or not he/she will assume anesthetic care for the patient.  If learner decides to let OB proceed without anesthesia present (+/- block), go to Table A.  If learner decides to proceed with MAC (+/- block), go to Table B.  If learner decides to proceed with spinal or GA, go to Table C.

**VERSION A: Table A – Learner decides to let OB proceed without anesthesia present (+/- block)**

Phase II:  HTN symptomatic

HR 48, sinus bradycardia  BP 200/105 (being checked q 5 min)  RR 20  SpO2 98% on room air  Flushing/sweating of face/neck/upper chest

Learner may instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure (stop offending stimulus).  Learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently (q 1-3 min).

**Operator:**  After D&C is started, RN calls learner back to L&D OR suite as patient is hypertensive and complaining of a headache.  Patient is complaining of stuffy nose and headache.

**Trigger:**  Patient gets progressively more bradycardic & hypertensive (HR to 43, BP to 210/110).

Phase III:  HTN worsening

HR 43, sinus bradycardia  BP 210/110  RR 22  SpO2 98%

If not already done, learner may instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure.  If not already done, learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently.  Learner may sit patient up.  Learner may identify any other precipitating factors (i.e., insert Foley catheter to drain possibly distended bladder, loosen any tight clothing or constrictive devices).  Learner may decide to pharmacologically treat HTN.  Nitroprusside.  Nitroglycerin.  Labetalol.  Esmolol.  Hydralazine.  Propofol.

**Operator:**  Patient complains of nausea and headache.

**Trigger:**  If after 5 min, learner does not instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure (regardless of whether pharmacologic treatment of HTN is initiated): Proceed to phase IV.  If learner instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure: HR 50, BP 190/95, RR 20, SpO2 98%. Transient improvement lasts for another 2 BP cuff cycles but will proceed to phase IV if learner does not initiate appropriate pharmacologic treatment of HTN (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin).  If learner correctly instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin): Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.

Phase IV:  Cardiorespiratory complications

HR 39, sinus bradycardia, occasional PVC’s  BP 220/115  RR 30  SpO2 94%

Learner may pharmacologically treat HTN.  Learner may place supplemental O2 on patient.

**Operator:**  Patient complains of difficulty breathing and blurry vision.  If learner auscultates the chest, state that lung sounds are clear bilaterally.

**Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically: Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.  If after 5 more minutes, learner does not identify/abate any precipitating factors and/or does not pharmacologically treat HTN: end scenario.

**VERSION A: Table B – Learner decides to proceed with MAC (+/- block)**

Phase II:  Start of MAC

HR 75, NSR  BP 110/62 (being checked q 5 min)  RR 20  SpO2 98% on room air

Learner may provide sedation for patient.

**Operator:**  Patient expresses how nervous she is and how she’s okay with sedation but doesn’t want to remember/hear events of procedure.

**Trigger:**  Patient is determined to be at an adequate depth of sedation by learner, and OB/GYN proceeds with D&C.  After a few minutes, the patient becomes slightly bradycardic and hypertensive (HR to 59, BP to 150/90).

Phase III:  HTN symptomatic

HR 59, sinus bradycardia  BP 150/90  RR 18  SpO2 100%  Flushing/sweating of face/neck/upper chest

Learner may instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure (stop offending stimulus).  Learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently (q 1-3 min).

**Operator:**  Patient complains of headache.

**Trigger:**  Patient gets progressively more bradycardic and hypertensive (HR to 45, BP to 200/95).

Phase IV:  HTN worsening

HR 45, sinus bradycardia  BP 200/95  RR 18  SpO2 99%

If not already done, learner may instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure.  If not already done, learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently.  Learner may identify any other precipitating factors (i.e., insert Foley catheter to drain possibly distended bladder, loosen any tight clothing or constrictive devices).  Learner may pharmacologically treat HTN.  Nitroprusside.  Nitroglycerin.  Labetalol.  Esmolol.  Hydralazine.  Propofol.  Learner may induce general anesthesia.

**Operator:**  Patient still complains of headache and now complains of nausea.

**Trigger:**  If after 5 min, learner does not instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure (regardless of whether pharmacologic treatment of HTN is initiated): Proceed to phase V.  If learner instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure: HR 50, BP 185/90, RR 20, SpO2 98%. Transient improvement lasts for another 2 BP cuff cycles, but then proceed to phase V if learner does not initiate appropriate pharmacologic treatment of HTN (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin).  If learner correctly instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin): Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.  If learner decides to proceed with GA, then GA is induced, airway is secured, D&C starts. Continue to phase III of Table C.

Phase V:  Cardiorespiratory complications

HR 39, sinus bradycardia, occasional PVC’s  BP 220/115  RR 30  SpO2 94%

Learner may pharmacologically treat the HTN.  Learner may increase supplemental O2.

**Operator:**  If learner auscultates the chest to listen to breath sounds, state that lung sounds are clear bilaterally.

**Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically: Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.  If after 5 more minutes, learner does not identify/abate any precipitating factors and/or does not appropriately pharmacologically treat HTN: end scenario.

**VERSION A: Table C – Learner decides to proceed with spinal or GA**

Phase II:  Spinal not feasible

HR 75, NSR  BP 110/62 (being checked q 5 min)  RR 20  SpO2 98% on room air

Learner may choose either spinal or GA.

**Operator:**  If learner decides to proceed with spinal, RN will remind learner that the platelets are low.  If learner decides to proceed with spinal despite the low platelet count, the patient will refuse a spinal.

**Trigger:**  If learner decides to proceed with “heavy” MAC, go to phase II of Table B.  If learner decides to proceed with GA, then GA is induced, airway is secured, and D&C starts. Continue to phase III.

Phase III:  HTN under GA

HR 59, sinus bradycardia  BP 150/90  SpO2 100%

Learner may instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure (stop offending stimulus).  Learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently (q 1-3 min).  Learner may deepen GA.

**Trigger:**  Patient gets more bradycardic and hypertensive (HR to 45, BP to 200/95).

Phase IV:  HTN worsening

HR 45, sinus bradycardia  BP 200/95  SpO2 99% on supplemental O2  Flushing/sweating of face and upper neck/chest

If not already done, learner may instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure.  If not already done, learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently.  Learner may identify any other precipitating factors (i.e., insert Foley catheter to drain possibly distended bladder, loosen any tight clothing or constrictive devices).  Learner may deepen GA.  Learner may pharmacologically treat HTN.  Nitroprusside.  Nitroglycerin.  Labetalol.  Esmolol.  Hydralazine.  Propofol.

**Trigger:**  If after 5 min, learner does not instruct OB/GYN to stop procedure (regardless of depth of GA or whether appropriate pharmacologic treatment of HTN is initiated): Proceed to phase V.  If learner instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure: HR 50, BP 185/90, RR 20, SpO2 98%. Transient improvement lasts for another 2 BP cuff cycles, but then proceed to phase V if learner does not initiate appropriate pharmacologic treatment of HTN (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin).  If learner correctly instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin): Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.

Phase V:  Cardiorespiratory complications

HR 39, sinus bradycardia, occasional PVC’s  BP 220/115  SpO2 94%

Learner may pharmacologically treat the HTN.  Learner may increase FiO2.

**Operator:**  If learner auscultates the chest to listen to breath sounds, state that lung sounds are clear bilaterally.

**Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs OB/GYN to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically: Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.  If after 5 more minutes, learner does not identify/abate any precipitating factors and/or does not appropriately pharmacologically treat HTN: end scenario.
