**VERSION B: Start of Scenario (Open Reduction and Internal Fixation for a Patient with Foot/Ankle Fracture)**

Phase I:  Baseline

Patient awake, at OR front desk on transport cart and transport monitors.  HR 65, NSR  BP 145/92  SpO2 98% on room air

Learner may assess patient, including brief assessment of airway, cardiovascular, pulmonary, and neurologic systems.  Learner may decide to proceed with MAC +/- regional or neuraxial anesthesia.  Learner may decide to proceed with GA.

**Operator:**  Begin scenario.

**Trigger:**  Learner communicates anesthetic plan with patient.  If learner decides to proceed with MAC +/- regional or neuraxial anesthesia, go to Table A.  If learner decides to proceed with GA, go to Table B.

**VERSION B: Table A – Learner decides to proceed with MAC +/- regional or neuraxial anesthesia**

Phase II:  Start of MAC

HR 65, NSR  BP 145/92  RR 20  SpO2 98% on room air

Learner may provide sedation for patient.  Learner may decide to proceed with GA if patient refuses offered regional or neuraxial anesthesia.

**Operator:**  Patient expresses how nervous he is and how he’s okay with sedation but doesn’t want to remember/hear anything.  If learner decides to proceed with regional or neuraxial anesthesia, patient will refuse.

**Trigger:**  If learner decides to proceed with MAC without regional or neuraxial anesthesia, the surgeon will proceed with the surgery once the patient is determined to be at an adequate depth of sedation by learner. After a few minutes, the patient becomes slightly bradycardic and hypertensive (HR to 59, BP to 150/90).  If learner decides to proceed with GA, go to Table B.

Phase III:  HTN symptomatic

HR 59, sinus bradycardia  BP 150/90  RR 18  SpO2 100%  Flushing/sweating of face/neck/upper chest

Learner may instruct surgeon to stop procedure (stop offending stimulus).  Learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently (q 1-3 min).

**Operator:**  Patient complains of headache.

**Trigger:**  Patient gets progressively more bradycardic and hypertensive (HR to 45, BP to 200/95).

Phase IV:  HTN worsening

HR 45, sinus bradycardia  BP 200/95  RR 18  SpO2 99%

If not already done, learner may instruct surgeon to stop procedure.  If not already done, learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently.  Learner may identify any other precipitating factors (i.e., insert Foley catheter to drain possibly distended bladder, loosen any tight clothing or constrictive devices).  Learner may pharmacologically treat HTN.  Nitroprusside.  Nitroglycerin.  Labetalol.  Esmolol.  Hydralazine.  Propofol.  Learner may induce general anesthesia.

**Operator:**  Patient still complains of headache and now complains of nausea.

**Trigger:**  If after 5 min, learner does not instruct surgeon to stop procedure (regardless of whether pharmacologic treatment of HTN is initiated): Proceed to phase V.  If learner instructs surgeon to stop procedure: HR 50, BP 185/90, RR 20, SpO2 98%. Transient improvement lasts for another 2 BP cuff cycles, but then proceed to phase V if learner does not initiate appropriate pharmacologic treatment of HTN (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin).  If learner correctly instructs surgeon to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin): Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.  If learner decides to proceed with GA, then GA is induced, airway is secured, surgery starts. Continue to phase III of Table B.

Phase V:  Cardiorespiratory  complications

HR 39, sinus bradycardia, occasional PVC’s  BP 220/115  RR 30  SpO2 94%

Learner may pharmacologically treat the HTN.  Learner may increase supplemental O2.

**Operator:**  If learner auscultates the chest to listen to breath sounds, state that lung sounds are clear bilaterally.

**Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs surgeon to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically: Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.  If after 5 more minutes, learner does not identify/abate any precipitating factors and/or does not appropriately pharmacologically treat HTN: end scenario.

**VERSION B: Table B – Learner decides to proceed with GA**

Phase II:  Regional/neuraxial anesthesia not feasible or not chosen

HR 65, NSR  BP 145/92  RR 20  SpO2 98% on room air

Learner induces GA and secures airway.

**Trigger:**  If learner decides to proceed with GA, then GA is induced, airway is secured, and surgery starts. Continue to phase III.

Phase III:  HTN under GA

HR 59, sinus bradycardia  BP 150/90  SpO2 98%

Learner may instruct surgeon to stop procedure (stop offending stimulus).  Learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently (q 1-3 min).  Learner may deepen GA.

**Trigger:**  Patient gets more bradycardic and hypertensive (HR to 45, BP to 200/95).

Phase IV:  HTN worsening

HR 45, sinus bradycardia  BP 200/95  SpO2 99% on supplemental O2  Flushing/sweating of face and upper neck/chest

If not already done, learner may instruct surgeon to stop procedure.  If not already done, learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently.  Learner may identify any other precipitating factors (i.e., insert Foley catheter to drain possibly distended bladder, loosen any tight clothing or constrictive devices).  Learner may deepen GA.  Learner may pharmacologically treat HTN.  Nitroprusside.  Nitroglycerin.  Labetalol.  Esmolol.  Hydralazine.  Propofol.

**Trigger:**  If after 5 min, learner does not instruct surgeon to stop procedure (regardless of depth of GA or whether appropriate pharmacologic treatment of HTN is initiated): Proceed to phase V.  If learner instructs surgeon to stop procedure: HR 50, BP 185/90, RR 20, SpO2 98%. Transient improvement lasts for another 2 BP cuff cycles, but then proceed to phase V if learner does not initiate appropriate pharmacologic treatment of HTN (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin).  If learner correctly instructs surgeon to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin): Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.

Phase V:  Cardiorespiratory complications

HR 39, sinus bradycardia, occasional PVC’s  BP 220/115  SpO2 94%

Learner may pharmacologically treat the HTN.  Learner may increase FiO2.

**Operator:**  If learner auscultates the chest to listen to breath sounds, state that lung sounds are clear bilaterally.

**Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs surgeon to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically: Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes.  If after 5 more minutes, learner does not identify/abate any precipitating factors and/or does not appropriately pharmacologically treat HTN: end scenario.
