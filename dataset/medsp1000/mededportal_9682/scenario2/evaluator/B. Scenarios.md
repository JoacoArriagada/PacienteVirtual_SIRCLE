1. **Instructional Notes:**
   1. **Facilitator Background & Briefing Information:** Autonomic hyperreflexia is characterized by acute generalized sympathetic hyperreactivity in response to a triggering stimulus that occurs in patients with spinal cord injury. The patients at risk of this condition are those patients whose spinal cord injury is above the level of the sympathetic output (aka splanchnic outflow from T5 to L2), classically described at T6. While autonomic hyperreflexia is a syndrome that an anesthesiologist is not likely to encounter often, it is a syndrome of extreme hemodynamic changes (i.e., bradycardia, hypertension) that must be addressed in a swift manner. In the event of an episode of autonomic hyperreflexia, it is imperative that the anesthesiologist must be able to recognize that such presenting hemodynamic changes are likely due to a stimulus below the level of the spinal cord injury. Failure to identify the underlying etiology of an autonomic hyperreflexia episode will lead to failure of successful treatment; that is, pharmacologic treatment of a patient’s severe hypertension during such an episode is unlikely to be successful if the precipitating stimulus is not identified and abated.
   3. **Goal Synopsis for Learner: (VERSION B)**
      * Identify the underlying etiology on an AH episode
      * Communicate with the surgeon to stop the procedure
      * Treat presenting hypertension and bradycardia
