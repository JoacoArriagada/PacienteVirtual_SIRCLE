| Phase I:  Baseline | Patient awake, at OR front desk on transport cart and transport monitors.  HR 65, NSR  BP 145/92  SpO2 98% on room air | **Learner Actions:**  Learner may assess patient, including brief assessment of airway, cardiovascular, pulmonary, and neurologic systems.  Learner may decide to proceed with MAC +/- regional or neuraxial anesthesia.  Learner may decide to proceed with GA.  **Teaching Points:**  Be compassionate as the patient is likely having bad memories of prior MVC/OR experience.  Just because a patient has no sensation to the area of concern (i.e., the foot/ankle in this case), this does not always negate the need for anesthesia care.  The most effective prevention of autonomic hyperreflexia in an at-risk patient (besides avoiding precipitating factors) is spinal or GA (not just MAC). Epidural blockade is less reliable. A regional anesthetic may be appropriate as long as all potentially affected areas are blocked. | **Operator:**  Begin scenario.  **Trigger:**  Learner communicates anesthetic plan with patient.  If learner decides to proceed with MAC +/- regional or neuraxial anesthesia, go to Table A.  If learner decides to proceed with GA, go to Table B. |

| **State** | **Patient Status** | **Student learning outcomes or actions desired and trigger to move to next state** | |
| Phase II:  Start of MAC | HR 65, NSR  BP 145/92  RR 20  SpO2 98% on room air | **Learner Actions:**  Learner may provide sedation for patient.  Learner may decide to proceed with GA if patient refuses offered regional or neuraxial anesthesia.  **Teaching Points:**  The most effective prevention of autonomic hyperreflexia in an at-risk patient (besides avoiding precipitating factors) is spinal or GA. Epidural blockade is less reliable. | **Operator:**  Patient expresses how nervous he is and how he’s okay with sedation but doesn’t want to remember/hear anything.  If learner decides to proceed with regional or neuraxial anesthesia, patient will refuse.  **Trigger:**  If learner decides to proceed with MAC without regional or neuraxial anesthesia, the surgeon will proceed with the surgery once the patient is determined to be at an adequate depth of sedation by learner. After a few minutes, the patient becomes slightly bradycardic and hypertensive (HR  to 59, BP  to 150/90).  If learner decides to proceed with GA, go to Table B. |
| Phase III:  HTN symptomatic | HR 59, sinus bradycardia  BP 150/90  RR 18  SpO2 100%  Flushing/sweating of face/neck/upper chest | **Learner Actions:**  Learner may instruct surgeon to stop procedure (stop offending stimulus).  Learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently (q 1-3 min).  **Teaching Points:**  Precipitants of autonomic hyperreflexia  Clinical signs/symptoms of autonomic hyperreflexia  Frequent monitoring of BP is needed for patient with symptomatic hypertension. | **Operator:**  Patient complains of headache.  **Trigger:**  Patient gets progressively more bradycardic and hypertensive (HR  to 45, BP  to 200/95). |
| Phase IV:  HTN worsening | HR 45, sinus bradycardia  BP 200/95  RR 18  SpO2 99% | **Learner Actions:**  If not already done, learner may instruct surgeon to stop procedure.  If not already done, learner may cycle BP cuff more frequently.  Learner may identify any other precipitating factors (i.e., insert Foley catheter to drain possibly distended bladder, loosen any tight clothing or constrictive devices).  Learner may pharmacologically treat HTN (recognizing symptomatic SBP ≥ 150 mmHg requires pharmacologic treatment).  Nitroprusside (onset of action 30 seconds, appropriate treatment)  Nitroglycerin (onset of action 30 seconds, appropriate treatment)  Labetalol (onset of action 5 minutes, not appropriate as beta-blocker contraindicated in bradycardia)  Esmolol (onset of action 1-2 minutes, not appropriate as beta-blocker contraindicated in bradycardia)  Hydralazine (onset of action 15 minutes, not appropriate as delayed onset of action)  Propofol (onset of action 30 seconds if bolus given, 2 minutes if infusion started/increased, may help with BP if heavy MAC or GA doses)  Patient is sedated, so sitting patient up may not be the best option (while this usually would be option in a non-sedated patient as it would provide orthostatic BP drop).  Learner may induce general anesthesia (recognizing that autonomic hyperreflexia is less likely to get worse under general anesthesia).  **Teaching Points:**  Management of autonomic hyperreflexia | **Operator:**  Patient still complains of headache and now complains of nausea.  **Trigger:**  If after 5 min, learner does not instruct surgeon to stop procedure (regardless of whether pharmacologic treatment of HTN is initiated): Proceed to phase V (where patient complains of difficulty breathing, blurry vision, HR  to 39, BP  to 220/115).  If learner instructs surgeon to stop procedure: HR 50, BP 185/90, RR 20, SpO2 98%. Transient improvement lasts for another 2 BP cuff cycles, but then proceed to phase V (where patient complains of difficulty breathing, blurry vision, HR  to 39, BP  to 220/115) if learner does not initiate appropriate pharmacologic treatment of HTN (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin).  If learner correctly instructs surgeon to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically (i.e., nitroprusside, nitroglycerin): Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes → *END (Debrief)*  If learner decides to proceed with GA, then GA is induced, airway is secured, surgery starts. Continue to phase III of Table B. |
| Phase V:  Cardiorespiratory  complications | HR 39, sinus bradycardia, occasional PVC’s  BP 220/115  RR 30  SpO2 94% | **Learner Actions:**  Learner may pharmacologically treat the HTN. If not, prompt learner with what they think of the BP and how to treat it.  Learner may increase supplemental O2.  **Teaching Points:**  Patient is still symptomatic with significant HTN requiring pharmacologic treatment. | **Operator:**  If learner auscultates the chest to listen to breath sounds, state that lung sounds are clear bilaterally.  **Trigger:**  If learner correctly instructs surgeon to stop procedure and also appropriately treats HTN pharmacologically: Patient’s symptoms gradually improve and vitals return to baseline over next 5 minutes → *END (Debrief)*  If after 5 more minutes, learner does not identify/abate any precipitating factors and/or does not appropriately pharmacologically treat HTN → *END (Debrief)* |
