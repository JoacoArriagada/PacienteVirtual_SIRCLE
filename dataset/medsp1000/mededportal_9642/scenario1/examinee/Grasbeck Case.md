# Clinical case presentation

# Megaloblastic Anemia + E. coli-induced diarrhea = trouble

# 1. Material given to students

# 1.1. Task

You are the physician on duty at the Campus Health Clinic. You will be presented with a patient, whom you have to examine.

# 1.2. Patient presentation

Jane Gutterand, a 5-year-old Caucasian girl, is brought to the campus clinic three days before the first school day of the on-campus day school. For the past 3 days she has been affected by non-bloody, watery diarrhea with the passage of 4-6 stools a day. She complained also of abdominal cramps, mild fever, and feeling faint upon standing. She is accompanied by her mother, who is an incoming first term student. Jane’s family came from Pennsylvania 2 weeks ago.

Prior to the families move to the University, Jane’s grandmother cared for her at home. Jane had neither attended day care nor preschool. Since her mother did not have health insurance, Jane had not received regular medical care other than routine vaccinations. Her mother says that Jane had frequent respiratory infections but that this was probably due to the older home in which they lived. In other respects her mother feels that Jane has been doing well. Her mother is concerned that if Jane does not recover soon, she won’t be able to attend the first day of school, which she is very excited about.
