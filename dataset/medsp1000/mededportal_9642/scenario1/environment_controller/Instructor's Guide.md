# Instructor Guide

# Megaloblastic Anemia + E. coli-induced diarrhea = trouble

This is an active learning activity presented to 2nd semester medical students. The main file contains:

Case description A child presented because of traveler’s diarrhea, but also having signs of an underlying chronic anemia. The job of the students is to order the tests that would help them make the correct diagnosis. They have a restricted budget (represented by tokens) to buy test results with.

lab tokens 1 sheet per group needs to be printed. These tokens are used by the students to pay for lab results. The number of available tokens should be sufficient to pay for all necessary results, and to allow students to side-track once without being unable to finish the exercise. However, it needs to be so restrictive that students cannot simply order all tests on the menu. Instructors may wish to adapt the design of the tokens to the needs of their school.

results of physical exams given out to students after payment with exam tokens lab results given out to students after payment with the required number of lab tokens.

growth chart for girls, as published by the CDC. Students should practice entering data into the growth chart and interpret the result.

The exam and lab data should be printed duplex (two pages on either side of a sheet of paper), cut as indicated and then folded in such a way that the title is readable, but the results are not. Paper cups were found convenient receptacles for them, these can be closed by folding after class for later evaluation of which tests were most popular. There need to be enough people (faculty or teaching assistants) available to distribute the results to students quickly, one per 3-4 tests.

The tokens are numbered, we ask students to spend them in numerical order. The spend tokens are placed into the same paper cups as the test results they bought so we can follow students thoughts afterwards. This is not a perfect solution, but it works surprisingly well.

# 1 Time management

Students need $5 { \mathrm { - } } 1 0 \operatorname* { m i n }$ to read the case and discuss it within their groups. This is followed by $1 5 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ during which they can buy physical exam results, either all together or one-by-one (their choice). After another $5 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ deliberation time the lab opens for $2 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ to sell test results. During that time most students figure out what the diagnosis is, but another 5 min of group deliberation may be given if necessary. This is followed by a discussion of the case, which is best led by a clinician. The key point here is how one can go from a clinical suspicion to a confirmed diagnosis. Time for “war stories”, however, should be strictly limited.

The entire exercise is designed to simulate the time pressures students experience in exams and physicians during their work.

Two hours are normally required to do a meaningful presentation of such a tokenbased case and to ask some 5–6 multiple choice questions relating to it. When too little time (only one hour) is available, outcomes and students satisfaction drop (see below).
