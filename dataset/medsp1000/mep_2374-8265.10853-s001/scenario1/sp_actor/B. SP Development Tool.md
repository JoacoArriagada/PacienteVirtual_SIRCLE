| Chief Concern: What the family member will say when greeted by the student. | The chief concern of the family member is that their family member just experienced a first-time seizure at home. |
| Context of Family Member involvement | The patient (simulated) presented to the emergency department by ambulance for a seizure at home. At the prespecified time in the simulation (while patient is seizing after physical exam was performed, see Appendix A for details), the Standardized Family Member enters, is very concerned and slightly intrusive, and repeatedly asks what is wrong in Spanish.  **The Standardized Family Member can only speak in Spanish and can only understand questions or information provided in Spanish***.* This can be done by the medical interpreter, or by the learner if they are able to fluently communicate in Spanish. If the communication does not occur in Spanish, the actor should respond “Disculpe, yo no hablo ingles” *(English translation: “Excuse me, I do not speak English”).* If the communication occurs in broken Spanish, respond “Disculpe, no te entiendo” *(English translation: “Excuse me, I do not understand you”)*, and continue to ask what is wrong in Spanish.  **The family member should not volunteer the patient’s recent obstetric history unless specifically asked (or if asked about recent hospitalizations).** |
| THE FAMILY MEMBER STORY / HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS FOR THE PATIENT: | My family member was at home and healthy when she complained of a headache and started to feel “funny”. She continued shaking for nearly 10 minutes and stopped after the paramedics gave her a shot. They then put her in the ambulance and drove away. |
