| Brief narrative description of case | *This is a case of an eclamptic seizure in a post-partum patient. The case is complicated by a language barrier, as the patient and her family member speak Spanish. The patient’s mental status remains depressed throughout the case, rendering it difficult to obtain a complete history. The case is designed to assess the learner’s ability to utilize a medical interpreter (or their own language skills if fluent in Spanish) to speak with a non-English speaking patient and family. The learner must also recognize and treat eclamptic seizures, as the patient’s post-partum status is not provided unless specifically asked for. As the learner enters the room, they receive EMS report, and the patient (mannequin) and nurse simulated participant are in the room. After the physical examination, the patient seizes again and the Spanish-speaking family member enters the room. The family member is concerned, intrusive and only speaking Spanish. The medical interpreter will enter the room to facilitate communication only if requested by the resident. If the resident recognizes eclamptic seizures and provides appropriate treatment with magnesium, the seizures will stop. Otherwise, the seizures will temporarily cease with anti-epileptic drugs but will reoccur after 1 minute and will then become continuous. This will necessitate intubation as the patient will become hypoxic. The case ends once the seizures are appropriately treated and the gynecologist has been consulted (by phone, voiced by the sim operator), or after 15 minutes elapse.* |
| Primary Learning Objectives | *By the end of this activity, learners will be able to:*   1. *Evaluate a learner’s ability to employ a medical interpreter in gathering a history.* 2. *Evaluate a learner’s ability to identify and manage postpartum eclampsia.* 3. *Evaluate a learner’s ability to communicate effectively with a family member and members of the healthcare team.* |
| Critical Actions | 1. *Requests interpreter OR speaks fluent Spanish* 2. *Utilizes interpreter appropriately (maintains eye contact and speaks directly with patient/family, uses lay terminology)* 3. *Checks airway, breathing, circulation* 4. *Examines patient, elicits appropriate history* 5. *Orders LFTs, fingerstick glucose, urinalysis* 6. *Inquires about allergies* 7. *Orders IV magnesium and appropriate antihypertensives* 8. *Treats nurse, consultant, family and patient with respect and provides care in a culturally appropriate manner* |

Ideal Scenario Flow

*This is a case of an eclamptic seizure in a post-partum patient. The case is complicated by a language barrier, as the patient and her husband speak Spanish. The patient’s mental status remains depressed throughout the case, rendering it difficult to obtain a complete history. The case is designed to assess the learner’s ability to utilize a medical interpreter (or their own language skills if fluent in Spanish) to speak with a non-English speaking patient and family. The learner must also recognize and treat eclamptic seizures, as the patient’s post-partum status is not provided unless specifically asked for. As the learner enters the room, they receive EMS report, and the patient (mannequin) and nurse simulated participant are in the room. After the physical examination, the patient seizes again and the Spanish-speaking family member enters the room. The family member is concerned, intrusive and only speaking Spanish. The medical interpreter will enter the room to facilitate communication only if requested by the resident. If the resident recognizes eclamptic seizures and provides appropriate treatment with magnesium, the seizures will stop. Otherwise, the seizures will temporarily cease with anti-epileptic drugs but will reoccur after 1 minute and will then become continuous. This will necessitate intubation as the patient will become hypoxic. The case ends once the seizures are appropriately treated and the gynecologist has been consulted (by phone, voiced by the sim operator), or after 15 minutes elapse.*

*When evaluating the learner’s performance, special attention should be placed on their communication skills with the family member. The learner should speak in fluent Spanish or utilize the medical interpreter to speak with the family member. If the interpreter is used, they should be utilized appropriately with the physician speaking directly to the patient*

Anticipated Management Mistakes

1. *Learner unaware of the availability of medical interpreter: We found on debriefing and feedback that some learners were unaware that they could request a medical interpreter. For this reason, we recommend including a note stating “A medical interpreter is available if needed” on the case briefing.*
2. *Failure to recognize eclampsia: Some of our learners did not recognize eclamptic seizures, typically because they failed to ask about the patient’s past medical history or obstetric history. The family member could volunteer the patient’s recent hospitalization after 5 minutes of history taking or nonrecognition of the diagnosis.*
