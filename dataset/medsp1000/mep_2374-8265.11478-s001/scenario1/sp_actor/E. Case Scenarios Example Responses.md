**Health Promotion and Disease Prevention: Health Behavior Counseling Case**

Case 1: M. is a 55-year-old female with diabetes, heart disease and clinical depression. During her medical visits, M. is typically quiet and passive. She is usually a compliant patient, but recently has had trouble with being able to afford all her medications. She sees multiple specialists and takes several prescription medications and complains of side effects such as diarrhea and headaches. M. has smoked cigarettes since she was 19. Her primary care physician has also encouraged her to set a smoking cessation goal. M. agrees with her doctor but struggles with quitting smoking.

Doctor: What brings you in today?

[after addressing patient’s health concern]

Doctor: Would you be all right with our discussing your smoking?

Patient: Yes, but I am tired of people preaching to me about quitting.

Doctor: Are you thinking of quitting smoking in the next 6 months?

Patient: No.

Doctor: How do you view your smoking?

Patient: It helps me de-stress. It is the one thing I do for myself. It gets me through my day.

Doctor: You enjoy smoking and rely on it.

Patient: Of course! That is what makes it hard to quit.

Doctor: Now imagine that you have quit smoking. How would your life be different?

Patient: I’d be less short of breath and able to play with my grandchildren.

Doctor: How else might quitting improve your life?

Patient: I’d also save money…

Doctor: Tell me more…

Patient: The money I would save could be used towards our family’s needs.

Doctor: What is a reasonable next step?

Patient: I will consider quitting in the next 6 months.
