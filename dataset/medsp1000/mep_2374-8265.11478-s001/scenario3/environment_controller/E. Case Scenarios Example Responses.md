**Health Promotion and Disease Prevention: Health Behavior Counseling Case**

Case 3: T. is a 48-year-old single mother of 3 school aged children. She has a history of heart disease and hyperlipidemia. Her primary care doctor and cardiologist have mentioned diet and exercise in addition to her medication regimen. T. mentions being too busy with work and virtually home schooling her children to be able to take care of her own health.

**5 As**

**Doctor:** Would it be all right if we discussed your exercise today?

**Patient:** Yes.

**Doctor:** When was the last time you exercised for 30 minutes straight?

**Patient:** It has been at least 10 years!

**Doctor:** What do you know about the link between physical inactivity and your blood pressure?

**Patient:** I guess it could raise my blood pressure?

**Doctor:** Would it be all right to share some information about exercise?

**Patient:** Yes.

**Doctor:** The American Heart Association recommends at least 150 minutes of exercise weekly. I’d set a goal to start with 5-10 minutes a day and slowly build up to 30 minutes a day. What do you think about that?

**Patient:** That is doable.

**Doctor:** What could help you start exercising? Strategies that have helped other patients include baby steps, keeping an exercise journal and telling friends about your goal for accountability.

**Patient:** I think I can try one of those.

**Doctor:** Let’s see you back in 3 months. Then we can see how I can further help with your exercise goal.

**FRAMES:**

**Doctor:** Would it be all right if we discussed your diet today?

**Patient:** Yes. I have been stress eating and am not deliberate about eating healthier.

**Doctor:** Do you see any connection between your diet and current levels of exercise and your test results

**Patient:** Yes, I suppose my diet and test results are related.

**Doctor:** Yes, there is a clear association between diet and heart disease.

**Doctor:** But the decision to change your diet is a decision only you can make.

**Doctor:** The American Heart Association recommends heart healthy diets such as the DASH diet and the Mediterranean diet. I’d recommend following one of those diets.

**Patient:** I want to change but don’t know how to stick with it.

**Doctor:** Here are some strategies that have helped my other patients: SMART goals, food diaries, online support groups, and support from others.

Patient: Those may help since I am extremely busy.

**Doctor:** You are motivated to lose weight but feel frustrated with your busy schedule. That is difficult!

**Patient:** Yes, it is hard, and I am not sure I can do it!

**Doctor:** Think back to a successful behavior change you made in the past. Can you share that with me?

**Patient:** I am drinking moderately these days—that was a big change I made.

**Doctor:** The fact that you successfully quit excessive drinking in the past makes me believe that you can accomplish this goal too.
