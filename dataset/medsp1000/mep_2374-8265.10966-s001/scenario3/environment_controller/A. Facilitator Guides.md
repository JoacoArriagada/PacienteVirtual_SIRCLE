# **COMMON CALLS: CHEST PAIN**

**CASE #3 (5-7 minutes):**

You are called about a patient with acute respiratory distress and excruciating chest pain. It seemed to have started suddenly according to the nurse, but the patient is too breathless to talk. You read your signout: “24 yo M with ESRD, came in because his dialysis graft stopped functioning. Going to receive dialysis tomorrow.”

Vitals always first: T 97, P 120, BP 92/70, O2 85% RA, RR 40. On exam, JVs distended to >20cm water. Trachea is shifted left. There are no breath sounds on the right chest wall, which is very tympanic to percussion.

In fact, a subclavian central line was just placed in preparation for dialysis.

The ECG shows sinus tachycardia and no other abnormalities.
