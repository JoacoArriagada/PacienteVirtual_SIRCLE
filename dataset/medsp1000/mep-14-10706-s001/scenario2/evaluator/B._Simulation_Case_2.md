**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

*The learners enter the room to find an agitated infant with low sPO2 and tachycardia. After completing a physical examination and obtaining an appropriate history, manual calming maneuvers are applied without effect. Hypoxemia continues, out of proportion to the known heart disease and the providers diagnose a Hypercyanotic spell. Supplemental oxygen is applied to maximize the pulmonary venous saturation but this has little effect on the systemic saturation. Knee to chest maneuvers and an IV fluid bolus is ordered with transient mild improvement, as these will increase venous return and augment more blood flow across the obstructing right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT). IV sedation is ordered to calm the patient and decrease the heart rate, both helping to augment more blood flow across the RVOT obstruction. IV phenylephrine will increase the systemic vascular resistance (SVR), increasing left to right shunting through the VSD and more pulmonary blood flow across the obstructing RVOT. IV beta blocker will decrease the heart rate and allow for more filling time and more blood volume through the obstructing RVOT, as well. The providers medically manage the patient while awaiting the surgical/catheterization teams to address the need for a more stable source of pulmonary blood flow.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. *Failure to diagnosis the Hypercyanotic spell: We found when using this case learners often forgot the history of prior dehydration and subsequent inciting agitation event (venipuncture), which together causes the Hypercyanotic spell. . They were falsely reassured by the low saturation given the diagnosis of TOF. The degree of concern and need for further intervention is marked by the degree of cyanosis.*
2. *Uncertainty about the physiology of the Hypercyanotic spell: There is increased obstruction across the RVOT leading to decreasing pulmonary blood flow. This in turn leads to increased right to left shunting across the VSD. The RVOT obstruction is made worse by tachycardia. It is often a viscous cycle-agitation, increased heart rate, hypoxemia, body increases heart rate to respond to low oxygen delivery and the obstruction worsens, and so on.*
3. *Failure to manipulate physiology to increase oxygen saturations. The systemic oxygen level will increase only as more blood flow is sent to the lungs. Oxygen will increase the pulmonary venous saturation but the underlying issue is getting blood flow out the RVOT and supplemental oxygen has little effect on this. The volume of blood filling the RV needs to be increased. This can be achieved two ways- by adding to the absolute volume returning to the RV (transiently with a knee to chest maneuver or more definitively by volume resuscitation) AND by slowing the heart rate and increasing the filling/diastolic time (sedation and/or beta-blocker). Lastly, the SVR can be increased with phenylephrine which will decrease the right to left shunting at the VSD, increase the RV volume and force more blood across the RVOT. All vital signs will improve as the physiology is properly manipulated to increase the oxygen saturations.*
