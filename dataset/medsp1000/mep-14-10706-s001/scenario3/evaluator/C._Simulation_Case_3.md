**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

*The learners enter the room to find an infant with a low sPO2. After completing a physical examination and obtaining an appropriate history, the low sPO2 is noted to be a change from baseline and the cardiac exam is different as well. Supplemental oxygen is applied with minimal effect. An IV fluid bolus is ordered with minimal improvement. The providers ask for an ECHO and find that the profound hypoxemia is due to decreased blood flow through the shunt. A heparin bolus is ordered. ECMO is ordered on stand-by given the high likelihood of hemodynamic compromise leading to cardiac arrest. The providers continue to augment pulmonary blood flow (PBF) with volume resuscitation and pharmacologic support. The mixed venous saturation is optimized with sedation and paralysis and the pulmonary venous saturation is maximized with intubation and delivery of 100% FiO2. The providers stabilize the patient while awaiting the interventional cardiologist/surgeons to arrive to revise/intervene on the shunt.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. *Failure to recognize details in the history and changes in the physical exam that support shunt occlusion as the likely cause of hypoxemia. We found when using this case learners often don’t realize that the patient’s history of treatment of a wound infection increases the likelihood of shunt complications. This historical information, coupled with the change in physical exam findings- the cardiac murmur is louder- should make learners’ most suspicious of shunt obstruction as cause for the worsening hypoxemia. The degree of concern and need for further intervention is marked by the history and the change in physical exam findings.*
2. *Uncertainty about how to augment PBF while awaiting shunt revision/intervention: Some learners did not recognize how medical management can increase PBF while they await the definitive catheter/surgical procedure. Increasing systemic vascular resistance (SVR) through volume resuscitation and phenylephrine will push more blood flow through the obstructed shunt and help increase PBF. A heparin bolus can decrease an existing clot or prevent clot propagation (if a clot is present or the shunt is narrowed by torsion) further augmenting PBF.*
3. *Failure to maximize both the mixed venous and pulmonary venous saturations. Often learners are so focused on the shunt that they forget to ensure that blood returning to the both the right and left atria are as fully saturated in oxygen as possible. By decreasing oxygen consumption through sedation and paralysis and increasing the concentration of oxygen in the blood by intubation and delivery of 100% FiO2, the mixed and pulmonary venous saturations will increase, respectively, and the overall systemic oxygen level will increase as well.*
4. *Failure to recognize the high likelihood of hemodynamic instability and the need for ECMO standby for this patient. Palliated single ventricle patients rely on well balanced physiology. When physiologic derangements are taking place, they have much less reserve than age matched two ventricle patients. This case is a medical emergency that has a high likelihood for hemodynamic instability leading to cardiac arrest. Having the ECMO tem on standby can ensure that these patients can quickly be resuscitated onto ECMO, if needed, in the pre-arrest phase, versus cannulation while active CPR is taking place.*
