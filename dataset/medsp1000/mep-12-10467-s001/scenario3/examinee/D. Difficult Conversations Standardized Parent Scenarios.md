Third Year Neonatal-Perinatal Fellow Standardized Parent Scenario—Disclosure of a medical error

For the fellow:

Miracle Smith is a former 25 week infant, now 43 weeks corrected, originally from The Outside Hospital. Infant was transported at 40 weeks corrected to The Children’s Hospital. She presented in distress and was quickly intubated. Two weeks ago, the infant had a tracheostomy placed for severe bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) and ventilator dependence, is receiving bolus nasogastric (NG) feeds, and has no IV. Miracle coded at 1600—chest compressions for 3 minutes, positive pressure ventilation for 10 minutes, formula suctioned from the airway. Care needed to be escalated and she is now on an oscillator. An intraosseous needle was placed for IV access. The NG tube was apparently inadvertently inserted into the lungs and the formula was therefore bolused into the lungs, resulting in the code. The neonatal nurse practitioner called the parents to update them and they are en route. The parents are known to be “difficult”—despite multiple family meetings and conversations, they have been unhappy with the care at The Children’s Hospital vs The Outside Hospital, often questioning the management decisions. The parents seem to have been very comfortable at The Outside Hospital with the personnel and care plan and are having a difficult time adjusting to the new hospital. They feel like Miracle was doing fine at The Outside Hospital and things have only gone downhill since admission to The Children’s Hospital—needing intubation, a tracheostomy, etc. Your job is to plan this meeting, disclose the medical error and have this difficult conversation.
