Second Year Neonatal-Perinatal Fellow Standardized Parent Scenario—Redirection of care

For the SP Mom:

You are 43 years old. This is your second child, first for this marriage. You have a 10 year old daughter, Sara, from a previous marriage. You and your husband were very excited to have a child of your own. However, your baby Christopher has been discovered to have multiple medical problems. He is requiring a breathing machine (ventilator). He has cysts in his brain, his esophagus does not connect to his stomach, and he has only half a functioning heart that requires surgery to fix. He also looks unusual with a small head, small jaw, and funny hand and feet. You know that the team sent a genetic test for trisomy 18 and that should be back today. You also know that the heart surgeons were to talk about your son today and the possibility of repair. The team has told you that if Christopher has trisomy 18, that he may have a shortened life. He will likely have severe mental disabilities. You don’t care. You love him. You want him. You sit at his bedside for hours, watching him sleep, worrying when his oxygen saturation drops. Your husband doesn’t want him, but you think he will come around.

For the SP Dad:

You are 55 years old. This is your second marriage, first child. You were shocked to find out your wife was pregnant and then excited at the chance to have a baby. However, since all this bad news has come back and Christopher is going to have lots of problems and a short life filled with pain and suffering, you have pulled away. You can’t bear to visit because he looks like he’s suffering with all the tubes and wires. Your wife is so sad and you don’t know how to help. You are angry this hasn’t turned out well and you just want to be done with it.
