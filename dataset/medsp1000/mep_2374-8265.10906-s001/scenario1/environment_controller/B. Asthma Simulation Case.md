I noticed him using his chest muscles more and breathing more quickly yesterday. We started giving him his albuterol that we got previously from our pediatrician yesterday afternoon, but this morning it seemed like it wasn't helping anymore. Every time he gets a cold, we seem to end up in the clinic or emergency department."  If asked:   * He has not had any fever, emesis diarrhea, rash, sore throat or ear pain. * He was taking 2 puffs every 6 hours yesterday. His mother has identified URIs as a trigger for his attacks. * The whole family has been sick with a cold for the past week. * He was not noted to choke on anything at home prior to developing distress. | Instructor Notes - Changes and CASE Branch Points | | |
| **Intervention / Time point** | **Change in Case** | **Additional Information** |
| Oxygen is placed on patient. | O2 saturation increases to 100% but there is no improvement in work of breathing |  |
| Patient is laid flat. | He develops increased agitation and worsened retractions. | The distress does not improve with a neck roll or jaw thrust, only with allowing the child to sit up. |
| Albuterol puffs are administered to patient. | No change in patient status | Parent states: “He is still having a lot of trouble breathing. I usually needs more than that when he is really sick.” |
| Albuterol treatment of at least 10mg is administered. | Patient heart rate increases by 10 beats per minute over the next 4 minutes up to 150 beats per minute. At the end of the treatment, the retractions remain the same. The patient now has diffuse expiratory wheeze with prolonged expiratory phase. He still has nasal flaring but no grunting. | Given that these treatments are often given over an hour in real life, the facilitators will likely want to “advance time” and state that the treatment has finished after several minutes.  Parents become more worried as they see an escalation of care without significant improvement. They start asking questions about whether he is going to be ok? Whether he will need to be hospitalized? And if they did anything wrong to make him this sick? |
| A second albuterol treatment of at least 10mg is administered. | The heart rate increases to 175 beats per minute. His respiratory rate decreases to 25 breaths per minute. At the end of the treatment, the patient has mild retractions. He has end expiratory wheeze throughout and no longer has nasal flaring. He continues to have prolongation of his expiratory phase, although improved. He is active and speaking to his parents in his normal manner. | Given that these treatments are often given over an hour in real life, the facilitators will likely want to “advance time” and state that the treatment has finished after several minutes. |
| Participant requests a dose of steroids be given to the patient. | No clinical change | Steroids may be given orally, intramuscularly or intravenously. The type and route may vary depending on local practices. |
| Participant requests finger stick blood glucose. | Glucose level is 90. |  |
| Participant requests a blood gas. | 7.42/35/85/+1 |  |
| Participant requests a chest radiograph. | A radiograph may be provided. See Appendix C. | The facilitator should wait an appropriate amount of time consistent with local practices before providing participants with the images. There may be a need to “advance time” once again, but some delay should be provided so that participants are not awaiting results in order to provide treatment. |
| Participant requests bedside electrolytes and or hematocrit. | Sodium 140  Potassium 3.5  Chloride 110  Bicarb 23  iCa 1.12  Hct 40 | Only provide these labs if they would actually be available at your site |
| Participant requests a fluid bolus. | No change in vital signs. |  |
| Participant requests a bolus of magnesium sulfate to be given over 20-30 minutes. | After administration, his respiratory rate decreases to 30 breaths per minute. His blood pressure decreases to 75/40. His O2 saturation is 89% if on room air. At the end of the treatment, the patient has mild retractions. He has end expiratory wheeze throughout and no longer has nasal flaring. He continues to have prolongation of his expiratory phase, although improved. He is more active and is crying asking for food. | If the participant requests the bolus to pushed, a facilitator should state, “I am worried that is too fast and will cause severe hypotension. It is usually given over 20-30 minutes for asthma.” |
| End of case. | If participants have not verbalized disposition plan a facilitator should as what the immediate plan is for disposition. They may act in the role of a supervising physician or nurse. | Students determine that child needs to be admitted for further treatment and evaluation. |
