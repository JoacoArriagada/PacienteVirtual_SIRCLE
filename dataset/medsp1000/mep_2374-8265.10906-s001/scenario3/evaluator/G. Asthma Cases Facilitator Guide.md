## SCENARIO 3 – Outpatient PCP follow-up visit (first time asthma exacerbation)

Goals

1. Practice eliciting an asthma history including possible environmental triggers
2. Practice ways to discuss mitigation of asthma triggers
3. Develop strategies for introducing different therapeutic options for asthma management including potential start of long acting inhaled steroids
4. Discuss reasonable outpatient follow up

Scenario - Eli returns to his primary care provider 3 days after being discharged from the hospital. He has been out of preschool since being discharged and he continues to have a runny nose, cough, no fevers. Parents report that he continues on the medications that he was given in the hospital and seems to be breathing easier and his energy level has picked up. In discussion with the family, it seems that Eli has been coughing more at nights than usual prior to his admission. His teachers noted that he had been taking breaks while playing outside because of his breathing in the month leading up to his admission. The parents recall that his pediatrician had mentioned wheezes once while they were being evaluated for a cold a few months ago. He had been prescribed albuterol at that time. The family has 2 cats and a dog.

1. Clinic Visit (30min) – in pairs, with one student taking on the role of the parent and the other as the physician, discuss the following topics and role play eliciting the following information:
   1. How the child has been doing since discharge?
      1. *Is he back to his baseline?*
         1. *Parent –* I believe he is, he doesn’t seem to be working as hard to breath and I haven’t seen any retractions like we learned about in the hospital. He’s eating more now and he just went to school for the first day yesterday. We have been giving him the every four hours or so and the steroid that was prescribed to us.
      2. *How do you think his respiratory status is?*
         1. *Parent –* I haven't noticed any of that belly breathing that he was doing before and when he breaths I don’t see his ribs. I also haven’t heard any wheezing like I did when we took him to the emergency room, that was really scary.
      3. *How is his energy level?*
         1. *Parent –* Overall, I think he is almost back to his normal energy level. We kept him out of school for the past few days, and he has been going stir crazy. He gets really shaky after the albuterol and I wonder if the steroids are making him a bit hyper?
   2. Is medication adherence and proper use of inhaler with spacer occurring?
      1. *What medications has Eli been on since discharge?*
         1. *Parent –* We have been giving him the albuterol puffer every 4 hours or so during the day as they told us to in the hospital. They said to continue this until we came in for our follow-up visit with you. We also are also giving him steroids once a day. I think they told us to give it for 5 days.
      2. *How has it been giving him the albuterol? Is he tolerating the mask?*
         1. *Parent –* He does tolerate it. Since the hospital he really doesn’t seem to mind it.
      3. *Is he tolerating the steroid? Have you noticed any side effects of the medications?*
         1. *Parent –*  He takes the steroid ok and was wondering about whether it is making him a little more hyper or moody than normal. I heard that these can be side effects of steroids.
   3. Eliciting symptoms and possible triggers for asthma exacerbations including environmental triggers.
      1. *Have you been able to pinpoint any triggers for Eli’s wheezing?*
         1. *Parent –* We really don’t know. We first thought it might be cats, but since that time we have really tried to limit his exposure. This time it really seems like it came out of nowhere. It does seems like every fall he seems to have more difficulty breathing.
      2. *Do you have pets? If so where do they spend most of their time? Do they sleep with Eli?*
         1. *Parents –* We do have a cat who is mostly indoors. She really doesn’t sleep in his room anymore. My parents have two dogs, and he is there a lot. He loves those dogs. Do you think we need to get rid of the cat? He really loves the dogs, do we need to keep him away from them?
      3. *Do you have carpets or wood floors? Do you use pillow or mattress covers?*
         1. *Parents –* We have both in our house, kids’ rooms are carpeted and the rest of the house is wood floors. We haven’t used pillow or mattress covers, do you think they would help?
      4. *Does anyone smoke at home?*
         1. *Parents –* My husband, he smokes, but only outside. He has tried to stop so many times, the last time was a year ago, but he’s back at it. He smokes about a pack a day. Is there any link between cigarette smoke and asthma? Maybe if he knew it wasn’t good for Eli, it would be more incentive for him to stop.
   4. How would you classify this child’s asthma? <https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/files/docs/guidelines/asthma_qrg.pdf> *is a helpful link to answer this question, if needed.1See Table 1 for summary.*

  1. How would you advise the parents about symptom recognition & trigger avoidance?
     1. *Know Eli’s triggers*
     2. *Anticipate situations where Eli may be exposed to triggers eg. If going to a birthday party where there are animals, may be appropriate to take an antihistamine/leukotriene receptor antagonist as well as limit exposure to the pets.*
     3. *Recommend environmental mitigation such as mattress and pillow covers, stress the importance of limiting pets in the bedroom.*
