## Scenario 4 – Outpatient – Chronic Management

Goals:

1. Practice obtaining a history of medication adherence and symptom severity in patients with asthma
2. Practice discussing use of long term inhaled steroids
3. Discuss expectations for Eli’s participation in activities and how asthma will affect his future
4. Discuss ways to decrease risk of asthma exacerbations

Scenario - It is now late September, 9-months after Eli’s hospitalization. Eli is 3 years old and has overall been healthy. He has been into Urgent Care 3 times in the past 3 months for acute exacerbations (once while at a birthday party with cats in the house, once in the setting of a URI and the last time when he was out on a camping trip). Each time he has required a short course of steroids. He continues on his albuterol inhaler as needed, usually around 2 times per week and sometime takes Zyrtec when he seems to be getting itchy. His parents report continued coughing at night. He is in clinic today before for the start of school to discuss his health and asthma in particular.

1. Clinic Visit (30 min) – in pairs, with one student taking on the role of the parent and the other as the physician, discuss the following topics and role play eliciting the following the information:
   1. How often is Eli using his inhaler?
   2. Is he able to participate in activities of his choosing? Is he, his parents or teachers noticing any limitations to his activities?
   3. Have parents identified any new triggers?
   4. Review urgent care visits.
   5. Classify asthma control & severity using NHLBI guidelines.
      1. *See Table 1.*
      2. *Given his continued symptoms, nighttime cough, and albuterol use he is Not Well Controlled.*
      3. *Per guidelines recommendations would be to consider stepping up in therapy to include low-dose inhaled corticosteroid (ICS.)2*
   6. Discuss initiation of inhaled corticosteroid (in particular: benefits, side effects and how it differs from albuterol).3
      1. *Benefits include: Better control of airway inflammation, fewer exacerbations and fewer missed school days, ability to participate more fully in activities, potentially less use of oral steroids*
      2. *Risks include both local and systemic effects and are dose related: Local- candidiasis, bronchospasm, dysphonia. The literature is mixed on whether or not ICS cause growth delay/suppression. This is in part due to the fact that chronic disease such as asthma in and of itself can adversely affect growth. Among the studies that show a growth delay, the mean reduction was ~1.5cm and was dose dependent. Whether or not the effect is a permanent or temporary effect is unknown. The risk of osteopenia/osteoporosis and decreased immunity is negligible and only at high doses. The risk of diabetes in children taking ICS has not been proven. In adults there has been an association with development and progression of disease in those taking ICS at high doses for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).*

*What is known is that the lowest dose possible should be administered and that local therapy is better than systemic therapy in terms of side effect profile.*
