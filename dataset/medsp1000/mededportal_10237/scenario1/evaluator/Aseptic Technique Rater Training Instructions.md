Learners are being tested on aseptic (sterile) technique, while performing an Incision & Drainage (I&D). While they will need to interact with the patient, the evaluation is based mainly on their technique. Supplies are available **outside the room** and the learners will choose what they require before entering the room. The rater (“mock” RN) is in the room to answer questions, assist, and evaluate the learner’s performance. The raters’ main purpose is assessment. The rater may assist with opening supplies or disposing of materials, if asked specifically by the learner. However, assistance should be minimal in order to accurately assess the learner’s skills. If the learner is asking for too much assistance, the nurse may respond with *“I need to see you do it.”*

The checklist gives the general flow of the session but everything does not have to be done in the exact order of the checklist.

The learner must:

* Identify the patient
* Discuss the procedure with patient
* Indicate that informed consent has been obtained and verify the correct site.
* The learner should then begin the procedure.

Supplies include:

1. Skin prep swabs (Betadine and Chloraprep): 1 per learner, ideally use the prep commonly issued at your institution
2. Unsterile and sterile gloves (various sizes to accommodate all learners)
3. Sterile sheet with opening (fenestrated): 1 per learner
4. Protective pad for under the arm (disposable underpad (e.g. Chux), which is not sterile): 1 per learner
5. Gauze sponges for dressing: 2-4 per learner (sterile) 4X4
6. Protective eyewear: 5-6 pairs total, these can be reused
7. Tape to secure the dressing: 1 box
8. Alcohol swab pads can be used to clean eye wear and spills

The learner need to:

1. wash their hands
2. place a Chux under the patient’s arm
3. open the sterile items
4. prep area
5. put on sterile gloves
6. drape area
7. simulate performing the “procedure”
8. apply clean dressing
9. take off gloves
10. tape dressing down
11. wash hands again

The exam table, in flat position, is the best place for supplies to be placed. Patient should sit on chair (not the stool), with arm on end of table. The Mayo Instrument stand can be used for the sterile field. While we do not provide a sterile sheet to empty sterile items on, the inside of opened sterile packages can be used.

“Time out” – Joint Commission's Universal Protocol for Preventing Wrong Site, Wrong Procedure, Wrong Person Surgery - "to conduct a final verification of the correct patient, procedure, site, and, as applicable, implants." Since the patient’s name has been verified in the beginning, learner just has to state that he is going to perform an incision and drainage on patients’ right arm.

Remember, this is about the use of aseptic technique, not about the incision procedure. Nothing is actually done to the patient. All instruments regarding incision are imaginary.

Learners are often nervous, so make them feel welcome and as comfortable as possible under the circumstances.

Reserve a score of 9 for the rare exception to expected performance. A master surgeon or surgical nurse would get an 8 or a 9. A new medical student would be a 1 or a 2 etc.
