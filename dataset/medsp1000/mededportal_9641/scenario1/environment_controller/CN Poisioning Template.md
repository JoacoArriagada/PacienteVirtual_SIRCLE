Cyanide Poisoning

# **Title: Cyanide Poisoning**

# **II. Target Audience**: medical students, residents

## Environment

1. Lab Set Up – ED
2. Manikin Set Up – The scenario is created for Sim Man, but could be easily adapted to another mannequin. A large bore IV line should be in place. IV fluids, equipment and appropriate medication for intubation should be available. Appropriate medication to treat CN poisoning should be available.
3. Props – Normal chest x-ray (pre and post intubation), normal CT head and cervical spine.

## Actors

1. Roles :

EMS—will stay if asked and give brief history.

Nurse —will perform nursing tasks appropriately.

Both may be played by students, other residents or faculty, nurses

1. **Case Narrative**
2. Scenario Background (must be elicited from EMS)
3. Chief complaint: Billy Brown is an 18 year old who was rescued from a fire in his mobile home. The fire was started by his uncle, who fell asleep on the sofa with a lit cigarette in his hand. The firefighters said that Billy inhaled “quite a bit of smoke” before they were able to rescue him from his bedroom. The uncle was pronounced dead at the scene.
4. PMH: none
5. Meds and allergies: none
6. Family/social/ occupational history: unobtainable.
7. Scenario conditions initially
8. History: given by EMS—see above.
9. Physical Examination:

BP 90/50mm/Hg; Pulse Ox 95% RA; RR 32/min HR 49/min

General: lethargic, moaning.

HEENT: dry mucous membranes. soot visible on nares and lips.

CV: No murmurs, rubs or gallops, bradycardic

Pulm: CTA B/L, tachypneic

ABD: non tender, normal bowel sounds.

Skin: Second and third degree burns on the lower back and bilateral buttocks and thighs totaling approximately 20% BSA.

Neuro: lethargic, moaning. Moves all extremities in response to deep painful stimuli.

1. Scenario branch points
2. If the participants do not recognize tachypnea and hypotension and immediately initiate appropriate treatment, the patient will progress to PEA arrest. Patient will become pulseless if not intubated within 7 minutes.
3. If the participants administer treatment for CN poisoning, the vital signs will gradually improve. Within 4 minutes, vital signs will be as follows: HR 55 BP 100/60 RR 24.
4. If the participants do not recognize or treat CN poisoning the patient will become increasingly bradycardic and hypotensive.
5. Cues for medical students and PGY1 learners: The nurse will point out that the patient does not seem to be improving and ask the residents what else they think may be wrong with the patient. She may suggest that the team perform an ABG. Additional prompting may occur at the instructor’s discretion (the nurse may mention that she had another fire victim with a low heart rate that had to be treated with “some kind of special medication” or she may mention a smell of bitter almonds).
6. Cues for PGY 2 and up learners: The nurse will point out that the vital signs are not improving, and ask the team what else they want to do.
7. If the CN poisoning is not treated within 15 minutes, the patient will progress to PEA arrest.
8. Atropine can be administered with no adverse effects, but it will not correct the hypotension and bradycardia.
9. **Instructors Notes**
10. Cues provided during the scenario should be varied according to the level of learner, as described above. The instructor may want to withhold laboratory findings until the basic resuscitation and intubation has been completed. Laboratory findings should otherwise be made available within 5 minutes of ordering.
