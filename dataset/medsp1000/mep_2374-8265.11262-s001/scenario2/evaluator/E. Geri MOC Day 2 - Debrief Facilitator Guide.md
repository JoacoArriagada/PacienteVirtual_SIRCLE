**Case 2:**

**Mr. Davis is a retired 77-year-old man living in an apartment two miles from your urban clinic with a history of CAD s/p stent, COPD, mild cognitive impairment, and PTSD who presents to your office after a recent hospitalization for COPD exacerbation that the team attributed to “poor medication compliance.” He never married and has no children; he has a niece in Virginia whom he calls once a week. Mr. Davis walks with a walking stick, has not fallen, and states he is independent in all his ADLs. He denies any difficulties with his IADLs but the inpatient social worker’s note comments that the neighbor who brought him in was worried about “trash smells” coming from the apartment. The social worker notified Adult Protective Services but has not heard back yet. Mr. Davis has a good work-related pension and social security. He did get himself to the clinic appointment on time though you notice his clothes are worn and there are stains on his clothes. He tells you he is feeling fine and doesn’t know what all the fuss is about.**

*Potential options for this patient:*

***Home-based primary care with home health aide****: Home-based primary care is* ***not an option*** *for Mr. Davis as he doesn’t qualify for home based care (e.g., he is ambulatory, brings himself into clinic, isn’t endorsing hardship from clinic visits). He might qualify, though, for a home-visit safety check to evaluate if he has home health aide needs for IADL assistance (e.g., housekeeping, laundry, medication management). He could also pay privately for assistance with IADLs.*

***PACE:*** *This is* ***not an option*** *for Mr. Davis. He does not have skilled needs that would warrant NH level care.*

***NH:*** *This is* ***not an option*** *for Mr. Davis. He does not have skilled needs that would warrant NH level care.*

***Senior Housing****: This is a* ***possible option*** *for Mr. Davis, though might be an unnecessary transition (would likely need to shortly after be placed into RCFE) with minimal benefit.*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *Could improve his socialization as well as “eyes on” time should something acutely happen to him* | *Mr. Davis has stable housing in the city at this time* |
| *Often provides a case manager on-site to coordinate community resources* | *Mr. Davis’s larger problem seems to be the need for IADL assistance (housekeeping, laundry, medication management) which is not provided at senior housing apartments* |

*\*****RCFE (Assisted Living or Adult Group Home):*** *This would be a* ***good/feasible option*** *for Mr. Davis.*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *Adult Group Home would be able to provide Mr. Davis with “supervised independence”* | *He would still need to agree to go, which might take many conversations!* |
| *Adult Group Home could help ensure his IADL needs are met (housekeeping, laundry, as well as cueing of, and storage of, medications)* | *It would be a monthly expense that he may not be prepared for; he might need to meet with a social worker to discuss his benefits as they relate to cost* |
| *Adult Group Home would also potentially provide him with some peer-support and socialization from other individuals living in the Adult Group Home* | *Adult Group Home facilities are all very variable in terms of the level of assistance they can provide* |
| *Adult Group Home would be less costly than an Assisted Living Facility given that he is on a fixed pension/social security income* |  |
