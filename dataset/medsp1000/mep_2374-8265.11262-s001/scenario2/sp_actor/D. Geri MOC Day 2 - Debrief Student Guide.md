**Case 2:**

Mr. Davis is a retired 77-year-old man living in an apartment two miles from your urban clinic with a history of CAD s/p stent, COPD, mild cognitive impairment, and PTSD who presents to your office after a recent hospitalization for COPD exacerbation that the team attributed to “poor medication compliance.” He never married and has no children; he has a niece in Virginia whom he calls once a week. Mr. Davis walks with a walking stick, has not fallen, and states he is independent in all his ADLs. He denies any difficulties with his IADLs but the inpatient social worker’s note comments that the neighbor who brought him in was worried about “trash smells” coming from the apartment. The social worker notified Adult Protective Services but has not heard back yet. Mr. Davis has a good work-related pension and social security. He did get himself to the clinic appointment on time though you notice his clothes are worn and there are stains on his clothes. He tells you he is feeling fine and doesn’t know what all the fuss is about.
