**Case 1:**

**Mrs. Jung is an 84-year-old woman living in a local suburb with a history of CHF, depression, moderate Alzheimer’s dementia, and severe bilateral knee osteoarthritis. She ambulates with a 4-wheel walker, has had some falls at home, and is now having difficulty getting out due to being afraid to navigate on her own the stairs from her front door to street level. She needs assistance with most IADLs including her medications, though she is still able to help prepare meals. She also needs assistance with ADLs, particularly getting dressed, taking showers, and sometimes getting to and using the bathroom. Mrs. Jung is widowed and her daughter and grandson moved into the second floor of her home 2 years ago to help out. Her daughter works from 9am-5pm and her grandson, who used to spend much of the day with her, is leaving for a new job out of state next month. Her daughter called your office yesterday, stating that she “just can’t keep doing all of this” anymore and cited concerns from her boss about the large amounts of time she was having to take off to coordinate Mrs. Jung’s care and take her to appointments. She wants to know what her options are and tells you “Mom would rather die than be put in a nursing home.”**

*Potential options for this patient:*

*\*****Home-based primary care with homemaker/home health aides****: This could be a* ***good/feasible option*** *for Mrs. Jung.*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *She would be able to stay in the home with her daughter as she desires* | *Homemaker/home health aides cannot always cover all the hours needed by a family and might be inconsistent* |
| *Appointments could be coordinated through a home-based primary care program (typically able to accommodate more of what the patient’s scheduling needs are) and reduce the amount of time the daughter would need to take her to appointments* | *Home-based primary care with home health aides would not necessarily help the social isolation that Mrs. Jung may be experiencing* |
| *Her Medicare insurance would cover the usual costs of the provider visits* | *Private pay home health aides are expensive* |
| *Home-based primary care team could help her get set up with county-based home health aide services if she qualifies for through Medicaid* |  |
| *Home-based primary care social worker could also help with utilizing private pay resources for daytime private home health aides* |  |
| *Daughter might feel less stressed knowing her mom was receiving assistance at home* |  |

***\*PACE****: This could also be a* ***good/feasible option*** *for Mrs. Jung.*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *Mrs. Jung does qualify for NH level of care (currently has 3 ADL needs)* | *Mrs. Jung might not want to leave her home to attend PACE, or change her PCP to the PACE physician* |
| *PACE could provide a NH level of care while keeping Mrs. Jung in her home with her daughter as she desires* | *Mrs. Jung would be able to utilize her Medicare benefit, but we would need to find out if she qualifies for Medicaid as well (left ambiguous in the case) to determine if all the PACE costs could be paid for without out of pocket expense* |
| *PACE day programs can compensate for the hours her daughter is at work; her daughter could continue to assist her in the home during the evening hours* |  |
| *PACE day programs could help Mrs. Jung get out of the home (provided she felt safe to do so), provide transportation, meals, and assistance with her showering/dressing at the PACE program* |  |
| *At PACE, Mrs. Jung can see her doctor and interprofessional care team members all in one place* |  |
| *PACE’s comprehensive services may reduce the daughter’s caregiver burden* |  |
| *PACE would assist with socialization and hopefully engage Mrs. Jung in a community of peers through the day program* |  |

***RCFE (Assisted Living or Adult Group Home):****This would* ***not be the best option*** *for Mrs. Jung.*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *RCFE would provide her with meals, housekeeping* | *Mrs. Jung doesn’t want to leave her home and has an alternative (her daughter who could care for her during the evening hours with additional daytime assistance)* |
| *RCFE could manage her medications and provide her with dressing and shower assistance (for a fee)* | *Mrs. Jung’s care needs (ADL assistance, medication management) would cost a significant amount of “extras” on top of an RCFE’s room and board costs* |
| *RCFE could provide socialization with peers* | *Mrs. Jung has an intentionally ambiguous financial situation, though could potentially sell the home she owns (and unfortunately also then need to evict her daughter) to be able to pay for RCFE level of care* |
| *Daughter’s caregiver burden may be reduced* |  |

***NH****: This would* ***not be the best option*** *for Mrs. Jung though possible.*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *NH could manage all her ADL needs, monitor for and help assess falls* | *Mrs. Jung doesn’t want to leave her home and has an alternative (her daughter who could care for her during the evening hours with additional daytime assistance)* |
| *NH could provide socialization with peers* | *Ambiguous Medicaid status; if she has Medicaid, will need to find a facility that accepts Medicaid* |
| *Daughter’s caregiver burden may be reduced* | *If she does not currently have Medicaid, there is usually a lengthy application process followed by a search for a SNF that would accommodate a long-term care patient on Medicaid* |
|  | *If not qualifying for Medicaid, Mrs. Jung would need to pay out of pocket for her nursing home room (expensive!)* |
