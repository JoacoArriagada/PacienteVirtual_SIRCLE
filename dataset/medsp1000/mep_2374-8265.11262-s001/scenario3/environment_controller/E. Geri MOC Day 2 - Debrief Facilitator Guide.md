**Case 3**:

**Mr. Collins is an 82-year-old Korean War veteran with a history of traumatic brain injury complicated by refractory epilepsy, moderate to severe dementia, PTSD, and osteoporosis, whose son brings him into the Emergency Room because he “just can’t take care of dad.” He has been living at his son’s home in a nearby rural county for the past 5 years. Mr. Collins walks with a 4-wheel walker and has had 2 falls in the past month due to unsteadiness. He is dependent for all IADLs and requires assistance with bathing, grooming, dressing, and is incontinent of urine. He takes 18 medications and his son has struggled to get him to take his seizure medications, so Mr. Collins has had 3 seizures in the past 6 weeks. After his seizures, Mr. Collins is usually “more confused” and becomes combative. His son states that he just got a new job that will require him to be away from the home (he used to work from home) for up to 10 hours a day and there are no other family members close-by. Mr. Collins has a VA pension and social security. His son looks fatigued and says, “I promised dad that I would never send him away.”**

*Potential options for this patient:*

***Senior Housing:*** Mr. Collins has high needs.

***Home-based primary care with home health aides:***

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| *Pros* | *Cons* |
| *Mr. Collins would be able to remain at home with his son* | *Home-based primary care would likely not be able to manage Mr. Collins’ acute issues (e.g., seizures, behaviors) as easily as if he were in a skilled facility; there would be no skilled staff in the house to assist with assessment and intervention* |
| *Home-based primary care would help with coordination of care; easier access to medical providers for Mr. Collins* | *Mr. Collins likely needs 24/7 assistance and supervision, amounting to >200 hours per month of assistance while his son is away. While home health aides can, in some counties, provide those kinds of resources, it is unlikely in a rural area that this is an option* |
| *Home health aides can assist with some IADL and ADL activities in son’s absence* | *Family could private pay for caregiving, however this would be expensive for the number of hours needed* |
| *Might assist with son’s caregiver burden* | *Home health aides would not be able to provide the more skilled needs related to a complex medication regimen, seizures, frequent falls, and behavioral challenges* |

***PACE:*** There are likely no PACE sites in his rural area. Many regions have no PACE options outside of major metropolitan areas.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *Mr. Collins qualifies for NH level care with ADL needs* | *No local PACE available* |
| *PACE could cover some hours son is at work; private caregivers or home health aides could be hired for the remainder* | *Additional non-PACE hours would require caregivers, which can be expensive* |
| *PACE could provide centralized medical care; providers would have good sense of patient’s acute changes/needs* | *Mr. Collins’ behavioral challenges might make participation in PACE difficult* |
| *PACE could provide ADL assistance with bathing, grooming, etc.* | *We are unclear from the case if Mr. Collins has Medicaid to assist with paying for PACE* |
| *Medicare could pay for provider visits within PACE model* |  |
| *Might assist with caregiver burden* |  |

***RCFE (Assisted Living or Adult Group Home):***

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *Could receive assistance with IADLs (housekeeping, meal preparation, laundry) and assistance with storage and cueing of his medications.* | *Mr. Collins needs constant supervision/care and assistance from trained staff members* |
| *Could receive assistance with ADLs if paid “extra”* | *His medication regimen is complex, contains medications that are potentially life-saving, require close monitoring (e.g., his antiepileptics), and potential adjustments when he refuses to take them* |
| *Would provide peer socialization and engagement for Mr. Collins* | *His functional impairments and gait abnormalities would require diligent observation of his mobility given his risk of falls* |
| *Might assist with caregiver burden* | *His behavioral challenges would likely lead to a denied admission to begin with, unless the RCFE had a dedicated dementia friendly/memory care unit; even there, it may be beyond the capabilities of an RCFE* |
|  | *The cost of an RCFE with all the needed ADL “extras” would also likely be above what his VA pension and social security income could cover; he has no assets (e.g., home) to sell to raise funds to pay for an expensive RCFE with multiple add-on costs to meet additional needs.* |

***NH:***

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ***Pros*** | ***Cons*** |
| *He has high skilled needs (all IADLs and nearly all ADLs), complex medication regimens, frequent acute events, and behavioral challenges* | *Mr. Collins has an ambiguous financial status to determine if he qualifies for Medicaid; if being placed in the community, family would need to start an evaluation for Medicaid eligibility* |
| *Regular access to a medical team (nurses, pharmacists, and physicians) would allow for closer adjustment of his care plan and responses to acute changes* | *Some NHs might still deny Mr. Collins admission based on his history of combative behavior* |
| *Many nursing homes have staff members trained in dementia care and other behavioral challenges* | *Son has “promised” Mr. Collins he wouldn’t send him to a nursing home. In terms of son’s concern about “sending him away,” unlike Mrs. Jung where there is a reasonable alternative, in this case there are no feasible alternatives to keep Mr. Collins in his home safely. Patient safety is a primary factor in making difficult decisions about placement.* |
| *He has a VA pension and his son and a case manager could investigate eligibility for the VA’s Community Living Center vs community NH placement for long-term care* | *Mr. Collins has an ambiguous financial status to determine if he qualifies for Medicaid; if being placed in the community, family would need to start an evaluation for Medicaid eligibility* |
