**Case 3:**

Mr. Collins is an 82-year-old Korean War veteran with a history of traumatic brain injury complicated by refractory epilepsy, moderate to severe dementia, PTSD, and osteoporosis, whose son brings him into the Emergency Room because he “just can’t take care of dad.” He has been living at his son’s home in a nearby rural county for the past 5 years. Mr. Collins walks with a 4-wheel walker and has had 2 falls in the past month due to unsteadiness. He is dependent for all IADLs and requires assistance with bathing, grooming, dressing, and is incontinent of urine. He takes 18 medications and his son has struggled to get him to take his seizure medications, so Mr. Collins has had 3 seizures in the past 6 weeks. After his seizures, Mr. Collins is usually “more confused” and becomes combative. His son states that he just got a new job that will require him to be away from the home (he used to work from home) for up to 10 hours a day and there are no other family members close-by. Mr. Collins has a VA pension and social security. His son looks fatigued and says, “I promised dad that I would never send him away.”
