# 3. Hypotension case: 1 student (10 minutes)

F6/581: FULL. Mr. Buck Doe is a 90-year-old man admitted from his nursing facility for confusion, found to have a urinary tract infection. He is on nitrofurantoin; got a liter of fluid in the ED before transferring up to the floor; is general care status given stability before transfer.

PAGE: Please call RN re: Mr. Doe; BP on PM vitals 82/38. (###-###-####)

# Notes for nurse

Other vitals at the time: BP 82/38, HR 112, RR 24, sat $9 7 \%$ on 2 L NC, T 101.4F   
If asked for a repeat set: BP 80/42, HR 117, RR 24, sat $9 7 \%$ , T 101.2F   
If asked about a trend in vitals – BP was 90s systolic in the ED, came up to 102 systolic after 1 L of fluid; HR has been above 100 but this is the highest; becoming more tachypneic, oxygen is on for lowest sat recorded $92 \%$   
Mentation has waned; is now only oriented to person though does answer questions; feels warm to the touch.   
Did get his dose of nitrofurantoin upon arrival to the floor.   
Does not have any central access; has one PIV placed in the ED.

# Desired learner actions:

Ask for a fresh set of vitals   
Verbal order for 1 L NS bolus using clear, closed-loop communication; inquire about access   
Agree to assess patient urgently   
Consider escalation of care to intermediate care status (higher is appropriate, too)   
$+ / -$ verbal orders for work-up and stablization but should see him immediately

# Teaching points:

Constellation of hypotension, tachycardia and fever is highly suggestive of sepsis – and   
worrisome for septic shock   
In nearly all cases of shock, unless there is a clear contraindication, patients need to be fluid   
resuscitated initially – do this while you figure out what is going on   
Similar to case above, it is important to provide some initial verbal orders for the nurse to   
execute while you are en route – repeat vitals, hang a liter of fluid, access if needed   
A finer point, but this patient will need his antibiotics changed – nitrofurantoin is inappropriate   
for this patient because he is male (and sick!)
