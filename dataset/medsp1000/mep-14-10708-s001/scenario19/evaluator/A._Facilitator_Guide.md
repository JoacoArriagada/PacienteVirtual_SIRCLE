4. Tachycardia (recognizing the need for cardioversion in patient with unstable atrial fibrillation); 1

student; 10 minutes

D6/552: FULL. Mr. Tracy Jordan is a 67 year old man admitted for CAP and COPD exacerbation – on ceftri/azithro and steroids; getting scheduled nebs.

PAGE: Please call RN re: patient Jordan; heart rate on telemetry 180s.

# Notes for nurse:

Vital signs: T 99.8F, BP 80/40 (from baseline 130s/70s), P 185, RR 28, sat $91 \%$ on 4L NC   
Patient feels terrible – worsened dyspnea, oxygen requirement went up, feels lightheaded and   
is beginning to have chest pain   
You aren’t sure because his heart rate is so fast, but telemetry is alarming for atrial fibrillation   
To your knowledge, patient has never had atrial fibrillation before

# Desired learner actions:

Ask for fresh set of vitals   
Inquire about symptoms & access   
Recognize patient is in an unstable tachycardia   
Verbal order for stat ECG

# Intern Prep Course: Medical Breakout Session Mock Pages & Mock Consults Facilitator Guide

Notify the nurse of your plan – which should include likely urgent cardioversion – have crash cart ready, sedation (e.g. 2mg versed and 50-100mcg fentanyl) Clear, closed-loop communication when relaying assessment and orders to nursing staff Agree to assess the patient urgently – call rapid response or cards or ICU

# Teaching points:

The determination of stable versus unstable atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response is   
of utmost importance – dictates pharmacologic versus electric therapy   
Unstable atrial fibrillation requires urgent cardioversion o This is true of most unstably atrial tachycardias – with with exception of sinus tachycardia, which you worry is compensatory, therefore, would not try to actively terminate this rhythm
