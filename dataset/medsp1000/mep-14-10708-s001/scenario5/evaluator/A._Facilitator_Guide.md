# 5. Chest pain case: 1 student needed (10 min)

D4/433: FULL. Mr. Kenneth Parcell is a 68-year-old man with h/o CAD and peripheral vascular disease and CKD admitted for cellulitis; on ceftriaxone.

PAGE: Please call RN re: patient Parcell. New complaint of chest pain. (###-###-####)

# Notes for nurse:

Chest pain came on rather abruptly, is substernal and unrelenting. Radiates into the left shoulder, also feeling nauseated and appears diaphoretic.   
VS: T98.6F, BP 100/60, P 108, RR 24, sat $9 3 \%$ on 2L NC   
Tried some Tums before calling, didn’t help   
Patient reports it feels somewhat similar to his last heart attack   
Has been ambulating and receiving DVT prophylaxis with sub Q heparin.

# Desired learner actions:

Ask for a fresh set of vitals & put on tele   
Inquire about character of chest pain   
Verbal orders for stat ECG and chest x-ray $( + / -$ troponin, this often waits until after evaluation)   
using proper closed-loop communication   
Agree to assess patient urgently

# Teaching points:

Rapid evaluation of chest pain should include history and physical, ECG and chest x-ray. While the vast majority of calls for chest pain overnight are non-cardiac, in patients at high risk take this complaint seriously and obtain ECG, chest x-ray and cardiac enzymes; recognizing that troponin may not be elevated acutely.   
You should contact a senior resident for back up if you are uncertain; or if your suspicion is high enough, a cardiology fellow.
