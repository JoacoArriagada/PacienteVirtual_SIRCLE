# 11a. Hyperkalemia case: 2 students needed for this case (one to answer page, one to call a consult); (12 minutes)

D4/417: FULL. Mr. Bob Jones is a 66-year-old man with chronic back pain and CKD stage IV admitted for acute kidney injury; checking potassium overnight, admission $K = 5 . 5$ .

PAGE: Please call RN – Patient Jones repeat potassium is 6.9. (###-###-####)

# Notes for nurse:

Patient has not received intervention yet for his hyperkalemia   
When asked to resend specimen, notify provider you have already done this   
Patient is stable, not complaining of any cardiopulmonary symptoms   
Patient is not on telemetry; provide some resistance to request for “stat” ECG – (“ECG techs are   
no longer in house and I have three other patients, could this wait until after my break?”)   
If asked, other labs were Na 132 CO2 14 BUN 85 Cr 3.95 with this last lab check

# Desired learner actions:

New vitals   
Ask about what has been done, if anything, for patient’s hyperkalemia   
Ask nurse to draw repeat potassium   
Ask to place patient in telemetry and obtain STAT ECG (SLIDES OF NEW & OLD ECGs are in   
power point)

# Intern Prep Course: Medical Breakout Session Mock Pages & Mock Consults Facilitator Guide

Verbal orders for albuterol nebulizer, calcium gluconate 1.0 gram or calcium chloride 0.5 – 1.0   
gram, 10 units of regular insulin, $_ { 2 5 - 5 0 ~ \mathsf { g } }$ glucose, and Kayexalate 15-30 grams   
Clear, closed-loop communication when relaying orders to nursing staff   
Notifies the nurse they will come to patient’s room/nurses station to assess telemetry and   
review ECG   
Attempt to help the nurse recognize the urgency of the situation when asked if the   
interventions can wait

# Teaching points:

Important to assess for ECG changes with significant hyperkalemia: new ECG with prolonged PR, peaked T waves, broad QRS complexes (severe: no p waves, AV block, sine wave, vfib…) Urgent interventions include calcium gluconate, insulin and D50, albuterol, kayexalate – only kayexalate would help in the long-term.   
Potassium is affected considerably if a blood sample is hemolyzed, so it is reasonable to redraw it if you don’t believe it   
If severe, call nephrology to for HD
