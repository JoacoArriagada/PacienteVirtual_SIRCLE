# 8. Low blood pressure (non-pathologic) 1 student; 5 minutes

D6/521: FULL. Ms. Christine Green 62 year old woman with alcoholic cirrhosis, admitted initially for pain control of flare of chronic low back pain; has been doing well in this regard, on oral pain medications. Plan for discharge tomorrow.

PAGE: Please call RN re: patient Green. Evening BP 86/44. (###-###-####)

# Notes for nurse:

VS: T 98.6, P 70, BP 87/50 (with recheck 90/52), RR 16, sat $9 8 \%$ on RA   
Patient is stable, sitting up in her chair, playing cards with her daughter   
BP trend if asked – 90s systolic for the majority of her hospitalization; called because the   
parameters in admission orders say to call for systolic $\mathtt { < 9 0 }$   
Team plans to discharge her in the morning; she has no ongoing symptoms with the exception   
of mild back pain which is controlled with acetaminophen, ibuprofen and lidocaine patch   
Ask if you should recheck around midnight or so (few hours); or when you should worry; can   
parameter be changed?

# Desired learner actions:

Ask for full set of vitals Ask about patient symptoms

Inquire about BP trend

# Teaching points:

Not all low blood pressure is pathologic; this is particularly true in patients with end-stage liver disease or heart failure; older, thin patients or young, healthy women.   
Assess for symptoms, particularly mental status, in patients with low blood pressure.   
Sometimes, you don’t need to do anything but recheck!   
Clear to RN about when you would be worried.
