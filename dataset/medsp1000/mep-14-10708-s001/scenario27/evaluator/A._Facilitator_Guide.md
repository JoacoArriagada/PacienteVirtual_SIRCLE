11b. Tachycardia/Sepsis—Delivering bad news to family. The second student will practice breaking bad news; Mr. Krump ended up being transferred to the ICU. He developed respiratory failure, requiring intubation. He developed progressive anuric renal failure. There was a family meeting due to his poor prognosis and they requested continued full support while they talk with other family members, but

understand he is not doing well. Overnight, he became unresponsive. A code was called, and found he was in PEA arrest and was unable to be resuscitated.

The RN asks you to notify his wife, Betty who is in the family/visitor room where there are many other families/people present.

# Notes for Betty (played by facilitator):

Ask if Harold is ok while the student is trying to set the scene.   
When asked her understanding of the situation, say something like “Well I know they said his chances were not good, but I know he is a fighter.”   
Make sure students address what resources are available—chaplain, social work, priest, etc.

# Desired learner actions:

Ask Betty to join you in a quiet environment away from other patients’ families; ask if there is anyone else she wants to join.   
Ask Betty her understanding of the current situation regarding her husband   
Use straightforward language without medical jargon to communicate what happened and that the patient died.   
Offer support and resources for her

# Teaching Points:

Balance being sensitive to family member’s needs while recognizing your own time constraints as a busy cross-cover intern; use other team members (patient’s RN, chaplain/priest) to help with support and additional resources
