# 6. Telemetry case: 1 student (10 minutes)

D6/515: FULL. Mr. Cooper Sam is a 70-year-old man with history of rate controlled atrial fibrillation on warfarin admitted for community acquired pneumonia, on ceftriaxone and azithromycin.

PAGE: Please call RN re: patient Sam. Telemetry showing atrial fibrillation with rates in the 140s. (###- ###-####)

# Notes for nurse:

When asked for a set of vitals: T 99.8F, P 144, BP 104/60, RR 26, sat $93 \%$ on 2 L NC; the respiratory rate has increased slightly from previous, oxygen sat has remained the same – heart rates at the time of admission in the 80s   
Patient is complaining of palpitations, but otherwise denies any cardiorespiratory symptoms; not feeling lightheaded   
Missed his evening dose of metoprolol 25mg PO BID, held at nursing discretion because his BP was 100/60 which the nurse felt was too low to receive metoprolol

# Desired learner actions:

Obtain a fresh set of vitals   
Ask about patient symptoms – is it new?   
Once established the patient is stable by repeat vitals and reports of no symptoms; verbal order   
for nurse to have an AV nodal blocking agent ready for administration – metoprolol 5 mg IV   
versus diltiazem 10 mg IV   
ECG, lytes with K, Mg   
Agree to assess patient urgently

# Teaching points:

It is common for atrial fibrillation to worsen in a patient who is acutely ill; unless unstable,   
would recommend not holding beta blockade due to the risk of rebound tachycardia   
It is of utmost importance to assess a patient with a fib with RVR for stability – if unstable, needs   
cardioversion; if stable, can try pharmacologic treatment (beta blockade, calcium channel   
blockers)   
Goal ${ \mathsf { H R } } < 1 1 0$ for patients with atrial fibrillation
