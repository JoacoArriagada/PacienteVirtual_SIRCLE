PAGE: Please call RN re: patient Johnson; hit a nurse while attempting to do cares. Restraint order needed. (###-###-####).

# Notes for nurse:

Patient has become increasingly delirious, while usually oriented to person and place is now only oriented to person. Seems to be making nonsense stories. While attempting to reposition patient in bed, he became verbally and physically aggressive (tried to hit a staff member). He is yelling in his room, disturbing other patients but is no longer displaying physical aggression. You are requesting soft wrist restraints and lorazepam to calm him; be persistent about this need.

Vitals most recently: T 100.8F, P 99, BP 98/67, sat $96 \%$ on RA, RR 18. Has had foley catheter in place for five days; urine appears cloudy. No respiratory or GI symptoms.

# Desired learner actions:

Agree to evaluate patient urgently.   
Acknowledge the desire for restraints, but communicate that you will not do so without evaluating the patient. Do NOT give lorazepam.   
Ask for fresh set of vitals – acknowledge fever.   
Verbal orders for urinalysis, clinical concern for CAUTI.   
Clear, closed-loop communication when relaying orders to nursing staff

# Teaching points:

Onset of altered mental status in a hospitalized patient should trigger consideration of an acute infection.   
In patients with a foley catheter, fever could represent CAUTI.   
Every patient should be examined before chemically or physically restraining them; physical restraints should be a last resort.   
Lorazepam can worsen delirium; consider a low-dose antipsychotic if needed (haloperiodol, quetiapine)—for dangerous agitation is appropriate. Try behavioral interventions first. Dementia is a major risk factor for the development of delirium.
