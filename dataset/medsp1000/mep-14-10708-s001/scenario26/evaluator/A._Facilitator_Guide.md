Mr. Harold Krump 70 year old man with lung cancer admitted for scheduled chemotherapy seven days ago. His hospital course has been complicated by sepsis, presumably from line-source. Line has been pulled; patient is on piperacillin-tazobactam.

PAGE: Please call RN re: patient Krump; telemetry with heart rates in 120s. (###-###-####)

# Notes for nurse:

VS: T 101.6F, P 126, BP 94/56, RR 22, sat $92 \%$ on RA – if asked for a second set of vitals – T 101.0F, P 130, BP 88/44, RR 24, sat $91 \%$ on RA; has become increasingly tachycardic over the course of the day; low BP is relatively new over the last 2-3 hours   
Patient has been complaining of lightheadedness; is newly disoriented to place (believes he is in the grocery store) and time (believes it is February 2005) and hasn’t urinated in the last 6 hours. Lines has been pulled out; was a PICC that was placed just last week.   
Has been getting piperacillin-tazobactam.   
Patient is currently general care – if student does not volunteer change in status, ask if they would like him to remain general care (“My nursing supervisor is wondering if you need him moved; beds are tight in the IMC/ICU – can we just manage this on the floor?”)

# Desired leaner actions:

Agree to assess patient urgently   
Verbal orders for 1 L NS bolus, transition to higher level of care – IMC v. ICU (will depend on   
their hospitals capabilities)   
ECG   
Clear, closed-loop communication when relaying orders to nursing staff   
Inquires about antibiotics, if they’ve received doses – start vancomycin   
Inquires about end-organ damage: mental status changes, urine output

# Teaching points:

Sinus tachycardia often signifies an underlying pathology – in this case, severe sepsis.   
When determining if a patient is “sick”, think about end organ damage – altered mental status, urine output are two that can be assessed easily at the bedside.   
When a patient is unstable, it is important to advocate for their needs – if they need to have a higher level of care, demand this occurs.   
Finer point, only if it comes up – he should be on vancomycin or other coverage for MRSA given he developed catheter associated bloodstream infection as an inpatient.
