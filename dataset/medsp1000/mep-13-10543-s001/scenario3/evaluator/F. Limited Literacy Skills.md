**Limited literacy skills**

**Scenario:**

Limited literacy skills

**Scenario goal:**

Discuss with patient/ family their reasons for non-compliance with medications and appointments

**Scenario objectives:**

1. Explore the reasons for lack of compliance to medications in a professional manner.
2. Explore the reasons for missing clinic appointments.
3. Allow patient and family to communicate their perceptions and understanding of their child’s disease and treatment.
4. Understand patient and family’s perspective in a respectful and empathetic manner and partner with the patient/family to help them overcome their challenges/barriers.

**Debriefing Objectives:**

1. Create a safe environment which promotes open feedback, communication and self-directed learning and assessment

**Role-play exercise:**

The following role-play exercise involves two characters, a patient’s mother and you. It takes place in the Pediatric Hematology/Oncology Outpatient clinic.

You are a pediatric hematology/oncology fellow taking care of Mary, a 6 year old Caucasian girl diagnosed recently with neuroblastoma. She started treatment 3 weeks ago and is here for a clinic visit. She missed a few appointments yesterday and the appointment office called you earlier saying that they left multiple messages on her cell phone. The nurse who did the medication reconciliation today tells you that she just doesn’t get it – the discharge summary has clear instructions on how to take the medications and in spite of that the mother has switched medicines around giving Bactrim daily and Zofran on weekends instead of the other way around. Also, the mother had called 2 days ago stating Mary was having redness and itching and you had requested she get over the counter Benadryl and hydrocortisone cream. It appears that Mary’s rash is now worse and the nurse tells you she has not been giving her the Benadryl or using the hydrocortisone.

You have also received multiple complaints that she gets angry and loud anytime someone asks her why she has been missing appointments and not following instructions well. The team has been going the extra mile in providing print outs of appointment schedules, chemotherapy calendars, education materials etc and so they feel frustrated that she hasn’t been following through.

You are going to meet the parent in the clinic to discuss concerns raised by the rest of the medical team as everyone is worried that if the mother is non-compliant so early on in treatment Mary will not get the right care that she needs.

**Points to remember:**

1. Obtain an interval history and discuss your concerns regarding non-compliance and missed appointments in a sensitive and respectful manner.
2. Engage parent to discuss her perceptions and beliefs of lack of compliance/adherence to prescribe therapy
3. Try to elicit the barriers to care and compliance
4. Establish a partnership and work towards establishing trust and offer solutions to overcome any identified barriers

**Limited literacy skills**

**Role-play exercise:**

The following role-play exercise involves two characters, a patient’s mother (you) and a physician. It takes place in the Pediatric Hematology/Oncology Outpatient clinic.

**Role-play Instructions:**

Here are some things you need to know about role-play.

1. Pay attention to the role details. The instructions have specific goals, which will become apparent.
2. Try to stay in the role. Try to think like the person described in the part.

**Mother’s role:**

You are a single mom with 3 children. Mary, your youngest daughter, was diagnosed with cancer 4 weeks ago. She was complaining of abdominal pain for a few months and you took her to your local doctor multiple times and were told she is ‘well’ and then at one visit the doctor does a few extra tests though he is sure there is nothing to worry about. The next day he calls you and tells you she has cancer.

You don’t understand how things changed in 1 day with a few tests when she has had pain for so many months.

Since your local doctor cannot give her the medicines she needs you now come to this big medical center that is 2 hours from where you live. The doctors have said she needs to come to the clinic weekly and sometimes has to be admitted for 2-3 days at a stretch. The day she was discharged from the hospital is a blur to you. You were handed a bunch of papers and told to follow all the instructions in it. You had expressed to the social worker that you cannot read or write when she asked you to fill up some papers but no one other than her seems to care about that.

You hardly have money to feed your kids every day. The other day you called because Mary had a rash and the doctor told you to get some over the counter medications but you couldn’t afford it. It has been hard watching her suffer but you don’t know what to do. You do not drive. You don’t have much support except for a friend. When you were younger you battled depression and over the years have come a long way. You however, don’t handle stress well. And the past few weeks have been very stressful.

Everyone you meet in the clinic doesn’t seem to care. You are also worried they see you as a mother who cannot take care of her child and if they will take her away from you.

**Points to remember:**

1. You cannot read or write and you have told the social worker this and yet everyone expects you to read the appointment guide and the medication instructions and follow through.
2. Your understanding of her cancer and the care she needs is very limited.
3. You could not afford the over the counter medications to help her rash due to lack of money and you are afraid to share this with your doctor. You are hoping they don’t find out.
4. You are scared they will take her away from you for not getting her medications on time and missing appointments.
