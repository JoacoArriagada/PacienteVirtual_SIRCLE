**Role-play exercise:**

The following role-play exercise involves two characters, a patient’s parent and a physician. It takes place in the Pediatric Hematology/Oncology Outpatient setting.

You are a pediatric hematology/oncology fellow caring for Pedro, 2 year old boy with a newly diagnosed brain tumor. He presented with a large third ventricular tumor and obstructive hydrocephalus. He underwent a biopsy and endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV) at an outside hospital. Since the ETV last week he is clinically doing well. Pathology is medulloblastoma.

You are going to meet with his family to discuss the diagnosis and an overall treatment plan (including further work-up and overview of treatment approach). His family recently moved to the US from Mexico.

**New Diagnosis**

**Role-play exercise:**

The following role-play exercise involves two characters, a patient’s parent (you) and a physician. It takes place in the Pediatric Hematology/Oncology Outpatient setting.

**Role-play Instructions:**

Here are some things you need to know about role-play.

1. Pay attention to the role details. The instructions have specific goals, which will become apparent.
2. Try to stay in the role. Try to think like the person described in the part.

**Parent’s role:**

You are an illegal immigrant from Mexico and have been in the US for the past 10 months. You moved to the US along with your son and two other children in the hope of a better life here.

You work both a day and night job.

You understand English and can have a conversation if it involves simple information.

Since the surgery last week your son has been doing very well. He is not sleepy and fussy anymore. You were not present when the surgeon met your wife at the time of discharge. Your wife’s understanding was that the surgery went well and they “got what they wanted” and “needs to follow-up at another hospital with more specialists” – which means they removed his tumor and you can now go back to your life as it was before and he just needs a few follow-up appointments at a bigger hospital.

You have no medical insurance and you have given your employer a false name. You have been worried if someone will find out and if it will affect all of you.

You are not sure why you are here in the clinic today and don’t know what a pediatric oncologist does.

**Points to remember:**

1. You are waiting for the pediatric oncologist to talk to you.
2. You are here thinking the entire tumor has been removed. You are not aware that Pedro has only had a biopsy and needs more treatment. This news is shocking to you.
3. You are being told he has cancer and you will do anything to make sure your child gets better but you are very scared. You don’t have insurance. You don’t have legal status.
4. You believe the doctors know best and are not used to questioning doctors or asking for clarifications when you don’t understand something.
