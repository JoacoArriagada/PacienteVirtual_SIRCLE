**End of life/hospice care**

**Role-play exercise:**

The following role-play exercise involves two characters, a patient’s parents and a physician. It takes place in the Pediatric Hematology/Oncology inpatient setting.

**Role-play Instructions:**

Here are some things you need to know about role-play.

1. Pay attention to the role details. The instructions have specific goals, which will become apparent.
2. Try to stay in the role. Try to think like the person described in the part.

**Parents’ role:**

You are a temporary immigrant from Riyadh staying in USA exclusively for the medical care of your child. You came to the United States with high hopes for a cure but you have seen your child’s health decline over the past few months. You believe in Allah as the supreme power and follow Islamic religious beliefs of not interfering with Allah’s plans. Your cousin just found out that there may be a clinical study in a different state that Amina may benefit from. You don’t want to give up on curing her.

You want the best for your child but also wish to take her back home if no curative options are available.

You are not sure what treatment options are available back home for a dying patient. In the unfortunate event of your child’s passing, you would like to follow your religious practices and bury her within a day.

Your child’s pediatric oncologists have been discussing various options; however, you are still holding on to hope and not prepared to discuss the possibility of death. You are not familiar with palliative care and hospice services – though that has been mentioned a few times. You are also not familiar with resuscitation measures and how that would affect the dying process. You would like to ensure that your wishes and religious beliefs/practices are honored in all situations.

**Points to remember:**

1. You are waiting to the pediatric oncologist to talk to you.
2. You are not prepared to face the situation that your child might be dying and no cure is available.
3. You are sad that you do not have adequate family/community support and do not have your religious guide to help you with making medical decisions.
4. You would like to take your child back home to spend her last few days with family. Her 4 siblings are back in Riyadh.
5. You are overwhelmed with the amount of medical care that she requires and not sure if you could keep her comfortable at home and wonder if you should just keep her here.
6. You have heard words like ‘DNR’, ‘DNI’, ‘hospice care’ being mentioned many times but you don’t understand what that means.
