**Medication non-compliance**

**Scenario:**

Medication non-compliance

**Scenario goal:**

Discuss with patient/ family their reasons for non-compliance with medications

**Scenario objectives:**

1. Explore the reasons for lack of compliance to medications in a professional manner.
2. Allow patient and family to communicate their perceptions and reservations regarding their child’s treatment.
3. Understand patient and family’s perspective in a respectful and empathetic manner.
4. Provide counseling to the family to help them understand benefits of therapy and offer feasible suggestions to facilitate compliance
5. Open discussion with family regarding other psychosocial circumstances that might affect patient’s compliance to therapy.

**Debriefing Objectives:**

1. Create a safe environment which promotes open feedback, communication and self-directed learning and assessment

**Role-play exercise:**

The following role-play exercise involves two characters, a patient’s mother/father and you. It takes place in the Pediatric Hematology Outpatient clinic.

You are a pediatric hematology/oncology fellow taking care of Olumide, a 6 year old African-American boy with sickle cell disease. He has had several hospital admissions for vaso-occlusive crises. You prescribed hydroxyurea a few visits ago, but have noticed a lack of laboratory and clinical response to therapy (e.g. MCV is not increased and fetal hemoglobin is not elevated).

You are going to meet the patient and parent in the clinic to discuss concerns of recurrent pain crises and lack of response to therapy prescribed.

**Points to remember:**

1. Obtain an interval history and discuss your concerns regarding lack of response to therapy due to suspected non-compliance in a sensitive and respectful manner.
2. Engage parent to discuss his/her perceptions and beliefs of lack or compliance/adherence to prescribed therapy
3. Address parent’s concerns and discuss beneficial effects of therapy
4. Offer feasible solutions, if possible, to encourage compliance with recommended therapy

**Role-play exercise:**

The following role-play exercise involves two characters, a patient’s mother/father (you) and a physician. It takes place in the Pediatric Hematology Outpatient clinic.

**Role-play Instructions:**

Here are some things you need to know about role-play.

1. Pay attention to the role details. The instructions have specific goals, which will become apparent.
2. Try to stay in the role. Try to think like the person described in the part.

**Mother’s/Father’s role:**

You are an African-American, single mother/father of three children and live an hour away from the clinic. Olumide, your oldest son (6 years old), has sickle cell disease. It has been a challenge to work and take care of school aged children with one of them having multiple emergency room visits and hospital admissions. Support is limited. Your mother helps you with the other children on occasions.

Your child’s pediatric hematologists prescribed hydroxyurea and provided limited information regarding the medication. You tried to fill the prescription but you do not have money for the co-pay and it is getting expensive to travel to the clinic for appointments.

**Points to remember:**

1. You are waiting for the pediatric hematologist to see your son. You are frustrated with the lack of resources to help you financially.
2. You have at least 3 cousins with sickle cell disease and you don’t recall anyone taking hydroxyurea.
3. You have not started hydroxyurea due to lack of money and you are afraid to share this with your doctor. You are hoping they don’t find out.
4. You have been reading on the internet that hydroxyurea is a chemotherapy drug and you are afraid of the side effects and you worry taking the medications will lead to more hospital visits with infections.
5. Your understanding of the benefits of hydroxyurea is very limited. More information will be helpful.
