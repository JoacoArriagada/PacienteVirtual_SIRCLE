**Pediatric High Value Care Vignette:**

**Community Acquired Pneumonia - Know Thy Number Needed to Treat**

**Learner Worksheet**

**Vignette 1:**

Greg Fields is a previously healthy 3 yo M who was in his usual state of good health until 4 days ago, when he started to develop low-grade fevers to 101 degrees F, rhinorrhea, congestion, and cough. He had some decreased oral intake, but was otherwise active and playful. At that time, he was seen by his pediatrician who diagnosed him with a viral upper respiratory infection (URI) and sent him home with instructions for supportive care. Four days later, he was again seen in his PCP’s office with improvement of rhinorrhea and congestion, but increased cough. His mother told the pediatrician that his fevers have continued and today he appeared less active, was eating less, and she measured his temperature at 101.1 degrees F. On physical exam, the pediatrician noted bilateral crackles in the bases of the lungs. The patient was also mildly tachypneic, but had no notable retractions or increased work of breathing. His vital signs were significant for RR of 39 and a pulse oximeter reading of 93%.

Greg attends preschool and daycare. He has received all of his immunizations to date.
