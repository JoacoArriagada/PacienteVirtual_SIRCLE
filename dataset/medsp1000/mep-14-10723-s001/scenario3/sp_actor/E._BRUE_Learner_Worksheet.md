**Learner Worksheet**

**Vignette 2: Patient with decreased responsiveness**Liam Smith is a 10 week old baby who presents to the emergency department after an episode where parents felt that he was not responsive. Liam was asleep in his bassinet, having eaten 2 hours earlier. He ate approximately 2 ounces and burped well. Mom went to go pick him up to feed him. Initially he appeared alert but suddenly he began to have some irregular breathing, tensed up and turned pale. He did not have any vomiting, choking or gagging. He has not been ill nor has he had any cough, congestion or rhinorrhea. The entire episode lasted approximately 30 seconds. No CPR or rescue breaths were provided. He quickly returned to baseline. This is the first time this has ever happened. Concerned, Liam’s parents brought him into the emergency department.

Liam was born at 36 weeks via spontaneous vaginal delivery and went home with parents without any complications.

He normally feeds about 2oz of formula every 2-3 hours. He usually does tend to spit up frequently.

There is no family history of sudden death, cardiac arrhythmias or seizures.

He lives at home with mother and father. He is the first child. No pets. No smoke exposure.

On physical examination, he is well appearing, non-toxic and interacting appropriately with mom. His temperature is 37.2C (rectal), pulse is 126 beats per minute, respiration rate is 40 breaths per minute and blood pressure is 86/58. His anterior fontanelle is soft and flat. He is tracking appropriately. His heart, lung and abdominal exams are normal. There are no bruises and he moves all his extremities equally. Neurologically, he is alert and his tone and strength are appropriate for age.

During the encounter the mother receives a phone call from Liam’s grandmother reminding her that Liam’s aunt had Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome (WPW) as a child.

Liam’s parents are very nervous about going home from the emergency department and would prefer to be admitted to the hospital. They are worried that the event will happen again and would feel more comfortable staying in the hospital overnight. They also want to take him home with a home apnea monitor, which is something they read about on the internet.
