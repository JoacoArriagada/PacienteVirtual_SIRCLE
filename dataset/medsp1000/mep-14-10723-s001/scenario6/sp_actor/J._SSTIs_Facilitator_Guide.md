**Pediatric High Value Care Vignette:**

**Skin and Soft Tissue Infections (SSTI)**

**Facilitator Guide**

**Vignette 2:**

Roy is a 7-year-old male who presents to Urgent Care with a two-day history of a red, painful area on his right buttock. He reports that there was no specific trauma to this area but that he had participated in several outdoor games over the past several weeks at various camps. He has had several bug bites from being outdoors. His mother believes he may have had a low-grade fever the night before but did not take his temperature. He has no chronic medical conditions and takes no medications. He has no known allergies to medication.

His physical examination is notable for an oral temperature of 37.7°C, a heart rate (HR) of 78 beats per minute (bpm), a blood pressure (BP) of 94/70 mm Hg, and respiratory rate (RR) of 12 per minute. He is anxious about having to lie on his back as he states it hurts to lie on the lesion. His CV, Lung and Abdomen exams are unremarkable. On examination of his right buttock, there is an erythematous, warm, tender, 4 x 3 centimeter area on the right, with a fluctuant area that has a small indentation noted in the upper pole with a small scab. No other remarkable findings.

After the procedure, Roy's mother is concerned about this recurring. After asking about her family and social history you determine that his father has a history of recurrent abscesses requiring treatment. Although Roy has never had a SSTI before, she would like to be able to prevent this from happening again.
