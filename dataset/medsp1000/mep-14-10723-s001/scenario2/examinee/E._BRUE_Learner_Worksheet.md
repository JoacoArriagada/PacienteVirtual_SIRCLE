**Pediatric High Value Care**

**Brief Resolved Unexplained Events:What’s *BRUE*ing?**

**Learner Worksheet**

**Vignette 1:**

Alison Jones is a 10 week old healthy term baby who presented to the emergency department for evaluation after a brief episode of decreased responsiveness. She was in her normal state of health until 930AM. Approximately 20 minutes after she fed 3oz of formula, mom laid her down and noticed that it appeared that she could not breathe. She seemed to not be breathing for about 20 seconds. She was looking straight ahead, her face turned red and she seemed stiff. Mom picked her up and patted her back. She had some formula come out of her nose and mouth and then she took in a big gasp of air. The entire episode lasted approximately 90 seconds. She is now back to her baseline.

She normally feeds about 3oz of formula every 2-3 hours. She does tend to spit up frequently. She usually feeds very well and does not appear to choke.

There is no family history of sudden death, cardiac arrhythmias or seizures.

She lives at home with her mother and father. She is their first child. No pets. No smoke exposure.

On physical examination, she is well appearing, non-toxic and interacting appropriately with mom. Her temperature is 37.2C (rectally), pulse is 126 beats per minute, respiration rate is 40 breaths per minute and blood pressure is 86/58. Her anterior fontanelle is soft and flat. She is tracking appropriately. Her heart, lung and abdominal exams are normal. She has no bruises and she moves all her extremities equally. Neurologically, she is alert and her tone and strength are appropriate for age.
