**Pediatric High Value Care Vignette:**

**Blood Cultures - Fevers, Fight Songs, and Fagan Nomograms**

Rosa is an 11 year-old fully immunized previously healthy girl admitted with an 11 day history of fever and dehydration. She was rehydrated with intravenous fluids and regained appetite by hospital day 3. By hospital day #5, her fevers had defervesced and she was clinically improved without receiving antibiotics.

This child is 11 years old and from a small community 3 hours away from the hospital. Her father coaches the local high school track team and her mom coaches the cheerleading squad. Homecoming is this upcoming weekend.

For children hospitalized with fever of unknown origin, these hospitalizations often are extremely stressful for parents. Typically, the child has been sick for over a week, had multiple visits to the PCP, numerous outpatient tests, and the patient and family are usually exhausted emotionally and mentally by the time they are admitted to the hospital. Additionally, fever phobia and the medicalization of elevated temperatures often lead families to have high expectations for “answers.” Coupled with the fact that the “easy” things “have been ruled out,” families are often afraid that their child may have a serious or life threatening disease such as cancer. Straightforward conversations eliciting the patient’s and family’s fears, concerns, and expectations can set up opportunities for shared decision making and matching the medical team’s goals of care to the family’s values.
