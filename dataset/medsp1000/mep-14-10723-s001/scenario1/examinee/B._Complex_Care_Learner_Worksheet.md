**Pediatric High Value Care Vignette:**

**Complex Care - The Complexity of the Complex Patient**

**Learner Worksheet**

**Vignette:**

Patient SM is a 4 year old female born at 26 weeks gestation with a history of chronic lung disease, tracheostomy without ventilator, congenital hydrocephalus with ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt, diabetes insipidus, hypothyroidism, feeding intolerance, gastrostomy tube (GT)-dependence, gastroesophageal reflux and constipation. The patient has had numerous hospitalizations for pneumonia over the years, with the most recent event being approximately six months prior requiring a month-long stay. She is presenting to the Emergency Department with one episode of desaturation to 81% overnight while sleeping, picked up on her home monitor. The oxygen saturation gradually improved over 10 minutes, increasing to >95% (baseline) with suctioning and repositioning. The desaturation was observed by home nursing staff who noted that initially, there was not a good wave form on the pulse oximeter. The patient remained stable through the episode, without any change in behavior. She had been mildly tachycardic over the last day, but otherwise has been at her baseline. The patient is not verbal, but uses gestures and facial expressions to communicate. The patient has remained afebrile. She receives 2 liters per minute of oxygen through her tracheostomy collar and has not needed increased suctioning for secretions. The mother notes no signs of any cough or choking. The patient is tolerating her GT feeds. She has had no issues with breakthrough urination and, when asked, she denies any headaches or dizziness. There have been no sick contacts.

On physical examination, she is well appearing and at her baseline per mother.

Her vitals are: temperature 36.9, pulse 117 beats per minute, respiratory rate 24 and blood pressure 92/58. Her oxygen saturations are 99% on 2 liters per minute via tracheal collar. She is awake, alert, and smiling. Her mucous membranes are moist, VP shunt is palpable, tracheostomy is in place with the surrounding tissue being clean, dry, and intact and secretions are thin and clear. She has symmetric chest wall movement with no retractions, good air movement and no wheezing, rales, or rhonchi. Her abdomen was soft, nontender, mildly distended, gastrostomy tube in place clean, dry and intact.
