Part 2: Disclosure after Adverse Event – Inadvertent Cystotomy after C/S on demand

You are Patty Brown, a 27 year-old woman, expecting your first baby. You are healthy and enjoyed an uncomplicated pregnancy.

You want a cesarean because you attended your sister’s first delivery, which was complicated by shoulder dystocia, and you were traumatized. You can’t sleep at night due to the anxiety, when your doctor thought the baby was breech, you were so excited because then you’d have an excuse to have a cesarean section, and then when the baby was head-down, you cried. Now you are convinced that you want a section. Your sister’s husband is smaller than yours!

Your doctor returned from his conference and agreed to perform the elective cesarean section for you, which was scheduled for this morning. An inadvertent cystotomy (hole in your bladder) occurred during the cesarean section, which was recognized. A urologist was consulted during the surgery, who has repaired the hole and then looked inside your bladder with a camera to make sure that the hole was repaired well. A Foley catheter was left in your bladder. You have not seen your doctor yet, but the doctor you saw at your visit a few weeks ago is coming to talk to you about what happened.

You are upset about the complication. The baby is doing well and you are stable and otherwise well.

Try to imagine yourself in a similar situation! You will be lying on the exam table during the meeting. You can be a bit groggy after general anesthesia

Ask what happened?   
Why did it happen?   
How’s my baby?   
What went wrong?   
Is this common?   
When will the catheter come out?   
Should I have another cesarean section next time?   
What are the long term effects of my bladder being cut?
