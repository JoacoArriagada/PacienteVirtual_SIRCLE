Part 1: Counseling a patient regarding management of breech presentation at term

You are Shannon Smith, a 27 year-old woman, pregnant with your first child, at 36 weeks and 4 days gestational age. You are healthy, and enjoying an uncomplicated pregnancy. At your most recent visit to your obstetrician, he was uncertain about the fetal presentation and had requested an ultrasound, which demonstrates a baby in breech presentation. You are now seeing one of your doctors’ colleagues for a routine visit and will discuss the results of the ultrasound.

If asked, this is the information you know about your history:

no significant gynecologic history   
got pregnant easily, no history of sexually transmitted infections, normal yearly   
Pap tets   
No Medical Problems   
Surgeries: had a tonsillectomy, no anesthestic complications, no transfusions   
No family history   
No allergies, only medication is prenatal vitamins   
Social Hx: Married, no smoking, alcohol, street drugs, employed as a teacher   
You’ve gained $1 2 \mathrm { K g }$ during the pregnancy   
All of your routine blood tests have been normal

# Currently:

feeling well   
good fetal movement   
no vaginal bleeding or fluid leakage, only occasional uterine tightenings   
no symptoms of pre-eclampsia (headache, spots in front of eyes, bad swelling,   
abdominal pain)

The resident may ask you what you know about breech deliveries and what your opinion is. You would rather avoid a cesarean section and are easily convinced to let them try to turn the baby. You are not sure how you feel about a breech vaginal delivery because you’ve heard it might be unsafe.

You can ask questions like:

What do you suggest?   
Do you think it will work? (the turning procedure)   
What could go wrong?   
Should I just have a cesarean? (You would rather avoid a cesarean unless necessary)
