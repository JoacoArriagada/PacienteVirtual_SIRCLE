Part 1: Counseling patient regarding Cesarean Section “on demand”

You are Patty Brown, a 27 year-old woman, expecting your first baby. You are 36 weeks pregnant. You are healthy and enjoyed an uncomplicated pregnancy. You are being seen for a routine antenatal visit and you’ve decided to talk to the doctor about having a cesarean section. The resident will counsel you about your options.

No past medical history   
Past surgery – appendix removed at age 9, was ruptured.   
Medications - diclectin for nausea   
Allergies None   
Drugs No   
PObHx Uneventful pregnancy   
No Gestational diabetes, no high blood pressure Social Married, dentist

You want a cesarean because you attended your sister’s first delivery, which was complicated by shoulder dystocia, and you were traumatized. You can’t sleep at night due to the anxiety, when your doctor thought the baby was breech, you were so excited because then you’d have an excuse to have a cesarean section, and then when the baby was head-down, you cried. Now you are convinced that you want a section. Your sister’s husband is smaller than yours!

Despite being counseled about the risks of cesarean, you will be adamant that you want to have a cesarean section. You don’t have to be confrontational if they are adamant that you can’t have one, but explain that you will bring it up with your own obstetrician when he returns from vacation next week.
