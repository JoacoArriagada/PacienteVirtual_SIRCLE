# IV. Environment

a. Lab Set Up –Acute care medical room or ED room, can be simulated in a lab or take place in a real patient room. Helpful to be as similar to learners’ real work environment as possible. Can either have facilitator watch from one-way mirror, video tape encounter or have facilitator in room filling the role of the “nurse” (see V: Actors below) depending on resource availability.

b. Manikin Set Up- A low or high-fidelity manikin that can mimic various respiratory sounds and can be adjusted to improve/worsen with time and in response to therapeutic maneuvers. The manikin should be dressed in a patient gown and placed on a patient bed. If no manikin is available, the facilitator can also verbalize clinical changes to learners to recreate experience. Videos of patients in respiratory distress can be used to reinforce the clinical exam if a high fidelity manikin is not available.

c. Props –

i. Labs and imaging: see appendix B and Scenario data PowerPoint ii. Lines: one peripheral IV   
iii. Stethoscope for each learner   
iv. Suction tubing/system v. Monitors: Blood pressure cuff, heart rate monitor leads, oxygen saturation probe vi. Medications: empty or water filled containers labeled with the following medications: albuterol nebulized, ipratropium bromide nebulized, racemic epinephrine, dexamethasone, magnesium sulfate, prednisolone, vancomycin, ampicillin, cefotaxime, normal saline   
vii. Nebulizer tubing/system   
viii. Oxygen delivery system/airway 1. Bag-mask system with multiple size masks 2. Nasal cannula 3. Simple mask 4. Non-rebreather

d. Distractors:

i. If resources allow, can have monitor alarms beeping to mimic real patient and stress urgency of the situation.

V. Actors

a. Roles – nurse   
b. Who may play them – i. If no one else is available, the facilitator can play role while also observing learners and adjusting manikin status. ii. If other individuals are available, students, residents or actors can fill this role.   
c. Action Role – Simulate placement of IV and monitors, simulate administration of IV medications, provide additional patient history and imaging/lab results when learners ask for additional information.

# VI. Case Narrative

# Scenario 1: Asthma

Chief complaint: James Smith is a 5 year old $2 0 \mathrm { k g }$ male who presents to the emergency room with difficulty breathing. (given)

History of present illness: The patient has had a cough and runny nose for the past few days and today seems to have difficulty breathing. He has been afebrile and eating well at home. (given)

Past medical history: He has a history of wheezing with colds and has eczema. No prior hospitalizations. Has used an “inhaler” a few times in the past, but his mom does not know what it is called or when he last used it. (must ask)

Meds and allergies: “inhaler”. Otherwise none. (must ask)

Family/social history: Mom is a smoker. Sister has eczema. Everyone else is healthy. (must ask)

Patient’s initial exam (must ask or elicit via exam, for parts not reproducible by manikin, facilitator can provide information to learner verbally):

$\bigcirc$ VS: HR 135, RR 40, BP 97/60, Pox $9 5 \%$ , T 37.4.   
$\bigcirc$ Gen: alert, answering questions appropriately but speaking in short sentences   
$\bigcirc$ HEENT: clear rhinorrhea, otherwise normal   
$\bigcirc$ Resp: nonproductive cough, faint expiratory wheezes throughout on auscultation, subcostal retractions   
$\bigcirc$ CV: tachycardic, regular rhythm, cap refill less than 2 seconds, no murmurs, rubs or gallops, $^ { 2 + }$ femoral and radial pulses   
$\bigcirc$ Abd: normal, no hepatosplenomegaly   
$\bigcirc$ Extremities: warm and well perfused

Labs/Imaging: See appendix B (must ask)

Patient’s physiology: asthma exacerbation with poor aeration. Normal perfusion and mentation.

Scenario branch points (Steps are divided up based on assessment, diagnostics and treatments.   
They do not/should not necessarily need to be performed in this order).

o Assessment  Ideal pathway:

Apply monitors, assess vitals, perform initial exam: observe relatively quiet lung exam, tachypnea and retractionsrecognize respiratory distress due to lower airway obstruction

 Prompt:

If learner does not ask for vitals or apply monitors, nurse can comment on patient’s fast breathing, ask if learner wants to know respiratory rate, etc.  If learner does not correctly assess lung sounds, nurse can comment on “quiet” lung exam

 Ideal pathway:

Obtain brief history

 Prompt:

 If learner does not ask for history details, nurse can state she talked to the family and has more information if needed

# o Diagnostic

 Ideal pathway:

 Consider chest x-ray to assess for additional pathology, other causes of wheezing as patient does not improve with inhaled bronchodilator recognize hyperinflation (see appendix B)  Consider blood gasif obtained early in case, provide “early” gas with respiratory alkalosis from tachypnea. If gas repeated or initially obtained late in case, provide “late” gas with rising pCO2 due to air trapping (see appendix B)

 Prompt:

 If learner does not obtain additional labs/imaging, nurse can ask if they want any additional studies

o Treatment  Ideal pathway:

Administer $1 ^ { \mathrm { s t } }$ inhaled bronchodilator $+ / -$ ipratropium bromide (may need to prompt for dose-(albuterol: 1 nebule [2.5mg/3 ml $( 0 . 0 8 3 \% )$ ] or 4-8 puffs) bilateral wheeze increases (because increased aeration,) heart rate 140 (due to side effect of bronchodilator)

 Prompt:

If no intervention, saturations decrease, heart rate and respiratory rate increase and mentation becomes impaired

 Ideal pathway:

$2 ^ { \mathrm { n d } }$ inhaled bronchodilator $+ / -$ ipratropium bromide given $\Rightarrow$ persistent wheeze/retractions and saturations drop to $8 5 \%$ (due to VQ mismatch)

 Prompt:

 If no intervention, saturations decrease, heart rate and respiratory rate increase and mentation becomes impaired

 Ideal pathway: Oxygen administered (may need to prompt for amount of flow)saturations increase to $9 2 \%$ , persistent wheeze/retractions

 Prompt:

 If no intervention, saturations decrease, heart rate and respiratory rate increase and mentation becomes impaired

 Ideal pathway

Steroids given (may need to prompt for dose, (prednisolone: 1-2mg/kg/day steroids) no change

 Prompt

 If steroids not given, nurse can ask if any additional medications are needed

 Ideal pathway:

Consider giving additional inhaled bronchodilator $+ / -$ ipratropium, continuous albuterol, IV magnesium no change

 Prompt

 If no intervention done, nurse can ask if any additional medications are needed

Consider calling for additional support (rapid response, critical care, etc. based on institutional standards/protocol)

 Prompt:

 If addition support is not called at an appropriate time, nurse can ask if she should call anyone else for help

# VII. Instructors Notes

a. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer

i. If learners do not perform the appropriate next step, are delayed in performing the appropriate next step or choose an incorrect next step, the “prompts” listed above for each case can be used to move the case along on the right pathway.   
ii. In each case, the patient will frequently continue to deteriorate despite appropriate interventions in order to allow for discussion of how/when to step up management or transfer to higher level of care.

b. Tips to direct actors

i. One of the key objectives to the workshop is teaching the learners how to use the equipment provided. Therefore, the learners should be encouraged to attempt to do so on their own. The nurse can stress that it is his/her first day on the job and he/she is unfamiliar with how the equipment works to force learners to attempt the hands-on skills on their own.
