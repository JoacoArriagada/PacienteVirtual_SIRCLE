# III. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives

a. Primary

i. Recognize respiratory distress versus respiratory failure   
ii. Summarize signs/symptoms of upper airway obstruction, lower airway obstruction and lung tissue disease   
iii. Summarize treatment of upper airway obstruction, lower airway obstruction and lung tissue disease

b. Secondary

i. Demonstrate correct administration of treatment for upper airway obstruction, lower airway obstruction and lung tissue disease   
ii. Communicate and work effectively as a team

c. Critical actions checklist

i. Clinical Performance Checklists (Appendix A) were developed for each of the scenarios to assess four key areas:

1. Clinical assessment   
2. Diagnostic workup   
3. Medical treatment   
4. Teamwork skills

# VI. Case Narrative

# Scenario 1: Asthma

Chief complaint: James Smith is a 5 year old $2 0 \mathrm { k g }$ male who presents to the emergency room with difficulty breathing. (given)

History of present illness: The patient has had a cough and runny nose for the past few days and today seems to have difficulty breathing. He has been afebrile and eating well at home. (given)

Past medical history: He has a history of wheezing with colds and has eczema. No prior hospitalizations. Has used an “inhaler” a few times in the past, but his mom does not know what it is called or when he last used it. (must ask)

Meds and allergies: “inhaler”. Otherwise none. (must ask)

Family/social history: Mom is a smoker. Sister has eczema. Everyone else is healthy. (must ask)

Patient’s initial exam (must ask or elicit via exam, for parts not reproducible by manikin, facilitator can provide information to learner verbally):

$\bigcirc$ VS: HR 135, RR 40, BP 97/60, Pox $9 5 \%$ , T 37.4.   
$\bigcirc$ Gen: alert, answering questions appropriately but speaking in short sentences   
$\bigcirc$ HEENT: clear rhinorrhea, otherwise normal   
$\bigcirc$ Resp: nonproductive cough, faint expiratory wheezes throughout on auscultation, subcostal retractions   
$\bigcirc$ CV: tachycardic, regular rhythm, cap refill less than 2 seconds, no murmurs, rubs or gallops, $^ { 2 + }$ femoral and radial pulses   
$\bigcirc$ Abd: normal, no hepatosplenomegaly   
$\bigcirc$ Extremities: warm and well perfused

Labs/Imaging: See appendix B (must ask)

Patient’s physiology: asthma exacerbation with poor aeration. Normal perfusion and mentation.

Scenario branch points (Steps are divided up based on assessment, diagnostics and treatments.   
They do not/should not necessarily need to be performed in this order).

Obtain brief history

 Prompt:

 If learner does not ask for history details, nurse can state she talked to the family and has more information if needed

 Ideal pathway:

 Prompt:

 If learner does not obtain additional labs/imaging, nurse can ask if they want any additional studies

Administer $1 ^ { \mathrm { s t } }$ inhaled bronchodilator $+ / -$ ipratropium bromide (may need to prompt for dose-(albuterol: 1 nebule [2.5mg/3 ml $( 0 . 0 8 3 \% )$ ] or 4-8 puffs) bilateral wheeze increases (because increased aeration,) heart rate 140 (due to side effect of bronchodilator)

If no intervention, saturations decrease, heart rate and respiratory rate increase and mentation becomes impaired

Steroids given (may need to prompt for dose, (prednisolone: 1-2mg/kg/day steroids) no change

 Prompt

 If steroids not given, nurse can ask if any additional medications are needed

Consider giving additional inhaled bronchodilator $+ / -$ ipratropium, continuous albuterol, IV magnesium no change

 Prompt

 If no intervention done, nurse can ask if any additional medications are needed

# VIII. Debriefing Plan

a. Method of debriefing

i. Individual or group discussion. ii. In addition to reviewing the questions listed below, the facilitator also should demonstrate any hands-on skills and show learners how to use any equipment that they struggled with during the scenario and allow them to practice those skills. b. Rules for the debriefing i. Remind learners that it is a safe learning environment, that they are not being judged and that their questions and actions are confidential.

c. Questions to facilitate the debriefing i. General questions to be asked after each scenario:

1. What went well?

2. What would they do differently next time?

4. How did you feel you worked together as a team Review their Teamwork scores from the Clinical Performance Checklist (appendix A) for each case.

5. What questions do they have about the scenario?

# ii. Scenario 1:

1. Is the patient in respiratory distress versus respiratory failure? Distress: Increased respiratory rate/effort to try to maintain adequate gas exchange despite airway obstruction   
2. Was this an upper respiratory, lower respiratory or lung tissue issue and why? Asthma $=$ lower respiratory. Tachypnea, increased respiratory effort (retractions, nasal flaring), wheeze (usually expiratory), prolonged expiratory phase, cough   
3. Summarize initial treatment of lower airway obstruction due to asthma Inhaled short acting beta agonist, oxygen, systemic enteral steroids, $I V$ magnesium sulfate, etc. Dosages of medications can be reviewed and stressed dependent on level of learner.   
4. How comfortable are they administering inhaled medications?   
5. Why did the wheezing get worse with initial treatments with inhaled bronchodilator? Initially quite lung exam due to poor air movement. Wheeze can increase with beta agonist due to improved aeration.   
6. Why did hypoxia get worse with beta agonist? V/Q mismatch can cause initial hypoxia which can worsen with beta agonist. Beta agonist can lead to pulmonary vasodilatation and increasing perfusion to poorly ventilated lung units, resulting in ventilation perfusion mismatch and causing hypoxemia.
