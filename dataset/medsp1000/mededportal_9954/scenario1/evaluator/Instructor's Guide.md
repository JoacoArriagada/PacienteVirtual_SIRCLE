**In-Situ Mock Code Anaphylaxis Instructor Guide**

Educational Objectives:

* Participants will be able to recognize and provide acute management for a patient with anaphylaxis
* Participants will demonstrate appropriate communication with family member in the setting of patient deterioration
* Participants will demonstrate appropriate team communication in an emergency scenario
* Participants will identify hospital resources available to the team in situations of escalation of care

**Initial Information:** (give this information to the nurse and then start case. Mock code residents and pharmacy should be paged as nurse is given the information):

6 month old infant directly admitted from an outside hospital(OSH) for febrile illness diagnosed as pneumonia. Unable to obtain IV at OSH so transferred here for further treatment. CBC, BMP, blood Cx, cath UA and urine culture obtained on arrival, but nursing was unable to start IV after multiple attempts so IM ceftriaxone was given approximately 30 minutes ago. Parent has called the nurses station because the infant started having persistent vomiting about 10 minutes ago and is now having respiratory distress.

**Initial Vitals:**

HR 190 RR 50 BP 57/35 T 38 POX 88 on RA

**Physical Exam Findings (may be given as residents perform appropriate assessment):**

* Patchy erythema to trunk
* Moderate respiratory distress, SC retractions, nasal flaring, diffuse wheezing
* Tachycardic, regular rhythm. No murmur. Pulses 2+. Cap refill 3 sec
* Swelling to tongue (if OP exam attempted)

**Lab Findings:**

* STAT CXR, iSTAT lytes, and VBG available as handouts
* If other lab obtained (CBC, BMP, etc) will be available in 30 minutes
* Labs from admit (documented on H&P)

-CBC: WBC 16 (60% Lymph, 30% Poly), H/H 12/38, Platelets 350

-UA WNL

-BMP: WNL

**Case Progression:**

* If placed on O2 by NC or mask, pulse ox improves to 90%
* Updraft treatment or nasal suction will result in no improvement in wheezing/respiratory distress
* Fluid bolus does not improve blood pressure
* If no epinephrine given after 5 min, decrease BP to 50/30, pt becomes unresponsive and residents will be prompted that rash is worsening and tongue is swelling (verbally or with image of facial angioedema)
* If IM epinephrine given, resolution of respiratory distress/wheezing and BP improves to 75/50, HR to 150, RR to 35, SpO2 to 98%
* If IV epinephrine given, resolution of respiratory distress/wheezing and BP increases to 155/98, HR to 180, RR to 35, SpO2 to 98%
* To end case residents must discuss further treatment that will be necessary: PICU transfer, Epi drip, steroids, etc.

**Debrief:**

* General
  + What went well during your performance of this scenario?
  + What would you change about your performance?
* Communication
  + General Questions for team
    - How was your communication as a team?
    - Was there a clear leader? What other roles were assigned?
    - Was your nurse/pharmacist/parent a part of your team?
    - Looking back what would you change as far as your team performance/communication?
  + Questions for Nurse
    - How was communication from your standpoint?
    - How could your role have been better utilized?
  + Questions for SP “Family Member”
    - How was communication from your perspective?
    - How could the team have improved communication of what was going on?
    - Did anything occur that made you more concerned or more reassured about the care your family member was receiving?
  + Questions for Pharmacy
    - How was communication from your standpoint?
    - How could your role have been better utilized?
* Hospital Resources
  + What resources would have improved your response to this scenario? (ex RT, SW, MET team, code team, etc)
  + Where is the nearest code cart?
  + What medications are available in the emergency drug box that you could have utilized in this scenario if pharmacy was unavailable?
* Medical management
  + What is the diagnosis?
  + What is optimal management of anaphylaxis?
  + What are the complications of delayed recognition and treatment of anaphylaxis?

**Teaching Pearls:**

* Anaphylaxis:
* Often under recognized in infancy
* Type I hypersensitivity reaction: Antigen binds to circulating IgE from previous exposure -> mast cells and basophils are activated and degranulation occurs -> histamine is released -> mucocutaneous edema and inflammation, bronchospasm, smooth muscle spasm, and increased capillary permeability result
* Food is most common trigger in infancy/childhood followed by medication; hymenoptera envenomation most common in adults
* Most common foods: peanuts, tree nuts, fish, shellfish, milk, eggs; Most common drugs: antibiotics (especially penicillins and cephalosporins) and NSAIDS
* Exposure may be oral, inhaled, parenteral, or transdermal ( remember latex in patients with spina bifida)
* Characterized by exposure to potential trigger followed by rapid onset of symptoms within minutes to hours
* Signs/symptoms: skin/mucosa involvement (urticaria, pruritis, flushing, angioedema), respiratory tract (rhinitis, dyspnea, bronchospasm/wheezing, laryngeal edema/stridor, hypoxia), hypotension, CNS dysfunction (hypotonia, dizziness, syncope, headache, seizure), GI symptoms (crampy abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea)
* 10-20% lack skin symptoms
* DDx should include other causes of acute urticaria (urticaria, mastocytosis, hereditary angioedema), acute respiratory distress (airway obstruction, FB aspiration, croup, bronchiolitis, asthma, congenital airway malformation), acute GI symptoms (pyloric stenosis, malrotation/volvulus, intussusception, AGE, protein induced enterocolitis), acute CNS symptoms (seizure, head trauma, CNS infection, stroke) or shock (septic, cardiogenic, hypovolemic, neurogenic)
* Respiratory arrest secondary to airway obstruction is the leading cause of death; mortality rate is <2% in children
* Treatment
* Removal of suspect inciting antigen (ex: stop medication infusion)
* Initial treatment1:1000 Epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg (max 0.3 mL) IM (preferred) or SQ, repeat Q 10-20 min prn, consider Epinephrine drip for long acting agent
* Volume resuscitation
* Supplemental oxygen
* Antihistamine(H1, H2 blockers)
* Inhaled Beta2 agonist
* Corticosteroids
