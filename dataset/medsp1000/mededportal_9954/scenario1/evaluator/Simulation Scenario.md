**Infant Anaphylaxis In-Situ Simulation Case**

Target Audience: Pediatric Residents, Pediatric Floor Nurses, Pharmacists

Learning Objectives

1. Primary
   * 1. Participants will assess a pediatric inpatient who develops anaphylaxis
     2. Participants will manage a pediatric inpatient who develops anaphylaxis
     3. Participants will demonstrate appropriate team communication in an emergency scenario
     4. Participants will demonstrate appropriate communication with family member in the setting of patient deterioration
     5. Participants will identify hospital resources available to the team in situations of escalation of care
2. Secondary
3. Participants will be able to recognize shock in a pediatric patient and distinguish the cause
4. Participants will be able to provide acute management for a patient with anaphylaxis
5. Epinephrine (including dosing and route of administration)
6. Fluid resuscitation
7. Oxygen administration
8. Stop inciting medication infusion if present
9. Other therapies (albuterol, steroids, antihistamines)
10. Participants will be able to find necessary resuscitation equipment and medications available in the Code Card and Emergency Drug Box
11. Critical actions checklist
    1. Activation of Medical Emergency Team or Code Team
    2. Use medical record and/or parent to obtain more history
    3. Designate team leader and assign roles to other team members
    4. Place patient on a monitor
    5. Perform brief, problem focused physical exam
    6. Administer oxygen
    7. Administer appropriate epinephrine dose, from Emergency Drug Box if pharmacy not present
    8. Isotonic fluid bolus
    9. Prepare second epinephrine dose for rebound symptoms
    10. Order steroids to prevent late phase response
    11. Transfer patient to a higher level of care for closer monitoring

Case Narrative

Initial information given freely to the nurse before entering the patient’s room: You are covering this patient while their nurse is on break. From report you know that the patient is a 6 month old infant directly admitted from an outside hospital for febrile illness diagnosed as “pneumonia.” They were unable to obtain a PIV at the OSH so the patient was transferred to our facility for further treatment. Complete blood count, basic metabolic panel, blood culture, urinalysis and urine culture were obtained on arrival, but nursing staff has been unable to start IV after multiple attempts. A dose of IM ceftriaxone was given approximately 30 minutes ago. The parent just called the nurses station because the infant started having persistent vomiting about 10 min ago and is now having difficulty breathing. (All other information should be obtained by reviewing the H&P or questioning the parent)

Information available from H&P or SP parent:

The patient, Penny Puffy, is a 6 month old female infant with 3 day history of fever and Tmax of 104. She has had reduced oral intake and only 2 wet diapers in the last 24 hours. No vomiting, diarrhea, cough or rhinorrhea. She was initially taken to an outside hospital where a chest x-ray was performed that was read as pneumonia by the emergency room physician. The OSH nurses were unable to obtain a PIV after multiple sticks and she was transferred to our facility late last night. The patient has up to date immunizations. The patient’s diet consists of breastfeeding ad lib. The patient has been developing normally so far.

Past medical history: No previous hospitalizations or surgeries, PCP has treated 2 ear infections in the last 2 months

Meds and allergies: no current medications, has previously had a rash with amoxicillin

Family/social history: The patient lives with mom, dad and a 4 year old brother in a local small town. No smoke exposure. No pets. The patient does attend daycare.

Scenario conditions initially

Standardized patient opening statement to initial responder (nurse): “Penny started vomiting about 10 minutes ago and now she is having trouble breathing”

Patient’s initial exam:

Vital signs: HR 190, RR 50, BP 57/35, Pulse Ox 88% on room air

Gen: Patient is in moderate respiratory distress

HEENT: swelling to tongue if (OP exam attempted)

Lungs: Diffuse wheezing, subcostal retractions, nasal flaring

CV: tachycardia, no murmur, 2+ pulses, capillary refill 3 seconds

Skin: diffuse patchy erythema to trunk

All other physical exam within normal limits

Scenario branch points

Response to treatments

If placed on oxygen via nasal cannula or mask, pulse ox will improve to 90%

If nasal suction performed no improvement in symptoms

If albuterol or racemic epinephrine updraft ordered RT is on the way (but never arrives)

If fluid bolus given no improvement in blood pressure

If IM epinephrine given resolution of respiratory distress/wheezing and BP improves to 75/50, HR to 150, RR to 35, SpO2 to 98%

If IV epinephrine given, resolution of respiratory distress/wheezing and BP improves to 155/98, HR to 180, RR to 35, SpO2 to 98%

Changes in patients condition

If no epinephrine given within 5 minutes of physician arrival decrease BP to 50/30, the patient becomes unresponsive and residents will be prompted that the rash is worsening and the tongue/mouth is swelling (or shown a picture of angioedema and or diffuse urticarial)

To end case residents must identify to instructor further treatment that they feel is necessary. This may include transfer to higher level of care, increased monitoring for a specific period of time, epinephrine drip, steroids, allergy alert, etc

Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

Scenario programming

Optimal management path: Optimal management in this scenario includes early recognition of anaphylaxis and treatment with IM epinephrine.

Potential complications path: Potential complications relate to missing the diagnosis of anaphylaxis. After 5 minutes if no epinephrine is given the patient will become unresponsive and team will be prompted with a description or picture of angioedema and diffuse urticarial.

Potential errors path: Potential errors for this scenario are treated the same as missing the diagnosis of anaphylaxis with physical exam information given to help return to the correct path.

Debriefing Plan

Questions to facilitate the debriefing:

Communication

How was your communication as a team?

Was there a clear leader? What other roles were assigned?

Was your nurse/pharmacist/parent a parent of your team? How was communication from their perspective? Could they have been better utilized?

Looking back, what would you change as far as your team performance/communication?

Ask SP parent: Did anything occur that made you more concerned or more reassured about the care your family member was receiving?

Hospital Resources

What hospital resources would have improved your response to this scenario? (Medical emergency team, code clue team, RT, social work, etc)

Where is your nearest code cart?

What medications are available in the emergency drug box that you could have utilized if pharmacy was unavailable?

Medical Management

What is the diagnosis?

What does optimal management include?

What are complications of delayed recognition and treatment?

What is needed for ongoing care
