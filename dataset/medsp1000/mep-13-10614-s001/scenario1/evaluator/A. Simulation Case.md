**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The learners enter the room to find a groaning and distressed patient as well as a frantic nurse. The learners should quickly assemble as team with a leader who has emerged to delegate roles: evaluate patient, review chart, discuss the situation with the nurse, and review intravenous infusions and patient monitors. The leader should then ask each team member what they have found and briefly review with the team to create a shared mental model. If a team member identifies the ECG changes consisted with hyperkalemia, which can be corroborated with an empty KCL infusion bag, then calcium chloride can be administered to avert an evolving PEA arrest. If they miss this brief ECG morphology change, then the team should quickly identify PEA arrest. The team should initiate ACLS therapy and identify the cognitive aid for ACLS management. The first page of the cognitive aid resource contains instructions for managing PEA arrest. The team should follow this guide, which calls for an arterial blood gas test. The blood gas results, which are on a sheet unavailable to the team, will reveal severe hyperkalemia. The will manage hyperkalemic arrest either from memory or by evaluating the cognitive aid booklet, which reveals appropriate therapy. The team should follow up this intervention with a nephrology consult for emergency hemodialysis.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. The learners do not assemble a team with distinct roles and responsibilities and a leader seldom emerges to assist with this division of labor. The team frequently evaluates each area of information either as a whole or in groups. The nurse can ask who is the leader of this team. They fail to use infection prevention gowns.
2. The learners do not share found information, nor do they think aloud and thereby allow the team to create a shared mental model. The nurse can continue to ask what they think is going on in order to facilitate a shared mental model.
3. The learners are unable to pick up the subtle and brief ECG changes consistent with hyperkalemic arrest prior to the onset of PEA. Subsequently, they need to work with the starting point of ACLS management of cardiac arrest. The nurse can assist by asking the group about the etiology of the cardiac arrest: PEA.
4. The learners often do not recognize the ACLS cognitive aid booklet, and subsequently poorly manage the patient’s cardiac arrest. The nurse can assist with identifying the cognitive aid booklet.
5. After identification and treatment of the hyperkalemic arrest, the team frequently fails to identify long-term treatment for hyperkalemia. The nurse can suggest definitive therapy for this patient and ask about hemodialysis.
6. There are many opportunities to identify lapses in team cognition, and the instructor should identify a few of the opportunities prior to initiating the simulation in order to exemplify the teams use or lack of use of this skill during the debriefing. Appendices C and E offer opportunities to identify these KSAs.
7. Additionally, the KSA review chart found in Appendix J should be provided for the learners after the debriefing to reinforce the debriefing discussion.
