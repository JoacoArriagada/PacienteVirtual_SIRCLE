CASE #2

**Mentor:** You have an opportunity to mentor Mark/Jackie as part of his/her POM experience.  You have seen her/him work and made some assumptions about what kind of mentoring he/she needed. You think that part of what you want to do includes 1) getting him/her to accept feedback in a less personal way, and 2) making certain he/she knows how others feel when he/she makes comments perhaps not appropriate for her/his training level and setting. (For example, ‘I ‘m not certain you are doing that exam correctly according to what I read in Bates’. This was expressed to the faculty and TALKs students leading her/his session. Another example is that he/she said  to her/his group “I  don’t get the importance of all this training with the readily available technology we could be using instead of this.’

Your teaching style is to facilitate self-directed learning and

**Mentee:** You have been given the opportunity of having a TALKS mentor and your major agenda is basically having the mentor ‘spoonfeed’ you. (Don’t offer this unless asked). You know the mentor is very skillful and knowledgeable and want to take advantage of his/her abilities. This would give you knowledge expeditiously and instantly as you are concerned about all the information you are needing to master. The mentor should be negotiating a relationship with you. Stick to your needs even if the mentor tries to steer you in another direction.
