MENTORING WORKSHOP PRACTICE CASES

CASE #1

**Mentor:** Mark/Jackie has need significant support in adjusting to the physical diagnosis skills class. He/She has done well academically but does not feel skills are her/his strength. He/She reads the assignments in Bates and seems to understand the material but has problems applying it. You have watched him/her on occasion in your POM (Is this correct…POM?) group and sense that he/she has some issues getting into self-directed learning and prefers an authoritative approach. You meet with him/her to try to establish a mentoring relationship.

**Mentee:** You are an achiever and have done very well in college and standardized test scores. You have also done well so far in the didactic portion of the medical school curriculum but have had problems in the POM course. Your learning style is to have the teacher lead and you have never been strong in skills assignments. You have an opportunity to develop a mentor-mentee relationship with a 4th year student.  He/She will start the conversation and will try to establish what you would most like from him/her. Your preference**, if asked**, would be a few one-on-one sessions to develop some confidence in your skills. You can also divulge your strengths (academic achievements, commitment, willingness to learn, etc) and weaknesses (new situations, skills-oriented topics, etc) **if asked**
