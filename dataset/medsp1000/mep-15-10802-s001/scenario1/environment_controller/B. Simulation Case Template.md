| **Appendix B: Simulation Case Template**  **SIMULATION CASE TITLE: Simulation of Urgent Airway Management in a Post-Thyroidectomy Hematoma** | |
| **PATIENT NAME:** Frank Marshall  **PATIENT AGE:** 68-year-old, male  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** Dizziness post-operatively when ambulating | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | Frank Marshall is a 68-year-old gentleman who underwent a total thyroidectomy two hours ago and has now been transferred to the inpatient floor. His nurse states that he is feeling more dizzy and lightheaded. On further questioning, the nurse and family member state that his neck appears more swollen compared to an hour ago. Frank is initially speaking but becomes more obtunded as the scenario progresses. His oxygen saturation continues to decrease until the hematoma pocket is incised and evacuated at the bedside and definitive plans made to return to the operating room. |
| **Learner Preparation** | Learners will receive a case stem: “The Case Stem provided for learners before entering the rooms was: “You and your team are carrying out afternoon ward rounds on the surgical ward. All surgeries are completed for the day and you are rounding on your post-operative patients from the day.” No other information is provided prior entering the simulation room. |

| **Actors and roles in the room at case start** | * Ward Nurse is first on the scene and asks the surgical team to enter the room stating that the patient is feeling dizzy and lightheaded. The nurse can offer suggestions regarding equipment availability and help the learners find what they are looking for but leaves the room intermittently to look for equipment. The nurse can offer to take the family member outside if the team does not suggest it. When the learners begin to discuss examining the airway or preparing to intubate, the ward nurse is hesitant and questions if the students should call the senior resident or ask if they can wait a bit longer to see if the patient recovers to encourage confidence in the learners’ decision making. * Family Member is sitting in the room with Frank and occasionally interrupts the nurses and learners to ask questions. The family member becomes more distressed and increasingly agitated when the learners decide to open the incision at the bedside, questioning whether this should be done at the bedside. If at any point a learner addresses the family directly and politely asks them to leave, they should comply. | | | | | |
