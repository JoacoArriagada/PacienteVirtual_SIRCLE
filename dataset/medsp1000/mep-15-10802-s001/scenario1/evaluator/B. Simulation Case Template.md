Learners should recognize the need for bedside hematoma decompression in a clinically deteriorating patient and make arrangements for definitive airway management. |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

The learners are given a case stem that notifies them that they are conducting afternoon rounds on the surgical ward. When the team enters the room, a ward nurse tells them that Frank has been feeling lightheaded. On further questioning, the nurse and the family member in the room state that Frank’s neck appears more swollen compared to an hour ago. The team obtains patient vitals, performs physical examination, and gathers history from the patient, a family member, and the ward nurse. The expanding hematoma begins to obstruct Frank’s airway resulting in a drop in his oxygen saturation and decrease in level of consciousness. The team decides to open the hematoma pocket and cuts the sutures of both the skin and the deeper strap muscles, which results in a transient improvement in the patient’s respiratory status. Learners recognize that re-expansion of laryngeal tissues is possible and can lead to further respiratory distress, therefore a definitive airway exploration and management in the OR is necessary. The team calls the OR to coordinate an emergency case and notifies their attending staff surgeon. The OR charge nurse tells the team that there will at least be a 30-minute delay due to an ongoing case and need to call a second anesthesiologist. In anticipation of a delay, learners decide to perform a bedside intubation or cricothyroidotomy.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Difficulty in identifying the hematoma: The delay in identification leads to rapid decrease in O2 saturation. Post-thyroidectomy hematoma should be rapidly suspected given the patient’s history.
2. Failure to release the deeper midline sutures holding strap muscles: The hematoma is not fully evacuated until both superficial and deep sutures are released. The hematoma pocket often develops deep to the strap muscles and failure to open these deeper sutures will lead to ongoing respiratory compromise.
3. Failure to consider definitive management options: Learners should recognize that the airway can become more edematous and the need to establish a definitive airway is required if there are any delays to transporting the patient to the OR emergently.
