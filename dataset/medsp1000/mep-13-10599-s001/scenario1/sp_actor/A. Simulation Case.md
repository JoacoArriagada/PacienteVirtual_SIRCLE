| **Appendix A: MedEdPORTAL Simulation Case Template**  **SIMULATION CASE TITLE:** Managing the Complex Issues of Pediatric Non-accidental Trauma: a simulation-based case of a critically injured child.  **AUTHORS:** Matthew Ryan, Peggy White, Sean Kiley, Heather Reed and Chris Giordano | |
| **PATIENT NAME:** Pat  **PATIENT AGE:** 16 months  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** Non-responsive | |

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial Presentation | | | |
| **Overall Appearance** | The mother and her boyfriend will arrive about 10 minutes into the simulation (this time can change depending on how the trainees perform, e.g., longer if the simulation team needs more time). The boyfriend will be unmoved emotionally regarding the patient’s condition. He simply states the patient was fine when he last saw her. | | |
| **Actors and roles in the room at case start** | * Paramedic who brings in patient. Paramedic provides a concise recap of the situation and interventions undertaken prior to arrival. * Mother. The mother is not initially present. The mother’s role is one of concern and while she is upset, she is cooperative and does not obstruct the medical care of her child in anyway. She wants to stay with her child but she does not want to interfere with the care she is receiving. The mother engages if addressed by team. * Boyfriend. The boyfriend is not initially present and arrives with the mother. The boyfriend is indifferent and if asked, he denies knowing anything that happened to the child.   Note, one actor can play multiple roles. For example, the paramedic can simply state the mother (or boyfriend) is in the waiting room and then leave to bring the family member into the trauma bay. At that time, they assume the role of the family member. | | |
| **HPI** | The patient is a 16-month-old child who was found unresponsive on the kitchen floor by her mother who just came home from work. The mother then called EMS. The patient currently has a GCS = 6. The cause of the injuries is currently unknown. | | |

| Instructor Notes - Changes and CASE Branch Points | | |
| **Intervention / Time point** | **Change in Case** | **Additional Information** |
| *Get report from EMS at the beginning of the case* | The patient arrives via EMS and is currently being bagged with increasing difficulty to maintain an oxygen saturation of greater than 90%. The patient is not wearing a cervical collar. A 20-guage intravenous catheter in the right antecubital fossa placed by EMS is noted. | EMS was called by the mother when she found her child unconscious on the kitchen floor. EMS could not intubate the patient. If asked why, EMS can tell trainees they had an equipment error or the patient’s airway was too anterior to definitively see or for fear of aspiration. EMS also did not have the appropriate size cervical collar. |
| *The mother and boyfriend arrive and are available to answer questions.* | The team should engage the mother and boyfriend to elicit more information about what happened to the child. The mother will state she found her child unresponsive on the kitchen floor and called 911 when the patient would not respond. The boyfriend will deny any knowledge of what happened indicating he fell asleep on the couch and that the child was fine when he saw her. | A team member can attempt to separate the mother and boyfriend to obtain further information from each. At this point, the mother can state she has been working a lot lately and her boyfriend has become more impatient with her daughter. The boyfriend will continue to deny any knowledge of the events leading to the patient’s injuries. |
| *The mother asks if she can stay during the resuscitation.* | The team will have to decide it they want to have the mother stay for the resuscitation. As the patient is critically injured, the team will need to address and articulate their decision as to whether the mother can stay or not. | The mother is noted to be concerned but cooperative. |
