# Disclosure of Complication: Tension Pneumothorax

RESIDENT INSTRUCTIONS

# THE PATIENT

George Miller is a 75-year male with colon cancer. Ten days ago he was admitted to the SICU with respiratory failure, intra-abdominal sepsis, and failure to thrive after Hartman’s procedure for a perforated colon cancer. He has been in the ICU for 10 days, and you have been taking care of him for the last three days. You were able to extubate Mr. Miller three days ago, but he still requires breathing support for thick secretions and a poor cough. You decided to place a new central line last night because Mr. Miller had persistent fevers and the first central line might have been the source. Prior to the placement you notified the family by phone, briefly discussing the risks but mainly focusing on the reasons for the line. The line placement was complicated by a tension pneumothorax, which you and the ICU team identified and treated with an emergency chest tube. During the sequence of events Mr. Miller became hypoxic and required re-intubation. The lung is now reexpanded and he will likely spend several days on the ventilator. You notified the family early this morning about the reintubation.

# MS. HELEN COOPER (ELDERLY SISTER)

Ms. Helen Cooper and Jenny, George’s daughter, are the patient’s only surviving family members. George has been a widower for many years and Jenny lives on the west coast; she only recently came into town to see her Dad. Ms. Cooper is the patient’s main caretaker. She has been visiting George every day in the hospital and obviously feels quite responsible for him. You have been talking to her for the last three days. It was Ms. Cooper whom you called last night to explain the need to replace the central line.

# MS. JENNY MILLER (DAUGHTER)

You’ve seen the daughter once or twice, but have never spoken with her.
