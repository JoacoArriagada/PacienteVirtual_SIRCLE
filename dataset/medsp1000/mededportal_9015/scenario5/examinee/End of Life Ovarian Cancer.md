# End of Life: Ovarian Cancer

RESIDENT CASE NOTES

# THE PATIENT

Mrs. Andrea Smith is a 65-year-old woman from St. Paul with diabetes who underwent pelvic exenteration for ovarian cancer 3 years ago. She was doing well at home until 6 months ago when she was diagnosed with metastatic, incurable disease. She has been hospitalized several times with obstructive uropathy and renal failure, but has never required ICU admission before. She was admitted to the hospital 5 days ago and has been in the ICU for the past 3 days. She was transferred from the floor with a deteriorating mental status. She was treated for kidney failure and presumed infection, possibly pneumonia. She developed worsening hypoxemia and has required intubation, and her mental status has not improved. Further evaluation showed a large stroke. The husband knows about the kidney failure, the possible infection, that she is on life support, and that she has had a stroke, but he does not know how severe it is. She is unresponsive without sedation. The team feels that the patient’s chance of meaningful survival is minimal. It is time to have an end-of-life conference with the family to determine code status and agree on a revised care plan.

# MR. MATT SMITH (HUSBAND)

You’ve seen the patient’s husband, Mr. Matt Smith, but have not had any significant personal interaction with him. You’ve heard from the nurses that he has been asking a lot of questions recently and is upset about the amount of equipment used to support his wife’s life and worried that she is in pain.

# MS. MARY WARNER (SISTER)

The patient’s sister, Mary, has just arrived from her home, which is three hours away, having been recently notified by Matt that Andrea was “going downhill.”
