# End-of-Life Kidney and Pancreas Transplant

# RESIDENT CASE NOTES

# THE PATIENT

Mr. Andrew Smith is a 65-year-old male from St. Paul with diabetes who had a successful kidney and pancreas transplant at the UMMC two years ago. He was doing well at home, but had not returned to work after his transplants. Mr. Smith had been hospitalized in the past for rejection, but had never before required ICU admission. He has been in the ICU for 5 days now. He was transferred from the floor with a deteriorating mental status. He was treated for presumed sepsis but soon thereafter required intubation with worsening hypoxemia. Further evaluation showed a large hemispheric stroke. The family knows he had a stroke. He is unresponsive without any sedation. The team feels that the patient’s chance of meaningful survival is minimal and any additional care would be futile. It is time to have an end-of-life conference with the family to discuss DNR/DNI and comfort care.

# MRS. MARY SMITH (WIFE)

You’ve noticed that the patient’s wife, Mary, has been at the hospital nearly every day but haven’t had much personal interaction with her. She seems fairly familiar with the medical environment, having been through the double transplant two years ago. You’ve heard from the nurses that she has been asking a lot of questions recently and is upset about the amount of equipment used to support her husband’s life and worried that he is in pain.

# MR. BILL SMITH (BROTHER)

The patient’s brother, Bill, donated a kidney two years ago to help his “little” brother survive. He has just arrived from Duluth, having been notified by Mary that Andrew was “going downhill.”
