1. **TITLE OF CASE:** Toxic Ingestion/Acute Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Ingestion
2. **ENVIRONMENT:**

**A. Lab set up** – Pediatric Emergency Department bed in simulation center or true emergency department.

**B. Manikin set up** – The manikin will be a clothed adult size manikin. At the beginning of the simulation, there are no monitor leads on the patient, no IV access has been obtained, and the patient is not on oxygen.

**C. Props** – Available for use will be a heart monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, and pulse oximeter. Supplemental oxygen by nasal cannula and face mask will be available. There will be a medicine cart or tray with a full complement of vasoactive agents, PALS medications, medicines necessary for sedation, rapid sequence intubation, and analgesia. In addition, a fully stocked code cart with defibrillator will be available for use, along with a selection of direct laryngoscopes and intubation supplies.

**D. Audiovisual** – (See Appendix A) Available for review when asked for by participants will be ECG and laboratory studies.

**E. Distractors** – Distractors can be added at the director’s discretion. Choices could include obtrusive or difficult family members, equipment malfunction, etc. However, as written, there is no need for distractors as the goal is simply to manage a medically complex patient.

3. **ACTORS:**
   1. Roles can vary depending on the number of participants in the simulation session and on potential actor availability. This case can be implemented using a single participant and one facilitator/operator who can provide oral feedback via overhead audio and play additional historical or consultant roles via voice only. Realism can be enhanced by using physical actors, such as nursing staff members, but these are not required to successfully implement this case.
   2. Roles may be played by resident or faculty physicians, nurses, or medical students.
   3. Actions for the roles will be as follows:
      1. Primary physician: The main scenario participant will act as the primary physician and do the primary evaluation of the patient to include obtaining a history, conducting a physical exam, and ordering any necessary medications or other interventions. The primary physician can perform any needed procedures, or can delegate these to other providers if multiple participants are present.
      2. Secondary physicians: Other participants in the scenario will serve as collaborators, assistants for any necessary procedures, and consultants.
      3. Nursing staff: The role of the nurse will be to administer medications, verify orders, and perform other tasks as directed by the physicians. The nurse can also make observations as needed to stimulate case progression.
4. **CASE NARRATIVE:**
5. **Doorway Instructions**
   1. Learner is to take a history, perform a physical exam, initiate appropriate treatment, and perform procedures as necessary for a simulated emergency department patient.
6. **Scenario Background**
   1. Chief Complaint: 16 year old female presents after a suicide attempt
   2. Triage Nursing Note: Patient took approximately three handfuls of nortriptyline approximately 30 minutes prior to arrival
   3. Vital Signs: BP 136/95 mmHg, Pulse 110 bpm, Resp 28/min, SpO2 99% on room air, Temp 98.9°C (oral), Weight 70 kg
   4. Past Medical History: ADHD
   5. Medications: methylphenidate (ER) 40mg po daily
   6. Allergies: No known allergies
   7. Family and Social History: Lives with mother and maternal aunt (who is prescribed nortriptyline 25 mg tablets). Denies ethanol, tobacco, or drug use.
7. **Initial Scenario Conditions**
   1. **History given by patient:** Patient reports she took 3 handfuls of her aunt's nortriptyline (25 mg tablets) in an attempt to kill herself. Her mother walked in and saw the pill bottle on the floor. The only pills she could find at the house were her aunt’s. She denies any report of other medication ingestion today or other daily medications.
   2. **Circumstances at symptom onset:** Patient reports she has been in a strained relationship with her mother, and they have been arguing more frequently. She has a history of ADHD and is followed by a counselor and psychiatrist. She reports approximately one month of increasing depressive symptoms for which she has an upcoming appointment with her psychiatrist. Review of the empty pill bottle reveals prescription for 25mg nortriptyline BID. It was recently filled with 30 pills missing from the bottle (total possible ingestion of 750mg).
   3. **Associated symptoms (given by system as review of system is completed):**  Review of systems positive for dizziness, suicidal ideas, depression, and anxiousness.
   4. **Initial Exam (findings to be verbally described by facilitator or simulated if high fidelity simulation equipment will allow for exam findings)**
      1. **General**: Patient is a well-nourished, normally developed teenage female. She is alert and oriented to person, place, and time.
      2. **HEENT**: Normocephalic and atraumatic. EOM are normal. Pupils are 7mm, sluggish reactivity.
      3. **Skin:** Skin is warm and dry. No rash noted. No flushing noted.
      4. **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia present. Exam reveals no gallop and no friction rub. No murmur heard.
      5. **Respiratory**: Tachypnea present. Breath sounds normal. She has no wheezes. She has no rales.
      6. **Abdominal:** Soft. Bowel sounds are decreased. She exhibits no distension. There is no tenderness.
      7. **Genitourinary:** Normal female genitalia.
      8. **Extremities:** Range of motion is normal in all four extremities. No edema is noted. No cyanosis is noted.
      9. **Neurological:** Patient is alert and oriented. She has no gross motor or sensory deficit.
      10. **Psychiatric**: Her speech is normal and behavior is normal. Her mood appears anxious. Thought content is not delusional. She expresses impulsivity. She expresses suicidal ideation. She expresses no homicidal ideation. She expresses suicidal plans.
   5. **Physiology (data points should appear when patient is placed on the appropriate monitoring devices)**
      1. Heart Rate 110 bpm, sinus
      2. Blood Pressure 136/95 mmHg
      3. Respiratory Rate 28/min
      4. Pulse Oximetry 100% on room air
      5. Weight: 70 kg
8. **Scenario Branch points**
   1. At presentation the patient will be alert and oriented, with narrow complex tachycardia on the monitor and vital signs as shown above (heart rate 110 bpm, blood pressure 136/95 mmHg, respiratory rate 28/min, oxygen saturation 100% on room air).
   2. After initial history and physical exam, the next branch point is stabilization measures (administer IV fluid bolus) and EKG #1.
   3. After appearing alert at time of initial examination and after stabilization measures are initiated, the patient decompensates. The patient should have increasing tachycardia and tachypnea with altered mental status (difficult to arouse even with painful stimulation, incomprehensible speech during stimulation, minimal localization to pain, and no eye opening). Repeat vital signs: BP 100/70 mmHg, Pulse 160 bpm, Respirations 35/min, SpO2 92% on room air. A nurse or the patient’s mother can prompt the learner to re-evaluate the patient (i.e. ‘my daughter won’t wake up’ or ‘the mental status of this patient is declining’) if this is not immediately recognized by the learner. Post-intubation CXR is provided in Appendix A.
   4. After intubation, patient should continue to decompensate due to cardiotoxicity. EKG #2 should be ordered and reviewed. If the wide complex tachycardia (WCT) is not recognized on EKG #2 and/or sodium bicarbonate bolus is not given, the patient’s rhythm should progress to EKG #3 with worsening cardiovascular physiology including cardiac arrest if worsening clinical status is not recognized and sodium bicarbonate not given. Again, the nurse can prompt the physician (i.e. ‘this rhythm looks different’) to maintain case flow. A post bicarbonate bolus rhythm strip is available (Appendix A). Of note, the dose of sodium bicarbonate bolus for TCA induced wide complex tachycardia is 1-2 mEq/kg (50 mEq per ampule), thus 2 or 3 ampules based on patient’s weight.
   5. Request for old records: None available.
   6. Intravenous fluid administration: An IV fluid bolus is indicated by the physical exam showing tachycardia. The patient may transiently improve with normal saline bolus. However, the patient’s VS will continue to deteriorate (BP 100/70 mmHg, Pulse 160 bpm) until further is administered, including additional fluids, sodium bicarbonate bolus, and intubation.
   7. Other medical interventions: As patient’s mental status deteriorates, endotracheal intubation is indicated due to inability to arouse patient and need for airway protection. Ideally, a bedside glucose should be checked prior to intubation and is normal.
   8. Laboratory studies: Laboratory studies can be found in Appendix A.
   9. Administration of medication: After recognition of tricyclic antidepressant toxicity with wide complex QRS on ECG, bicarbonate bolus should be initiated. Some learners may additionally start the patient on a bicarbonate infusion. After securing the airway with intubation, some learners may consider administration of activated charcoal.
   10. Consultation of specialist: An intensivist will be available by telephone consult. Neither should direct management, nor be obstructive. If the intensivist is consulted prior to diagnosis, they should be unavailable until the recognition of TCA toxicity and appropriate management, including intubation and bicarbonate boluses. If the poison center is consulted for management, then they should be available to confirm treatment decisions which have already occurred. They should not be available to lead/direct management.

**VII. Instructor Notes:**

1. **Scenario flow** – Instructors can directly influence the flow of the scenario by providing the initial patient history and prompting reassessment as necessary.
2. **Scenario programming** – The settings for a high-fidelity patient simulator are fairly straightforward for this scenario and do not require specific programming. Our program typically presents this scenario with the initial vital signs as presented above, with the major branch points of fluid resuscitation, sodium bicarbonate bolus, and recognition of need for intubation.
