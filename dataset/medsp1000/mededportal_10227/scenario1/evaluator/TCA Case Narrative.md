1. **TITLE OF CASE:** Toxic Ingestion/Acute Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Ingestion
2. **TARGET AUDIENCE:** Medical students, emergency medicine residents, pediatric emergency medicine fellows
3. **LEARNING OBJECTIVES:**
   1. **Primary Learning Objectives**
      1. Recognize the neurologic and cardiovascular effects of acute TCA toxicity
      2. Identify symptoms of anticholinergic toxidrome in the patient examination
      3. Implement fluid resuscitation for cardiovascular effects
      4. Interpret the wide complex ECG changes as Na-channel blockade effect of TCA toxicity
   2. **Secondary Learning Objectives**
      1. Recognize risks and benefits of activated charcoal administration
      2. Use appropriately dosed sodium bicarbonate bolus to treat Na-channel blockade
      3. Detect neurologic deterioration and appropriately intubate the patient secondary to altered mental status
   3. **Critical Actions**
      1. Perform focused history and physical exam based on reported ingestion
      2. Obtain ECG and recognizes Na-channel blockade/QRS widening
      3. Initiate normal saline fluid resuscitation
      4. Administer sodium bicarbonate bolus for QRS widening
      5. Perform endotracheal intubation for airway protection
      6. Consider/discuss utility of activated charcoal
      7. Discuss case with consultants (critical care for admission, poison center for management recommendations)

**VII. Instructor Notes:**

1. **Scenario flow** – Instructors can directly influence the flow of the scenario by providing the initial patient history and prompting reassessment as necessary.
   1. Key aspect of case – Identification of ingestion in general and TCA in particular is essential. Fluid resuscitation and the initiation of bicarbonate bolus must be performed for patient survival. The patient should be intubated secondary to mental status decline.
2. **Information for actors** – This case can easily be presented without formal “actors” and by using other participants to serve as the nursing staff or other collaborating physicians. Any specific actors used outside of participants should be briefed about the critical actions and anticipated flow of the case ahead of time.
3. **Scenario programming** – The settings for a high-fidelity patient simulator are fairly straightforward for this scenario and do not require specific programming. Our program typically presents this scenario with the initial vital signs as presented above, with the major branch points of fluid resuscitation, sodium bicarbonate bolus, and recognition of need for intubation.

**VIII. Debriefing Plan:**

1. **Method of debriefing** – A post-case debriefing conference is recommended immediately following the end of the scenario. Consider including some of the following elements:
   1. Open-ended questions by facilitator – Consider beginning the session with a question to the primary participant about how they felt the scenario went. This often leads to extensive participant-led discussion that will touch on many of the major issues in the case. Invite any secondary participants and/or observers to comment constructively about the case.
   2. Brief didactic review – Some possible materials for review after the scenario have been provided in Appendix B. They address several major teaching points related to the case; however, individual participants may raise additional questions.
   3. Formal participant evaluation – Our group does not use this case for formal evaluations and we have no standardized form or format for such feedback. However, this case can easily be adapted to the standardized evaluation method used at your institution using the objectives in Section III (above).
2. **Actual debriefing materials** – See Appendix B for debriefing materials.
3. **Rules for the debriefing** – Often, an open-ended question to the participants will provoke a discussion of aspects of the case management that were good and those areas where improvement can be made. Encourage your participants to discuss the case management decisions in a non-judgmental way. When this case is used for experiential learning (without formal participant evaluation), discussions may vary as compared to situations where formal feedback or grading is anticipated. We have found that placing an emphasis on the learning that occurs from experiencing a case like this is very effective, rather than focusing on the “right answer” or whether the specific management decisions were precisely as the leader would have performed.
4. **Questions to facilitate the debriefing –**
   1. What are the pharmacodynamics effects of TCA?
   2. What are the classic EKG findings of TCA toxicity?
   3. What specific changes on the EKG predict clinical toxicity?
   4. Name other causes of toxin induced sodium channel blockade.
   5. Review the main clinical effects of TCA toxicity.
   6. Describe the signs and symptoms of an anticholinergic toxidrome.
   7. Discuss the general indications for the use of activated charcoal in overdose.
   8. What is the role of sodium bicarbonate in TCA toxicity? When should it be administered?
   9. What is the dose of sodium bicarbonate in TCA WCT?
   10. What drug therapies should be avoided/or would exacerbate TCA toxicity?
