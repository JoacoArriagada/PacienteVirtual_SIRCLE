# #15. Breastfeeding anticipatory guidance

A. Subject: Breastfeeding (oral question)

B. Question: A first time breastfeeding mother is in with her full term infant for his initial visit 48 hours post discharge. The baby is well appearing and not jaundiced. Mother has many questions, including: does her infant need any vitamin supplementation? If so, which one(s)? (if resident does not ask about mom’s diet, prompt him/her by saying “I am vegan”) She has been noticing persistent pain while the baby nipples and wonders if this is normal and what she can do to alleviate the pain. (Resident should state that he/she would like to evaluate the latch, and should be able to describe components of a good latch. Should be prompted if fails to initiate latch eval). Finally, she thinks her “milk came in” this morning: her breasts were engorged and painful and the baby had a difficult time latching on. She would like some advice for this as well.

C. Set up: Oral question with Chief Resident or Program Director playing the part of the parent

# D. Correct Answer:

The infant should receive supplemental vitamin D, and if mother eats a vegan diet, B12.
While transient sore nipples are common with breastfeeding, persistent pain should be evaluated. The most common reason for pain with breastfeeding is a poor latch. It is important to observe the latch and breastfeeding during an office visit so problems can be addressed and corrected early.
The components of a good latch include: baby’s nose touches the breast, lips are flanged out, the infant grasps the areola (not just the nipple), chin is against the breast, more areola is visible above the baby’s upper lip than below the lower lip.
Suggestions for easing nipple discomfort include air drying the nipples after feeding, applying expressed breast milk or lanolin cream to chafed nipples, feeding the infant on the unaffected side first, evaluate for and treat candida infection.
Treatment for engorgement is milk removal. Some strategies to reduce engorgement and discomfort include: warm shower or warm pack to relax mom and enhance milk flow, massage or hand/pump expression of milk to soften areola so baby can latch, frequent feedings, use of anti-inflammatory medication for pain if needed.

E. Scoring: % point for Vit D supplementation, % point for asking about mom’s diet w/out prompting; % point for B12 supplementation; % point for wanting to observe latch w/out prompting; 1 point for description of correct latch (at least 4 elements); 1 point for management strategies of sore nipples (at least 4); 1 point for management of engorgement
