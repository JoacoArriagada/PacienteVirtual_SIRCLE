VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants (specify if given freely or must be asked for) 1. Given freely: Chief complaint – Seizures EMS Report: “Family called us for a 45 year old male who has been having seizures for the past 15 minutes.” Family Report: He’s never had seizures before. He’s just been depressed for the past 2 weeks.

2. Must be asked for:

a. Past medical history – Depression, positive PPD
b. Meds – “antibiotic,” Paxil
c. Allergies – NKDA
d. Family/social history – smokes otherwise noncontributory

B. Scenario conditions initially

1. Patient with altered mental status. Actively seizing

2. Patients initial exam

• BP – 150/95
• HR – 115
• RR – 24
• Pox – 97% NRB Temp – afebrile
• General – Unresponsive, with active seizing. Diaphoretic HEENT – EOMI, pupils $3 \mathrm { m m }$ unreactive
• Neck – supple, no JVD Respiratory – Clear to auscultation bilaterally CV – tachycardiac, otherwise normal heart sounds Abdomen – benign Ext – Benign Neuro – Unresponsive. Moves all extremities. Multiple seizu without return to normal mental status.

3. Patients physiology

• Hyperadrenergic • Worsening hypoxia and acidosis with continued seizure activity

C. Scenario branch points

1. Recognize status epilepticus – benzodiazepines and phenytoin do not break
seizures
2. Phenobarbital and propofol do not break seizures
3. Pyridoxine breaks seizure activity within 5 minutes of administration
4. Refractory seizure will require intubation due to worsening hypoxia

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer 1. Provide continued seizure activity 2. Worsening hypoxia if students fail to progress down the status epilepticus algorithm
B. Tips to direct actors 1. Family should fixate on depression history, and be somewhat elusive regarding history of positive PPD and isoniazid use. 2. EMS should stress failure of patient to return to normal mental status between seizures.
C. Scenario programming 1. Standard Man (Meti HPS/ECS) i. Presentation – 5-15 minutes 1. Eye blink control to closed 2. Baroreceptor minimum pressure to $9 0 \mathrm { m m } \mathrm { H g }$ 3. Baroreceptor maximum pressure to $1 9 0 \mathrm { m m H g }$ 4. Heart rate factor to 1.15 5. Seizure (If available. Otherwise facilitator can shake bed, or a portable back massager can be placed under manikin to simulate vibration) ii. Status epilepticus – 15-25 minutes 1. Shunt fraction to 0.5 iii. Treatment 1. Baroreceptor minimum pressure to $4 0 \mathrm { m m } \mathrm { H g }$ over 5 minutes 2. Baroreceptor maximum pressure to $1 2 0 \mathrm { m m H g }$ over 5 minutes 3. Heart rate factor to 1.0 iv. Intubation 1. Shunt fraction to 0
E. Potential complications/errors path(s) 1. Failure to progress down status epilepticus algorithm $\to$ subtle status epilepticus worsening hypoxia and acidosis cardiac arrest

2. No pyridoxine continued seizure worsening hypoxia and acidosis cardiac arrest

3. Suspected intracranial trauma $ \mathrm { { C T } }$ Head continued seizure worsening hypoxia and acidosis cardiac arrest

i. If student follows the path of intracranial hemorrhage, the instructor should stress the lack of any signs of head trauma
ii. If student insists on sending patient to CT head, the instructor can acutely worsen patient’s hypoxia forcing the student to secure an airway before transporting patient to CT suite
iii. If student still sends patient to CT, the patient should then have a cardiopulmonary arrest

4. Suspected meningitis/encephalitis $ \mathbf { C T }$ Head continued seizure worsening hypoxia and acidosis cardiac arrest

i. If student follows the path of meningitis/encephalitis, the instructor should stress the lack of fever in the patient
ii. If student insists on sending patient to CT head, the instructor can acutely worsen patient’s hypoxia forcing the student to secure an airway before transporting patient to CT suite
iii. If student still sends patient to CT, the patient should then have a cardiopulmonary arrest
