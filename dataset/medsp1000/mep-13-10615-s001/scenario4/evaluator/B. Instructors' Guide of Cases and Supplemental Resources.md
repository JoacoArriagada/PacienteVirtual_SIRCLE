*Section 4, (Supporting Dani) learning objectives*

* Discuss clinical presentation and treatment of transgender individuals
* Identify the major risks and benefits to consider when starting hormonal therapy and contraception
* Describe current medical options for the transgender adolescent/young adult and how they may differ depending on pubertal staging.

*Section 4, (Supporting Dani) discussion topics*

* Management and treatment options of individuals who are potentially transgender or gender questioning
* Intervention strategies for teens/young adults who are transgender
* Terminology (“treatment” vs. “gender affirming” procedures)

*Section 4, (Supporting Dani) potential probe questions to ask as learners discuss the case material*

* *How does one create a treatment frame that allows for safe exploration of one's gender identity without making assumptions and categorizing individuals?*
* *What are the different roles that the interprofessional providers can play at this time?*
* *How would you document your note in a situation where the patient's legal name and biological sex differs from that of their affirmed name and pronoun?*
* *How would you respond to a transgender individual who feels that you are pathologizing them as mentally ill when you require them to see a mental health provider?*
* *How do you differentiate between gender dysphoria, gender discordance, and gender nonconformity in youth who present gender identity issues?*

*Section 4, (Supporting Dani) key take home points (can be summarized into bullet points and read aloud, or displayed on a lecture slide)*

* Gender affirming procedures for children and adolescents who identify as transgender (e.g., pubertal suppression) are the topic of much debate. Learners should emerge from the discussion with some familiarity regarding the pros and cons of such interventions, including whether youth are able to adequately judge the benefits and risks of such interventions or provide informed consent, and the role of caregivers and the health care systems in managing such interventions.
* Risk for denying gender affirming interventions, such as pubertal suppression, for transgender youth include depression, strong disgust for the body, bullying, and use of illegal or unregulated sources of treatments and gender affirming interventions (e.g., hormones, silicone injections).
* Benefits of providing pubertal suppression include the delay of onset of secondary sexual characteristics, fewer complications when pursuing more invasive gender affirming interventions (“top” and “bottom” surgery), and lower risks of treating “false positives” (e.g., gender nonconforming youth who later decide that they do not want to pursue gender affirming surgeries).
