**Module 1 - CULTURAL HUMILITY, INTRODUCING ABE**

Timeline for module management (45 Minutes)

* Section 1: Brief Overview of Curriculum, Assignments, and Module 4 Debate (10 Minutes)
* Section 2: Cultural Humility Discussion (20 Minutes)
* Section 3: Supporting Abe (15 minutes): Distribution of Section A with discussion. Learners identify questions in preparation for Module 2.

Section 3: Supporting Abe, Part A (15 minutes)

The final part of Module 1 is introducing case material on “Abe” (Sexual Orientation and Adolescent Health Care, Appendix C), which sets the context for the reading assignments and preparatory activities for Module 2.

Instructors begin this section by indicating that the class will now discuss a patient case. The instructor will hand out the first page of Appendix C (Module 1 section) and announce that the learners are seeing Abe in the context of a Health Maintenance Exam (HME) visit.

The instructor will ask a learner (or 3 learners, taking turns reading each paragraph on the page) to read aloud the initial information on Abe. After reading all of the material, instructors will start the discussion by asking learners, “What do you next?”

*Section 3 (Supporting Abe) specific learning objectives*

1. Use skills from cultural humility to identify assumptions they have about Abe based on the material distributed in Part A
2. Identify topics to research prior to Module 2

*Section 3 (Supporting Abe) discussion topics*

* Provider assumptions
* If and when one should inquire about sexual orientation or gender identity

*Section 3 (Supporting Abe) potential probe questions to be asked as learners discuss the case material*

* *Do you have any assumptions about Abe's appearance or identity? Where do those assumptions come from?*
* *What issues need to be addressed in this case?*
* *Looking at this case from the perspective of different providers, what might be of greatest concern to a: medical provider (i.e., physician and nurse or nurse practitioner); mental health provider (i.e., social worker or psychologist); and a nutritionist?*
* *What will be your next step in the visit?*
* *What is the best way to separate Mom and Abe?*
* *What approaches do you use in developing a rapport with Abe? How would the MD/NP’s use HEADSS interview in talking to Abe? How do the mental health and nutrition staff establish rapport?*
* *If a provider wants to inquire about Abe's sexual or gender identity, what are some approaches to doing so, and what are some of the potential strengths or weaknesses of these approaches? For example, do you ask with the mother present? Might asking the question offend Abe?*
* *What do you want to do for follow-up? Phone, e-mail (portal) with Abe?*

*Section 3 (Supporting Abe) take home points (can be summarized into bullet points and read aloud, or displayed on a lecture slide)*

* Although Abe's appearance and mannerisms may signal that he is not heterosexual or someone who conforms to traditional gender norms, it is important not to make assumptions about how he might identify with regards to his sexual orientation or gender identity. Providers must acknowledge what assumptions arise when they confront a patient who might be a gender or sexual minority youth, where those assumptions come from, and how those assumptions might influence the course of care. For example, Abe is visiting as a part of an HME. Has any information been presented to indicate that his sexuality or gender expressions are of concern to him or his mother? Are there more exigent health issues that need to be addressed?
* Based on the prior discussion of cultural humility, learners should be able to begin to think of potential strengths and weaknesses of inquiring about a patient's sexual or gender identity.
