Section 2: Introducing Dani (30 minutes)

Instructors will begin Section 2 by informing learners that they will be starting a new patient case. This patient is 14 years old and reporting to the adolescent medicine clinic with a chief complaint of “gender issues.” Instructors will distribute case text “Supporting Dani, Part A” (Module 3, Handout 1).

Instructors will ask 1-2 learners to read aloud the case material. After the case material is presented, the instructors will ask, “What do you do next?”

*Section 2, (Introducing Dani) learning objectives*

* Understand the difference between gender identity and gender expression
* Fluidity of gender identity and expression

*Section 2, (Introducing Dani) discussion topics*

* Addressing transgender and gender nonconforming patients (i.e., use of pronouns)
* Supporting family members of transgender and gender nonconforming youth
* Development of gender identity and gender expression across time

*Section 2, (Introducing Dani) potential probe questions to ask while discussing case material*

* *What do you want to know?*
* *Is Dani transgender?*
* *How do you address a patient like Dani, what pronouns do you use?*
* *What resources would be helpful?*
* *What would be different if Dani were presenting at age 11?*
* *How do you support Dad and the other family members?*
* *What might be different if Dani were of a different racial/ethnic or class background?*
* *How do you more fully understand Dani’s feelings? What research has she done? Is she involved with anyone? Has she reached out to any resources?*

*Section 2, (Introducing Dani) key take home points (can be summarized into bullet points and read aloud, or displayed on a lecture slide)*

* Gender nonconformity does not necessarily equate with transgender identity. These can be part of the same continuum.
* We cannot assume that a patient has a transgender identity without knowing how an individual identifies their own gender identity. The best source of information for how a patient identifies their gender in terms of labels and pronouns is the patient themselves.
* Although gender identity and expression may be fluid during adolescence, older adolescents will likely have a more firm sense of their gender identity than children or younger adolescents. For families with transgender adolescents, the adolescent’s gender identity and gender transition is likely to affect the whole family. Both gender nonconforming and transgender adolescents and their families need support from providers.
