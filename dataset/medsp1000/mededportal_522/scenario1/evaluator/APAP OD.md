# REVIEW OF CASE CHECKLIST

- There is no toxidrome presenting with APAP overdoses.

- APAP is a frequent component of medications, and the patient may not have even considered the APAP component of the medication.

- There was no clinical opioid toxidrome presenting in this case, and monitoring was sufficient to manage the opioid portion of this case. In the case of an opioid toxidrome, consisting of miosis, coma, and respiratory depression, administration of naloxone to reverse respiratory depression would be indicated.

- The quantity of the APAP ingestion in this case is quite unclear,as is the need for the initiation of antidote therapy.Blood levels may help guide the need for therapy, but should not be taken until 4 hours after acute ingestion. Levels taken prior to this time period will not be reliable in determining the need for therapy.

- The Rumack-Matthew nomogram is only useful for single, acute ingestions (not multiple or chronic toxicity). When considering ingestions of multiple times of ingestions and chronic toxicities, it is more warranted to base initiation of therapy on a calculation of toxicity. If a toxic dose ingested meets or exceeds 140 mg/kg, then antidote therapy should be initiated.

- If a patient has a massive or clearly convincing toxic ingestion based on calculation, particularly if beyond the time for any benefit from activated charcoal, then initiation of antidote therapy may be warranted on that basis alone, as earlier administration of antidote may improve outcomes.

- The antidote is N-acetylcysteine (NAC). NAC was available only orally until recently, with a loading dose of 140mg/kg, then followed by 70 mg/kg x 17 doses every 4 hours. NAC has a foul odor and taste, and frequently will cause nausea and vomiting. Intravenous use of NAC is now approved, with a the regimen consisting of a 150 mg/kg loading dose over 1 hour, followed by 50 mg/kg over the next 4 hours and 100 mg/kg over the following 16 hours.

- Charcoal binds APAP well, and is useful if the ingestion is within an hour or so. As this ingestion was well beyond an hour, its usefulness is limited. Use of charcoal does not preclude the use of NAC.

- The patient had an intentional overdose, and has at least some availability of other dangerous medications, including a tricyclic antidepressant. Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) have numerous pharmacologic properties, but the lethal effects are from Na channel blockade resulting in conduction disturbances. An EKG may be performed at the outset of this case with continued cardiac monitoring. Classic EKG findings seen in TCA toxicity include a widening QRS with a right axis deviation of the terminal 40 milliseconds, but the most common finding is sinus tachycardia. An EKG with a QRS over 100 milliseconds should prompt initiation of treatment. Antidote therapy for TCAs consists of sodium bicarbonate, with a serum pH target of 7.5 to 7.55.
