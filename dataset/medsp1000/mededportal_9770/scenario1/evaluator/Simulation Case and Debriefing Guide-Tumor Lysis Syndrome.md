1. **TITLE: Tumor Lysis Syndrome**
2. **TARGET AUDIENCE:** Medical students, residents
3. **LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

**A) Primary**

1. By the end of this simulation learners will be able to recognize Tumor Lysis Syndrome by obtaining an appropriate and accurate history.

2. By the end of this simulation learners will be able to recognize Tumor Lysis Syndrome by obtaining an appropriate and accurate physical exam.

3. By the end of this simulation learners will be able to recognize and manage hyperkalemia associated with Tumor Lysis Syndrome.
4. By the end of this simulation learners will be able to manage hypocalcemia associated with Tumor Lysis Syndrome.
5. By the end of this simulation learners will be able to manage hyperuricemia associated with Tumor Lysis Syndrome.

**B) Critical Actions Checklist**

Clinical State #1: Presentation

1. Collect the appropriate history
2. Place patient on monitor
3. Order appropriate lab tests
4. Recognize hyperkalemia on EKG
5. Treat hyperkalemia with insulin, glucose, albuterol, bicarbonate, kayexelate
6. Treat hyperuricemia with IVF, loop diuretic, Rasburicase
7. Consult oncology and renal

Clinical State #2: Worsening hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia and dehydration

1. Identify worsening vital signs

2. Identify widening QRS on monitor or on EKG

3. Give aggressive IVF

4. Give calcium for wide QRS in setting of hyperkalemia.

Clinical State #3: Stabilization of patient, admission to ICU

1. Call patient report to ICU physician
2. Repeat EKG after treatment of hyperkalemia
3. **ENVIRONMENT**
   1. **Lab Set Up:** Simulation room with same capabilities of urban emergency department resuscitation room. There may be a control room with a one-way mirror where simulation case directors can observe the case. They can also communicate with the nurse in the simulation room via radio. Alternatively, a simpler set up may be used whereby the instructor is in the same room as the manikin and learners. Simulator participants may call consultants and admitting teams via telephone in the simulation room that connects to the control room.
   2. **Manikin Set Up:** The simulated emergency department is equipped with a simulation manikin that permits the creation of multiple physiologic states, which can be programmed to worsen with time and to respond to therapeutic maneuvers, or which can be manipulated directly by the instructor. The mannequin should be dressed in normal casual clothes. The initial set of vital signs that the mannequin should display are HR 120 BP 100/60 RR 20 Temp (if asked for) 98.7 Pulse Ox 98% RA.
   3. **Props:**
      1. Resuscitation cart with equipment for endotracheal intubations, central lines, arterial lines, resuscitation medications, IV equipment, personal protective equipment.
      2. “ECG machine” and ECG or just ECG:
         1. Sinus tachycardia with peaked T waves.
         2. Sinus tachycardia with wide QRS
      3. “Portable X-ray Machine” and/or normal CXR
      4. **Me**dications: Insulin, glucose, bicarbonate, albuterol, calcium gluconate, kayexelate, Rasburicase, antibiotics, normal saline,
4. **ACTORS**
   1. Roles
      1. Nurse (RN): Nurse can be played by any medical personnel familiar with the manikin and set up of the resuscitation room. The nurse should have an earpiece/radio in order to communicate with the case instructors behind the mirror. If there are no extra personnel, the facilitator can play the role of the nurse verbally through the microphone.
      2. ECG and X-ray Technicians, Respiratory Therapist, and Pharmacist: Any additional personnel may act as technicians, therapists, or pharmacists.
      3. Patient (in control room): Patient will be the manikin. Simulation directors in the control room may speak through a microphone acting as the patient.
      4. Consultants (in control room, speaking by intercom phone): May be played by any instructors running the case.
      5. Participant (resident, fellow, or senior student):
      6. Secondary participants: May be medical students or other residents/fellows assisting in case. They may page consultants or admitting teams. They may also assist with procedures.
      7. The case may be run with fewer personnel and the same person may carry out multiple roles.

2. **Critical Action Check List with Cues and Guidelines. - Tumor Lysis Syndrome**

CLINICAL STATE 1 - PRESENTATION

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Collects appropriate history of lymphoma diagnosis and recent initiation of chemotherapy

**Guide**: If the learners do not elicit a history of lymphoma and recent initiation of chemotherapy, the nurse may ask the patient, “Do you have any other medical history?.” At this point, patient may tell the learner that they have a recent diagnosis of lymphoma and started chemotherapy 3 days ago.

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Places patient on monitor and obtains full set of vital signs

**Guide**: If the learner does not ask the nurse for a full set of vital signs and to place the patient on the monitor, the nurse may ask the learner if he/she would like vital signs and/or a monitor

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Orders appropriate labs

Y [ ]  N [ ]  CBC

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Chemistry panel

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Uric acid

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Lactic acid

**Guide**: If learner does not order any labs, nurse should ask if he/she would like to order tests?

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Orders EKG

**Guide**: If learner does not order and EKG, nurse may say, “that monitor looks a little strange. Would you like any more tests?”

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Recognizes hyperkalemia on EKG

**Guide**: The initial EKG is significant for peaked t waves. If the learner does not recognize the peaked t waves, the nurse may say look at the EKG and say, “that EKG doesn’t look normal to me.”

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Recognizes and treats hyperkalemia

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Insulin & glucose

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Albuterol

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Bicarbonate

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Kayexelate

**Guide**: If the patient’s hyperkalemia is not aggressively treated, the patient should decompensate quickly. The learners should treat the hyperkalemia before the potassium result returns. The nurse should not cue treatment of hyperkalemia unless the patient starts to decompensate (wide QRS, altered mental status, hypotension). If the learners, at this point, still have not treated the hyperkalemia, nurse may be cued to give the learners the potassium level as a critical result. If the learner does not treat the hyperkalemia after it is resulted as high, the patient should progress to cardiac arrest with sine wave on monitor. At this point, the nurse may ask if the learner would like to treat the abnormal potassium result.

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Recognizes and treats hyperuricemia

Y [ ]  N [ ]  IV fluids at least 2 Liters

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Loop diuretic

Y [ ]  N [ ]  Rasburicase

**Guide**: Nurse can give the result of the elevated uric acid to the learners. If the learners do not treat with IV fluids, a loop diuretic and Rasburicase, the nurse should ask if there are any other treatments they should give. If learners are unsure of what treatment they should give, the nurse can ask if they would like to consult oncology?

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Consults Oncology

**Guide**: If the learners consult Oncology early in the patient’s course, the Oncologist should be unavailable. Only after the diagnosis of Tumor Lysis Syndrome is consider and after the patient has been resuscitated with IVF and hyperkalemia treatment will the Oncologist be available. If the learners are really struggling with making the diagnosis, the facilitator may make the Oncologist available sooner.

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Consults Renal

CLINICAL STATE 2 – WORSENING DEHYDRATION and HYPERKALEMIA

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Identifies worsening vital signs

**Guide**: if hyperkalemia is not treated and if patient is not aggressively volume resuscitated, patient starts to deteriorate. Tachycardia and hypotension should worsen.

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Aggressive IV fluid resuscitation with at least 2L IVF

**Guide**: If 2 liters of IV fluid are not ordered, tachycardia and hypotension worsen. At this point, nurse may say, “the patient is still hypotensive, would you like any more IVfluids?”

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Identifies widening QRS on monitor or EKG

**Guide**: The monitor should be changed to a wide QRS as the patient is decompensating. If the learners do not recognize this, the nurse may again say, “That QRS on the monitor looks different to me. Should we do anything?”

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Treats widening QRS with calcium

**Guide**: If the widened QRS is not treated with calcium immediately, the patient should go into a cardiac arrest with a sine wave on the monitor.

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Reevaluates QRS after treatment with calcium

**Guide**: Learners should note the EKG or monitor after the patient is treated with calcium. If they do not, nurse may ask, “Does that monitor look better to you?”

CLINICAL STATE 3 – STABILIZATION and ADMISSION of PATIENT

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Admits patient to the ICU

**Guide**: Ultimately, this patient needs a disposition. After the patient has been treated, the learners should call the ICU doctor to admit the patient. If they do not, nurse may say, “We need to open up this room. What do you want to do with this patient.” When the learners call pass off to the ICU doctor, this is a nice time to have them recap what they think the patient’s diagnosis is and what they have done for treatment.

1. Y [ ]  N [ ]  Repeat EKG after hyperkalemia treatment

**Guide**: The EKG should be repeated after hyperkalemia treatment. If learners do not order a repeat EKG, the nurse may ask if they would like one.
