**Guidelines to provide Feedback**

(for role-play student observers and facilitator)

(handout for faculty development session)

Feedback is an objective description of a clinical performance intended to guide a future performance by the learner. **Feedback can be thought of as a method that connects assessment with instruction**. When learners are assessed, they need to receive this information so that they know what they did well and what needs to be improved.

Purposes of feedback are to:

* Provide information to the learner on what the learner did well
* Identify for learner deviations from established standard of performance
* Correct a learner's inaccurate assumptions and/or undesirable actions
* Provide to the learner information on their strengths and weaknesses
* Provide specific directions to improve the learner's performance

Ensure that attention is given to non-verbals, ensuring that your tone, body posture, etc, match the verbal message. For example, it would be confusing to give feedback using a joking tone for a serious clinical error. Non-verbals carry more weight to the listener than do verbal. Therefore, make non-verbal and verbals match for maximum impact.

Important characteristics of feedback:

1. Confirm and use learner's own reflective observations as the basis for your own feedback comments.
2. Be **descriptive** rather than judgmental - avoiding evaluative language reduces the occurrence of defensive reactions.
3. Be **specific** rather than general - specific information is more useful than general information for promoting behavioral change. Vague criticisms such as "You lack an adequate knowledge base" are not helpful in learning and changing behavior. Also vague positive comments as "You're doing a fine job" does not reinforce and guide learning as well as, "You recognized the need to do a chest X-Ray on the child and helped to obtain the test while keeping the child calm."
4. Be **sensitive** to the needs of both the receiver and the sender of the feedback.
5. **Direct feedback toward behavior** that the receiver can do something about.
6. Positive, when deserved (reinforce good performance).
7. **Constructive** comments, not demeaning.
8. For example, provide information to the learner about what was on target, what needs to be corrected.
