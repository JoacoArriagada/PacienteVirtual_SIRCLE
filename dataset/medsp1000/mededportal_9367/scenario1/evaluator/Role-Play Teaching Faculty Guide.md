# TEACHING WORKSHOP/ROLE-PLAYING EXERCISE

### Teaching Faculty Guide

1. Ask students to generate one or several case examples of real clinical situations in which they had participated in patient resuscitation and witnessed the news of resuscitation and patient outcomes communicated to a family member (critically-ill news related to prognosis or news of death).
2. Review general guidelines to effective communication skills and using student offered cases for illustration, discuss guidelines for ‘Breaking Bad News’.

(Use Fast Fact on delivering bad news or mnemonics like ASCEND or SPIKES as a guide--citations and ASCEND’SPIKES mnemonic are listed below as resources).

1. Ask students to form pairs or triads (1-2 family members/physician--rest of group as observers).
2. Select the relevant role-playing communication exercise case (see below). Ask students to reflect back on their recently concluded simulated resuscitation session for medical/science/technical related details.

* Students should spend 5-7 minutes role-playing each communication case scenario.
* Encourage students who role-play the family members to adopt one of the various potential emotional reactions to receiving news of resuscitation: angry, hysterical, profound sadness, or disbelief.
* Following the role-play, have the "family member" and observer students complete the feedback form and return to facilitator.

1. Depending on time, students can reverse roles.
2. Please note that student peer observers should hand in the *anonymously* filled feedback form after end of role-play *to facilitator.*
3. Facilitator will also complete learner feedback form and use the collected feedback forms to guide conversation in debriefing.
4. Debrief the student experience--what worked well, what didn't, what was hard, what was easy? (see guidelines for debriefing post resuscitation).
5. Faculty may wish to demonstrate their own technique at delivering bad news, either at the start or conclusion of the student role-playing.
6. Conclude with discussion of self-reflection techniques on how to critically self-reflect as a tool for personal growth (see Guidelines to encourage student self-reflection).
7. Hand out student self-reflection essay assignment.

**The ASCEND Communication Pathway\***

**A*NTICIPATION***

* Before proceeding, set a goal for the interaction; Include care team
* Announce the names and roles of all participants; Sit down
* Assess patient capacity, desire for information (patient or family member)

**S*UMMARY***

* Encourage the patient/family to summarize current treatments/ condition, as well as expectations
* If the patient/family is not aware of information yet or have misconceptions, provide updated summary/clarifications

**C*ONCERNS***

* Pause to identify any specific concerns before moving on to “new” information
* Acknowledge concerns even if they cannot be addressed immediately

**E*XPLORATION/*E*XPLANATION***

Depending on the goal for the interaction and the needs of the patient/family:

* Present new information
* Explore possible goals, patient preferences
* Recommendations based on patient goals: define long- and short-term risks/benefits and alternatives
* For options of uncertain benefit, recommend a “trial of treatment”

**N*EXT STEPS***

* Define a clear plan (include how conflict or uncertainty will be addressed), and how progress will be monitored

**D*OCUMENTATION***

* Document A-S-C-E-N, time spent
* Encourage patient/family to write down information, questions

**\* Depending on the nature of the interaction, steps may be emphasized or eliminated**

Adapted from Knops K, Lamba S. "Clinical Application of ASCEND: A pathway to Higher Ground for Communication." *Journal of Palliative Medicine*. 13.7 (2010): 825-830.

**The SPIKES Communication Pathway\***

**S*ETTING UP***

* Arrange for privacy
* Involve significant others
* Sit down
* Make connection with the patient and family
* Manage time constraints and interruptions

**P*ERCEPTION***

* Ask what the patient or family knows

**I*NVITATION***

* Explore the patient's wishes regarding knowing his or her diagnosis

**K*NOWLEDGE***

* Warn the patient or family that bad news is coming and explain the diagnosis

**E*MOTIONS/*E*MPATHY***

* Observe for emotions
* Identify emotions
* Identify the reason for the emotion
* Provide empathetic statements

**S*TRATEGY/*S*UMMARY***

* Discuss treatment options if the patient is ready
* Confirm the patient's and family understanding of the diagnosis and plan

\*Adapted from Baile WF, Buckman R, et al. SPIKES-A six-step protocol for delivering bad news: Application to the patient with cancer. The Oncologist. 2000;5:302-311.
