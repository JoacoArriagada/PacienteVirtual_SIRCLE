**Goals, Objectives, Methods and Assessment of Educational Activity**

**Patient Resuscitation Related Communication Skills Education Module**

**Goal for education project**:

1. Senior medical students improve skills and knowledge to effectively communicate with family members of critically ill or injured patients who undergo resuscitations.
2. Senior medical students improve communication and self-reflection skills, both during and after patient resuscitations.

**Targeted Learners:** 4th year Medical students during a mandatory 4 week Emergency Medicine clerkship

(Module is easily modifiable for learners in graduate/undergraduate medical education).

**Project Objective:** To describe student outcomes associated with communication and self-reflection educational module surrounding resuscitation.

**Student Outcomes:**

**Reaction Level:** Student satisfaction with each component (Simulated resuscitation highlighting HCW-HCW team communication; Role play highlighting HCW-FAM communication; self-reflection).

HCW-healthcare worker; FAM-Family

**Learning Level:**

*Knowledge:*

* Use of SPIKES or ASCEND mnemonic in communication
* Use of closed-loop communication during resuscitation

*Skills:*

* Students demonstrate closed loop communication with team members
* Students deliver bad news with empathy and effective use of silence or body language during role-play
* Students are able to self-reflect for transformative learning.

*Attitudes:*

* Valuing team member opinions during simulated resuscitation
* Demonstration of empathic communication with family during role play
* Self-reflection exercise

.

**Behavior Level:** Learner feedback form – formative assessments rated by students and faculty in role-play (feedback form in resources).

**Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) Competencies**: effective communication; lifelong learning in medicine; self-awareness, self-care, personal growth and professional behavior; and diversity and the social and community contexts of health care.

1. Demonstrates effective patient-centered communication skills and basic approaches to exploring and handling strong emotions in families (ACGME Competencies: PFC, ICS).
2. Demonstrates basic family-centered communication skills, including active listening, empathic statements, use of silence, exploration of emotions, and appropriate non-verbal behaviors (ACGME Competencies: PFC, ICS).
3. Assesses family understanding of illness, and explores initial goals and values that might inform treatment decisions (ACGME Competencies: PFC, ICS).
4. Uses feedback to improve performance (ACGME Competencies: PBLI).
5. Uses strategies for self-care and self-reflection.

**Learning Activities**

**Learning activity #2**

**Crucial conversations around patient resuscitation:**

*Communication skills: HCW to Family communication during simulated resuscitation*

**Goal 2:** Students will be proficient in conducting difficult conversations with family members of patients who undergo resuscitation.

**Objective 2:** Students will be able to describe and practice the appropriate behaviors of responding to emotion, comforting someone in emotional shock when breaking bad news, and when delivering news of death to family survivors.

**Methods:**

1) Introduce mnemonic aids as a means to assist students in remembering key concepts to physician-family communication (ASCEND/SPIKES).

2) Highlight family-centered communication skills, including active listening, empathic statements, use of silence, exploration of emotions, and appropriate non-verbal behaviors (sit down, eye contact, warning shot, responding to emotion, silence when appropriate etc.).

3) Students will role-play as physicians and family members in an uncertain/poor prognosis post-resuscitation scenario.

**Assessment:** Students will be assessed by course faculty and student peer-observers

at the end of role-play, using the “Learner Feedback Form” (attached).
