Case #2, Round 3: Penetrating injury, adds BREATHING pneumothorax and chest tube

Brief narrative description of case

18-month-old toddler who was shot in the chest and abdomen and has tension pneumothorax, hypovolemic hemorrhagic shock, and altered mental status requiring airway protection.

Primary Learning Objectives (Additional to Rounds 1+2)

1. Recognize tension pneumothorax.
2. Prioritize management of tension pneumothorax over circulation and altered mental status.

Critical Actions (Additional to Rounds 1+2)

1. Perform needle decompression.
2. Perform chest tube placement.

Initial Presentation

Initial vital signs

Heart Rate (HR) 180, Blood Pressure (BP) 62/32, Respiratory Rate (RR) 12, Temperature (T) 96.5°F, SpO2 90% on non-rebreather, moaning during examination

Room Setup

Changes from prior round: move gunshot wounds to new locations: right anterior chest and right anterior abdomen. If unable to perform needle decompression and chest tube placement on primary mannequin, have additional task trainer ready.

Ideal Scenario Pre-arrival

Receive phone report. Activate Level I Trauma Code, request blood to bedside, request chest tube, assign roles and plan initial actions after arrival.

Primary Survey

Primary Survey Physician: "Airway is clear, shifted to left, breath sounds are decreased on right, slow respirations and hypoxia noted so will begin ventilation with bag-valve-mask device, patient is tachycardic to 170, and hypotensive to 62/32 with bullet wounds noted on the right chest and right upper abdomen. GCS 3."

Team Lead: "Continue ventilating the patient. Please needle decompress right chest with large-bore IV catheter. This is obstructive shock."

Procedural Physician performs needle decompression and follows with chest tube placement.

Team Lead: "As we relieve obstruction, this patient likely has hypovolemic hemorrhagic shock, from intraabdominal hemorrhage, please initiate 10 ml/kg of plasma (or pRBCs), what is the weight?"

Bedside provider: "Weight is 10 kg."

Team Lead: Give 100 mL of plasma (or 100 mL of pRBCs, or 200 mL of crystalloid if blood not available) and let's look for other gunshot wounds by looking head to toe and then rolling the patient, and as we find them cover with dressings and mark them with a radio-opaque marker please. We will reassess mental status as we ventilate and give fluids, but this patient will likely require airway protection.

E-FAST finds free fluid in peritoneum and hemothorax on the right.

As blood given, HR and BP and SpO2 improve as noted above.

Team Lead Mental Model: "To summarize, we have two bullet wounds on right anterior abdomen and right chest. We have a right sided chest tube placed with 200 mL of output. We have given 1 x 10 ml/kg bolus of plasma and have improved perfusion. The next step will be to secure the airway with intubation, unless the operating room is ready."

Surgery Attending: "Since the operating room is getting prepped, let's proceed with intubation prior to transport."

Intubation takes place with etomidate and rocuronium for induction and paralysis. Endotracheal intubation with video assistance. Subsequent x-ray performed to check tube placement as well as evaluate for bullets in the chest and abdomen.

Anticipated common errors:

Intubation prior to recognition and treatment of tension pneumothorax.

Chest tube placement/needle decompression can be difficult and require additional training.
