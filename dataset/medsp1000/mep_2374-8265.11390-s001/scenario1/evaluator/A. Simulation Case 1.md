Appendix A: Simulation Case #1

SIMULATION CASE TITLE #1: Blunt Trauma Case

LEARNER AUDIENCE: Surgery physician trainee (1 or 2 upper-level residents or fellows) or surgery advanced practice provider (APP) (2), Pediatric Emergency Medicine (PEM) physician (1-2 fellows or attendings), Emergency Center (EC) nurses (2) or Emergency Medical Technician (EMT) (2), EC Respiratory Therapist

PATIENT NAME: Charles

PATIENT AGE: 12 months old

CHIEF COMPLAINT: Intracranial injury, intra-abdominal injury, seizure

PHYSICAL SETTING: Resuscitation Bay of Emergency Center

Blunt Trauma Case

Brief narrative description of case

12-month-old child who has intracranial and intraabdominal injuries from undisclosed abusive trauma.

Primary Learning Objectives

1. Identify and assign roles at pre-arrival and arrival.
2. Demonstrate the coordination of receiving the patient and Emergency Medical Services (EMS) handoff.
3. Perform primary survey in order, and within 5 minutes.
4. Use crisis resource management skills (assume/assign clear roles, use closed loop communication, share mental model).
5. Perform secondary survey.
6. Consider inflicted physical abuse as diagnosis.

Critical Actions

1. During preparation phase assign roles and prepare equipment.
2. Activate trauma alert and neurosurgery call.
3. Perform primary survey. Items that are often missed:
   a. Maintain c-spine immobilization during airway assessment.
   b. Initiate fluid resuscitation (blood or crystalloid).
   c. Use modified Glasgow Coma Score (GCS) for non-verbal child.
   d. Consider mannitol or hypertonic saline.
   e. Obtain vital signs including temperature.
4. Express differential diagnosis including abusive blunt trauma with mismatch of injury pattern and history provided.
5. Perform secondary survey from head to extremities and back.

Heart Rate (HR) 100, Blood Pressure (BP) 65/30, Respiratory Rate (RR) 18, Temperature (T) 97.8°F, O2 saturation (SpO2) 95% on Room Air (RA)

INSTRUCTOR NOTES

Expected Intervention / Time point: Identify need for upgrade to Code 1 trauma based on vitals

Change in Case: If not identified, further drop blood pressure to 55/25

Expected Intervention / Time point: Provide supplemental oxygen via non-rebreather

Change in Case: Improve saturations to 100%

Additional Information: If decline in mental status not noted, decrease RR to 10, sats to 89%

Expected Intervention / Time point: Give blood: plasma or pRBC 10 ml/kg

Change in Case: Lower HR by 5, Increase BP to 100/40

Additional Information: Patient in hemorrhagic and neurogenic shock, HR depressed by increased ICP

Expected Intervention / Time point: Identify traumatic brain injury, consider hypertonic saline or mannitol

Change in Case: No further decline in respiratory or mental status if given

Expected Intervention / Time point: Identify abdominal bruising, concern for abuse, order trauma labs and consider CT abdomen/pelvis

Change in Case: If not identified, have embedded participant prompt removal of diaper revealing bruising

Additional Information: Provide lab results if appropriate indicating elevated transaminases

Expected Intervention / Time point: Stabilize for transport to CT

Change in Case: If not identified or suggested transport directly to OR, have surgery/neurosurgery team member request imaging

Ideal Scenario Actions

Pre-arrival: Team lead activates Code 2 trauma alert, leads pre-arrival team huddle and pages neurosurgery for suspicion of intracranial injury.

Arrival: Upgrade to Code 1 trauma

Airway/Breathing:

Provide supplemental oxygen via non-rebreather.

Maintain c-spine stabilization, place C-collar.

Consider and set up for intubation given declining mental status.

Cardiovascular:

Identify hemorrhagic shock: start with 10 ml/kg of blood products, plasma if available, or pRBC. If no blood products, give 20 ml/kg isotonic fluid.

Consider focused ultrasound examination to look for intra-abdominal hemorrhage.

Disability:

Recognize declining mental status GCS 13 to 9, traumatic brain injury, order CT head.

Consider hypertonic saline and/or mannitol to treat increased intracranial pressure.

Exposure:

Maintain temperature while performing head to toe examination.

Recognize abdominal bruising and concern for abusive/inflicted injuries.

Order full trauma panel of labs, consider CT abdomen/pelvis.

Summary and hand-off:

Team lead to provide mental model summarizing statement to lead surgeon and prepare team to transport to CT.

Lab and Imaging Results to provide team when asked:

Hemoglobin 8, AST 356, ALT 492, VBG 7.38/45, lactate 3

Chest X-ray normal

E-FAST results: free fluid in the right upper quadrant and suprapubic space
