• What is it and why did it happen to my baby?

o Genetic condition caused by presence of all or part of a third copy of chromosome 21 (also known as T21).   
o Approximately $9 5 \%$ of cases are due to spontaneous mutations (non-disjunction).   
o Generally, the recurrence risk is approximately $1 \%$ although this has some variability based on maternal age-related risk (i.e. higher risk of recurrence with increased maternal age).   
o About $3- 4 \%$ of cases are due to chromosomal translocations with approximately $1 / 3$ of these (i.e. $1 \%$ of all cases of Down Syndrome) occurring due to hereditary inheritance patterns.   
o The remaining $1 \%$ of cases is due to mosaicism.

• Why do you think my child has Down syndrome?

o Children with T21 are individuals but they share many physical characteristics including: hypotonia; epicanthal folds; flat nasal bridges; upslanting palpebral fissures; speckling of the iris; abnormal auricles; hyperflexibility; excessive skinfolds in the posterior aspect of the neck; a single transverse palmar crease; and a wide gap between the first and second toes.

How common is Down syndrome?

o Estimates vary but the evidence shows that, overall, approximately 1 out of every 700 babies born is diagnosed with having Down syndrome.   
o Although the incidence increases with maternal age, the majority of children (i.e. approximately $80 \%$ ) with T21 are born to women under 35 years old as this is in keeping with the baseline higher fertility rates.

Could I have prevented this?

o There is prenatal screening that can help to diagnose T21 before your child is born but, because most diagnoses are sporadic, there is nothing you could have done to prevent it from happening.

# • What is my baby at risk for right now?

o Most common conditions affecting neonates with T21 at less than 1 month old include:

Hypotonia (correlates to feeding difficulties and aspiration risk) Feeding problems – frequently includes gastroesophageal reflux and constipation and can increase frequency of respiratory infections if patient aspirates

Hidden malformations:

Congenital heart disease $( 4 0 - 5 0 \% )$ ) • Polycythemia $( 1 8 - 6 4 \% )$ ) • Transient myeloproliferative disorder $( 1 0 \% )$ ) • Gastrointestinal atresias $( 1 2 \% )$

Hearing problems $( 7 5 \% )$ – unlikely to impact newborns less than 1 month old; adverse effects may emerge after 1 month of age so infants with T21 should have hearing re-tested regardless of result on initial newborn hearing screen ▪ Cataracts $( 1 5 \% )$

# • What will be done for my baby in the first few weeks of life?

o 1) Diagnosis will be confirmed through a chromosomal analysis   
o 2) Assessments will be performed to rule out frequently associated conditions which carry morbidity for the baby including:   
Echocardiogram to screen for cardiac malformations   
Swallowing study to assess for aspiration risk   
Upper GI series if warranted to look for intestinal malformations   
Eye exam to assess for cataracts   
CBC to screen for polycythemia and transient myeloproliferative disorders   
Newborn metabolic screening which includes TSH to assess for congenital   
hypothyroidism

# • What is the life expectancy of individuals with Down syndrome?
