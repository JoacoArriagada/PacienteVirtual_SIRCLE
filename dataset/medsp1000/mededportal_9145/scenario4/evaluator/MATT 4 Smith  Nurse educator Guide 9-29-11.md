# MATT Team Case 4: Darrell Smith Nurse Educator Guide

Written for 3G Siim

# Learner Prep:

Bedside Nurse will be given script describing the change in patient status requiring an assessment and initiation of a MATT call. The Bedside Nurse will have time to briefly review the chart and be oriented to the mannequin and available equipment. They should expect a Resident Physician, an ICU Nurse, and a Respiratory Therapist to respond to the MATT call.

# Objectives:

Work as member of MATT team   
Identify progressive respiratory distress   
Recognize indication for Bipap   
Consider alternative causes for dyspnea (particularly CHF, PE with PMHx below)   
Optional Conversion to SVT (stable): Identify and treat new rhtyhm

# Intended Level:

MATT team with junior resident leader and junior nursing staff

# Setting:

COPD/Pneumonia medical ward:

# Background:

68 year old male admitted to hospital 8 days prior for bronchitis and exacerbation of COPD. MATT team called because of increased SOB and difficulty breathing.

# To Prep Floor Nurse:

Have them review the script below, ask them to assess patient as they would and call MATT   
Tell them they are to assume a role on team   
Introduce them to simulator, physical findings (pulses, lung and heart sounds, pupils, verbal responses).   
Give them bedside chart and the “floor nurse script” page that has data below:

# Bedside Nurse Script:

The patient has known hx of COPD, CAD, diastolic CHF, hx of multiple DVTs, OSA w/ nocturnal BIPAP and was admitted to hospital 6 days prior (11/22/2008) for pseudomonas bronchits and COPD exacerbation. He initially improved and today has been progressively SOB over 2 hours (11/28/08 at about 1800). The bedside nurse had noted his oxygen sat at $8 9 \%$ and increased his oxygen from 4 to 8 liters per minute about 30 minutes before the MATT call.

Bedside Nurse can provide info including patient lives alone and has participated in PT and OT until today.

# Needs for Sim Room:

Nasal cannula oxygen Hospital Chart IV heptrap in place Crash cart available Bipap available

# CASE:

68 year old male with progressive dyspnea.   
Patient is awake and interactive but moderate dyspnea at rest.

# History:

Developed progressing dyspnea over 2-3 hours. Has some cough, but not worse. No fever, no chest pain.

PMHx: COPD/OSA uses nocturnal BIPAP at home; CAD with multiple stents, diastolic CHF, multiple DVTs (last greater than a year ago), morbid obesity, GERD, BPH,

PE: RR 28, BP 152/88, HR 95, temp 37 C. No JVD,   
Bilateral wheeze without rales,   
No asymmetric leg edema   
LABs:   
WBC 11.4   
INR 3.2   
Hgb 10.7 (stable)   
Initial ABG 7.23/62/103/26 on 8 liters oxygen (labeled #1)   
If bipap given 7.39/54/75/32 on 15/5 (labeled #2)

# Case Tips:

He is very SOB at rest and ABG shows acute respiratory acidosis. Trend with push decline if no BIPAP at 7 minutes

Level 2 Optional Case Complication:   
If selected, the case will go into a stable SVT. The ACLS algorithm should be followed. Can revert to sinus tachycardia if adenosine is given.

# Evaluating Performance:

Passing performance (3 or greater): Adequeate team leader

Considers differential diagnosis including at least 2 of (CHF with diastolic dysfunction, PE (hx of PE, not on anticoagulation), cardiac ischemia, COPD exacerbation Identifies indication for BiPAP

Superior Performance (4) Considers differential diagnosis of at least 3 of above Orders appropriate BiPAP settings Identifies stable SVT and treats appropriately
