# MATT Team Case 5: Dotty Green Nurse Educator Guide

Written for: 3GSim

# Learner preparation:

Bedside Nurse will be given script describing the change in patient status requiring an assessment and initiation of a MATT call. The Bedside Nurse will have time to briefly review the chart and be oriented to the mannequin and available equipment. They should expect a Resident Physician, an ICU Nurse, and a Respiratory Therapist to respond to the MATT call.

# Objectives:

work as team (identify self, assign roles, use closed-loop communication, think aloud) manage stable tachycardia (atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular rate) transfer to higher level of care

# Level 2 objectives:

Recognize development of unstable tachycardia Appropriately cardiovert unstable patient

# Intended learner level:

Seasoned MATT team with junior resident leader

# Setting:

General medical floor

# Background:

Case of Dotty Green, an 87 year-old admitted 3 days ago with community acquired pneumonia. Has been asymptomatic, but assessment by Bedside Nurse will reveal a new irregular heart rate of 140.

# To prep floor nurse:

Prep floor nurse for 30-60 seconds.   
Tell them script below, ask them to assess patient as they would and call MATT Tell them they are to assume a role on team   
Introduce them to simulator, physical findings (pulses, lung and heart sounds, pupils, verbal responses).

Give them bedside chart and the “floor nurse script” page that has data below:

Floor Nurse script:

Case of Dotty Green, an 87 year-old lady admitted 3 days ago with community acquired pneumonia. Has been improving but likely will need rehab before returning home. She has been asymptomatic today with normal heart rate and blood pressure, and a stable O2 saturation in the low $9 0 ' s$ . It is time for you to perform a routine assessment.

# Needs: (extra equipment necessary for case scenario)

Wig for female patient   
IV access in place without fluids   
BP cuff and pulse oximeter attached to patient   
Bedside chart “Green, MATT 5”   
Prepare to have O2, fluids, cardiac medications, defibrillator for cardioversion

Case: Dotty Green, an 87 year-old, was admitted 3 days ago with community acquired pneumonia. Has been asymptomatic, but assessment by Bedside Nurse will reveal a new irregular heart rate of 140.

# HX:

She denies any symptoms currently.

PMH: COPD, CAD with stent last year, diastolic heart failure due to HTN, normal systolic function by echo, hypothyroidism.

CURRENT MEDS: Plavix 75 daily, Lopressor 50 BID, synthroid 50mcg daily, asa 81 mg daily, Rocephin/ zithromax, prednisone 40 daily, Duonebs every 6 hours prn, heparin 5000 units Q8 hours.

PE: Vitals: T 98.6 RR 14 HR 140 (irreg) BP 100/50 O2 Sat $9 0 \%$ on room air

LABS: (in chart)   
Resolved leukocytosis, stable Hgb, normal chem 8 for 3 days.   
Echo: normal LV size with EF $6 1 \%$ , abnormal diastolic function, otherwise unremarkable.

Available if asked: EKG showing atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response Labs and ABG will be normal.

# Case Tips:

Patient will be completely asymptomatic. May show some anxiety if team members do not explain to her what is happening.

SIM Driver may raise blood pressure if fluids are given and O2 if oxygen is increased.

Heart rate will improve when a rate control medication is given.

Resident should plan transfer to higher level of care and explain plan to floor nurse to complete scenario. Prompt them to clarify the plan for the team if they do not do this spontaneously.

Patient can progress to unstable tachycardia with hypotension, which responds only to cardioversion. Patient may complain of SOB or chest pain to increase urgency.

Cardioversion: Recommended technique for unstable AF is to consider sedation (2-5mg versed $+ / -$ morphine) and then give a Synchronized shock at 100-200 J.
