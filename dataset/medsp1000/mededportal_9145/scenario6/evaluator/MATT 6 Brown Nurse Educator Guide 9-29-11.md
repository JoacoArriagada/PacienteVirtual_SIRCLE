# MATT Team Case 6: Richard Brown Nurse Educator Guide

Written for: 3GSim

# Learner preparation:

Bedside Nurse will be given script describing the change in patient status requiring an assessment and initiation of a MATT call. The Bedside Nurse will have time to briefly review the chart and be oriented to the mannequin and available equipment. They should expect a Resident Physician, an ICU Nurse, and a Respiratory Therapist to respond to the MATT call.

# Objectives:

Appropriately evaluate high risk patient with chest pain   
Recognize STEMI   
Activate STEMI protocol orders   
Demonstrate appropriate communication & leadership in MATT situation   
Appropriately hand off patient to cardiologist

# Intended learner level:

Second year resident

# Setting:

Cardiac unit (N2W)

# Background:

Case of Richard Brown, an 81 yo admitted a few hours ago with uncomplicated NSTEMI, pain-free, awaiting cath.

# To prep floor nurse:

Have them review the script below, ask them to assess patient as they would and call MATT   
Tell them they are to assume a role on team   
Introduce them to simulator, physical findings (pulses, lung and heart sounds, pupils, verbal responses).   
Give them bedside chart and the “floor nurse script” page that has data below.

# Bedside Nurse Script:

Richard Brown is an 81 yo admitted a few hours ago with uncomplicated NSTEMI, painfree, awaiting cath. Bedside nurse finds patient to have acute 10/10 substernal chest pain. Vitals are stable with BP 160/90. One sublingual nitroglycerin is ordered, with no change in pain or clinical status. MATT Team is called.

# Needs for sim room:

Dressed/gowned as male, on telemetry and pulse ox monitor with O2 at 2L NC   
Has IV access with NSS running at 100cc/hr   
Bedside chart   
Available if requested:   
Monitor, cardiac meds   
Hard copy STAT EKG in facilitator folder

# Case:

Richard Brown is an 81yo retired executive, admitted with chest pain.

# History:

The chest pain started five days ago. Pain was a mid-chest heaviness associated with fatigue & SOB. Came on with activity & improved with rest. He didn’t want to come to the hospital, but felt very weak & his wife and PCP made him come. He had no pain when he came to the ER but was told he had had a heart attack and need to have a catheterization.

About 10 minutes ago the pressure returned while he was just lying in bed, and it quickly became severe (10/10). He feels sweaty, nauseated, and anxious & SOB. This never happened before. The nitro pill did not help.

# PMHx:

Hypertension, high cholesterol, diabetes on pills. He quit smoking pretty long ago. He takes 3 pills twice a day but can’t recall the names while he is in pain. No prior heart problems.

In the chart: Admitted as NSTEMI based on high troponins and ST depressions.

No known drug allergies.

Home meds: avapro, metformin, gemfibrozil, Vit E.

Current meds in hospital: lopressor, ASA, heparin/ integrelin, avapro, lopid, NSS $\textcircled { a } 1 0 0 \mathrm { c c } / \mathrm { h r }$ .

PE: No change with SL NTG x 1 dose. 160/90, HR 80s, afeb, RR 20, O2 sat $9 0 \%$ . Labs: (in chart) WBC 11, Hgb 15, CPK 398 MB 14 Trop 16.11, BUN 30, Cr 1.09

# Case Tips:

Goals are to recognize high risk cardiac patient and quickly identify STEMI, to appropriately lead MATT Team, and to activate STEMI protocol orders to get patient quickly to cath lab.

Patient will not spontaneously worsen. Sim Driver may initiate low BP or add PVCs if moving too slowly.

Should look to alleviate patient’s discomfort but quickly recognize the need for/ initiate plan for emergent cath. Sim Driver should be prepared to call back as the cardiologist (610-988-9106) for an appropriate hand-off from the resident. Resident should identify

self, level of urgency/ reason for calling (STEMI Alert), patient name/ location, active CP with STEMI on EKG, treatment thus far, and current vitals.
