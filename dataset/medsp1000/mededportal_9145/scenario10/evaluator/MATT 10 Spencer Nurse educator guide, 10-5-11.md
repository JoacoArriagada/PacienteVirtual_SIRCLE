# MATT Team Case 10: Spencer Nurse Educator Guide

# Learner prep:

Bedside Nurse will be given script describing the change in patient status requiring an assessment and initiation of a MATT call. The Bedside Nurse will have time to briefly review the chart and be oriented to the mannequin and available equipment. They should expect a Resident Physician, an ICU Nurse, and a Respiratory Therapist to respond to the MATT call.

# Objectives:

Work as a member of MATT team   
Consider differential diagnosis of mental status change including: alcohol withdrawal syndrome/delirium tremens and increased intracranial pressure   
Recognize increased intracranial pressure signs (Cushing’s reflex: HTN, bradycardia, respiratory depression)   
Optional conversion to bradycardia arrest: identify and treat symptomatic bradycardia

# Intended learner levels:

MATT team with junior resident leader, bedside nurse, respiratory therapist and ICU nurse

# Setting:

General medical floor

# Background:

81 year old male with hx of CAD, alcohol abuse admitted one day prior after being found at the base of his steps with apparent fall and probable syncope. He fell attempting to go to the bathroom in his hospital room about two hours ago.

# To Prep Floor Nurse:

Have them review the script below, ask them to assess patient as they would and call MATT   
Tell them they are to assume a role on team   
Introduce them to simulator, physical findings (pulses, lung and heart sounds, pupils, verbal responses).   
Give them bedside chart and the “floor nurse script” page that has data below:

# Case Tips:

The case begins two hours after a fall.   
The patient has confusion with signs of intracranial pressure increase (HTN, bradycardia, hypoventilation).

Expect learner to consider ICP increase, alcohol withdrawal and perhaps COPD exacerbation with CO2 retention

Two minutes after “MD ARRIVES” will develop progression of HTN, bradycardia, eyes will close and should be less responsive. A Glasgow score will go to less than 8 if loses withdrawal to pain, eyes open to pain only and speaks only inappropriate words.

No action will halt progression but expect to identify ICP increase and prepare for CT scan, speak to neurosurgery and transfer level of care. Considerations of treatment may include ICP monitoring (in ICU), refrain from tx BP unless $\mathrm { C P P } { > } 1 2 0$ (MAP –ICP), mannitol, hyperventilation (may consider intubation) but not steroids.

CT ordered one hour ago because of the fall may be available if CT scan mentioned (shows new subdural hematoma)

# Level 2 Optional case complication :

If Bradyarrest is selected, the case will move to HR in low 30s and BP in 60s systolic. Appropriate actions may include converting to a Code Blue, initial atropine treatment and consideration of external pacing

# Evaluating performance:

Passing performance (3 or greater)

Considers differential diagnosis of mental status change: including ICP increase, alcohol withdrawal syndrome, and perhaps COPD exacerbation w. CO2 retention, or medication side effect.

Recognize urgency to proceed with CT scan of brain and increase level of care.

Superior performance (4)   
Considers options of ICP monitoring or therapy such as mannitol   
Appropriate management of Bradycardia arrest
