**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

*As the students enter the room, they find the patient in respiratory distress. Upon identification that the patient is in respiratory distress and hypoxemic, the patient should initially be placed on a high flow oxygen face mask (non-rebreather face mask) as one of the students begins to find out more information about the patient’s shortness of breath (performing a focused H&P). Another student could be reviewing the admission H&P to familiarize the group with why the patient was admitted to the hospital. A physical examination would reveal; accessory muscle use and wheezes. The admission chest radiograph should be reviewed and a new radiograph ordered. The radiographs are unchanged (the same radiograph and ECG can be used). The x-ray demonstrates hyperinflation and a right upper lobe infiltrate. If an ABG is ordered the results would be; ABG on 2L NC: pH 7.29, PaCO2 68, PaO2 44, SaO2 87% or on high flow oxygen pH 7.29, PaCO2 68, PaO2 64, SaO2 92%. The patient should be placed on BiPAP because of continued respiratory distress despite high flow oxygen via face mask. Medical therapy should be initiated with albuterol, ipratropium, systemic corticosteroids and magnesium. Regardless of the extent of the medications administered or respiratory support provided, the patient has more respiratory distress and rapidly becomes bradycardic, apneic and loses pulses at 5 minutes into the case. The initial resuscitation is started with BVM ventilation and chest compressions. If the senior resident has not already been called by the students to respond to the exam room, the nurse will call the hospital operator to call a code, the senior resident will then respond to the room to run the code. The patient has a return of spontaneous circulation after approximately 4-5 minutes of chest compressions and 2 rounds of epinephrine. The care givers can then arrange for the patient to be transferred to the ICU. The scenario typically ends with a few minutes available for the senior resident confederates to provide feedback to the students.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. *Difficulty with simulation: Although our students have prior experience with simulated patient encounters, some still find the process artificial. Because of this, we remind the students at the beginning of the session that they should provide care to their simulated case as they would any other patient. They are reminded that they should perform a focused H&P and initiate care (both diagnostics and therapeutics). They are also advised that the preceptor in the control room will role play the patient and nurse when applicable.*
2. *Difficulty with examining the simulator: At times, we find that some students have difficulty identifying rales when listening to the simulator. This may at times be a limitation of the simulator. If the students report that they auscultate clear lungs, they are informed that the patient has rales. Other aspects of the physical examination will be provided when asked (skin exam, extremity exam, etc.).*
3. *Difficulty addressing respiratory distress in a timely fashion: For this case, we find that some student groups either delay the management of the patient’s respiratory distress as they gather data from the H&P or provide minimal treatment initially in the form of nasal cannula oxygen while the patient remains hypoxemic and in respiratory distress. This is addressed in the debriefing and an approach to the initial management of a patient with dyspnea is reviewed.*
4. *Remembering medication doses: It is common that the students are not familiar with certain medication doses. We encourage the students to use available resources such as their smart phones to confirm medication doses. During our debriefing, we review proper medication selection and dosing.*

*If medications are ordered without a specific dose or an inaccurate dose, the preceptor role playing the nurse will ask the student’s to confirm the medication dose.*
