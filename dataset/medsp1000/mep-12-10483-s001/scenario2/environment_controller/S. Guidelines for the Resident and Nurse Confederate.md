# Role of the Senior Resident

# Sign Out

1. Provide a written sign-out to the night float teams. Graduating students will be in groups of 3 and will be rotating through 4 clinical simulation stations. A written sign out will be provided for you to verbally review with the 4 night float teams. Copies will be provided for each student. Each sign-out will include 4 patients.   
2. The sign-out’s will occur at 1pm, 2:30pm and 4pm each day.   
3. A senior resident confederate will be available in the simulation control room for phone consultation for all 4 cases.   
4. Throughout most of the workshop, the senior resident can stay in the simulation control room to observe the groups. When available the senior resident will answer the phone in the simulation control room when the students are calling for help. If the senior resident is unavailable, a faculty member may answer the phone and pose as the senior resident.   
5. For all cases the key will be to encourage critical thinking. DO NOT tell the students what to do. You can probe their thought process by asking them what they think is wrong with the patient, what they have done so far, how the patient’s condition has changed since they started to care for the patient, and what they think needs to be done. Try to get them to commit to a diagnosis or work-up. Try to persuade them away from just repeating their bedside findings and help them formulate an assessment and plan. Again, DO NOT tell them what to do, let them figure it out on their own. It is OK to tell them you can come to the room, however the ONLY case you actually respond to the bedside is the COPD / PEA arrest case.   
6. COPD / PEA arrest case. For this case, the senior resident will respond to the room at the appropriate time (30-40 seconds after the cardiac arrest). If the students do not call for assistance by the time the patient goes into cardiac arrest, the “Nurse” will call a code. You will take over the resuscitation and run the code. Please task the students with team member activities (chest compressions, BVM ventilation, etc.). After two rounds of medications, the patient will have ROSC. You can briefly review post code management (vent settings, transfer to ICU, post intubation CXRAY, ABG, etc.) as well as debrief the case with the students.

# Role of the Nurse

1. For the COPD / PEA case a confederate nurse will be in the exam room and should help out the student group as needed. This patient will go into a PEA arrest after about 4 minutes regardless of the therapy provided. If the senior resident has not already been called, the nurse will pick up the phone and call a code. The nurse will also help with connecting the patient to the defibrillator and other related tasks as needed.
