**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*As the students enter the room, they find the patient in respiratory distress. Upon identification that the patient is in respiratory distress and hypoxemic, the patient should initially be placed on a high flow oxygen face mask (non-rebreather face mask) as one of the students begins to find out more information about the patient’s shortness of breath (performing a focused H&P). Another student could be reviewing the admission H&P to familiarize the group with why the patient was admitted to the hospital. A physical examination would reveal accessory muscle use and wheezes. The admission chest radiograph should be reviewed and a new radiograph ordered. The radiographs are unchanged. The x-ray demonstrates hyperinflation and a right upper lobe infiltrate. If an ABG is ordered the results would be ABG on 2L NC: pH 7.29, PaCO2 68, PaO2 44, SaO2 87% or on high flow oxygen pH 7.29, PaCO2 68, PaO2 64, SaO2 92%. The patient should be placed on BiPAP because of continued respiratory distress despite high flow oxygen via face mask. Medical therapy should be initiated with albuterol, ipratropium, systemic corticosteroids and magnesium. Regardless of the extent of the medications administered or respiratory support provided, the patient has more respiratory distress and rapidly becomes bradycardic, apneic and loses pulses at 5 minutes into the case. The initial resuscitation is started with BVM ventilation and chest compressions. If the senior resident has not already been called by the students to respond to the exam room, the nurse will call the hospital operator to call a code, the senior resident will then respond to the room to run the code. The patient has a return of spontaneous circulation after approximately 4-5 minutes of chest compressions and 2 rounds of epinephrine. The care givers can then arrange for the patient to be transferred to the ICU.*
