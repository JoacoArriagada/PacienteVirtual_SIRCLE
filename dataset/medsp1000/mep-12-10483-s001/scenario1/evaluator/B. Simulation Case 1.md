**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

*As the students enter the room, they find the patient is in respiratory distress. The students verbalize for the nurse (the preceptor in the simulation control room would respond as the nurse) to connect the patient to the heart monitor and obtain a full set of vital signs. Upon identification of respiratory distress and hypoxemia, the patient should initially be placed on a high flow oxygen face mask (non-rebreather face mask) as one of the students begins to find out more information about the patient’s shortness of breath (performing a focused H&P). At this time, another student could be reviewing the admission H&P to familiarize the group with why the patient was admitted to the hospital. A physical examination would reveal JVD, accessory muscle use and rales. A chest radiograph is ordered along with a 12-lead ECG. The chest radiograph demonstrates pulmonary edema, the ECG shows sinus tachycardia. Initial laboratory studies can be ordered (CBC, BMP, troponin, etc.), the results are not immediately available. If an ABG is ordered the results would be ABG room air: pH 7.38, PaCO2 42, PaO2 54, SaO2 88% or on high flow oxygen; pH 7.38, PaCO2 42, PaO2 88, SaO2 96%). The patient should be placed on BiPAP because of continued respiratory distress despite high flow oxygen via face mask. Medical therapy should be initiated with sublingual followed by intravenous nitroglycerin. As the respiratory status and BP improves, the patient can also be administered an intravenous ace inhibitor (enalaprilat) and furosemide. The care givers can arrange for the patient to be transferred to the ICU.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. *Difficulty with simulation: Although our students have prior experience with simulated patient encounters, some still find the process artificial. Because of this, we remind the students at the beginning of the session that they should provide care to their simulated case as they would any other patient. They are reminded that they should perform a focused H&P and initiate care (both diagnostics and therapeutics). They are also advised that the preceptor in the control room will role play the patient and nurse when applicable.*
2. *Difficulty with examining the simulator: At times, we find that some students have difficulty identifying rales when listening to the simulator. This may at times be a limitation of the simulator. If the students report that they auscultate clear lungs, they are informed that the patient has rales. Other aspects of the physical examination will be provided when asked (skin exam, extremity exam, etc.).*
3. *Difficulty addressing respiratory distress in a timely fashion: For this case, we find that some student groups either delay the management of the patient’s respiratory distress as they gather data from the patient or provide minimal treatment initially in the form of nasal cannula oxygen while the patient remains hypoxemic and in respiratory distress. This is addressed in the debriefing and an approach to the initial management of a patient with dyspnea is reviewed.*
4. *Remembering medication doses: It is common that the students are not familiar with certain medication doses. We encourage the students to use available resources such as their smart phones to confirm medication doses. If medications are ordered without a specific dose or an inaccurate dose, the preceptor role playing the nurse will ask the student’s to confirm the medication dose. During our debriefing, we review proper medication selection and dosing.*
