**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

*As the students enter the room, they find the patient who appears to be uncomfortable. The patient reports that he/she is having a lot of abdominal pain that got much worse about 20 minutes earlier. The students should review the admission H&P. If asked, the patient will report that he has been having upper abdominal pain for a couple of days and vomiting dark material today. If asked, he will report using Motrin (600mg 4-5 a day for the past week). He is also a smoker (1/2 pack per day for about 35 years) and drinks beer a couple of times a week. If pressed on his alcohol consumption, he reports that he drinks about 2 six packs of beer a week. Ideally, the students would recognize that the patient is having progressive tachycardia and that his BP is decreasing as a result of an abdominal emergency. Initial management would include administering intravenous normal saline solution and analgesia. Obtaining an upright chest radiograph will confirm the diagnosis of pneumoperitoneum. Antibiotics should be administered and the patient should have a nasogastric tube inserted (can be verbalized). Surgical consultation should be obtained.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. *Addressing pain: At times with this case, we see that there is a delay in treating the patient’s pain. The students will be reminded a few times throughout the case by the patient that he/she is still in pain if this has not been addressed.*
2. *Failure to appreciate the change in the blood pressure: As in real life, the BP is a static vital sign that has to be periodically rechecked. As opposed to the cardiac monitor where the student can see incremental changes in the heart rate, the BP has to be recycled to recheck its value. At times during this case, students will need to be prompted by the faculty preceptor (role playing the nurse) to recheck the BP.*
3. *Delay in diagnosis: With the patient already having a known upper GI bleed and suspected peptic ulcer disease vs gastritis, some students will experience a delay before obtaining the upright chest radiograph which will provide the information necessary to confirm the diagnosis.*
