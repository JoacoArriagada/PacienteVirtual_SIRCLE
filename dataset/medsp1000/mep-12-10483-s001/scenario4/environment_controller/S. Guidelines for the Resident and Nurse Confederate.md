# Role of the Senior Resident

# Sign Out

1. Provide a written sign-out to the night float teams. Graduating students will be in groups of 3 and will be rotating through 4 clinical simulation stations. A written sign out will be provided for you to verbally review with the 4 night float teams. Copies will be provided for each student. Each sign-out will include 4 patients.   
2. A senior resident confederate will be available in the simulation control room for phone consultation for all 4 cases.   
3. Throughout most of the workshop, the senior resident can stay in the simulation control room to observe the groups. When available the senior resident will answer the phone in the simulation control room when the students are calling for help. If the senior resident is unavailable, a faculty member may answer the phone and pose as the senior resident.   
4. For all cases the key will be to encourage critical thinking. DO NOT tell the students what to do. You can probe their thought process by asking them what they think is wrong with the patient, what they have done so far, how the patient’s condition has changed since they started to care for the patient, and what they think needs to be done. Try to get them to commit to a diagnosis or work-up. Try to persuade them away from just repeating bedside findings and help them formulate an assessment and plan. Again, DO NOT tell them what to do; let them figure it out on their own. It is OK to tell them you can come to the room, however the ONLY case you actually respond to the bedside is the COPD / PEA arrest case.
