**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*As the students enter the room, they find the patient who appears to be uncomfortable. The patient reports having a lot of abdominal pain that got much worse about 20 minutes earlier. The students should review the admission H&P. If asked, the patient will report having upper abdominal pain for a couple of days and vomiting dark material today. If asked, the patient will report using Motrin (600mg 4-5 a day for the past week). The patient is also a smoker and drinks beer a couple of times a week; if pressed on alcohol consumption, the patient reports drinking about 2 six packs of beer a week. Ideally, the students would recognize that the patient is having progressive tachycardia and that the BP is decreasing as a result of an abdominal emergency. Initial management would include administering intravenous normal saline solution and analgesia. Obtaining an upright chest radiograph will confirm the diagnosis of pneumoperitoneum. Antibiotics should be administered and the patient should have a nasogastric tube inserted (can be verbalized). Surgical consultation should be obtained.*
