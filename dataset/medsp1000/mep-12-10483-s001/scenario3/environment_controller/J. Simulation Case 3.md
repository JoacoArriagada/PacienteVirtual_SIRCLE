| **Brief narrative description of case** | The patient was admitted to the hospital this morning with chest pain and shortness of breath. An incomplete H&P was performed which led to the patient being admitted for a rule out MI. The patient has ongoing sharp, pleuritic CP and shortness of breath which is as a result of a missed pulmonary embolism. |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*As the students enter the room, they find the patient in the hospital bed that is a bit uncomfortable but is in no apparent distress. On further discussion with the patient, the patient informs the students that the patient is continuing to have ongoing chest pain and feels a little short of breath. Only if prompted and asked will the patient elaborate that the chest pain is left sided, anterior chest pain and sharp in character that gets worse with taking a deep breath. Ideally, the students will inquire about the chest pain and begin to suspect that this is more than a low risk rule out MI case. The students should review the admission H&P, review the original chest radiograph and ECG and repeat the studies. The repeat studies are the same as original ECG and chest radiograph. In addition, the student should uncover the recent travel history and findings of left calf pain and tenderness. The students should order a D-Dimer (which will be elevated), followed by a CT angiogram of the thorax and will find out that the patient has a pulmonary embolism and should initiate anticoagulation. Some students may decide to directly order a CT angiogram and forego the D-Dimer test.*
