**Case 1- Wrong Patient/Wrong Medicine**

**Training Materials for Standardized Patient**

**Family Member Profile:**

1. Age: 20-70
2. Gender: Male or Female
3. Affect (mannerisms, behavior): Confused, stunned, eventually distrustful and angry
4. Social History/Lifestyle: lives with patient, healthy
5. Occupation: working in business
6. Marital Status: single
7. General appearance: neat in appearance

**Scenario:**

Lana Archer, a 45-year-old female, was seen in the emergency department earlier today for fever, cough, and shortness of breath. You dropped her off this morning on your way to work and received a call several hours later from Lana asking for a ride home. She told you that she was diagnosed with pneumonia and was being sent home with an inhaler and another antibiotic.

You are Lana’s only local relative, and you have a good relationship. You have been trying to get her to quit smoking for several years, as you have had family members pass from lung cancer. You have not had an advance directive conversation with Lana, as she is relatively young and healthy, but believe that Lana is a Full Code and would like any interventions done.

You are confused, as you came to the front desk of the waiting room to pick up your family member and have been redirected to a quiet room. You figure they just do not want more people back in the department with flu season, and so are sitting in a chair reviewing work emails when the medical student enters the room. You put down your phone to greet the resident. After it is disclosed that Lana received ceftriaxone, you become progressively angrier and more distrustful, asking several questions in sequence and not always waiting for an answer from the resident.

**Instructions for the Family Member**

1. **How the family responds to the physician’s initial inquiry**
   1. When the medical student enters the room, ask, “Where is Lana?”
2. **The family member’s demeanor at the beginning and throughout the encounter (affect, non-verbal behavior)**
   1. You are confused, then angry and overwhelmed by the unexpected news. At the beginning, you are still expecting Lana to come home that day.
3. **The family member’s concerns regarding his/her understanding**
   1. You are surprised by the events and are concerned about Lana continuing to receive care at a hospital who gave her a medication to which she is allergic. Ask questions about this accordingly, but do not escalate to insisting Lana be transferred elsewhere.
4. **How the family member will respond to different interviewing styles**
   1. If the student is evasive and does not mention that an error occurred, continue to question the events surrounding Lana’s current situation
   2. If the student declines to offer an apology or empathetic statement, become angrier and more accusatory
5. **Questions the family member will consistently ask during the encounter**
   1. “Who is responsible for this?”
   2. “How did this happen?”
   3. “Is she going to be alright?”
   4. “Did her smoking cause this?”
   5. “Who is going to pay for this?”
   6. \*\*In response to medical student inquiry about further questions:
      1. “How do I know this won’t happen again?”
6. **The challenges the family member will present to the physician**
   1. You are struggling to understand how your family member who was supposed to be going home is now on life-support in the ICU. You also may not be able to answer all the physician’s questions.
