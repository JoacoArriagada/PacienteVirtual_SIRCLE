1. **Emergency Medicine Pre-Code Scenario: Theophylline Overdose**
2. **Learning Objectives**
   1. **Primary**
      1. Demonstrate appropriate assessment of a patient with tachycardia, vomiting and hypotension
      2. Consider underlying causes of the presenting condition: Theophylline Toxicity
      3. Identify causative agent through history, physical, exam and laboratory values
      4. Initiate an appropriate treatment plan including aggressive nausea control, cautious electrolyte replacement, and drug elimination with gastrointestinal decontamination using activated charcoal and extracorporeal removal by hemodialysis
   2. **Secondary**
      1. Identify appropriate specialties for consult including Toxicology and Nephrology
   3. **Critical Actions Checklist**
      1. Perform a rapid assessment: CAB, vital signs
      2. Perform a focused history and examination
      3. Initiate treatment:
         1. Oxygen
         2. IV Fluids
         3. Aggressive treatment of active vomiting
         4. Place patient on cardiac monitor
         5. Assessment of cardiac arrhythmia with overdose-appropriate treatments
         6. Cautious correction of hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia and hyperglycemia
         7. Consultation with Toxicology for treatment plan review
         8. Gastrointestinal decontamination with activated charcoal once vomiting controlled
         9. Referral to Nephrology for consideration of hemodialysis
      4. Request appropriate tests
         1. Lab tests
         2. Chest x-ray
         3. EKG

6. **Case Narrative**
   1. **Scenario Background**
      1. Chief Complaint: A 55 year old patient presents to the ED with the complaint of 4 hours of severe nausea and vomiting accompanied by a new coarse tremor noted in the arms and legs bilaterally. Secondary complaints include palpitations, abdominal pain, and lightheadedness
      2. History: A medical history of moderate to severe COPD controlled with a multi-drug regimen at home. The patient had some worsening of COPD over the last 48 hours and recognized this as a COPD exacerbation. The patient believed that they had taken 10 tablets of prednisone at 8 hours and 1 hour before presentation.
      3. Extended History: The true story is that the patient has accidently taken 20 theophylline tablets, not prednisone.
      4. Medications: The patient has a medication list in the pocket and will identify the medication taken as theophylline when asked.
   2. **Scenario Initial State**
      1. Patient Habitus and Presentation: Sitting up in bed actively vomiting when the medical learner first arrives. Active coarse tremor visualized. No tachypnea is seen and the patient is not wheezing.
      2. **Physiology:** T 37.2, P 130, BP 98/58. O2 saturation 93%. Supraventricular Tachycardia on monitor
      3. **Focused exam:** Patient in distress with nausea, abdominal pain, tremor, and palpitations.
   3. **Scenario Branch Points**
      1. **Scenario C:** The patient is experiencing severe nausea, vomiting, and palpitations. The telemetry strip shows SVT. This rhythm will either briefly respond to IV adenosine or not respond at all. The heart rate will respond to IV dosing of a calcium channel blocker as well as IV beta-blocker therapy. The scenario director has the option of progressing the rhythm to a ventricular tachycardia, and this rhythm will respond to IV amiodarone. Emergent endotracheal intubation can also be simulated.
      2. **Laboratory Studies:** Laboratory results are located in **Appendix J**
      3. **X-ray and EKG:** Results are located in **Appendix H and Appendix I**
      4. **Medications:**
         1. IV Normal Saline bolus improves baseline blood pressure slightly
         2. Lorazepam may or may not convert SVT
         3. Activated charcoal is important to long term stabilization
         4. Ondansetron 8mg IV stops nausea and vomiting
         5. Adenosine may briefly convert SVT or not respond
         6. Amiodarone prevents progression to unstable ventricular tachycardia if given early in wide complex tachycardia
         7. Calcium channel blockers reduce tachycardia to the 90-100 range
      5. **Consultation of Specialist(s)**
         1. A Toxicologist should be consulted once the diagnosis of theophylline overdose is made
         2. A Nephrologist should be consulted for consideration of hemodialysis
         3. After sedation is given and intubation takes place, Critical Care Service should be consulted for focused hand-off and admission

**Key Learner Events**

1. Order oxygen
2. Request appropriate tests: Lab, EKG, Chest x-ray, ABG
3. Identification of the causative agent: Theophylline poisoning
4. Assessment of cardiac arrhythmia with overdose-appropriate treatment
5. Early cardiovascular support with initiation of IV fluids
6. Consideration of activated charcoal
7. Cautious correction of the electrolyte abnormality
8. Request appropriate consults: Toxicology, Nephrology and Critical Care
