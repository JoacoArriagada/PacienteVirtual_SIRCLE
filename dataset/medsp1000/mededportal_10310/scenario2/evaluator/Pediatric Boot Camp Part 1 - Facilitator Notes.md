Differential diagnosis includes: asthma exacerbation (status asthmaticus), pneumonia (community acquired and/or aspiration), etc…

2. Respiratory: Patient still demonstrating signs of respiratory distress and requiring aggressive bronchodilator therapy and supplemental oxygen; continuous albuterol at 20mg/hr - titrate/wean as appropriate; provide supplemental $\mathsf { O } _ { 2 }$ via facemask to maintain $S _ { 9 } 0 _ { 2 } > 9 2 \%$ ; continue course of oral steroids for treatment of status asthmaticus; monitor respiratory status with pulse oximetry, continuous cardiorespiratory monitors and frequent bedside assessments

4. ID: No history of fever and normal chest x-ray make bacterial infectious process unlikely; likely viral pathogen - will follow-up viral respiratory panel; monitor for fever and/or worsened respiratory status - consider repeat chest x-ray if concerned for developing pneumonia

5. Neuro: No current signs of seizure activity but patient known to have lowered seizure threshold in the setting of acute illness; continue home doses of levetiracetam, topiramate and clonazepam; seizure plan for seizure ${ \ge } 5$ min or $^ { > 2 }$ seizures in 1 hour is to give lorazepam 0.1 $\mathsf { m g / k g } \mid \mathsf { V } _ { i }$ ; will make neurology aware of patient’s admission
