# For small group leader only. Assessment and Plan

IMPRESSION: 10 week old girl with likely pyelonephritis leading to vomiting and dehydration. Differential diagnosis includes pyelonephritis with/without underlying GU abnormality, sepsis, appendicitis, intussusception, malrotation w/ volvulus, etc.

(Students should justify the diagnoses included their differential - example: Pyelonephritis likely given presence of fever and vomiting in child with urinalysis suggestive of urinary tract infection. Meningitis may be in the differential here as well.)

# Plan (problem-based):

1. Pyelonephritis

a.Empiric antibiotics - Continue ceftriaxone to broadly cover for common urinary pathogens, follow-up urine and blood culture/sensitivity results, transition to oral antibiotic therapy when able to tolerate PO

b.Renal imaging - Ultrasound ordered to evaluate for evidence of abscess, hydronephrosis or renal structural abnormality. Urology consult if abnormality detected, consider out patient VCUG if abnormalities are present to evaluate for reflux

2. Dehydration a. IV fluids - continue fluid resuscitation given continued tachycardia and abnormal exam findings b. Reassess HR, BP, urine output and physical exam after additional fluid bolus

3. Vomiting, likely related to systemic illness a. IV fluids - resuscitation, replacement and maintenance fluids as needed b. Allow to drink clears when interested, if able to tolerate advance diet

4. Disposition a. Patient currently requires inpatient hospitalization for IV hydration and parenteral antibiotics until vomiting improved b. Will facilitate renal ultrasound during hospitalization and possibly out patient VCUG pending results of this test c. Coordination of PMD follow-up after discharge
