# Assessment and Plan

IMPRESSION: 11-month old girl with vomiting, abdominal pain, fever and dehydration. Differential diagnosis includes: early acute gastroenteritis, pneumonia, acute appendicitis, intussusception, pyelonephritis, malrotation with volvulus....   
(Students should justify the diagnoses included their differential - example: intussusception possible given presence of vomiting without diarrhea in sleepy child with intermittent periods of abdominal pain).

# Plan (system based):

1. FEN/GI: Patient appears mildly dehydrated. Patient has received one $2 0 \mathsf { m l / k g } \mathsf { N S }$ bolus. Will repeat NS bolus and continue IV rehydration as necessary. Start ondansetron PRN emesis. Consider abdominal ultrasound to evaluation for intussusception. Consider stool culture and ova and parasite studies if ultrasound is normal and child begins stooling. Repeat abdominal exams.

2. Respiratory: Patient with possible right upper lung pneumonia on outside x-ray but has no cough or respiratory findings to support this diagnosis. Will have our radiologist review films and will monitor patient’s respiratory status.

3. CV: Mild tachycardia possibly due to fever, dehydration or pain. Blood pressures normal/elevated now but will monitor for signs of hemodynamic compromise.

4. ID: Possible viral infection vs pneumonia on x-ray. Patient received ceftriaxone at outside hospital. Will follow up on culture sent there. After obtaining read of chest x-ray will determine whether antibiotics should be continued.

5. Neuro: Patient is sleepy at times. Consider causes of altered mental status - ingestion, electrolyte abnormality, hypoglycemia, hypoxia, hypercarbia, intussusception, etc... Monitor neuro status closely.

6. Dispo: Patient requires inpatient care until she is able to tolerate PO and abdominal pain improves

She presented earlier today from an outside ED with 2 days of non-bloody, non-bilious emesis and poor oral intake. She was found to be febrile then, as well. CBC was unremarkable and electrolytes just demonstrated a decreased bicarbonate and elevated BUN consistent with dehydration. She has received two $2 0 \mathrm { m l / k g \ N S }$ boluses and maintenance fluids. She also received a dose of IV ceftriaxone prior to transfer due to concern for right upper lobe pneumonia. Clinically she does not appear to have pneumonia, and our radiologists agreed that the outside chest x-ray is not consistent with lobar pneumonia. Since admission she was noted to have abdominal pain and a bloody stool. US confirmed an ileocolic intussusception. Her abdomen is not rigid at this time. A contrast enema has been ordered and radiology will be ready to perform it in 30 minutes. She should be kept NPO. Surgery has been consulted to evaluate her prior to the procedure, but they have not seen the patient yet.
