**I . Adrenal Crisis Simulation**

**II. Target audience:** Emergency medicine or internal medicine residents, medical students

**III. Learning Objectives:**

A. Primary Objectives

1. Describe the approach to patients with unexplained hypotension.
2. Discuss differential diagnosis of hypotension**.**
3. Discuss management of adrenal crisis.

B. Secondary Objectives

1. Initiate emergent diagnostic and therapeutic management of a patient with altered mental status and hypotension.
2. Generate appropriate differential diagnosis of hypotension, perform appropriate investigative studies
3. Perform comprehensive physical exam. Obtain history by phone from wife. Identify adrenal crisis
4. Manage adrenal crisis with steroids and IV fluids.

C. Critical Actions:

1. Recognize that the patient is critically ill. Place the patient on a monitor, obtain IV access, obtain complete set of vital signs
2. Recognize hypotension—initiate appropriate resuscitation with IV fluids and pressors
3. Perform focused history and physical—recognize adrenal crisis
4. Initiate appropriate treatment with IV steroids
5. Admit patient to the ICU.

**IV. Environment:**

1. Lab set up: ED
2. Manikin set up: The scenario is created for Sim Man, but could be easily adapted to another mannequin. A large bore IV line should be in place. IV fluids, appropriate antibiotics for pneumonia, IV pressors and medication to treat adrenal crisis should be available.
3. Props: CXR demonstrating lobar pneumonia, gray wig for mannequin

**V. Actors:**

1. EMS—will stay if asked to give history
2. Wife—(by phone) will give history as indicated below. Will be appropriately concerned
3. Nurse—will give medications and assist the team as requested. Will prompt the team as indicated below.
4. ICU fellow—will take history from the team. Will ask for diagnosis if not readily given by the team.

**VII. Case narrative:**

1. Chief complaint: Mr. Brown is a 75 year old male who presents to the ED with altered mental status and hypotension. He is oriented x 1 and unable to give history, although he is awake and protecting his airway. He will be lethargic and hypotensive on arrival to the ED.
2. PMH: (given if asked for) Sarcoid, HTN
3. Medications: (given only if asked for): Metoprolol, Norvasc, prednisone (given only if team specifically asks about steroid use).
4. Family History: Father died of MI at 65. Social history: non smoker, no ETOH or drugs
5. History—Patient is altered and unable to give history. If team attempts to contact wife, she will report that he has a history of sarcoid. If asked about steroid use, she will report that he has been on chronic high dose prednisone for several years. She has recently broken her leg and has been unable to get his medication for the last week. He has become increasing lethargic over the last two days. Today, he was confused, which prompted her to call EMS.
6. Physical exam:

BP: 80/50 HR 123 RR 20 Pulse ox 92% on nasal cannula Temp 94.0 oral

General: lethargic, moaning

HEENT: normocephalic, atraumatic

CV: tachycardic, no murmurs or rubs

Pulm: CTA B/L

Abd: mild diffuse tenderness

Ext: no edema, intact distal pulses

Neuro: oriented x 1. Moves all extremities, CN intact

7. Scenario branch points:
8. The initial fingerstick glucose will be 35 mg/dL. If the team does not perform a fingerstick within 5 minutes, the patient will begin to seize. If the team does not give glucose within 10 minutes, the patient will progress to PEA arrest.
9. The patient will become progressively more hypotensive until adrenal crisis is recognized and steroids are given. The team should recognize hypotension and give IV decadron. When this is done, the blood pressure will begin to gradually trend up. The blood pressure will increase no matter what steroid is given.
10. IV fluids and pressors should be started, but will have no effect on the blood pressure.
11. The patient has sepsis caused by the pneumonia. The team should recognize the pneumonia and treat with broad spectrum antibiotics.
12. Labs should be given to the team once the team has recognized hypotension and started pressors.

**VII. Instructors Notes:**

1. If the team fails to recognize adrenal crisis and start steroids, the nurse can prompt them by asking why the patient is hypotensive. Additional prompting may be needed and can be given at the discretion of the instructor—for example, the wife can mention that this happened before when the patient stopped taking his medicine.

**VIII. Debriefing plan:**

1. Following this scenario a 15-20 minute debriefing should be conducted. The debriefing should focus on the medical decision making involved in the scenario. The clinical manifestations and causes of adrenal insufficiency should be discussed. In this case, both the abrupt cessation of chronic steroids and the sepsis contributed to this patient’s adrenal crisis. The role of stressors such as sepsis and trauma in precipitating adrenal insufficiency should be discussed. The attached debriefing materials can be used as a guide.
