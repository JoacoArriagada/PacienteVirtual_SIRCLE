**CASE 6 (from student handout)**

You are called by a nurse at 3:00 AM to evaluate a 49 year old woman admitted to another intern’s team. Her sign-out to you was that the patient had pneumonia. The woman complains that her heart is beating rapidly. The nurse tells you that the patient is alert and oriented and has only right pleuritic chest pain but no dyspnea. The patient’s heart rate is 135 beats per minute, her blood pressure is 150/90 and her lungs have minimal right base crackles. The nurse is obtaining a 12-lead ECG but requests that you come evaluate the patient immediately.

**Case 6 faculty notes:**

1. This is really a case of just sinus tachycardia as an appropriate response to this patient’s combination of fever (103), hypoxia, pain.
2. Allow students to ask for ways to evaluate the differential diagnosis (get temperature, oxygen saturation, etc.) and intervene (acetaminophen, analgesia, oxygen, etc).
3. Make the patient decompensate if they do not ask for appropriate clinical information and instead start giving ACLS drugs for various tachy-arrythmias.
