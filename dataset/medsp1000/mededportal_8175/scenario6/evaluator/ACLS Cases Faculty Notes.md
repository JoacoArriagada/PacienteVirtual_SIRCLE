**Case 6 faculty notes:**

1. This is a little bit of a curveball to get students to THINK. I stress that, with the exception of VF/VT, there is usually time to think before reacting even in “seemingly” acute cardiac presentations. This is really a case of just sinus tachycardia as an appropriate response to this patient’s combination of (your choice and let students stumble upon what to ask for) fever (103), hypoxia, pain. Allow students to ask for ways to evaluate the differential diagnosis (get temperature, oxygen saturation, etc.) and intervene (acetaminophen, analgesia, oxygen, etc).
2. Make the patient decompensate if they do not ask for appropriate clinical information and instead start giving ACLS drugs for various tachy-arrythmias.
3. Variation: patient was really *mis*diagnosed as pneumonia by the misguided intern who signed out to you. The patient is not febrile, does have dyspnea and really has a pulmonary embolism. The student should review the chart, x-ray, clinical picture and always think, and keep an evolving differential diagnosis in mind.
4. You should use other strips noted to review the tachycardia algorithm one more time
