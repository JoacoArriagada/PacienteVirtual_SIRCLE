**Case 3 faculty notes:**

1. Same initial issues of assessment and getting help. Do not forget quality CPR and directing others so that someone is doing it (teamwork).
2. Use strips #1 and/or #10 to go down the adult cardiac arrest algorithm unless they seem to have it down cold by now.
3. Strip #9: torsades de pointe: get them to recognize this and treat with Mg. You may add the history that the patient is alcoholic (prone to hypomagnesemia).
4. Strip # 2 and 3: Review the asystole branch of the adult cardiac arrest algorithm (agonal rhythm treated as asystole/PEA). Make sure to confirm asystole in another lead.
   1. Get students to apply a differential diagnosis and possible evaluation/treatment to this particular patient:
      1. Likely diagnoses: hypoxia (from post-op pneumonia or pneuomothorax); pulmonary embolism; hypokalemia or hyperkalemia ( from IVF’s); pre-existing acidosis (sepsis, obesity hypoventilation)
      2. Less likely: hypothermia (unless she was immediately out of the operating room), drug overdose
5. Variation: have them look at strip # 19 but make sure they still look for breathing/pulse. Describe that there is no pulse and review Pulseless Electrical Activity and the differential diagnosis pertinent to this presentation to include: hypovolemia; hypoxia; hypo/hyperkalemia; acidosis; tension pneumothorax (in OR yesterday); pulmonary embolism.
