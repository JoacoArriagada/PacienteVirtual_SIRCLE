**Appendix B: Weight Loss Sim Case 1**

**SIMULATION CASE TITLE:** Geriatric Weight Loss Case #1

**PATIENT NAME:** Gregg Smith  
**PATIENT AGE:** 71-year-old  
**CHIEF COMPLAINT:** weight loss

**Logistics**: This activity occurs in a simulation, standardized patient laboratory. The setting for the encounter is a primary care clinic, outpatient. The simulation lab should have otoscope and/or penlight used to evaluate the oral cavity, stethoscope, wheelchair (for patient to sit in). Two standardized patients required- one to play the patient and one to play the wife. The learner(s) should review the patient’s responses to the weight loss tool before entering the room. Need 1 instructor to lead the session and one simulation staff to ensure simulation runs properly and supports issues that may develop.

**Brief narrative description of case**  
Mr. Smith is a 71-year-old male, who resides with his wife, who is present during the interview. He has a past medical history of Alzheimer’s dementia (diagnosed 2 years ago), obstructive sleep apnea, urinary incontinence, and hyperlipidemia. He has lost about 20 pounds in the past five months. In previous goals of care discussions, his wife has agreed to no further cancer work up and minimal invasive interventions. She would like to discuss his weight loss today.

**Learner Preparation**

- Prior to entering simulation room, the learner(s) will be provided with the patient’s responses to the weight loss tool. They will have 5 minutes to review the results before entering the room.

**Initial vital signs**  
Heart Rate 75, Respiratory Rate 15, Blood Pressure 135/78, Pulse Oximetry 98% on room air, weight 141 pounds, height 74 inches

**Overall Appearance**  
An elderly gentleman, slightly disheveled dress, food stains present on shirt, unshaven, ungroomed hair, appears older than stated age. He is slouched in his wheelchair with his eyes closed. He is accompanied by an attentive, elderly, well-groomed female who appears younger than her stated age.

**Actors and roles in the room at case start**  
Elderly gentleman- plays the main character, Gregg Smith, must be older than 65 years old, thin (roughly 140-150 pounds).  
Elderly female- plays the main character’s wife, Jean Smith, must be older than 65 years old.

**HPI**  
Of note, most of the history is offered by the wife because the patient is somnolent and has decreased responsiveness. He is easily arousable but falls back asleep often. Mr. Smith has been steadily losing weight for the last five months and has lost ~20 lbs. His wife began to notice when she had to start using a different belt loop hole while dressing him. She states he was diagnosed with dementia two years ago. He has had a steady decline in his ability to perform ADLs since that time. She is currently assisting him with making his meals, bathing him, dressing him. She places the meals in front of the patient, but has not been feeding him because she did not want to treat him “like a baby”. He does not ambulate; he gets around with a wheelchair.

**Past Medical/Surgical History**

- Alzheimer’s dementia with depressive symptoms- most recent SLUMS exam done 3 months ago
- Hyperlipidemia
- Obstructive sleep apnea
- Urinary incontinence

**Medications**

- Zoloft (sertraline) 50 mg by mouth (PO) daily
- Namenda (memantine) 5 mg PO daily
- Aricept (donepezil) 5 mg PO daily
- Simvastatin 40 mg PO daily

**Allergies**

- Metformin- anorexia and nausea

**Family History**

- The wife believes his mother had colon cancer, but she is unsure of the rest of the family’s medical problems. They have no children.

**Physical Examination**

- General: An elderly gentleman, slightly disheveled dress, food stains present on shirt, unshaven, ungroomed hair, appears older than stated age, thin.
- HEENT: Pupils equal and reactive to light, arcus senilus bilaterally, he has both upper and lower dentures. Oral exam- see appendix C. No asymmetries, changes in color/pigmentation, swelling, palpable lesions, or irregularities in mucosa and gingiva noted, bilateral temporal wasting noted.
- Neck: No cervical lymphadenopathy noted, no thyromegaly, no tenderness or masses noted.
- Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally anteriorly and posteriorly, no WRRR noted. Able to see multiple ribs on exam due to muscle wasting.
- Cardiovascular: S1/2 heard, no murmurs, gallops, or rubs appreciated, PMI not displaced.
- Abdomen: Soft, scaphoid abdomen, nontender, nondistended, normal bowel sounds.
- Neurological: Patient sleeps through most of the exam, easily arousable but falls back asleep often. When awake he is able to follow questions somewhat. He is oriented only to self and wife. He does not know day, date, month, or location.
- Skin: Tenting of the skin on the back of hands noted, age spots bilateral hands noted, no erythema/ecchymosis noted.
- GU: Deferred.
- Psychiatric: If SLUMS or MOCA is attempted or discussed, learner will be handed a form with the results of previous SLUMS performed 3 months prior with score 10/30. Wife will be very resistant to repeating the exam, stating she will want to focus on his weight loss and will not want to tire out patient.

**Instructor Notes - Changes and CASE Branch Points**

- Prior to starting the simulation case, the learner(s) will have 5 minutes to review the CGWEP tool and discuss strategy for interview.
- If learner(s) attempt oral examination, patient and/or caregiver hand the learner(s) a picture of the lesion located under the patient’s dentures.
- If learner(s) explore oral findings, the case includes dentures and the oral picture addendum should be used for the lesion under the dentures.
- If learner(s) explore altered mental status or prior cognitive testing, the wife states he has not had any abnormal behavior, inattention, or altered level of consciousness. He does have disorganized thinking, but this is not new. Learner will be handed a SLUMS exam from 3 months ago. If learner attempts to repeat exam, wife becomes frustrated and states, “that is NOT the reason we came in today! Can we focus on his weight loss? Everyone always wants to check his memory.”
- If learner(s) explore medication-related contributors, the source text lists these effects: Zoloft (sertraline)- anorexia, nausea; Namenda (memantine)- nausea/vomiting; Aricept (donepezil)- nausea/vomiting, anorexia; Simvastatin- nausea.
- If learner(s) explore ADLs or IADLs, wife assists with all of it. Wife assures learner(s) that she helps him with all ADLs and he has access to food, but it does become apparent that she needs to assist him more with meals. She has not been assisting him because she did not want to embarrass him or “treat him like a baby”.
- At 20 minutes, instructor notifies learner(s) of 10 minutes remaining.
- At 25 minutes, learners are notified of 5 minutes left.
- At 30 minutes, end of case.
