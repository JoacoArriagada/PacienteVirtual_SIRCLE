**CASE 2**

Mr. Gates is a 57-y/o African American man who presents for a follow-up visit. He is well known to you.

His medical history includes emphysematous COPD (GOLD 3/severe), PTSD and depression, ongoing alcohol use disorder, and active tobacco use.

His COPD remains rather symptomatic. He has not been hospitalized for an exacerbation in several years, but he has dyspnea on exertion that limits his walking to 1-2 blocks at a time.

Despite his heavy smoking history, he has no history of coronary disease or other cardiovascular disease. He is not a diabetic. He has a BMI of 23. His recent lipid panel shows a total cholesterol of 112 and an HDL-C of 45. His blood pressure today is 106/62. He is not on a daily aspirin.

He has a live-in girlfriend; his family is mostly estranged. He has a 60 pack-year smoking history and continues to smoke 1 ppd now.

***Additional patient quotations:***

* “Obviously with the breathing problem, I just spend most days sitting around the house watching TV. I used to golf a few years ago, but that’s out now.”
* “I’ve tried everything to quit smoking and drinking. I simply can’t and don’t think I ever will. I guess I’m too depressed.”
* “I know I could die from lung cancer someday. I honestly wouldn’t be surprised if I did. But if there’s a test to pick the cancer up early, maybe that would be a good thing… I don’t know.”
