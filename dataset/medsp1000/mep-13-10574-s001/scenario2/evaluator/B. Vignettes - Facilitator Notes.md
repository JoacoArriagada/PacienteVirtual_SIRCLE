**CASE 2**

***KEY QUESTION:*** Should you screen Mr. Gates for lung cancer?

This case is also based on a real patient, and I have recommended against screening this patient. The untold back story is that this patient had a history of chronic pancreatitis and a lot of incidental pancreatic lesions over the years. He had been subjected to false alarms of pancreatic cancer from previous abdominal imaging, and he did not handle this anxiety very well at all. He turned to drinking even more heavily whenever he was faced with the prospect of bad news.

In my experience in leading this TBL, student generally recommend for screening him. Obviously, they do not have the entire back story when making this recommendation.

***KEY ISSUES TO ADDRESS:***

List any additional pieces of data about Mr. Gates that would help you make a decision about appropriateness of screening him.

In addition to many of the pieces of data cited in the first case, students focus on this patient’s mental health and ongoing tobacco use as potential barriers to screening.

List and quantify 1-2 specific benefits of screening him for lung cancer.

These are included in the Powerpoint. Students generally cite the cancer-specific mortality benefit as the most compelling reason.

List 3 specific harms as possible if he decides to proceed with lung cancer screening. For each individual harm, discuss why you believe he is below average risk, average risk, or above average risk.

See the Powerpoint. I tend to highlight that the diagnostic cascade in these patients can be particularly harmful, particularly given the rate of pneumothorax with CT-guided lung nodule biopsy. I note that – while uncommon – patients can potentially die from the work-up for a lung nodule as demonstrated in the NSLT (*N Engl J Med 2011; 365:395-409*) data that underpins the USPSTF recommendations.

List 1-2 misconceptions about lung cancer screening that may be useful to discuss with Mr. Gates up front.

These are nicely highlighted in Box 2 of the 2015 *JAMA Internal Medicine* article that is required reading for this session.
