**CASE 3**

Ms. Cabrera is a 42-y/o Puerto Rican woman presents for her annual wellness visit. Her medical history includes morbid obesity (BMI > 40) and a family history of diabetes.

She has no significant family history, and she has no first degree relatives who have had breast cancer.

She is currently married. She is a G1P1 with menarche at age 12. Her first and only child was born when she was 40 years old. She breastfed her child for 3 months while on leave, but she then returned to work as busy lawyer for juvenile court.

She is a lifelong non-smoker and drinks virtually no alcohol.

***Additional patient quotations:***

* “My husband and I are trying really hard to have another child before I am too old to have any more. My periods are already becoming unpredictable, but we’re really hopeful we can conceive.”
* “I don’t have any family members with breast cancer, so on the one hand I’m not too worried.”
* “But you know, I do have a friend my age that had a mammogram and found a suspicious tumor. She’s going to have surgery next week since they say it’s early cancer. So who knows, the mammogram might have saved her life.”

***KEY QUESTION:*** Should you screen Ms. Cabrera for breast cancer?

***KEY ISSUES TO ADDRESS:***

Outline the major harms and benefits of screening her for breast cancer.

Provide a recommendation for or against screening this patient.
