# PART II: Evidence Based Medicine

# Sexually Transmitted Infections (STI)/HIV Prevention Counseling

Results from randomized controlled trials demonstrate that compared with traditional approaches to providing information, certain brief risk reduction counseling approaches can reduce the occurrence of new sexually transmitted infections by $2 5 \% { - } 4 0 \%$ among Sexually Transmitted Disease (STD) clinic patients. Key feature of this brief (40 minutes), interactive counseling process is that it is aimed at personal risk reduction rather using a general knowledge/lecture based approach.

Reference: Kamb ML, Fishbein M, Douglas JM, et al. HIV prevention counseling reduces high risk behaviors and sexually transmitted diseases: results from a multicenter, randomized controlled trial (Project RESPECT). JAMA 1998;280:1161--7.

# Harm Reduction11

o According to the World Health Organization based on 200 studies commissioned and 52 cities surveyed, the use of needle exchange programs (NEP) contributed to a $5 . 8 \%$ decreased in the HIV rate compared to the increase of $5 . 9 \%$ HIV infection in cities not able to offer a NEP. According to these studies, the WHO suggests there is no evidence of major unintended consequences of providing a NEP, such as initiation of injection among people who did not previously inject drugs or an increase in the frequency of illicit drug use.12   
o Needle exchange programs have been shown to be a useful method of reducing harm in IV drug users. Through a randomized control trial it has been shown that the use of harm reduction counseling has reduced unsafe sexual practices.13   
$\bigcirc$ A cohort study found that users of a needle exchange program were 5 times more likely to enter into a methadone treatment program.14   
o Needle exchange programs (NEP) and harm reduction counseling have shown to decrease risky behaviors such as needle sharing, reuse of injection equipment, and risky sexual behaviors.15
