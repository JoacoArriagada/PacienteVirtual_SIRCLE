# CASE #2A: CARDIAC DISEASE CASE MATERIALS FOR DISCUSSION

# PART I: Patient Presentation

Mr. Rudy Buck is a 55 year old Viet Nam era veteran who is frequently assessed for chest pain in a local emergency room that sees many indigent patients in the Milwaukee area. Mr Buck has no sustained income beyond the occasional odd job. Mr. Buck states all his family members have passed away and though he wasn’t born or raised in Milwaukee he considers it to be his home. He is pleasant when he presents and cooperative with the care given to him.

Mr. Buck has been hospitalized 3 times in the last year alone and has had 2 confirmed MI's. He has severe uncontrolled HTN. His lipids are normal. When he leaves the hospital he always states convincingly that he will do his best to continue with the recommended treatment though he has never made a follow up appointment or refilled a prescription. He smokes 3ppd and has expressed that he wouldn't mind quitting. He values his independence and refuses to go to a shelter or low income housing when advised to saying "I've always been able to take care of myself". Mr. Buck is currently living in a 1991 van. He has had no mailing address or phone for many years so contacting him is difficult. He has never directly harmed himself or hurt others

# PART II: Evidence Based Medicine

Cardiac Disease: ED Use and Prevention7

1. Most people in the United States have access to emergency care; however, access to followup care and preventive medicine is a problem which plagues the U.S health care system.

A. Over 114 million visits to emergency rooms will have been made in 2005. More than $80 \%$ of these people will be treated and discharged with a recommendation for follow-up with a primary care physician.   
B. However, many of these patients lack a primary physician and will only return to the emergency room with future complaints. There are a multitude of reasons for lack of proper follow-up care.

2. A study reported in JAMA 2005, reported that in 499 randomly selected ambulatory clinics throughout the U.S., between $4 7 . 2 \%$ (uninsured) and $6 4 . 4 \%$ (insured) of patients were unable to obtain urgent follow-up within one week of the emergency room visit.8

Cardiac Risk Factors: A sedentary lifestyle is a risk factor for heart disease. The majority of adults in the US are not as active as is thought necessary to influence cardiac health.

1. A Cochrane review suggests that physician advice/prescription to our patients to increase their physical activity has a moderate effect on self reported physical activity and cardiorespiratory fitness, but not on achieving a level of physical activity we would consider desirable. Exercise can reduce the risk of dying from cardiac disease.

2. A Cochrane review found strong evidence that quitting smoking after a heart attack or cardiac surgery can lead to a risk reduction of death of $36 \%$ . The risk reduction from quitting smoking in someone with serious heart disease may be as great as/or greater than other possible interventions. The effect is noted to be substantial.

Health Care Outcomes Associated with Homelessness

1. Homelessness is associated with poor nutrition, poor hygiene, exposure to elements, communicable diseases, stress of unstable housing, physical abuse, alcohol and other drug use, and fatigue. These factors are then associated with adverse health outcomes.

Reference: National Health Care for the Homeless Council. (Available on-line).

# PART V: Actual Patient Disposition / Outcome

1. Mr. Buck is willing and motivated to attend smoking cessation clinic and stopped smoking after 3 tries. He formed a bond with the Psychologist who ran the clinic.

2. When his van broke beyond repair he turned to this Psychologist who was able to help arrange an admission to a VA residential program.

a. In the program he took his medications and with encouragement began to go on daily walks with a staff member and 2 other patients.   
b. With gentle reminders he made his outpatient appointments and his hypertension came under control with no further heart attacks.   
c. He eventually made a friend in the residential program and started to attend the occasional social outing when his friend went.   
d. He works in the VA medical center 5 days a week doing an incentive therapy job helping a ward clerk and is known for his never missing a day of work.   
e. He shares a room with 1 other patient and when asked states he’s happy living there. He desires nothing further.
