# CASE #2A: CARDIAC DISEASE CASE MATERIALS FOR DISCUSSION

# PART I: Patient Presentation

Mr. Rudy Buck is a 55 year old Viet Nam era veteran who is frequently assessed for chest pain in a local emergency room that sees many indigent patients in the Milwaukee area. Mr. Buck has no sustained income beyond the occasional odd job. Mr. Buck states all his family members have passed away and though he wasn’t born or raised in Milwaukee he considers it to be his home. He is pleasant when he presents and cooperative with the care given to him.

Mr. Buck has been hospitalized 3 times in the last year alone and has had 2 confirmed MI's. He has severe uncontrolled HTN. His lipids are normal. When he leaves the hospital he always states convincingly that he will do his best to continue with the recommended treatment though he has never made a follow up appointment or refilled a prescription. He smokes 3ppd and has expressed that he wouldn't mind quitting. He values his independence and refuses to go to a shelter or low income housing when advised to saying "I've always been able to take care of myself". Mr. Buck is currently living in a 1991 van. He has had no mailing address or phone for many years so contacting him is difficult. He has never directly harmed himself or hurt others

# PART III: PATIENT PERSPECTIVES: VALUES-BELIEFS Patient’s Answers to Kleinman’s Questions

57. What do you think has caused your problem?

“I think I have some heart problems but they’re not too bad.”

58. Why do you think it started when it did? “I’m older now and it probably has something to do with that.”

59. What do you think your sickness does to you? How does it work?

E. “It’s hard to catch my breath sometimes but it doesn’t hurt me. Coming to the hospital seems to fix it.”   
F. “My heart doesn’t get enough oxygen sometimes and that causes it to not work well.”

60. How severe is your sickness? Will it have a short or long course?

E. “It doesn’t seem to slow me down so I don’t think it’s as bad as people say.” F. “My father had heart problems like mine and he lived to be 69 years old so I think I’ll make it to that too”.

61. What kind of treatment do you think you should receive?

“I’m happy with the hospital treatment except sometimes people here seem angry at me. Coming to appointments is hard for me with my work.”

62. What are the most important results you hope to receive from this treatment? “I would like to come to the hospital less often. I would like to stop smoking – it’s very expensive.”

63. What are the chief problems your sickness has caused for you? “Not much.”

64. What do you fear most about your sickness?

“One Doctor told me I might end up in a nursing home if I had a stroke – I wouldn’t like that.”

# PART IV: SOCIAL CONTEXT – REVIEW OF SYSTEMS Patient and Physician Perspectives

Patient’s Perspective: Support

36. How is your spouse (or children, parents, partner, etc.) affected by this illness?

A. I divorced when I was 23 and haven’t had a girlfriend since.   
B. My parents have been dead for 10 years now.   
C. I keep to myself so my problems are my own.

37. What are you hearing from others about your illness?

A. The Doctors and Nurses tell me I should do better with taking care of myself. B. One person told me I was wasting money that might be used for someone who wanted to get better – that hurt my feelings a bit.

38. Tell me what has changed in your relationships since the illness began.

A. People here used to like me better, I think.   
B. It’s harder to get jobs because I can’t go at it for as long as I could previously without getting tired.

39. How would things be different at home if you were well?

A. I feel well now.
