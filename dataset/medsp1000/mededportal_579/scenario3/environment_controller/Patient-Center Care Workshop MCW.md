# PART II: Evidence Based Medicine

Cardiac Disease: ED Use and Prevention9

1. Most people in the United States have access to emergency care; however, access to followup care and preventive medicine is a problem which plagues the U.S health care system.

A. Over 114 million visits to emergency rooms will have been made in 2005. More than $80 \%$ of these people will be treated and discharged with a recommendation for follow-up with a primary care physician.   
B. However, many of these patients lack a primary physician and will only return to the emergency room with future complaints. There are a multitude of reasons for lack of proper follow-up care.

2. A study reported in JAMA 2005, reported that in 499 randomly selected ambulatory clinics throughout the U.S., between $4 7 . 2 \%$ (uninsured) and $6 4 . 4 \%$ (insured) of patients were unable to obtain urgent follow-up within one week of the emergency room visit.10

Cardiac Risk Factors: A sedentary lifestyle is a risk factor for heart disease. The majority of adults in the US are not as active as is thought necessary to influence cardiac health.

3. A Cochrane review suggests that physician advice/prescription to our patients to increase their physical activity has a moderate effect on self reported physical activity and cardiorespiratory fitness, but not on achieving a level of physical activity we would consider desirable. Exercise can reduce the risk of dying from cardiac disease.

4. A Cochrane review found strong evidence that quitting smoking after a heart attack or cardiac surgery can lead to a risk reduction of death of $36 \%$ . The risk reduction from quitting smoking in someone with serious heart disease may be as great as/or greater than other possible interventions. The effect is noted to be substantial.
