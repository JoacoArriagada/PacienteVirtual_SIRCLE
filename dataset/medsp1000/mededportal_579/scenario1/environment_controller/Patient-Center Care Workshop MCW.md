# CASE #1: 28 YEAR OLD PREGNANT WITH HIV CASE MATERIALS FOR DISCUSSION

# PART I: Patient Presentation

You are seeing Ms. Sadie Smith a 28 year old woman living in the city of Milwaukee in your clinic. This is Ms. Smith’s first prenatal visit at thirteen weeks. This is Ms Smith’s fourth pregnancy. You began caring for Ms. Smith after her third pregnancy which is when Ms. Smith advised you that she was HIV positive. Her third child has not tested positive for HIV. Her previous three children have all been by different fathers. As her physician, you had discussed the importance of condom use if/when she chose to be sexually active. You were not anticipating that Ms. Smith would be pregnant.

You have just recommended to Ms. Smith that she begin taking AZT to reduce the chance of HIV transmission to her baby. Ms. Smith responded by saying “God will take care of me and my baby, so I do not want any HIV medicine for me or my baby”. Ms. Smith went on to emphasize that she would not take AZT during her pregnancy, the delivery, and/or approve AZT for her baby post delivery.

# PART II: Evidence Based Medicine

Per the “best-evidence” in the literature, you know that Ms. Smith’s best chance for not transmitting HIV to her baby (vertical transmission) would be for her to receive AZT during her pregnancy, at the time of her delivery and for the baby to receive AZT in the new born period.

# PART V: Actual Patient Disposition / Outcome

1. The patient was willing to talk to the HIV resource center, an organization that she was already familiar with.   
2. Counseling sessions were held with the patient and leaders with her church. Church leaders supported Ms. Smith’s decision to not take AZT.   
3. Physician discussions emphasized evidence-based findings that Ms Smith could decrease the probability of HIV transmission to her child.   
4. Mother refused this. Mother not coerced to receive AZT herself.   
5. Ultimately, the child was delivered vaginally with attention to protocol for avoiding HIV transmission (e.g., not breaking the bag of water, cleansing the baby vigorously at time of birth).   
6. A court order was obtained for the baby to receive AZT orally for twelve weeks. The mother did comply with the court order re: giving the baby AZT orally. The mother stated “the court has ordered AZT and even though it’s against my faith, I abide by the law as my babies need me”.   
7. Baby is HIV negative and mother and child are still this physician’s patient.
