# CASE #1: 28 YEAR OLD PREGNANT WITH HIV CASE MATERIALS FOR DISCUSSION

# PART I: Patient Presentation

You are seeing Ms. Sadie Smith a 28 year old woman living in the city of Milwaukee in your clinic. This is Ms. Smith’s first prenatal visit at thirteen weeks. This is Ms Smith’s fourth pregnancy. You began caring for Ms. Smith after her third pregnancy which is when Ms. Smith advised you that she was HIV positive. Her third child has not tested positive for HIV. Her previous three children have all been by different fathers. As her physician, you had discussed the importance of condom use if/when she chose to be sexually active. You were not anticipating that Ms. Smith would be pregnant.

You have just recommended to Ms. Smith that she begin taking AZT to reduce the chance of HIV transmission to her baby. Ms. Smith responded by saying “God will take care of me and my baby, so I do not want any HIV medicine for me or my baby”. Ms. Smith went on to emphasize that she would not take AZT during her pregnancy, the delivery, and/or approve AZT for her baby post delivery.

# PART III: PATIENT PERSPECTIVES: VALUES-BELIEFS Patient’s Answers to Kleinman’s Questions

49. What do you think has caused your problem?

“I know HIV is a virus – so this bug has caused it. I feel fine.”

50. Why do you think it started when it did?

“I don’t know why it started.. but it might be God’s way of testing my faith. I was not as church going when I got pregnant with my third child and this was his way of testing me. He’s testing my faith.

51. What do you think your sickness does to you? How does it work? It hasn’t done much of anything to me. I feel fine. I read a little about it and my first doctor told me about it. He said it “affects my immune system, so I’m more likely to get more infections”. But I’ve always gotten a lot of female infections.

52. How severe is your sickness? Will it have a short or long course?

“I feel fine, God will take care of me.” I know that lots of people are HIV positive and they are still alive. People used to die from HIV – but I don’t think that happens as much anymore. They take drugs but I know that my faith is stronger than a lot of drugs.

53. What kind of treatment do you think you should receive? “I don’t need any treatment at this time. I’m not sick, I feel fine”.

54. What are the most important results you hope to receive from this treatment? Not applicable.

55. What are the chief problems your sickness has caused for you? “None really.”

56. What do you fear most about your sickness?

“I fear that I won’t be able to live up to God’s faith in me, that I won’t be strong enough in my faith in God”.
