# CASE # 4: 75 YEAR OLD BREAST CA CASE MATERIALS FOR DISCUSSION

# PART I: Patient Presentation

Mrs. Marie Szabo is a 75 year old woman whose primary doctor found a lump in her breast. Mammography and biopsy showed the lump was infiltrating ductal carcinoma and she was scheduled for mastectomy.

Mrs. Szabo is in the hospital awaiting her surgery. You are a third year medical student who is working with a third year surgical resident. Your resident has just been assigned to explain the plan for treatment to Mrs. Szabo and obtain Mrs. Szabo’s informed consent. When you and the resident enter Mrs. Szabo’s room, you find her daughter, Mrs. Toth, sitting with her. Though Mrs. Szabo has been living in the US since 1989, she does not speak English well, and Mrs. Toth helps to translate. Mrs. Szabo, a widow, lives with Mrs. Toth and her family. Mrs. Toth seems very attentive and protective of her mother. Mrs. Toth asks to speak to the resident (and you) in the hall.

“We are not telling Mama that she has cancer,” she tells you both. “That would frighten her too much. In Hungary, a cancer diagnosis was a death sentence. Dr. Nagy (Mrs. Szabo’s primary internist) told her it was a ‘growth’ that had to be removed.”

# PART III: PATIENT PERSPECTIVES: VALUES-BELIEFS Patient’s Answers to Kleinman’s Questions

# As Translated by Mrs. Szabo’s Daughter – Mrs. Toth

81. What do you think has caused your problem?

“Industrial pollution in the Danube River and radioactive fallout from the Chernobyl disaster.”

82. Why do you think it started when it did? “It’s probably been growing slowly for a long time.”

83. What do you think your sickness does to you? How does it work? “I am afraid it will spread to other parts of my body and kill me.”

84. How severe is your sickness? Will it have a short or long course? “How can I answer that? No one will tell me whether or not this is cancer.”

85. What kind of treatment do you think you should receive? “I want everything done.”

86. What are the most important results you hope to receive from this treatment? “I want to be able to do my housework, to get out, to be with my grandchildren. If I can’t have that, I want to die quickly with no pain.”

87. What are the chief problems your sickness has caused for you? “It has frightened me. It has put me here waiting for surgery. And it has caused my daughter to treat me like a child.”

88. What do you fear most about your sickness? “I am afraid of being in pain and afraid of being a burden to my children.”
