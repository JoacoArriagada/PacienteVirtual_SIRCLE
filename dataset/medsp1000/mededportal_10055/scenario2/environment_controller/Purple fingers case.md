**Purple fingers**

**You are: The physician for this patient**

- 8 month old girl presents to the ED with 3 days vomiting, diarrhea, not drinking a t2 AM
- HR 192, RR 42, 80/52, SaO2 99%, weak cry, very dehydrated
- I.V. placed right antecubital fossa after many attempts. Lab studies sent, fluid resuscitation
- Looking better, not taking po, bed Peds ward bed requested.
- No floor beds, boarding in Peds ED
- You see the infant at the start of you shift at 0800, the infant’s right hand cool, pale, finger tips dusky, right radial pulse absent
- I.V. removed with brief, bright red, pulsatile bleeding from puncture site. In a few minutes, her hand was warmer, but 3 fingertips remained dusky.
- Family arrives back to room while team is restarting the I.V.

**The rest of the story**

You are virtually certain that the catheter was placed in the artery and are concerned about circulation to the right hand. However, the patient has not vomited in several hours, stooling has slowed, and she is looking much better than she apparently was on presentation several hours ago. HR is now 142, RR 36, skin turgor looks good, and the infant has passed about 100 cc of urine while crying with tears. Your early and informal assessment is that the last ED shift did a great of fluid resuscitating an infant who had been seriously ill.

**Purple fingers**

**You are: The patient's mother (Mrs. Perez)**

As you are return to the ED after getting breakfast, you hear her crying. You then see two women and a man (all in scrubs) bending over your daughter. One of the women turns to you and explains what they are doing by saying, "we're just trying to start an IV so she can keep giving her fluids." This confuses you because you know that an IV had already been put in her arm. You recall the ED doctor who was taking care of your daughter when she arrived at 2 AM said, "We either need to get this IV started or were going to have to put a large needle into her bone so she can get the fluids she needs to save her life." You therefore now ask why an IV is necessary, since your daughter already had one.

**The rest of the story**

Your 8 month-old infant daughter (Maria Elena) is the youngest of your for children and your only daughter. You are especially close to her because of this and you've always responded quickly so that she cries as little as possible. None of your children have ever been hospitalized or required emergency care in the past. In the last week all 4 of your children have been ill with vomiting and diarrhea and you are pleased at how well you were able to take care of your 3 older sons. However, it frightened you to see how quickly Maria Elena became very seriously ill. You are both exhausted and terribly worried that you have done something wrong. You wonder if the fact that this team is again starting an IV means that Maria Elena is doing poorly and begin thinking about the "big needle in her bone" that may be needed. You are also worried about the time you're going to need to take off work and know that you'll need a letter from Maria Elena's doctor explaining that she is in the hospital. Because you need to call your employer within the next 45 minutes to explain your absence, you're determined to get such a letter from these doctors.
