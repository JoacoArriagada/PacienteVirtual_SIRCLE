**Medication Allergy Case: PHYSICIAN**

Background: Patient Nancy Roberts, 92 y.o. female patient with moderate dementia and moderate, controlled hypertension, arrived at the UC Davis ED from a local assisted living residence at 2000. Before admission, she became increasing SOB, with yellow-greenish sputum, and febrile to 38.5 with pulse oximetry = 88. The assisted living residence staff were concerned Mrs. Roberts had developed pneumonia and, after receiving permission from the patient's son/daughter, called an ambulance for transport to the ED.

Admission Status: You were involved in the aftermath of a difficult and unsuccessful resuscitation attempt when Mrs. Roberts arrived, so an intern working in the department did the initial physical exam and got a chest x-ray, and labs. At 2100, you picked up the case at and reviewed the results, reexamining Mrs. Roberts to confirm the diagnosis of pneumonia. You ordered Cefepime and Vancomycin IV. First dose was given at 2200. Because of continued oxygen requirement, dehydration, and poor po intake, you contact the Hospitalist Service to admit the patient. You continue to see other ED patients.

At 2230, you are called to urgently evaluate Mrs. Roberts. She is experiencing an anaphylactic reaction involving flushing, itching, difficulty swallowing, coughing, wheezing, hypotension and difficulty breathing. You directed the management of Mrs. Roberts including intubation and treatment with epinephrine, steroids and fluids. She is stabilized and transferred to the MICU.

Current: An ED nurse informs you that Mrs. Roberts had a cephalosporin allergy documented in the medical records that accompanied her from the assisted living residence. The cephalosporin allergy was noted on a face page but you never saw these records. Based on the assisted living residence records, Mrs. Roberts developed hives, pruritis and shortness of breath with ceftriaxone she received for a UTI 1 year ago at UC Davis ED. The allergy was not noted in allergy banner in EMR, but was noted in the ED progress note from that visit.

Situation: Mrs. Robert’s son/daughter has arrived at the ED. You are going to talk with the family about the events of the evening and why his/her mother is going to the ICU.

**Medication Allergy Case: NURSE**

Background: Nancy Roberts, 92 y.o. female patient, arrived at the UC Davis ED from a local assisted living residence at 2000. Before admission, she became increasing SOB, with yellow-greenish sputum, and febrile to 38.5 with pulse oximetry = 88. The assisted living residence staff were concerned Mrs. Roberts had developed pneumonia and, after receiving permission from the patient's family member, called an ambulance for transport to the ED.

Admission Status: You helped admit Mrs. Roberts in the ED. It was difficult to examine she because she moaned when uncovered. You helped get a chest x-ray, labs and do an initial assessment. Everything confirmed pneumonia. The physician ordered cefepime IV. You gave the first dose at 2200 and within 20 minutes she experienced an anaphylactic reaction involving flushing, itching, difficulty swallowing, coughing, wheezing, hypotension and dyspnea. With her already compromised pulmonary status, Mrs. Roberts needed to be intubated. She responded well to intubation and epinephrine, steroids, and fluids for the anaphylaxis.

Mrs. Roberts was to remain on the ventilator for a few hours to protect her airway until the allergic reaction resolved. She was transferred to the MICU about 0200.

Current: While preparing for the transfer of Mrs. Roberts to the MICU, you find the medical records that had accompanied Mrs. Roberts from the assisted living residence in the exam room. You had glanced through the records when Mrs. Roberts arrived. An allergy to cephalosporins was noted on a face page but this page was out of order in the record. You now learn that Mrs. Roberts developed hives, puritis, and shortness of breath with ceftriaxone she received for a UTI 1 year ago at UC Davis ED. The allergy was not noted in EMR

Situation: Mrs. Robert’s son/daughter has arrived at the ED and would like to talk with you and the resident who took care of their mother about the events of the evening.

**Medication Allergy Case: PHARMACIST**

Background: Nancy Roberts, 92 y.o. female patient, arrived at the UC Davis ED from a local assisted living residence at 2000. Before admission, she became increasing SOB, with yellow-greenish sputum, and febrile to 38.5 with pulse oximetry = 88. The assisted living residence staff were concerned Mrs. Roberts had developed pneumonia and, after receiving permission from the patient's son/daughter, called an ambulance for transport to the ED.

Admission Status: Mrs. Roberts received chest x-ray, labs and physical exam in ED confirming diagnosis of healthcare-associated pneumonia. As the ED pharmacist, you confirmed the order for cefepime IV at 2130 and this dose was administered at 2200. Within 20 minutes Mrs. Roberts experienced an anaphylactic reaction involving flushing, itching, difficulty swallowing, coughing, wheezing, hypotension and dyspnea. Mrs. Roberts responded well to intubation and epinephrine, steroids, and fluids for the anaphylaxis. Mrs. Roberts was transferred to the MICU about 0200 while mechanically ventilated.

Current: You run into the ED nurse who was caring for Mrs. Roberts at about 2300. He lets you know about the allergic reaction. On your review of the medical records from the assisted living residence, you note the allergy to ceftriaxone. You also read that the patient has been at this assisted living residence for 6 months due to increased confusion and need for assistance with activities of daily living. She has had a progressive decline. Her adult child who lives in Grass Valley is her closest family member and durable power of attorney for health care decisions. From the chart, this adult child appears to be very involved in his/her mother's care and visits frequently.

On your re-review of Mrs. Roberts EMR record, you find notation in a progress note about a cefalosporin allergy from one year prior when the patient received ceftriaxone in the UC Davis ED.

Situation: Mrs. Robert’s adult son/daughter has arrived at the ED. The ED physician and nurse who treated Mrs. Roberts are also reviewing the EMR after having learned of the cephalosporin allergy. They tell you the patient’s son/daughter has asked to talk with the team about the events of the evening and why his/her mother is going to the ICU.
