# SESSION 3: FAMILY MEETING SIMULATION SESSION

# GOAL

To provide residents with an opportunity to practice their communication skills in depth and receive formative feedback from faculty and peers.

# LEARNING OBJECTIVES

At the end of the session the learner will be able to demonstrate several skills learned during Sessions I and II; e.g., eliciting family understanding of patient’s goals and values, using Ask-Tell-Ask when sharing information and responding to emotion.

# SESSION DESCRIPTION

This last session is held in our simulation center where residents are asked to: (1) simulate a patient admission to the ICU and (2) meet with family to provide updates and discuss goals of care.

# Simulation Overview & Instructions

Provide residents with an overview of the simulation session.

Take a few minutes to talk about the role-play process. Encourage residents to practice a few skills during the role-play rather than trying to incorporate everything at once.

Based on the Communication Guide, assign specific elements for residents to observe for and be prepared to give specific feedback on during the debriefings.

# Role Play 1: Managing a Critically Ill Patient in the ICU

Before starting the simulation, introduce the clinical case (see Crosby GI Bleed document). Remind the residents that they will manage the patient together, but the resident doing the first role-play will lead the simulation.

The simulation center staff RN will then invite the residents into the ICU simulation room to meet and manage the patient.

At the 10-minute mark, call the telephone in the patient room and inform the group that the family is here and is waiting to speak with the doctor.

# Family Meeting #1 (Pre-Meeting Huddle & Role Play)

During the pre-meeting huddle identify the primary goals of this initial family meeting: to establish relationship, set an agenda, share information about patient clinical status using Ask-Tell-Ask, responding to emotion by pausing and acknowledging the emotion.

Have the assigned resident do the role-play while the rest of the group remain in the control room to observe the interaction.

After the resident has had ample time to practice, call the phone in the family meeting room to ask the resident to wrap up.

# Debrief Family Meeting #1

Start by thanking the role-playing resident for their work. Ask the resident how they are feeling about the role-play and invite them to ask family members for feedback to specific questions they have. Then ask the resident if there is something in particular they want feedback on.

Try to be specific in your feedback and give examples of skills that were effective and why. Avoid using comments like “good job” as an overall assessment because these are too vague and not instructive.

# Note on giving feedback:

With respect to giving feedback, choose only one or two topics for positive or negative feedback. Start by previewing what you would like to discuss and give a specific description of what you observed, why it strikes you as important, then open for group discussion.
