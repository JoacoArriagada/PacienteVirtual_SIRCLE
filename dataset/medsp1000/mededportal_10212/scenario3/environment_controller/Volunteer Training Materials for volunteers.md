# Session 3: Simulation Patient Case: Background for Family Member Volunteers Patient Case: Roger Crosby returns with GI bleed

Patient background:

Roger Crosby is a 76 year old man with a history of COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) diagnosed at age 60. He was admitted to the ICU for 3 weeks with a severe COPD related failure of his breathing, with pneumonia as well. There was a painful series of family discussions about whether he would recover to a life he would choose, or whether he would even be able to come off the ventilator. The family (particularly Roger’s HCP, his sister, Teresa) decided he should have a chance at recovering because while they were still deciding he started to improve his breathing after 2 weeks of little progress. He had a tracheostomy and went to a nursing facility that handles ventilators, where he improved over 6 weeks, and finally went home with some home health support.

It has been six months since he returned home. He has been doing fairly well. He can walk 4 blocks, stopping a few times to catch his breath, but tries to do it every day. He goes to lunch every week with friends he has made in his assisted living community, which he has come to enjoy. He uses oxygen at night and during his walks. He uses oxygen at night or when he needs to walk long distances. He has osteoarthritis in both knees that further limits his walking ability.

# Current Medical Information:

Roger was at his weekly lunch with his buddies this morning at around 11 at Bertucci’s. He got up to use the bathroom and was found unconscious in the bathroom. Paramedics found him in atrial fibrillation (family members will most likely be unfamiliar with that term and instead will say that they were told there was something wrong with his heart or heart beat). He regained consciousness within a couple of minutes and said that he had “a lot of blood when he thought he had to go to the bathroom.” He was brought to BIDMC ED where he had 2 more episodes of bloody stool. He was lightheaded, sweaty, lethargic.

Teresa got to the hospital within 2 hours of being called. She is very concerned because he wasn’t very interactive and he had a tube in his nose, which seemed very uncomfortable and there were so many doctors and nurses running around. Now Roger is back up in ICU. The family doesn’t really know what is going on except that he is losing blood from his bowels and his heart beat has been abnormal.

# Family concerns:

Despite the difficult situation Roger’s sisters and son/daughter were in during his last critical illness, they have not extensively discussed what happened with Roger and therefore don’t really know how he felt about going through that illness and long recovery. He seems fairly happy but he is a stoic man and he is definitely not as energetic or active as he was prior to that illness. The family is relieved at first that this illness doesn’t seem to be about his failing lungs. They feel stressed to be back in this familiar place where they experienced a lot of confusion, anxiety, sadness.

# Meeting # 1

The resident will explain the “GI bleed” and you should be unfamiliar with that term. They may also explain the “atrial fibrillation,” which should also be foreign. You should not easily appreciate how sick he is. You will be relieved that this is not a recurrence of the breathing problems he had last admission, not necessarily appreciating that this may be just as bad, or worse.
