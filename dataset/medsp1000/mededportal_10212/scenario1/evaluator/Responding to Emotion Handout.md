# ICU Communication Course Responding to Emotion

# GENERAL RULES:

Recognize emotion. Respond by pausing and offering nonverbal and verbal support.   
Avoid premature reassurance.   
Your calm and compassionate presence is what is most important.

# CONTEXT: INITIAL INTERACTION

# CONTEXT: PROGNOSIS UNCERTAIN

# Recognize& Respond to Emotion

# Overwhelmed/exhausted

This must be overwhelming. How are you coping?

I know it can be so frustrating not to know. We should have a better idea in the next day or two.

# Frustrated

# Worried

I know you are worried about your sister. Can you tell me what is of most concern to you right now?

# Confused

Would it be helpful if lexplainedmoreabout your husband's illness? What questions do you have about this?

# Hopeful

Hope is important. I hope she gets better too.

Anger is an indication that an important need has not been met. Resist the urge to be defensive.

Depending on the situation you may want to: just listen and allow person to vent their feelings,

inquire about reasons for anger e.g.“tell me more."

Validate emotion e.g.“l can see why you would be angry.”

Actively listening will help guide when to explore further or provide nonverbal/verbal support.

# CONTEXT: PATIENT DYING/PATIENT DEATH

EMOTION& POSSIBLERESPONSES

# Grief/crying

l am so sorry. [Pause, offer tissues, touch on arm if appropriate]

# Guilt

[If family member expresses guilt about decisions made] There was nothing more you could have done. The team did everything they could to turn this around,but he was very, very sick.

It is hard not to feel responsible. You helped us understand her values and wishes and that allowed us to make the medical decisions that she would have wanted.
