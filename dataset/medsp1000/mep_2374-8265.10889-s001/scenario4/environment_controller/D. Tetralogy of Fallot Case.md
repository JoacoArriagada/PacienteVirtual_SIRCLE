| **PATIENT NAME:**  Enrico  **PATIENT AGE:** 6 months old  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** cyanotic spells | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | Enrico is a 6-month-old ex-full-term boy with PMHx Tetralogy of Fallot/Double Outlet Right Ventricle who is transferred from an outside hospital for hemodynamic monitoring and pre-operative evaluation. He initially presented with increased frequency and severity of cyanotic spells over the past 2 weeks after developing fever and upper respiratory infection symptoms. He is now relatively stable on 2L supplemental oxygen via nasal cannula, but he continues to have frequent hyper-cyanotic episodes that last up to 10 minutes at a time. The present episode was triggered by phlebotomy (a few moments ago). |

| **HPI** | Enrico is a 6-month-old ex-full-term boy with PMHx Tetralogy of Fallot/Double Outlet Right Ventricle who is transferred from an outside hospital for hemodynamic monitoring and pre-operative evaluation. In the first 5 months of life, his hyper-cyanotic spells were usually brief and easily resolved with comforting. However, 3 weeks prior, these spells started to last longer. Ten days prior he developed fever and URI symptoms; of note, patient’s older sister had similar symptoms and tested positive for influenza virus. As such, mother brought Enrico to an outside hospital emergency department. On arrival, Enrico was noted to be febrile, tachypneic, and hypoxic; he was placed on 2L NC and admitted. Over the past week he has been relatively stable on 2L NC, but is having hyper-cyanotic episodes that are increasing in frequency (~ 1 x per hour), duration (up to 10 min), and severity (saturations down to 40%). He is now transferred to the PICU, and he just started having an episode after the nurse attempted a blood draw. | | |
