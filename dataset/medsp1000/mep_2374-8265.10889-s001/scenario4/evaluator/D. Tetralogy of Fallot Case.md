|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | Enrico is a 6-month-old ex-full-term boy with PMHx Tetralogy of Fallot/Double Outlet Right Ventricle who is transferred from an outside hospital for hemodynamic monitoring and pre-operative evaluation. He initially presented with increased frequency and severity of cyanotic spells over the past 2 weeks after developing fever and upper respiratory infection symptoms. He is now relatively stable on 2L supplemental oxygen via nasal cannula, but he continues to have frequent hyper-cyanotic episodes that last up to 10 minutes at a time. The present episode was triggered by phlebotomy (a few moments ago).  Overall learner goals are to recognize a cyanotic spell in a child with Tetralogy of Fallot and to appreciate the underlying pathophysiology of such an episode by employing appropriate measures to promote right ventricular flow into the pulmonary circulation. |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | Primary Objectives   1. Recognize a hyper-cyanotic spell in a child with Tetralogy of Fallot and appreciate the underlying pathophysiology of such an episode 2. Demonstrate appropriate initial management of a cyanotic episode by promoting right ventricular flow into the pulmonary circulation (i.e., having mother hold child and bringing child’s knees to the chest) 3. Escalate to pharmacologic measures (i.e., morphine, beta blockers, phenylephrine, and fluid administration if an IV is already in place) in a hemodynamically unstable child with Tetralogy of Fallot   Secondary Objectives   1. Establish the need for Cardiology and/or Cardiothoracic Surgery in the overall disposition of a patient with Tetralogy of Fallot, especially one who has developed more frequent spells 2. Utilize techniques (i.e., “Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation” aka SBAR) and closed loop communication to work as a highly effective team unit during resuscitation |
| **Critical Actions** | Assess and manage the patient’s airway by verifying patency, evaluating the presence and effort of breathing, and initiating appropriate oxygen supplementation as needed (via nasal cannula, facemask, non-rebreather mask)  Connect patient to cardiopulmonary monitor (if not on monitor already) and correctly identify the presence of sinus tachycardia, tachypnea, and persistent hypoxia  Establish IV access; however, if establishing IV access or drawing additional labs further agitates this patient, these endeavors must be abandoned in an effort to calm the child  Verbalize a differential diagnosis for hypoxia including hyper-cyanotic spell in the setting of known Tetralogy of Fallot  Employ early measures to reduce pulmonary vascular resistance (i.e, put the child in mother’s arms to calm him, abandon additional needle-sticks or other noxious stimuli) and increase systemic vascular resistance (i.e., bring child’s knees to chest, or allow child to assume a position of comfort)  Consider additional pharmacologic therapies such as morphine 0.1 mg/kg for perceived pain or propranolol 0.1 mg/kg to relax right ventricular outflow tract (infundibular spasm)  If these measures are not effective, administer phenylephrine 5-20 mcg/kg bolus dose (followed by continuous infusion) to increase systemic afterload and promote flow through the right ventricular outflow tract into the pulmonary circulation  Involve Cardiology and/or Cardiothoracic Surgery in the care of the patient who will need timely corrective cardiac surgery (BT, modified BT, or aortopulmonary shunt)  Other actions that may be taken throughout this case are:  - Initiating fluid resuscitation for hypotension  - Weigh pros and cons of drawing labs (e.g., ABG, CBC, BMP)  - Administering bicarbonate if there is documented metabolic (lactic) acidosis as acidemia is associated with elevated pulmonary vascular resistance |

We have seen and believe that the more you put into the exercise, the more that you and your colleagues will get out of it. |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*The nurses will receive sign-out on the patient prior to entering the room; they will be specifically told that the patient has recently received a dose of propranolol by the previous team before change of shift. (Of note, the MDs will NOT receive this information in their sign-out). The nurses initially enter the room to find a patient in significant respiratory distress and cyanosis. After initial assessment (ABCs) of baby and conversation with mother (standardized patient), the nurses call in the pediatric residents to evaluate the patient and communicate pertinent medical information. The pediatric residents complete a focused history and physical examination. They place the patient on a bedside monitor and recognize that the patient is tachycardia, hypoxic, and hypotensive. Supplemental oxygen is provided via facemask. IV access is attempted, but patient becomes more agitated and the spO2 worsens. The Pediatric ICU Fellow is called, and pertinent medical information is communicated once more. The fellow attempts to promote more right ventricular flow into the pulmonary circulation by aborting IV attempt, placing the child in mother’s arm for comfort, and bringing the child’s knees to his chest. The patient’s hypoxia improves minimally. The fellow appreciates the need for medical intervention and asks for a dose of propranolol. The nurse interjects that the patient has already received a dose of propranolol recently. Consequently, the fellow asks for a dose of morphine as well as a bolus dose of phenylephrine to be followed by a phenylephrine drip when the bolus is finished. Cardiology/Cardiothoracic surgery is called and made aware that this patient may need corrective surgery sooner than planned. The simulation ends.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. *Failure to enact measures to calm patient: Many of our learners proceeded immediately to medical workup and management without attempting to calm the child using conventional techniques. Further, many of our learners continued to provide noxious stimuli to the patient (e.g., IV access, lab work, etc.) despite worsening agitation and hypoxia.*
2. *Failure to recognize that a dose of propranolol had already been given: This case involves specifically providing the nurses with key information about a previous propranolol dose that is purposely withheld from the MDs. In some of our simulations, an inappropriate second dose of propranolol was given due to a lapse in communication between the nurses and the MDs.*
3. *Failure to involve Cardiology/Cardiothoracic surgery: Many of our learners were so focused on the acute medical management of a hyper-cyanotic spell they failed to appreciate that this patient with increased frequency of spells will need more definitive treatment (i.e., surgery) sooner than initially anticipated.*
