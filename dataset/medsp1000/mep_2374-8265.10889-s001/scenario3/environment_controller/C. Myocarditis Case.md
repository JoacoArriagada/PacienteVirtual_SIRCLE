| **PATIENT NAME:** Enrique  **PATIENT AGE:** 5 year old  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** decreased activity, diminished urine output | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | Enrique is a 5-year-old boy with no significant past medical history who presents with a 4 day history of progressively decreasing activity, exercise intolerance, and decreased urine output. In the ED, patient was noted to be moderately dehydrated on exam. He was admitted to the general Pediatric floor for rehydration in the setting of a suspected viral illness. While on the floor, the nurse called a rapid response because patient looked ill; a fluid bolus was started, and the PICU was called to evaluate. |

| **HPI** | Enrique is a 5-year-old boy with no significant past medical history who presents with a 4 day history of progressively decreasing activity, exercise intolerance, and decreased urine output. Over the past 4 days, the patient has complained of not feeling well, and mom has noticed that he has been unwilling to play at home or at school. He is having decreased oral intake to solids; despite adequate fluid intake he is having less urine output than normal. He was seen by his PMD 1 day prior, who sent a nasal swab for influenza. Mom was given reassurance and instructed to provide symptomatic management. Last evening patient had 2 episodes of emesis and was noted to have faster breathing afterwards, which prompted mom to come to the hospital. In the ED, patient was noted to be moderately dehydrated on exam. He was admitted to the general Pediatric floor for rehydration in the setting of a suspected viral illness. While on the floor, nurse called a rapid response because patient looked ill; a fluid bolus was started, and PICU was called to evaluate. | | |

| Instructor Notes - Changes and CASE Branch Points | | |
| **Intervention / Time point** | **Change in Case** | **Additional Information** |
| Beginning of Case | When asked, Mother (standardized patient) will explain that patient has had decreased activity and decreased appetite for the past 4 days |  |
| 2-min: Patient placed on monitor (if not on monitor already); nurse completes initial assessment | Monitor will initially show patient with HR 158, RR 38, and spO2 95% |  |
| Second peripheral IV is placed | Facilitator will communicate to the team whether the peripheral IV is working  (facilitators can state peripheral IV is not working if obtaining IV access is a desired nursing skill to practice) | If the peripheral IV is blown, intraosseous access must be obtained |
| Fluid resuscitation is initiated (e.g., 10-20 cc/kg NS bolus) | Monitor will update to show patient now with HR 180, RR45, spO2 93%. Occasional PVC’s develop with frequency of 6-10 per minute.  spO2 will continue to fall to 88% | If team asks for blood pressure it will read 82/39.  If team asks for temperature patient will remain afebrile (37C). |
| Patient is provided supplemental oxygen | Minimal immediate improvement in patient’s clinical status |  |
| Labs are ordered (e.g., CBC, BMP) | No change | Instructor lets team know the labs have been sent |
| Chest X-Ray is ordered | No change | After brief delay, instructor will provide the team with x-ray film demonstrating cardiomegaly and pleural effusion |
| EKG is ordered | No change | After brief delay, instructor will provide the team with EKG demonstrating nonspecific ST segment elevation and premature ventricular complexes |
| ECHO is ordered | No Change | Instructor will inform team that Cardiology is aware and on their way |
| Fluids are discontinued | Minimal immediate improvement in patient’s clinical status |  |
| Inotropic agent is administered (e.g., dobutamine, dopamine, milrinone) | Minimal immediate improvement in patient’s clinical status. Frequency of PVC’s increase 10-15 per minute |  |
| Diuretic medication is administered (e.g., Lasix) | Minimal immediate improvement in patient’s clinical status | Despite inotropic meds and diuretics, patient continues to have worsening respiratory distress with retractions and hypoxemia. spO2 now 88%. Blood pressure now reads 40/30 |
| Vasoactive medication is initiated (e.g., epinephrine) | Patient’s blood pressure improves, but respiratory distress worsens; spO2 now reads 85%. There are now 10-20 PVCs on the monitor. | If team asks for ABG, lactate is rising (i.e., 4) |
| Patient undergoes rapid sequence intubation | Patient’s respiratory distress and hypoxia are resolved. However, now monitor shows multiple runs of non-sustained ventricular tachycardia. | Defibrillator pads should be on during administration of sedatives and with conversion from negative to positive pressure ventilation. |
| Team prepares for tachyarrhythmia by requesting antiarrhythmic (e.g., lidocaine or amiodarone) and defibrillator | Simulation has ended |  |
