| **Appendix B: MedEdPORTAL Simulation Case Template**  **SIMULATION CASE TITLE:** “Kawasaki Arrest: A Pediatric Simulation Case for Pediatric ICU Fellows, Critical Care Nurses, and Pediatric Residents”  AUTHORS: Charles Bergman, M.D., Joy Howell, M.D. | |
| **PATIENT NAME:** Kendall  **PATIENT AGE:** 4 years old  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** abdominal pain and vomiting | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | Kendall is a 4-year-old girl with a past medical history of recently diagnosed Kawasaki disease who presents to the ED with 1-day history of abdominal pain and vomiting. Six weeks prior she was diagnosed with Kawasaki disease, and her initial echocardiogram was normal. After being treated with 2 rounds of IVIG and methylprednisolone, she was discharged home with aspirin. She had her 6 week follow up with Cardiology 1 day prior, at which time a repeat echocardiogram showed giant aneurysms of both the right coronary artery and the left circumflex artery. She is admitted to the PICU for cardiopulmonary monitoring and management. |

| Instructor Notes - Changes and CASE Branch Points | | |
| **Intervention / Time point** | **Change in Case** | **Additional Information** |
| Beginning of case | When asked, Mother (standardized patient) will explain that patient has been complaining of stomach pain for the past day |  |
| 2-min: Patient placed on monitor (if not on monitor already); nurse completes initial assessment | Monitor will initially show patient with HR 105, RR 25, and spO2 100% | Patient will eventually develop tachycardia to HR 160, and rhythm will change to ventricular tachycardia |
| Peripheral IV is placed | Facilitator will communicate to the team whether the peripheral IV is working  (facilitators can state peripheral IV is not working if obtaining IV access is a desired nursing skill to practice) | If the peripheral IV is blown, intraosseous access must be obtained |
| Fluid resuscitation is initiated (e.g., 10-20 cc/kg NS bolus) | Minimal immediate improvement in patient’s clinical status |  |
| Ventricular tachycardia is established | Patient continues to moan and will continue to have a pulse | At this point, if team is anticipating the progression to pulseless ventricular tachycardia they may request the defibrillator to have on hand and may go as far as placing defibrillator pads on the patient.  RR increased to 28;  BP drops to 69/23 |
| Patient stops moaning | Patient will no longer have a pulse | HR and RR - 0  BP and spO2 – unavailable (this may have to be communicated verbally by facilitator depending on limitations of the monitor) |
| PALS Pediatric Cardiac Arrest Algorithm is initiated; high quality CPR with 15:2 BVM ventilation begins | Patient continues to have pulseless ventricular tachycardia | Mother (standardized patient) will become upset and ask/demand to know what is going on and why this is happening |
| Defibrillator pads are correctly attached to patient and an initial shock of 2J/kg is delivered | Patient continues to have pulseless ventricular tachycardia | High quality CPR should be resumed immediately after shock is delivered |
| After 2 minutes, a second shock of 4J/kg is delivered | Patient continues to have pulseless ventricular tachycardia | High quality CPR should be resumed immediately after shock is delivered; shocks can be delivered every 2 minutes |
| After second shock, administer Epinephrine (1:10,000) 0.01 mg/kg IV | Patient continues to have pulseless ventricular tachycardia | Epinephrine can be administered every 3-5 minutes |
| Administer bicarbonate for acidosis | Minimal immediate improvement in patient’s clinical status |  |
| Cardiology is paged/called | Facilitator will communicate to the team that Cardiology is on their way |  |
| After several rounds of appropriate defibrillation and high quality CPR , ROSC is achieved | Patient will begin moaning/crying again, and monitor will show sinus tachycardia | Simulation has ended |
