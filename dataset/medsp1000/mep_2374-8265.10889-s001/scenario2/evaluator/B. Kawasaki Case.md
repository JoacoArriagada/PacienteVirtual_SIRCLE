| **Brief narrative description of case** | Kendall is a 4-year-old girl with a past medical history of recently diagnosed Kawasaki disease who presents to the ED with 1-day history of abdominal pain and vomiting. Six weeks prior she was diagnosed with Kawasaki disease, and her initial echocardiogram was normal. After being treated with 2 rounds of IVIG and methylprednisolone, she was discharged home with aspirin. She had her 6 week follow up with Cardiology 1 day prior, at which time a repeat echocardiogram showed giant aneurysms of both the right coronary artery and the left circumflex artery. She is admitted to the PICU for cardiopulmonary monitoring and management.  Overall learner goals are to appreciate Kawasaki disease as a leading cause of non-congenital myocardial ischemia, correctly identify ventricular tachycardia, understand and implement the PALS Pediatric Cardiac Arrest Algorithm in the setting of ventricular tachycardia without a pulse, and familiarize his/herself with using the defibrillator. |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | Primary Objectives  1. Demonstrate appropriate initial recognition of ventricular tachycardia; evaluate for the presence of a pulse, especially in the setting of acute clinical changes  2. Implement the PALS Pediatric Cardiac Arrest Algorithm once pulseless ventricular tachycardia is established  Secondary Objectives  3. Practice and gain comfort using the defibrillator appropriately  4. Utilize techniques (i.e., “Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation” aka SBAR) and closed loop communication to work as a highly effective team unit during resuscitation |
| **Critical Actions** | Assess and manage the patient’s airway by verifying patency, evaluating the presence and effort of breathing, and initiating appropriate oxygen supplementation as needed (via nasal cannula, facemask, high-flow nasal cannula, CPAP or BiPAP)  Connect patient to cardiopulmonary monitor (if not on monitor already) and correctly identify the presence of ventricular tachycardia (patient will initially be moaning and will have a pulse)  Establish IV access; if peripheral IV cannot be established rapidly then intraosseous access must be obtained  Anticipate the progression of ventricular tachycardia with a pulse to ventricular tachycardia without a pulse (i.e., request for defibrillator, assignment of CPR roles/responsibilities)  Assess patient for the presence of a pulse when there is an acute clinical change (patient will stop moaning)  Recognize the presence of pulseless ventricular tachycardia and employ PALS Pediatric Cardiac Arrest Algorithm by immediately beginning high quality CPR with synchronous BVM ventilation  Administer initial shock energy for defibrillation at 2J/kg and resume high quality CPR immediately after shock delivery  After 2 minutes with no change or improvement, administer second round of shock energy for defibrillation at 4J/kg and resume high quality CPR immediately after shock delivery  After second shock administer Epinephrine (1:10,000) 0.01mg/kg IV  Repeat defibrillation at >4J/kg (maximum of 10J/kg) every 2 minutes; repeat administration of Epinephrine every 3-5 minutes  Other actions that may be taken throughout this case are:  - Initiating fluid resuscitation  - Drawing labs (e.g., BMP) or obtaining other studies (e.g., EKG) to help establish underlying cause of patient’s acute presentation  - Requesting lidocaine, amiodarone, or procainamide as anti-arrhythmic options  - Requesting magnesium or calcium for cardio-protection  - Administering bicarbonate in patient with concern for metabolic (lactic) acidosis  - Involving Cardiology in the care of a patient with coronary artery aneurysms secondary to Kawasaki Disease |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*The nurses initially enter the room to find a patient in moderate acute distress with moaning/crying. After initial assessment (ABCs) of baby and conversation with mother (standardized patient), the nurses call in the pediatric residents to admit the patient and communicate pertinent medical information. The pediatric residents complete a focused history and physical examination. They place the patient on a bedside monitor and recognize that the patient is demonstrating ventricular tachycardia. The pulse is immediately checked and present. Upon recognition of ventricular tachycardia, the Pediatric ICU Fellow is called and pertinent medical information is communicated once more. An IV fluid bolus is ordered. In anticipation of progression of ventricular tachycardia with a pulse to ventricular tachycardia without a pulse, the fellow requests for the defibrillator and assigns roles for CPR to all involved participants. The patient will stop moaning abruptly, and this prompts an immediate pulse check. The pulse is no longer present, and the patient is determined to have pulseless ventricular tachycardia. PALS Pediatric Cardiac Arrest Algorithm is initiated with high quality CPR with 15:2 BVM ventilation. Defibrillator pads are correctly applied to the patient. Once ready to shock (after electricity dose selected and defibrillator is charged), every participant removes his or her hands from the patient, supplemental oxygen is turned off if applicable, and the PICU fellow makes sure everyone is “clear”. An initial shock of 2J/kg is administered, and high quality CPR is resumed immediately afterwards. After 2 minutes with no change, a second shock is delivered at 4J/kg, and high quality CPR is resumed immediately afterwards. After the second shock, Epinephrine (1:10,000) is administered at 0.01 mg/kg IV. Shocks are delivered every 2 minutes (energy may be increased up to 10J/kg) and epinephrine is administered every 3-5 minutes. After several rounds of shocks, epinephrine, and high quality CPR, the patient starts moaning again, and the simulation will end.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. *Difficulty recognizing ventricular tachycardia: We found that some of our learners had difficulty determining the heart rhythm to be that of ventricular tachycardia. Some learners became so focused on obtaining a thorough history and physical that they did not re-assess the rhythm on the monitor.*
2. *Delay in recognizing pulseless ventricular tachycardia: We found that some of our learners did not immediately check for pulses when the patient stopped moaning abruptly, thus delaying the diagnosis and treatment initiation of pulseless ventricular tachycardia. During the debriefing sessions, some learners admitted they incorrectly assumed the abrupt cessation of moaning was an artifact of the manikin.*
3. *Uncertainty of proper use of the defibrillator: We found that some of our learners were not familiar or comfortable with using the defibrillator. Specifically, some learners had difficulty with proper pad placement, selecting the correct settings, selecting the correct shock energy, and charging the defibrillator before administering the shock.*
