| Appendix A: Marijuana Ingestion Simulation Case  SIMULATION CASE TITLE: Pediatric Emergency Medicine Simulation Curriculum: Marijuana Ingestion  AUTHORS: Carson Burns, MD, Rebekah Burns, MD, Elizabeth Sanseau, MD, MS, Suzan Mazor, MD, Jennifer Reid, MD, Kimberly Stone, MD, Anita Thomas, MD, MPH | |
| PATIENT NAME: Andrew  PATIENT AGE: 6 years old  PATIENT WEIGHT: 20 kg  CHIEF COMPLAINT: “Lethargic” | |
|  | |
| Brief narrative description of case | Andrew was found to be somnolent and difficult to arouse this morning. Although his parent was able to wake him up for a few minutes at a time, he never became fully alert and could not sustain a period of wakefulness. When he was briefly awake he complained of abdominal pain, His parent brings him into the emergency department (ED) for evaluation of lethargy. Initially, the patient is somnolent with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) of 11 (eye opening to pain 2, confused 4, localizes pain 5), but other vital signs are hemodynamically stable and within normal limits for age. |

| HPI | “I could not wake Andrew up this morning! He would wake up for a few minutes and was saying that his stomach hurt, but then would fall right back asleep.”  When asked about events leading up to the event (SAMPLE):  SAMPLE history:  Signs/symptoms (sx)- “I had trouble waking Andrew up this morning. He was completely fine yesterday and he went to bed normally. He has been sleeping in my bed over the past few days because he has been having nightmares recently. I did notice that Andrew had weird twitching movement of his arms and his head overnight, but I thought they might be sleep movements. I was also half asleep when these happened. I think that these episodes lasted about 5 or 10 minutes, but I’m not sure. I couldn’t get the movements to stop with gently shaking, and they eventually stopped. I thought he might have been dreaming or something so I didn’t think it was a big deal, especially since the movements stopped, he did not pee or stool himself, or bite his tongue. His sleep seemed normal afterwards. He had febrile seizures when he was a baby and the movements kind of looked that that, but he feel asleep afterwards, so I didn’t think they were seizures.”  Allergies- none  Medications- none  Past Medical History: “He had a febrile seizure at age 18 months. He was born full term, no complications. His immunizations are up to date. Andrew has never been hospitalized or had surgery. As far as I know, he has not had any trauma or any sick contacts.”  Last meal: “He had dinner at 8 pm last night.”  Events preceding: “He went to bed normally last night.”  If asked for review of systems:  Positive for mild nasal congestion, cough, and abdominal pain. Parent denies fevers, headaches, eye redness or discharge, shortness of breath, vomiting, diarrhea, bloody stool, abnormal bleeding, bruising, musculoskeletal or skin abnormality.  If asked about home environment/social history:  “He lives at home with us (parents) and he is an only child. He is in 1st grade. My partner and I are the only caregivers for him and I am not worried about anyone harming him. As far as I know, he has not been exposed to anyone who is ill. He has no access to any drugs or alcohol in our house (including THC/methadone if asked). He also has no access to medications in our home, including over-the-counter medications such as acetaminophen, ibuprofen, aspirin, or diphenhydramine.”  If asked about family history:  “My partner has a history of seizures as a child, but she/he does not take any medications for this now.” | | |
