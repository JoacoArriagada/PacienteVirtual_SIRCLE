| Brief narrative description of case | Andrew was found to be somnolent and difficult to arouse this morning. Although his parent was able to wake him up for a few minutes at a time, he never became fully alert and could not sustain a period of wakefulness. When he was briefly awake he complained of abdominal pain, His parent brings him into the emergency department (ED) for evaluation of lethargy. Initially, the patient is somnolent with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) of 11 (eye opening to pain 2, confused 4, localizes pain 5), but other vital signs are hemodynamically stable and within normal limits for age.  Anticipated interventions include primary and secondary surveys, evaluation of altered mental status in a child, including supporting airway, breathing, circulation, establishing vascular access, obtaining appropriate laboratory values, and considering imaging, various consults. |
| Primary Learning Objectives | By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:   * Formulate a systematic approach to the evaluation and management of altered mental status * Demonstrate teamwork using the principles of crisis resource management * Describe the signs and symptoms of marijuana intoxication in a pediatric patient |
| Critical Actions | 1. Perform initial primary survey (including ABCDE, GCS) 2. Obtain intravenous or intraosseous (IV/IO) access 3. Evaluate and manage altered mental status 4. Perform secondary survey and recognize potential ingestions (alcohol (ETOH)/drugs) 5. Division of labor to accomplish multiple tasks simultaneously 6. Obtain and interpret laboratory studies, including point of care glucose 7. Clear communication with team members 8. Discussion amongst participants about likely diagnosis and appropriate management 9. Open communication with the parent even if there is uncertainty |

Ideal Scenario Flow

*The learners enter the room to find a lethargic patient with bedside monitors being placed.*

*They immediately assign team roles. They assess the patient’s bedside monitors and recognize that the patient’s vital signs are essentially within normal limits for age but that he remains somnolent with a GCS of 11 while performing the primary and secondary surveys.*

*Team considers the differential diagnosis of altered mental status in a pediatric patient. IV access is secured (IO if desired as per scenario above), and initial serum labs are obtained, start IVF 20cc/kg bolus. These initial labs are unremarkable.*

*Recognize need to evaluate a broad differential including but not limited to ingestion/toxic exposure, intussusception, non-accidental trauma, trauma, encephalitis/meningitis, sepsis, shock, seizure, hypoglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, malignancy/space occupying lesion, electrolyte abnormality, stroke, and uremia. Obtain a urine toxicology screen, glucose, capillary blood gas (CBG), electrolytes, liver function tests, Tylenol, salicylate, EtOH levels. Consider use of naloxone reversal agent to assess for potential opiate ingestion. Lack of response to naloxone is recognized immediately. Consider use of Ativan for possible seizure.*

*Patient is reassessed multiple times (at least three) during the scenario and ideally after each intervention. Each reassessment reveals that the patient’s exam is unchanged. ECG, CXR, abdominal xrays, head CT, abdominal ultrasound, and EEG are all considered. Lumbar puncture is considered and consent is attempted, although parent refuses. Recognize need for expert consultation such as neurology/PICU.*

*Chest xray and abdominal xrays are unremarkable, ECG is normal sinus rhythm, and Head CT is within normal limits.*

*Immediately after the learner provides the neurology or PICU confederate a case summary and consult question, the urine toxicology results are announced as positive for THC. This prompts a team discussion on how to manage the patient next. This result is discussed with the parent, the team asks specifically about exposure to marijuana products at home, and they have a discussion about possible referral to child protective services, and admitting the patient for observation until resolution of altered mental status versus monitoring at home.*

Anticipated Management Mistakes

1. *Failure to recognize altered mental status. Most of our learners were able to recognize altered mental status, but not everyone was able to verbalize the patient’s GCS during the primary survey and articulate that he did not require intubation at that point. We found reviewing GCS scoring or have a confederate ask for the GCS score during the simulation to be helpful.*
2. *Failure to perform secondary survey, including a focused neurologic exam. Most of our learners started a secondary survey, but not everyone was able to complete the full secondary survey without assistance from fellow learners. We found it helpful to review the secondary survey during the debrief session.*
3. *Failure to directly ask about potential medication or recreational drug exposures at home, including examples of medications and specifically naming marijuana products, or obtain a urine toxicology screen. Most learners eventually ordered a urine toxicology screen; however, most did not ask the parent explicitly if there were marijuana products in the home. We found reviewing direct questions about potential exposures to medications, alcohol or recreational drugs during the debriefing session.*
4. *Failure to consider trauma, secure cervical-spine precautions, undress patient and roll to examine total spine. Many of our learners recognized altered mental status, but few properly secured cervical spine precautions while the potential for cervical spine injury was assessed. It maybe helpful to have the parent ask if he could have hit his head during the simulation, and to discuss these precautions during the debriefing session.*
5. *Dosing error and failure to adhere to dosing references. We had a Broselow tape and PALS cards available for learners to utilize as references.*
