**Mrs. George**

**TRAINING MATERIALS for SP/CI**

**PRESENTING COMPLAINTS:** Fall and confusion

**PATIENT NAME: Alice George**

**PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:**

**Age** 82

**Sex:** Female

**Race:** Caucasian or African American

**PROFILE:**

Mrs. Alice George is an 82 year-old female who presents to the Emergency Room on a Saturday morning after a fall down her front porch steps. For the last couple of days she has not been feeling well. Her adult child does not know what specific symptoms she was having but does know that her mother was staying in bed and did not have much of an appetite (was not fixing meals for herself as she usually does). The adult child decided to check on Mrs. George this morning. On arrival, the adult child saw Mrs. George fall down the front porch steps and did not think that Mrs. George seemed injured. However, Mrs. George appeared very confused so the adult child brought her to the Emergency Room to get “checked out.”

Mrs. George normally lives alone in the house where she raised her three children with her now deceased husband, Ed. One adult child lives in her neighborhood and checks on her almost daily. This adult child is with her now in the hospital.

Mrs. George was a math teacher but her family noted she was having trouble with her bills about a year ago. She was diagnosed with mild dementia and her family has been managing her finances. Overall, she does all of her self-care and is still driving, but the family is worried about that (they would never ride with her!). She recognizes all family members and friends and still goes to church and reads the daily newspaper (but how much she understands is the question!). She is still able to cook and clean but her house is cluttered and not well kept.

Mrs. George consumes at least 2 glasses of red wine with dinner, maybe some with lunch, a habit she has had her entire adult life. She has not had a drink for 2-3 days because she was not feeling well.

**PRESENTATION:**

**Patient:**

Mrs. George appears nervous and restless. She pulls at her IV, gown, and seems to startle easily. She cannot follow a conversation and changes the subject when the doctors try to engage with her. She does not follow commands well and gets most memory questions wrong (for example, thinks the year is 1922, the season is winter, etc). She has no complaints, denies any pain when asked or answers the question inappropriately. She may even appear to see small animals in the room or comment on “all the little cats on my bed.” She is not injured from the fall nor is she in any pain.

***Goal:*** Your goal is to appear **inattentive and disorganized** in your thinking and not eager to engage with the interviewer. You are very distracted by something in the room, perhaps you are seeing small animals or thinking that you see small animals.

***Questions to anticipate:***

Mrs. George, where are you right now?

o Why yes, I need a zipper to make this right. I need to go and get one. I really must take care of these cats.

Mrs. George, are you in any pain?

o Are these your cats? I cannot sit here any longer listening.

Mrs. George, what month is it?

o Hmmm? This is very distressing. I really must be left alone.

**Family:**

The family member is an adult child of Mrs. George (biological or adopted) who lives in the same neighborhood as Mrs. George and sees her about once a day. Important: They do not live together. The family member should appear to have a good relationship with Mrs. George and be appropriately concerned about her symptoms.

***Goal:*** In the interview, your goal is to communicate that the patient is “not herself” and that this is a “change” from her baseline. Do not offer the alcohol history unless asked. You also try to reassure the patient at times, “It’s o.k., Mom. There are no cats here.”

***Opening line:***

In response to the typical opening question from the learner, “What brings you to the hospital?”

The family member appears concerned, states “Mom fell and now she is not herself! I mean, she is even seeing things! I think she could be in pain or something.”

***Questions to anticipate:***

Does she have trouble with constipation?

o “She tends to get constipated easily.”

If asked about her alcohol use, see above:

o “Mom definitely enjoys her wine. I think she has several glasses a day but I am not sure as I am not there all the time. I know when she puts out her recycling it looks like she has about 5 or 6 bottles there each week.”

If asked about her memory:

o “Her memory has definitely has gotten worse over the last year. She used to do all of her bills but about a year ago I found that she was missing payments and donating too much to all of these charities so I took over that for her. I also think she has gotten lost when driving so I am really hoping that she gives that up soon. I would never ride in the car with her!”

If asked about her vision,

o “Oh yes, Mom’s eyesight is terrible and she usually wears glasses. I think that she wasn’t wearing them when she fell and then we forgot about them. Should we bring them in?”

If asked about eating/drinking,

o “She hasn’t really wanted anything to drink since we got here.”

Example questions to ask team:

o “Does she need a valium or something?”

o “What’s the plan? Is there anything I can do to help?

PROPS**:** IV, Gown

Patient appearance: Patient should appear to be an older adult (>65).
