Case Title: First-Trimester "Septic" Abortion

Target Audience: medical students, residents, attending physicians

Primary Learning Objectives: key learning objectives of the scenario

1. Identify a gynecologic etiology of undifferentiated low abdominal pain in a reproductive-age female patient

2. Demonstrate effective communication skills with an underage patient and her parents when evaluating a pregnancy-related complication

3. Effectively and appropriately manage a first-trimester pregnancy complication (septic abortion)

4. Consult obstetric/ gynecologic specialists in a timely fashion

Secondary Learning Objectives: detailed technical goals, behavioral goals, didactic points 1. Administer appropriate antibiotics for patient with septic abortion 2. Determine maternal Rh status, and treat with RHO immune globulin (RhoGAM) when indicated for an Rh negative mother 3. Differentiate appropriate diagnostic imaging (abdominal and/or pelvic ultrasound) to identify retained products of conception

Critical actions checklist

1. Identify Septic Abortion/Infectious cause of patient's symptoms
2. Demonstrate Effective Communication Skills with Patient and Parents
3. Administer Appropriate Broad Spectrum Antimicrobials
4. Obtain Blood Type and Rh
5. Consult Gynecology

Environment (if using as a simulation case)

1. Lab Set Up - The scenario is to take place in an Emergency Department setting.

2. Manikin Set Up - A full body low-fidelity manikin will be used to run the scenario only if a standardized patient is not feasible. It will be placed on a stretcher and dressed in a gown and wearing a wig. An anatomic model for the pelvic exam can be used to further assess specific clinical skills, based on the learner (eg, basic pelvic examination skills for medical students and interns). In addition, an anatomic model for obtaining simulated pelvic ultrasound images can be added for appropriate learners (medical students, residents, attending physicians). Alternatively a high-fidelity manikin can be used to allow the learner to obtain clinical information directly from the manikin/patient (eg pulse, breath sounds, abdominal sounds). The manikin physical exam will mimic that outlined below. For both the low-fidelity and high-fidelity manikin it is best for the voice of the mannequin to come from the instructor/faculty member in order to assess learner communication skills. The case can be run following provision of vital signs; no programming is required.

3. Props - A cardiac monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, and pulse oximeter will be available, as well as IV supplies, IV Fluids, and laboratory supplies. A code cart with ACLS medications/equipment and airway management supplies will be in the room. In addition, a full complement of antibiotics and analgesia medications will be in room.

4. Audiovisual - A transvaginal ultrasound image will be available as well as laboratory studies including CBC, BMP, UA, UHCG, Quant HCG, LFTS, Blood Type and RH.

5. Distractors - As written, this is the only case to be taken care of. However, at the instructor's discretion, any distractors may be added, to include but not limited to patients to be cared for simultaneously, distraught family members, etc.

# Actors

1. Roles - It is optimal to have a standardized patient for this scenario. If this is not feasible, a low-fidelity or high-fidelity manikin can be utilized as an alternative. Necessary additional roles include a treating physician being evaluated and one nurse, as well as the instructor who will control the scenario and act as both the historian and any consulting physicians. Other participants may serve as additional staff to be directed by the treating physician and/or family.

2. Who may play them - Attending physicians, resident physicians, medical students, other healthcare providers, instructors or actors may play the roles. Physicians and physicians-in-training may play the role of treating physician or consultant. The instructor may play the role of consultant as well. Other healthcare providers may play pertinent roles, based on their background and experience. For instance, if desired, team training can be performed, in which nurses may be played by nurses-in-training. Actors may play the role of patient, family or others.

# Action Role

1. Treating physician - to be played by the attending physician, resident or medical student who is being evaluated. The role is to coordinate the team, obtain a history and perform a physical exam, order studies and treatments, and perform any advanced procedures.

2. Nurse(s) - to be played by the remaining participants. The role is to assist the physician in any requested fashion. This may include minor procedures (IV access), placing the patient on monitoring equipment, performing CPR, and giving medications.

Case Title: First-Trimester "Septic" Abortion

# CASE SUMMARY

# CORE CONTENT AREAS

Gynecology/Obstetrics, Infectious Disease, Pediatrics (adolescent medicine)

# SYNOPSIS OF HISTORY/ Scenario Background

Some items may be given freely or must be requested. It is often helpful to provide the learner with a prologue to describe the setting/surrounding of the scenario.

Chief Complaint, triage note, medic report:

17 YO female arrives to the ED with her parents. The patient was sent from her pediatrician's office for "abdominal pain, rule-out appendicitis" per note written by her PMD on a blank prescription pad.

Past medical history:

The patient's PMD did not perform a urine pregnancy test (UPT) or a pelvic examination in the office. The patient is alert and conversant, but has very overbearing parents who answer questions and finish sentences for her, making it very difficult to obtain a history from the patient with the parents in the room. As it turns out (when patient is spoken with alone only, as the parents do not know this history) she missed her last menstrual period, took a home pregnancy test that was positive, and attempted to use a suction extraction device she ordered from the internet to induce an abortion. Patient has been complaining of low abdominal and cramping, increasing over past 24 hours. Review of systems is positive for fever and chills, nausea and an episode of emesis, and vaginal spotting and discharge. The review of systems is otherwise negative. Specifically, the patient denies urinary symptoms, flank pain, changes in bowel movements, and vaginal itching.

Medications and allergies:

Seasonal allergies only. There are no known drug/medication allergies. The patient denies current or recent medication use.

Family and social history:

The patient has two younger siblings, both of which are healthy. Parents are healthy, without reported medical problems. The patient denies alcohol or drug use. She is a high school senior and lives at home with her parents. She has a boyfriend from school.

# SYNOPSIS OF PHYSICAL

Initial vital signs on arrival to the ED: Temperature = 102.2 degrees Fahrenheit Heart Rate = 122 beats per minute Blood Pressure = 108/74 mmHG Respiratory Rate = 22 breaths per minute Oxygen Saturation = 98% on room air

Physical examination reveals a non-toxic, yet uncomfortable female lying supine in the gurney holding her low abdomen with both hands. Skin is hot and moist. Respiratory and cardiovascular examinations are unremarkable, except for the mildly elevated heart and respiratory rates as noted in the presenting vital signs. Abdominal examination reveals bilateral low abdominal tenderness with voluntary guarding in the right lower quadrant (RLQ) greater than the left lower quadrant (LLQ). Pelvic examination reveals the presence of cervical motion tenderness, and a small amount of puss/bloody discharge from a closed cervical os, and a mildly enlarged/diffusely tender uterus. The remainder of the physical examination is unremarkable.

# CRITICAL ACTIONS

# SCENARIO BRANCH POINTS/ PLAY OF CASE GUIDELINES

Key teaching points or branch points that result in changes in patient's condition

# 1. Identify Septic Abortion

The overall goal in this case is to identify a gynecologic etiology of undifferentiated low abdominal pain in a reproductive-age female patient. It is critical that the candidate checks a urine (or blood) pregnancy test, and identifies features suggestive of an infectious process (fever, puss from cervical os, leukocytosis, etc.). In addition, it is crucial to order an abdominal and/or pelvic ultrasound examination to identify retained products of conception. Using the specific term "septic abortion" is not the objective of this critical action, per se; rather, it is the identification of an infectious process in the setting of retained products of conception that is the key.

Cueing Guideline: Prompt the candidate by having the nurse ask whether you would like them to check a pregnancy test on the patient, and whether a pelvic ultrasound is necessary. In terms of the infectious process, the gynecologic consultant should ask the candidate whether they have treated the patient with antimicrobials for presumed endometritis (fever, tenderness, leukocytosis) in the setting of retained products of conception.

# 2. Demonstrate Effective Communication Skills with Patient and Parents

A key component in this case is being able to interview the patient alone, in the absence of her over-bearing parents, whose intrusive nature makes it very difficult (if not impossible) for the candidate to obtain a complete history. Effective management includes offering to speak with the patient alone, and then with the parents alone to obtain an effective history. Note that depending on jurisdictional issues, the candidate may not be able to provide details of the patient's symptoms or findings to the parents. For the purposes of this exercise, this component is left to the examiner's discretion based on local procedures.

Cueing Guideline: Have the patient ask - "Mom/Dad - can you let me talk with the Doctor for a few minutes...I'm 17 years old now, and I can speak with my doctor." Also, the nurse can ask for the parents to step out of the room for a few minutes so she and the doctor can finish their evaluation.

# 3. Administer Appropriate Broad Spectrum Antimicrobials

In this patient with fever, leukocytosis, pain, tenderness, and retained products of conception demonstrated on abdominal or pelvic sonogram performed in the ED, it is prudent to initiate empiric broad-spectrum antimicrobials to cover common pathogens responsible for pelvic infections/endometritis.

Cueing Guideline: In terms of the infectious process, the gynecologic consultant should ask the candidate whether they have treated the patient with antimicrobials for presumed endometritis (fever, tenderness, leukocytosis) in the setting of retained products of conception.

# 4. Obtain Blood Type and Rh

In this patient with first trimester vaginal bleeding and fetal loss, it is critical to determine the maternal Rh status to guide decision-making regarding RHO immune globulin (RhoGAM) administration.

Cueing Guideline: Have the gynecologic consultant ask regarding the Rh status of the mother and the potential need for RhoGAM.

# 5. Consult Gynecology

Gynecology consultation to aid in decision-making regarding medical and surgical management (via dilation and curettage) is critical in this case of an infected abortion.

Cueing Guideline: Have the patient ask whether she needs to see a gynecologist - "I have never had a pap smear or anything like that before - will I need that today?"
