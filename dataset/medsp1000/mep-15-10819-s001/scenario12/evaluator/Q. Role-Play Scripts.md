Module Four Role Play Scenario Two

**Facilitator (to learner)**: Linda Williams is your patient. She is a 65-year-old woman who was diagnosed with advanced ovarian cancer 2 months ago when she presented with marked ascites. After 2 cycles of chemotherapy, her ascites regressed substantially; she lost about twenty-five pounds. When her chemotherapy orders are written for the third cycle, her original weight is inadvertently entered for the dose calculation rather than the current weight. This error is unfortunately not caught on subsequent review. Accordingly, she receives too high a dose of chemotherapy and is admitted to the hospital with a neutrophil count of 10 and high-grade bacteremia. She recovers with antibiotics and Neupogen. She is in the hospital for a week and then in rehabilitation for another week. You have asked her and her husband and daughter to come in to discuss the situation.

**Linda:** How could this have happened? *(exhibit disbelief followed by anger throughout scenario)*

Learnershould use **MR.** **SPIKES** protocol to discuss what happened.

MR – Mindfulness/rehearsal – self-awareness of hesitancy to initiate difficult discussion, anticipate anger, concerns about delay of ongoing therapy.

Set up and assessing perception (S and P) should not be necessary. Obtaining invitation (I) can be as simple as “I have something potentially upsetting to discuss with you, is now a good time?” For giving knowledge (K): Should start with preparatory statement (warning shot), such as “I have to tell you that an error was made in your chemotherapy dosing.” Lead in narrative such as “Recall you had a lot of fluid in your body when the treatment was first started” should be quickly followed by an explanation of the error that occurred. Explain that this kind of miscalculation happens rarely because of double and triple review but is more likely to occur if there is a dramatic change in weight.

E – Emotion/empathy – Validate anger on patient’s part about the error.

S’s – Strategy – Explain that all efforts are being made to address the oversight in her case.

Survey thoughts/concerns – Address potential delay in treatment may affect cancer control.

State ongoing partnership (and some strategy and summary): Attempt to re-establish trust – “I know an incident like this can make one lose trust in a doctor and treatment system. I would like to continue to be your doctor and get you back on track. Our head chemotherapy nurse would also like to meet with you and your family as well.”

*If learner is sincere and follows above:*

**Linda:** It’s still hard to accept that such a mistake was made in my care. But I appreciate your honesty. I want to start treatment again.
