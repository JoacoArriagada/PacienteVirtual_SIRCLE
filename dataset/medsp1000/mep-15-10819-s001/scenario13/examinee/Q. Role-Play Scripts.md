Facilitator (to learner): Your patient is Wilma Harrison. She is a 48-year-old single never married woman who presented with metastatic ER negative breast cancer to bone from a left breast cancer. She has not had regular screening mammography, last was 2 years ago. She has a history of anxiety disorder. She has inconsistently been followed by a psychiatrist and is on a small dose of an SSRI. Her main support is a brother who comes with her to visits. She has a strong religious faith and goes to church regularly. When you first told her that she needed chemotherapy but that it was not curative, she said “I’m going to beat it.” You started her on oral chemotherapy. There was initially improvement in the disease. On each follow-up visit she re-affirms the fact that she will be cured. You sense underlying anxiety and do not feel it wise to challenge her as she is doing well but worry about what will happen if she progresses. As she has only bone metastases, you monitor her course by tumor markers which have normalized.

On a follow-up visit, she complains of pain on her right side: “I’m sure it’s nothing.” Tumor markers have now risen. She agrees to CT scan which shows two new liver lesions, one of which is abutting the liver capsule in the right lobe thus accounting for the pain. You bring her and her brother in to discuss the news.
