Module Four Role Play Scenario Three

Facilitator (to learner): Your patient is Wilma Harrison. She is a 48-year-old single never married woman who presented with metastatic ER negative breast cancer to bone from a left breast cancer. She has not had regular screening mammography, last was 2 years ago. She has a history of anxiety disorder. She has inconsistently been followed by a psychiatrist and is on a small dose of an SSRI. Her main support is a brother who comes with her to visits. She has a strong religious faith and goes to church regularly. When you first told her that she needed chemotherapy but that it was not curative, she said “I’m going to beat it.” You started her on oral chemotherapy. There was initially improvement in the disease. On each follow-up visit she re-affirms the fact that she will be cured. You sense underlying anxiety and do not feel it wise to challenge her as she is doing well but worry about what will happen if she progresses. As she has only bone metastases, you monitor her course by tumor markers which have normalized.

On a follow-up visit, she complains of pain on her right side: “I’m sure it’s nothing.” Tumor markers have now risen. She agrees to CT scan which shows two new liver lesions, one of which is abutting the liver capsule in the right lobe thus accounting for the pain. You bring her and her brother in to discuss the news.

*Note for Wilma: Although you have a strong faith, you do not believe you are “being tested.” You knew you might have had breast cancer earlier on but were afraid of the possibility so delayed seeking attention. Your biggest fear is being consumed by the disease and the total loss of control it poses. You compensate for this by suppressing thoughts of progression.*

M Mindfulness

R Rehearse conversation

S Set up the interview

P Assess the patient’s perception – Wilma: “It’s nothing, right?”

I Obtain the patient’s invitation

K Giving knowledge to the patient. Learner should use lead-in narrative, then use “a part of” language: “I know that you are very positive and hopeful, yet I wonder if **a part of you** isn’t worried. I want to speak to that part.” Learner should give knowledge in small amounts while monitoring your reaction/emotion.

E Address emotions (RAP), empathy. Learner should reflect your denial: “it’s very difficult for you to talk about this.” *If learner is empathic and understanding, you can break down and express your overarching fear of being overcome by the cancer and have uncontrollable pain. Learner should validate this as a very common concern.*

S

Summarize the discussion

Strategize going forward: referral to psychiatry for psychotherapy and medication to improve coping

Sustain hope for control from second line chemotherapy – not cure, but something to hope for in addition to maintaining hope that her symptoms will be well controlled in your care.

Survey of concerns

Support coping – Brother very supportive, offers to have her move in with him for time being; recruit clergy from her church

Statement of support: Offer ongoing, personal support
