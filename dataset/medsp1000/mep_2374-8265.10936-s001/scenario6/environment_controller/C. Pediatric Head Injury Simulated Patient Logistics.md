Appendix C. Pediatric head injury simulated patient encounter logistics

This is a tabletop exercise that follows the Emergency Medicine oral boards simulated patient encounter exercise. No moulage or additional equipment is needed because this is a case discussion format. The examiner needs to be familiar with the case and the shared decision making framework. A video demonstrating this format may be accessed here, under sample traditional case: <https://www.abem.org/public/become-certified/oral-exam/types-of-cases-and-samples>.

To begin the case, the examiner hands the ABEM emergency department intake form (page 6 of appendix D, stimulus #1) to the resident and states: “Here is the ED intake form and let me know when you are ready to begin.” The resident may be given a pen and clean sheet of paper to take notes during the case.

The case begins when the resident states to the examiner: “I am ready to begin. Hello, I’m Dr. (name of resident). What brings you to the emergency department today?” The resident asks questions to the examiner, who plays the role of the father when giving the history and states the physical exam findings when specifically asked. The examiner only answers the questions asked by the resident. The examiner only communicates the physical exam findings when asked by the resident. If the resident orders lab tests, then the examiner may show the lab results (page 7 and 8 of appendix D, stimulus #2 and #3) and imaging results (page 9 of appendix D, stimulus #4).

Given the transient loss of consciousness and one episode of vomiting, this patient meets intermediate risk for clinically important head injury, so either head computed tomography or observation is reasonable. Dad states that he has a younger child at home with the babysitter who needs to leave, so he prefers CT.
