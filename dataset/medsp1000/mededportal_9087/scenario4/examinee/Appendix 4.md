REASON FOR VISIT: weight loss and recent fall

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: A 79-year-old woman with HTN, mild cognitive impairment, depression, GERD, asthma, D.M. type 2, Osteoarthritis, CHF with diastolic dysfunction, hypothyroidism, and A-Fib comes in today accompanied by her daughter for the complaint of weight loss. Her weight two months ago was 165 lbs. Now she weighs 142 lbs. She reports a poor appetite. She lives with her daughter, who has little time to cook. When she does cook, her mom often just picks at the food, which is very frustrating for the daughter. When the daughter works, she leaves half a bologna sandwich, chips, and cookies for her mom. Sometimes it is still there, uneaten when she returns home from work.

The patient fell two days ago and has multiple bruises from the fall. Due to her cognitive impairment, the circumstances of the fall are unclear. She was alone when she fell. The daughter says that her mother has fallen a number of times and crawls to a chair to pull herself up. She is alone much of the time, while her daughter is at work. She needs assistance with dressing, bathing, and grooming. She can toilet herself, but is experiencing some incontinence.

The patient had two appointments (July and Aug) where she was a “no show.” The daughter says the reason for the missed appointments is that her work interferes with bringing her mom. So consequently, Mrs. J. visits the ED fairly frequently. This year, she was admitted to the hospital three times for dyspnea, chest pain, dehydration, and hypoglycemia.

The patient brought meds in a paper bag. On examining the medications, there is a bottle labeled Coumadin 4 mg and one labeled Warfarin 4 mg. She has been taking both. She has not had her INR checked in three months.

We had advised her to bring her blood sugar log, but she did not bring it. Her daughter admits that she does not have time to check her mother’s glucose regularly.

The daughter appeared to be very stressed with caring for her mother and her additional responsibilities. She is a single, divorced mom and has been living with her mother in her mother’s older two-story home for the last five years. She has three teenagers, and one of them is pregnant. The daughter (J.) works as a waitress and sometimes works extra shifts to get by. Her income, along with her mother’s Social Security, is just barely enough to meet the needs of the family. Her mom is sometimes incontinent, which adds to the work and laundry. She also feels very irritable with her children and her mother. She was somewhat defensive when questions were asked. She was unaware of any resources to help her. She does know that if her mother were placed in a nursing home, she would probably not be able to support her family as the Social Security money would go to the nursing home. Her last statement to me was “I don’t know where to turn.”
