# Resources to Assist the Caregiver

Training Notes

Daughter of Patient, Age 44

Setting: At home

Telephone Conversation with: Your mother’s physician

# Background:

This is Part B of a 2-part case. A summary of Part A of this case is provided below to give a better foundation of the patient’s problems and the family dynamics involved.

# THREE MONTHS EARLIER:

You accompanied your mother, the patient, to the clinic because of a significant weightloss problem she had experienced. In just two months, her weight had dropped from 165 to 142.   
 Your mother did not say much during this visit; you did most of the talking in part because Mom had been somewhat forgetful lately.   
 You also wanted to make sure the doctor knew you were doing the best job you could taking care of Mom, but you were quite overwhelmed with all of the responsibilities of family, work, and financial concerns.   
Your mother had missed previous appointments because she had forgotten them and because it was hard for you to get away from work to take her.   
 Mom had three emergency room (ER) visits over the last few months with dyspnea (trouble breathing), chest pain, dehydration, and hypoglycemia (low blood sugar).   
 As requested by the doctor’s staff, you took all of Mom’s medicines when you went to the appointment. As the doctor reviewed everything from the bag, he saw that she had a bottle labeled Coumadin $4 \mathsf { m g }$ and one labeled Warfarin 4 mg. Your mother had been taking both of these. Neither you nor your mother knew they were the same drug, so she had been double-dosing. Subsequently, the doctor took her off the Warfarin. Because of your busy schedule you had not been able to take care of routine record keeping for Mom such as having her INR checked (how thin the blood is), maintaining her Blood Sugar Log, or checking her glucose level regularly. The log was buried in a stack of papers you hadn’t had time to go through, so you couldn’t take it to the clinic for the appointment.   
. At that time, Mom had problems with walking and maintaining her balance, so she used a walker when she left home, but used the furniture, walls, etc. to help her get around while she was at home.   
 You knew that she had occasionally fallen at home and when this happened she crawled to a chair to pull herself up if you were working and she was home alone.

 Mother’s Physical Exam at that time:

o Scrapes and many bruises on both shins and on arms o Sores on her toes and the soles of her feet; also dark areas on her heels (indicating pressure) o She did poorly on 2 tests which measure gait and balance.

At that time, you were somewhat defensive as you responded to questions because you were doing the best job you could under the circumstances (job, caring for home, mother, children, and anticipated grandchild).   
 You did not have the necessary skills to seek out agencies for help.   
 After awhile you found that the doctor was not being judgmental of you, so you softened and listened; he had some good ideas to help.

# Results:

. Doctor contacted the IL Dept. on Aging (IDA) and reported case of passive neglect caused by caregiver stress.   
. IDA contacted Senior Services of Sangamon County; a homemaker was scheduled for several days each week to assist with the patient’s care.

# Social and Family History:

 Your mother has been widowed for 14 years and lives on a small pension from Social Security. For the past five years you and your children have been living with your mother in her older, 2-story house; the three bedrooms are on the second floor.

You are 44 years old and a single, divorced mother with four children; the eldest is age 27 and in the service; the younger three are teenagers (ages: 13, 15, and 17) and live at home.   
 Your 15-year-old daughter is six months pregnant and wants to keep the baby   
 You dropped out of high school in ${ 1 0 } ^ { \mathrm { t h } }$ grade; you have worked as a waitress for many years. Your hours at the restaurant vary from one week to the next.   
Between what you earn and your mother’s Social Security, there is just barely enough to meet the needs of the family, so you often work extra shifts to make sure the bills are paid.   
 You love your mother and feel it is your responsibility to take care of her; however, exhausted from your job and all the extra work at home, you found yourself irritable with the children and with Mom.   
 Mom did not always make it to the bathroom in time, and often you had to get up in the night and change soiled sheets. In addition to losing sleep, this also created much more laundry.   
 Your mother ate poorly; you left a sandwich and chips for her before you left for work; but later you found she had eaten very little. When you did have time to cook a large meal, Mom just “picked” at the food.

# TODAY’S PRESENTING SITUATION:

While you were at work yesterday, Mom fell when she caught the toe of her shoe on a piece of tile that was coming up on the kitchen floor. Fortunately, the Homemaker sent by Senior Services was there at the time and called 911 for an ambulance to take her to the ER at Hospital Y.

While waiting for the ambulance to arrive, the Homemaker called you at work, told you what happened; you left immediately and met them at the ER.   
 X-rays were taken, but no injury was found, so she was sent home to rest.   
. You took her home, but needed help getting her into the house. Your son came and together you carried Mom into the house and onto the sofa, but she was in considerable pain and distress.   
 Your mother cannot walk and can only stand for a second or two.   
 She cannot get to the bathroom, so you scoot her in a chair and get as close as you can to the bathroom, then lift her from the chair onto the stool. She cries out in pain when you lift her and swing her around to the stool.   
 Since Mom could not walk upstairs to the bedroom, she slept on the sofa last night. You didn’t want to leave her alone so you tried to sleep in the recliner.   
 Mom couldn’t sleep because she was so uncomfortable; she kept calling for you all night, so you couldn’t sleep either. You’re exhausted and overwhelmed by the situation.   
 The phone rings; it is the doctor checking on your mother’s condition and wanting to give her additional information from yesterday’s visit to the ER. You tell the doctor that your mother cannot come to the phone; she’s on the sofa and in a lot of pain. The doctor checks the records and sees that your mother has indicated on the HIPAA (confidentiality) form that authorization has been given for you to discuss her health-care needs. The doctor tells you that when the Radiologist re-read your mother’s x-rays, there was an injury found, a non-displaced pubic inferior ramis fracture (left).   
 You ask if she broke her hip (hopefully the doctor will explain in more detail).   
 This news is very upsetting to you; you’re already overwhelmed with your situation as caregiver to your mother, your teenage children, and now a new grandchild.   
 The situation you found yourself in previously has become an even greater burden complicated by the additional responsibilities of a new grandson and now a serious injury to your mother.   
 You need help and are expecting the doctor to find ways to address the many issues faced by your family—you, your mother, your children, and grandchild.

# History of Your Mother’s Present Condition:

Three months ago you took your mother for a doctor’s visit. The physician arranged for the local Senior Center to send a Homemaker to help your mom for three hours per day.   
This help has been of great benefit to you and your mother. For example, six months ago, Mom weighed 165 pounds and in just two months, she dropped down to 142. Since the Homemaker has been there fixing hot, nutritious lunches (and sitting down with your mother, encouraging her to eat), Mom has regained eight pounds over the last three months and now her weight is up to 150.   
. The Homemaker helps Mom monitor her glucose and keep her blood-sugar log up to date; she has also taken Mom to medical appointments.   
 The Homemaker has assisted with light housekeeping, laundry, and helping Mom with dressing and grooming.   
. Previously Mom was taking two medications with different names but the same drug. Her doctor noticed this and removed one of the drugs from Mom’s medication regime.   
 Your Mother walks with a walker, but usually doesn’t use it at home. Instead, she moves about the house holding on to furniture. Both you and the Homemaker have encouraged her to use the walker while at home, but she says it’s clumsy and gets in the way.

# Social and Family History (Updated):

Two weeks ago your 15-year-old daughter gave birth to a baby boy; as a single teen mother, she needs considerable help managing the responsibilities of parenthood.   
 With the extra help Mom gets from the Homemaker, your caregiving responsibilities for her have been lessened somewhat, but have now been replaced by helping your daughter take care of her new infant son.   
. Between working at your job, managing the household, and taking care of the needs of the family, you have little time for rest. You think that no matter how hard you work, you never seem to have time to just relax. You are exhausted and feeling overwhelmed with all of your responsibilities, and once again, you have found yourself becoming impatient and even yelling at the children and your mom over small matters.   
You love your mother and feel that it is your responsibility to take care of her. You are grateful for the extra help provided by the Homemaker, but your caregiving burdens are still considerable.   
. You also are very worried about financial matters. You work as many extra hours as you can, but your responsibilities at home tire you to the point you can barely stand on your feet for your regular food-service shift.   
 You know that if your mother is placed in a nursing home, you probably would not be able to support your family on just what you make. Your mother’s monthly Social Security check is needed for the family to just barely get by.   
 You feel you are doing the best you can to take care of your mother, the kids, and grandson, but it just doesn’t seem to be enough.   
 Other than the services provided by the Senior Center for Mom, you are not aware of any community resources available to help.   
 You are desperate and feeling there is just no hope or help for your family—you just don’t know where to turn.
