# Setting

The resident is with the patient in the out-patient clinic.

# Participants

Patient: Patient 1 (age 62) (played by a standardized patient).

# Scenario

Patient 1 is a 62-year-old-Caucasion woman who presents in an outpatient clinic at 2:00 PM with no physical complaints. She is well-dressed, and neatly groomed, and speaks clearly and articulately. Her vital signs are normal. Her blood pressure is 120/80. She is $5 \ ' 6 \ '$ and weights 150 lbs. When asked the reason for her visit today, she responds that she has felt depressed and would like a prescription for anti-depressants. Specifically, she states that she has taken Lexipro in the past; it worked well, and she would like to have thirty $2 0 ~ \mathsf { m g }$ pills with six refills.

# Testing Situation

There is a patient chart with the nurse’s notes on it (above information). The patient is new to the clinic, having made an appointment the week before, taking the first available opening for non-urgent care. The resident may write on the chart while deciding how to handle the patient.

# Desired Outcome

The resident should explore the depression, determine what medications (over-the-counter and prescription) the patient currently takes, and why the patient needs Lexapro from this clinic rather than from her usual primary care provider. The resident should uncover the addiction, help the patient establish a care plan, address concern over being “fired,” identify resources, and make referrals.
