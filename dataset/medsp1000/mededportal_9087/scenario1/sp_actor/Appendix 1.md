# Training Notes for Patient (Age: 40)

# Presenting Situation:

You are a 40-year-old female who has come to the clinic following a back injury that occurred as you were putting away Christmas decorations in the attic. Your back pain is not as severe as it was four days ago, but you wanted to check this with your physician.

Also, you are interested in whether there are preventive health-care issues that should be addressed for both you and your husband who is the same age. You brought a list of questions to ask the doctor.

# History of Presenting Illness:

You were carrying heavy boxes upstairs to the attic to put away Christmas decorations and felt pain several times as you lifted or placed the boxes on the floor. Before you could finish putting everything away, the pain had gotten considerably more severe and was radiating down the back of your thighs. You stopped, took Tylenol, and rested for the remainder of the day.   
. There was no direct trauma—you did not hit your back against the rafters, as has happened in the past.   
 You have not noticed any weakness in your legs.   
 There have been no bladder or bowel changes that you have noticed.   
 You have had backaches occasionally in the past, but there previously had not been the radiation down into the back of your thighs. Usually the discomfort resolved within 24 hours. The pain was pretty severe at first. You took Arthritis-Strength Tylenol (2 tabs, 1,000 mg each) about every three hours for the first four days. “I took them that often because Tylenol is ‘harmless,’ right?”   
 On a scale of 1-10, the pain was about “7” at first, but is now at a “2.”   
. The radiation into your thighs subsided within the first 24 hours and you are gradually improving, but you wonder if there is something else that should be done. You ask the doctor if any further evaluation or tests are needed.

 You have a history of hypertension that is controlled with medication.

# Preparation of the Simulated Patient for this Exercise

The patient should be an individual 35-50 years of age, in good health, with just a history of hypertension that is well controlled on hydrochlorothiazide. She injured her back while putting away Christmas decorations after the recent holiday. She was bending over and lifting heavy boxes and stacking them on shelves. The pain is getting better, but has persisted for a little longer than previous episodes, and initially radiated into the posterior thighs. The thigh discomfort is now gone. There was no direct injury to the back. There are no signs of change in leg muscle strength, or sensation and no bladder or bowel changes. No maneuvers such as bending, coughing, or sneezing worsen the pain. She has tried some Tylenol; she took arthritis-strength Tylenol 1000 mg about every three hours for the first four days. She has seen some improvement and has resumed her usual activities, but is interested in whether further evaluation and treatment is necessary for the back. Specifically, does she need an x-ray or MRI of the spine, and if not, why not?

In addition, since she is seen so infrequently, she wants to review her preventive health-care status with the doctor and comes prepared with a card listing some questions. She also has a card to give to the doctor if s/he asks for it.
