# Patient 2 Chronic Disease Management (Substance Abuse) Training Notes

You are a 52-year-old-Caucasian woman who lives between towns X and Y on a rural state road. Your husband is a truck driver who is gone three to four days a week and provides a good income to you. He turns his check over to you every week to deposit and take care of the house. Your two children (son and daughter) are both grown and live in Chicago and St. Louis, neither is married, and they don’t have stable lives. Both children have drug issues, but no legal problems, or financial needs. Your husband is a quiet man who likes to watch TV when home. If asked, your husband does have a temper. He hits, slaps, and hurts you, but only when you do not live up to his expectations. This has been a pattern for as long as you have been married. The children were not physically abused, but occasionally witnessed your husband’s violence toward you. You have few close friends anymore, and have lost interest in activities you used to enjoy. You spend a good deal of time alone in the rural home longing for years past when the house was busy with children and their school activities.

When you were young your father used to physically abuse you which broke bones including those in your spine on several occasions. When you were in your 30s you sought medical care for your back pain and you were treated with narcotics. You received Hydrocodone, which worked, and you have been on it ever since. You are concerned now because the back pain is worse and the Hydrocodone is not helping anymore. As the doctor tried to wean you off the drug, you became angry, fired him and began doctor shopping. When the internet came to your home in the 1990s you found you could get both Hydrocodone and Oxycodone from internet doctors for the right price. In addition, your children are both narcotic users with access to the larger markets in both St. Louis and Chicago. Both children supply your habit, and you occasionally use street drugs such as morphine and heroin. Although you have never injected yourself, you have learned to snort (inhale) the drugs, as well as take them orally.

Not long ago, you ran out of internet narcotics when your on-line pharmacy was shut down by the DEA. Your children could not arrange any shipments from Chicago or St Louis. You called your previous doctor for a prescription, but found out he is now retired. After a few days you were anxious, irritable, and had a lot of aches and nasal congestion. You were upset and felt you needed to get the meds no matter what!

Your symptoms for back pain are as follows. The pain is dull and aching. It is along the entirety of your spine. It gets worse with activity like walking. It is painful after a night’s rest, as well. The pain does not move anywhere. It has been slowly getting worse over the past couple of years. There is no difficulty with your bowels or bladder function. You do not have any weakness or numbness in your legs. You do not have a fever. You have not lost any weight. There is also no associated headache. You are uncomfortable sitting, standing, or lying down. If asked about taking x-rays, you agree, but with some frustration indicate that they probably won’t show anything different than in the past--arthritis of the back.

You use no other prescription drugs. You do not drink or smoke. You do not take any over-the-counter drugs. You currently use eight to ten $1 0 ~ \mathsf { m g }$ tablets of Hydrocodone each day (10/500mg), but will use street narcotics or other narcotics if your usual supply runs out.

You admit to being a little “down,” but deny being depressed. You indicate that the pain is worsening so you need to increase the medication, especially on days when the pain is really bad.

As the resident asks questions, answer honestly, but do not volunteer any information unless specifically asked. Answer only the questions asked.
