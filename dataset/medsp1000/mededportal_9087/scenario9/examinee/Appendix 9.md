# Presenting Scenario

This is what the Resident will read on the Professional Development Laboratory (PDL) door.

Patient. The patient, DOB: 07-04-1936, is a 75-year-old woman who presents to the hospital with atypical chest pain. She was initially seen in the ED and admitted to the floor. The first-year resident on the other team completed the history and physical examination, including the dictation, writing the initial orders, seeing the patient that morning (along with the attending hospitalist physician), and signing the patient out to your team. However, it is the weekend, the admitting team’s residents and the hospitalist attending physician are gone, and you are the senior resident on-call paged by the floor to see the patient because of the recurrence of chest pain. A cardiology consultation was written for, but there is no documentation that the cardiologist received the call and no documentation that Cardiology has seen the patient. You have examined the patient, believe her condition to be unstable, and must now contact the cardiologist on-call to expedite the consultation before writing your note. A repeat ECG and cardiac enzymes are pending. You have the chart available to contact the cardiologist on-call who is Dr. Hart and you have his pager number.

Chart Highlights. The following are the highlights of what the chart will contain.

The patient has multiple co-morbid conditions including Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia, all of which are well-controlled and stable. Her past medical history is remarkable for a previous cholecystectomy for gall stones, bilateral cataracts with intraocular lens implants, and an admission the preceding year for community acquired pneumonia. Her outpatient medications include:

Metformin 1000 mgs twice a day Glipizide 5 mgs a day

Simvastatin 20 mgs a day at bedtime   
Lisinopril 10 mgs a day   
Aspirin 81 mgs a day

She has no known medication allergies.

ROS, PMH, SOC HX, unremarkable except as mentioned above.

Her initial physical examination was unrevealing except for her cholecystectomy scar.

Her initial laboratory studies were also normal and her ECG showed sinus rhythm with mild nonspecific ST-T wave changes. The chest x-ray done through the ED was also unrevealing. Serial cardiac enzymes overnight were normal.

The patient is presently on subcutaneous heparin and IV nitroglycerin. Metoprolol 25 mgs twice daily was added to her usual home medications, as well as Protonix 40 mgs a day.
