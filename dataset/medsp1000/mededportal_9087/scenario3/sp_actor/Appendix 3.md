# Patient Sign-out and Hand-off Case

# Training Notes

# The Patient

Setting: Hospital room where you are lying in the bed.

# Presenting Situation:

You are 65 years old, retired, and live in Houston, Texas. Late last night you were brought to the Emergency Room (ER) when you became ill while visiting your daughter and her family in another state.   
Dr. Smith examined you in the ER. A chest x-ray was ordered and you were started on oxygen and an antibiotic treatment.   
. Dr. Smith diagnosed your condition as Community Acquired Pneumonia and admitted you to the hospital for further treatment.   
. You are lying in the hospital bed with the head of the bed elevated and with an oxygen tube in your nose. Your color is a little dusky. You are very nervous, fearful, and anxious.   
. You had a very restless night, and are still in some distress with shortness of breath and a slight hacking cough.   
， Also, it was hard for you to get comfortable; you had some pain on the right side of your chest, just to the right of the breastbone. It is about 11:00 AM, and a new doctor enters your room to take care of you; you have not seen this doctor before.   
. During the exam you are breathing rapidly; sometimes your nostrils flare. If the doctor asks you to sit on the side of the bed for an examination, you splint (hold in place) the ribs on your right side.   
 You also complain of feeling a little lightheaded.   
When the doctor asks you where to pain is, you point to the right side of the breastbone. You describe the pain as sharp and stabbing. It seems worse when you take a deep breath, cough, or bend.

# History of Present Illness:

You drove to your daughter’s house three days ago to see her, your son-in-law, and grandchildren   
. You wanted to get to her house in time to see one of you grandchildren play in a basketball tournament, so you only stopped long enough to get gas.   
 Yesterday, after visiting a presidential museum with your family, you started feeling rather ill and short of breath.   
 You noticed you had a slight hacking cough, but didn’t seem to bring anything up when you coughed.   
. You also had some discomfort on the right side of your chest and had difficulty catching your breath.   
. You haven’t eaten much, but have been drinking plenty of water. You haven’t felt like eating, in addition, you have a lot of food allergies. You are afraid to eat food in restaurants and not sure what to order.   
None of the other family members in the household have any similar symptoms.

# Past Medical History:

You live alone in a small home in an active senior community in Houston, Texas. After you took early retirement from your state job, you moved there to be near two adult children and their families and to enjoy a warmer climate.   
 You were diagnosed with GERD (“reflux”) in 2005.   
 Occasionally there is some swelling in your legs at the end of the day.   
 You smoke about $\%$ pack of cigarettes a day; this has been your routine for the past 50 years.   
 You drink alcohol socially.   
 You do not take any drugs other than what is prescribed.   
o No weight loss or gain over the last few years   
o No changes noticed in skin, hair or nails   
o No blurred vision, hearing loss, sore throat, or sinus congestion   
o No problems with breathing or cough   
o No past surgeries

# Medications:

Omeprazole 40 mg po qday

# Family History:

 Your mother died of a “blood clot” in her lung.  Your father had high blood pressure . Your paternal grandmother had osteoporosis.

# Allergies:

 No allergies to medications, but you have multiple food allergies

# Other Notes:

You are experiencing some anxiety and nervousness, in part because you are in a hospital far from home; also you are having difficulty breathing and this is frightening to you.   
 This is a different doctor than the one who treated you last night when you came to the ER. The doctor should introduce her-/himself to you and explain that s/he is taking over for Dr. Smith, who had to leave the hospital.   
 The doctor should examine you and discuss the findings. If the resident does not recognize that your problem is not pneumonia, say “Do you think it could be something else other than pneumonia? I’ve had pneumonia and it didn’t feel like this. Was my chest x-ray okay?” The doctor should relate to you that because s/he has to be in clinic this afternoon, Dr. White will be caring for you for the remainder of the day.   
. The doctor should leave your room and go to the residents’ lounge to discuss the details of your condition with Dr. White, who will assume your care.   
. After the encounter, the resident should clip a new order sheet and a new yellow card to the outside of the chart.
