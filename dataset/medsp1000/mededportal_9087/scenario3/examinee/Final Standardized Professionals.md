# Scenario 3: Patient Sign-out and Hand-off

# Scenario

The patient came to Springfield three days ago to visit family and see the Lincoln Museum. She is from Texas. She became ill last night, and was evaluated in the Emergency Department (ED). She was admitted to the hospital with a diagnosis of Community Acquired Pneumonia, and Levofloxacin was begun.

It is 11:00 AM, the morning after the admission, and the resident is examining the patient. This is the resident’s first time to see her. Dr. Smith, the resident who admitted her, has gone home because of personal illness. The patient is afebrile with a minimal non-productive cough. The chest x-ray is negative and the patient’s main complaint is right-sided chest pain and shortness of breath. These symptoms have not improved since admission, and the patient is in moderate respiratory distress, despite oxygen therapy. An arterial blood gas (ABG) test drawn on admission showed significant hypoxia and an elevated A-a gradient. The resident evaluates the patient and writes orders on the chart to further investigate the right-sided chest pain and shortness of breath.

Soon, the resident will need to leave the hospital to attend afternoon clinic, so s/he will need to sign-out to Dr. White, a co-intern, who will assume care of the patient.

# Presenting Situation

This information is presented to the resident before entering the room.

Setting: Part 1 takes place in the hospital room for the patient examination. Part 2 occurs in the residents’ lounge, where the transfer of patient care is conducted.
