**Instructor’s Guide**

1. **Duration –** 10-15 minutes, progression to ventricular fibrillation as described in narrative
2. **Environment**
   1. **Lab Set Up –** Simulation emergency department
   2. **Manikin Set Up**
      1. Advanced medical simulation manikin
         1. Respiratory rate 26
         2. Atrial fibrillation at 50-60
         3. Blood pressure 92/42
         4. Irregularly irregular rhythm w diastolic murmur
      2. Male patient in street clothing
   3. **Props**
      1. **EMS rhythm strip:** Atrial fibrillation, ST depression in lead II
      2. **ECG:** Atrial fibrillation, ST elevation in III>II and AVF, ST depression V2-3. If obtained, ST elevation in V4-6R
      3. **CXR:** No acute findings
      4. **Cardiac ultrasound:** No acute findings
      5. **Laboratory values:** See Appendix A in `R STEMI.md`
   4. **Distracters –** None
3. **Implementation**
   1. Initiate and walk through case as listed in narrative
   2. Provide props when requested as listed in narrative
   3. **Pitfalls**
      1. Students may stray from the case, either following alternative diagnoses or ordering unnecessary, time consuming tests. Although allowing some autonomy to proceed with care may be instructive, the instructor must balance autonomy with the desired teaching points of the case. Students can be redirected with worsening vital signs or cardiac arrest. In some cases, a well timed question from nursing can redirect the student.
      2. Many cases involve recurrent cardiac arrest. At this point, ACLS inadvertently becomes the focus, and many teaching points are never reached. A debriefing covering initial treatment mistakes should be provided. The student should then retake the same case. The retake allows the student to implement these lessons and progress to the latter parts of the case, thinking through the further teaching objectives.
   4. Students become more proficient as they gain experience in the simulation environment. After working through even unrelated cases, performance will improve.
4. **Limitations**
   1. **Relies on appropriate initial treatment**
      1. If a student does not recognize the presenting diagnosis or initial treatment, the middle and end the case may not be reached, limiting intended teaching points. A debriefing followed by a retake of the case may provide further instruction. Making the patient more stable may also allow the student to progress. However, students tend to stray from the case more quickly with such a scenario.
