1. **Scenario**
   1. **Location**
      1. **Primary scenario –** Tertiary medical center with PCI capability
   2. **Background** (provided initially)
      1. **Chief complaint –** 59 year old male brought by EMS after having sudden onset chest pain at the golf course.
      2. **EMS report –** “Patient presents with sudden onset, substernal chest pressure constant for 45 minutes along with shortness of breath, diaphoresis, and mild nausea without vomiting. He was awake, alert, and in obvious distress upon arrival to scene. +diaphoresis, +difficulty breathing. The rest of his exam was normal. 2L NC started.”
      3. **EMS rhythm strip –** Available upon request
   3. **History**
      1. **History of present illness –** When asked, the patient reports constant substernal pressure without improvement with ASA. + mild radiation to back, +diaphoresis, +nausea, +lightheadedness, +fatigue. No sharp pain, no radiation, and no change with movement or inspiration. He has never had similar pain.
      2. **Medical History** – Atrial fibrillation, HTN, Hypercholesterolemia, GERD
      3. **Surgical History –** none
      4. **Medications –** Toprol XL, Lipitor
      5. **Allergies –** NKDA
      6. **Family History –** Father with MI at age 61
      7. **Social History**
         1. Lives at home with wife
         2. Smoked ½ pack per day for 40 years
         3. No alcohol or IV drug use
      8. **Narrative**

A roughly 100kg male is lying on the stretcher complaining of chest pressure and trouble breathing.

The ECG will be available 1 minute after request. I-STAT labs will be available 5 minutes after request. Events will proceed as described below.

**If NS within 5 minutes:** If normal saline bolus (500mL) given, BP will improve to 115/75 with HR 79. Pt continues to complain of chest pressure and shortness of breath. Benchmarks should be completed, and cardiology should be contacted for immediate PCI. The use of Plavix (300-600mg) and a glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor can also be discussed with cardiology. Alternatively, if PCI is more than 90 minutes away, TPA should be started.

**If no IVF given after 5 minutes:** If no fluid given, trends down to 75/40 with HR unchanged. Patient becomes less responsive with RR 12. If a normal saline bolus (500mL) is given within 2 minutes, his BP will respond, and the case will progress as above. Without a bolus after 2 minutes, his BP continues to drop, and he enters v-fib arrest.

**If at any time nitro is given:** His BP falls to 75/40 with HR unchanged and the case progresses as above. If further nitro is given, BP will continue to fall, and the patient will enter v-fib arrest.

**If at any time morphine/fentanyl is given:** His BP will fluctuate at the discretion of the simulation proctor. If his BP falls below 75/40 without fluid resuscitation, his BP continues to fall, and he eventually enters v-fib arrest as above.

**If at any time inotropes/pressors are started:** BP will respond accordingly with appropriate dose given.
