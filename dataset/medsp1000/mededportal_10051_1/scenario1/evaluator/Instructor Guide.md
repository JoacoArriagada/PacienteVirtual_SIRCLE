**Instructor Guide: Disclosing the Death of a Child**

**Learning Objectives**: At the end of this activity, the learner should be expected to:

1. Describe the steps involved in a death disclosure.
2. Perform a death disclosure to a patient’s family member.
3. Reflect on a death disclosure and identify areas for improvement.

**Conceptual background**

Delivering a death disclosure is a routine, but high stakes part of the practice of an emergency physician. However, this is a skill that is practiced with a wide range of competence both by residents and faculty, and one that can be improved through practice [1] [2] [3]. Communication skills necessary for such a conversation are a focus of the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education milestones in emergency medicine (EM) (Sub-competency 18. Patient Centered Communication (ICS1), Level 4). This case was created as part of a hands-on session for emergency medicine interns to increase their comfort and competence with death disclosures. There is clear evidence describing validated methods of delivering this information to patients and families [3]. However, there is a dearth of published cases to use for practice with standardized patients with faculty observing.

The session was conducted in June, at the end of intern year with groups of approximately 20 interns, and has been repeated annually for more than ten years Six faculty participate annually, and include emergency medicine faculty as well as palliative care faculty and fellows.

Prior to the execution of this case, all SP’s were provided with both written scripts and one hour of live training. Faculty raters also received the materials, as well as guidelines on death disclosure in advance. Because of the emotional nature of the subject material, SP’s are given the opportunity to decline the role ahead of time, and those who are known to have experienced similar circumstances are not cast for the role. In addition, time is made for debriefing after the day, and for decompressing during the day. Many of the actors have their own processes for coming out of emotionally draining characters and they are encouraged to use these. These are standard procedures within our SP program. Each SP will perform the same case about eight times in one day.

A one hour didactic session on death disclosure for the interns preceded the SP encounters. Each intern rotated through two different cases, of which this case was one. During the scenario, they were observed by the SP, the faculty rater, and a peer, all of whom delivered feedback. The faculty rater completed the direct observation tool which was created using language which maps to the EM milestones. The tool was reviewed for content validity by palliative care experts.

Debriefing following the session takes place with the faculty, the resident, a peer, and the SP all present. The learner is invited to provide a self-critique first, followed by the peer and SP, and finally the faculty member. Discussion focuses on the emotional toll of this conversation on both the learner and the SP, as well as the resident’s ability to utilize the format presented in the didactic session.

Materials needed:

* Timer/Watch
* Rater checklist and pen
* Two chairs or one chair and one examination table
* Box of tissues

Session Length: 20 minutes, divided evenly between scenario and debriefing
