Simulation Flow

1. Patient presents complaining of “feeling bad”, “nervous, shaky”, “feel clammy”. Initial vitals: HR 118, R 22, BP 184/102, T 37.9o C.
2. Trend should immediately begin gradually increasing HR and BP over next 20 minutes.
3. Monitor, IV, oxygen saturation should be initiated. If not vitals should worsen quickly.
4. Physician should do initial history and physical.
5. If social history is asked, patient should initially state “I don’t drink.” Physician should ask if he ever drank and how much; then will get history of quit drinking 2 days ago. Patient was drinking 1 bottle (750ml) whiskey a day.
6. Patient should complain of nausea during history. If given anti-emetics, will resolve and not vomit.
7. If social history skipped, patient should either vomit/aspirate requiring intubation (if no anti-emetics) or have a seizure and be post-ictal for remainder of the case.
8. If patient give IVF bolus, slight decrease then, HR continues to increase.
9. If patient given lorazepam 2mg for seizure it will resolve, but only minimal decrease in HR.
10. If MD talks to wife, the history of heavy alcohol abuse for years and recently quitting will be given.
11. If HR reaches 170s, EKG will begin to show ischemic changes.
12. Small doses of benzodiazepines will improve HR and BP for 1-2 minutes then upward trend continues.
13. Large benzodiazepine doses or will improve HR and BP for 3-5 minutes, but upward trend should not stop until given 2 or 3 doses or drip started.
14. Patient vitals should stabilize once given 2 or 3 large doses of benzodiazepines, on benzodiazepine drip or if intubated and on propofol drip.
15. Patient should be admitted to Intensive Care Unit for close observation and potential for worsening symptoms.
