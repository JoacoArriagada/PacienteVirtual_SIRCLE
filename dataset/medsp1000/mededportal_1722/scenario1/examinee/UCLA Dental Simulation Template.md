# Scenario #1: Syncope/neurogenic shock

Mr. Molár is a 30 year-old Hispanic male. He is a painter by trade and took the day off to come into the walk-in clinic for care. He has no significant medical history other than his current complaint.

Today, Mr. Molár presents complaining of two-day history of severe pain to his lower left jaw. He states that he has been unable to chew food for the last $2 4 +$ hours. He has also taken 3 Tylenol tablets to no effect. Mr. Molár states no other health problems.

# Objective:

T: $3 7 . 4 \mathrm { { ^ { \circ C } } }$   
RR: 18 breaths per minute   
General: Healthy appearing, but in obvious distress due to complaint of pain.   
Oral exam: inflammation and erythema to gum around his lower left $2 ^ { \mathrm { n d } }$ molar. CVS: \*Will assess in lab.   
Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally, no wheezing, rales, rhonchi.   
Abdomen: Normal bowel sounds, soft, non-tender, no masses.   
Extremities: no cyanosis/edema.   
Oral intake: Pt has had nothing to eat for the last 12 hours due to pain and discomfort.

# Case Narrative:

Mr. Molár is taken for an x-ray by the assistant. He is seated in the upright position during the x-ray. There were no issues other than the patient having difficulty holding the $\mathbf { X } \cdot$ -ray film in his mouth due to pain.

Following x-ray, Mr. Molár is returned to the exam room and seated upright in the exam chair. The dentist enters the room to examine the patient and discuss the procedure.
