# IV. Environment

These case based simulations used a Medical Education Technologies Inc. (METI) Emergency Care Simulator (ECS) human patient simulator (HPS).

B. Set Up for Small Group Laboratory: The HPS was set up in the middle of the physiology lab (500 ft2) which can accommodate up to 25 students per session. The room was equipped with surgical lamps which were focused upon the HPS. There

were an adequate number of stools for student seating near the HPS. The monitor display was projected on an adjacent screen visible to all students. All students and instructors wore white laboratory coats. Other equipment and supplies were located adjacent to the HPS. These included a stethoscope, dental syringe packed in sterilized pouch and a defibrillator.

# VI Case Narrative: Lecture Outline and Two Cases for the Laboratory.

# B. Laboratory Cases:

For the first scenario, the student is asked to stop and review the monitor tracings for any changes at each number $( ^ { * } 1 , ^ { * } 2$ , etc.). The vital signs from the monitor will now read the values as shown in the instructor version table. The scenario continues through each numbered segment with different students. At the end, the patient is physically lowered to a full supine position to simulate the action plan for syncopal recovery.

# Scenario #1: Syncope/neurogenic shock

Mr. Molár is a 30 year-old Hispanic male. He is a painter by trade and took the day off to come into the walk-in clinic for care. He has no significant medical history other than his current complaint.

Today, Mr. Molár presents complaining of two-day history of severe pain to his lower left jaw. He states that he has been unable to chew food for the last $2 4 +$ hours. He has also taken 3 Tylenol tablets to no effect. Mr. Molár states no other health problems.

# Objective:

T: $3 7 . 4 \mathrm { { ^ { \circ C } } }$   
RR: 18 breaths per minute   
General: Healthy appearing, but in obvious distress due to complaint of pain.   
Oral exam: inflammation and erythema to gum around his lower left $2 ^ { \mathrm { n d } }$ molar. CVS: \*Will assess in lab.   
Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally, no wheezing, rales, rhonchi.   
Abdomen: Normal bowel sounds, soft, non-tender, no masses.   
Extremities: no cyanosis/edema.   
Oral intake: Pt has had nothing to eat for the last 12 hours due to pain and discomfort.

# Case Narrative:

Mr. Molár is taken for an x-ray by the assistant. He is seated in the upright position during the x-ray. There were no issues other than the patient having difficulty holding the $\mathbf { X } \cdot$ -ray film in his mouth due to pain.

Following x-ray, Mr. Molár is returned to the exam room and seated upright in the exam chair. $\pmb { \mathrm { \nu } } _ { 1 }$ The dentist enters the room to examine the patient and discuss the procedure. She tells the patient: “Based on your complaint and our findings, think it is best to extract the tooth immediately.” The patient agrees to the extraction and reminds the dentist about his distress due to the pain. $^ { * 2 }$

Upon application of the topical anesthetic patient gags in response.

When the dentist presents with the syringe for injection and asks Mr. Molár to open his mouth, she notices that he is sweating and slightly pale. $\pmb { * 3 - 4 }$ Mr. Molár begins to feel “light headed”, but does not express this to the dentist. Just as the dentist is starts to insert the syringe into the patient’s mouth, Mr. Molár’s eyes glaze over and diverge as the he immediately looses consciousness. $^ { * 5 - 7 }$
