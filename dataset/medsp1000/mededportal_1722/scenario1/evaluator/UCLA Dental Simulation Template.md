# Scenario #1: Syncope/neurogenic shock

Mr. Molár is a 30 year-old Hispanic male. He is a painter by trade and took the day off to come into the walk-in clinic for care. He has no significant medical history other than his current complaint.

Today, Mr. Molár presents complaining of two-day history of severe pain to his lower left jaw. He states that he has been unable to chew food for the last $2 4 +$ hours. He has also taken 3 Tylenol tablets to no effect. Mr. Molár states no other health problems.

# Teaching Points: [Instructor Version Only]

1. Syncope and neurogenic shock physiology.

2. Predisposing factors – young adult males between 16 and 35 years. Administration of local anesthetic, fear of a dentist.

3. Presyncopal signs/symptoms.

Early – warm feeling, pale or ashen skin tone, tachypnea, “faint”, nausea, tachycardia   
Late – pupil dilation, hyperpnea, cold and clammy hands/feet, hypotension, bradycardia, dizziness, loss of consciousness

4. Vital sign assessment.

5. Properly position patient to minimize risk of syncope – supine or semi-supine in dental chair.

6. Emergency management – discuss airway protection, lower head, raise feet. With delayed recovery, call 911.

7. Prevention – provide comfortable environment, encourage patient to eat prior to visit and/or maintain adequate hydration $( O J )$ , minimize stress to patient, conduct thorough pre-procedure assess of patient medical history.
