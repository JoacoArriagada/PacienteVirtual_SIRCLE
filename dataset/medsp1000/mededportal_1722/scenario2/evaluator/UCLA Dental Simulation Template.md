# Scenario #2: Angina Pectoris

Mr. Denton is a 68 year-old Caucasian male. He is a retired middle school teacher and now leads a sedentary lifestyle, living at home with his wife.

Mr. Denton has a history of heart failure secondary to CAD (coronary artery disease) and high cholesterol. He was a smoker for over 30 years, but quit about 14 years ago. He is rated as ASA-II (can walk 1 flight of steps and two level blocks before becoming winded). He currently is taking $\beta$ -blockers, diuretics, and $\alpha$ -blockers for his condition. He is regularly taking aspirin, but stopped taking it 4 days prior to today’s appointment. He also has a prescription for Nitroglycerin to take as needed. You consulted Mr. Denton two weeks ago and referred him to his internist to get a medical clearance for dental treatment.

Mr. Denton has now come to you for a scheduled appointment for multiple tooth extractions with medical clearance.

# Teaching Points: [Instructor Version Only]

1. Angina (stable vs. unstable) physiology.

2. Predisposing factors – CAD due to atheroslcerosis, dysrhythmias.

3. Angina can be precipitated by exertion, stress, extreme environmental changes, large meals, stimulant ingestion, fever, smoking, smog, altitude.

4. Signs/symptoms:

Dull, aching, substernal chest pain Pain radiating laterally to jaw, shoulders, arms, and abdomen

5. Emergency management – [No technical training is given at this time]

Assess and protect airway Keep patient in a semi-reclined position if tolerated Immediately provide supplemental oxygen, if available Assess vital signs Consider administering nitroglycerine to the patient Reduce precipitating factors that may be contributing to the onset of pain If stabilized: use gauze to slow blood, suture wound closed & transfer to oral surgeon. With delayed recovery, call 911

6. Prevention – Angina is often triggered by stress or exertion. Consider length of appointment, pain control during treatment, and consider using psychosedation (based on medical assessment). Consider administering patient prescribed nitroglycerin prophylactically prior to start of procedure. Consider referring to oral surgeon initially.
