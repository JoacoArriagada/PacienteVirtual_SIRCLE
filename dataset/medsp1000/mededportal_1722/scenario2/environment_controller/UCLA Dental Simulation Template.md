# IV. Environment

These case based simulations used a Medical Education Technologies Inc. (METI) Emergency Care Simulator (ECS) human patient simulator (HPS).

B. Set Up for Small Group Laboratory: The HPS was set up in the middle of the physiology lab (500 ft2) which can accommodate up to 25 students per session. The room was equipped with surgical lamps which were focused upon the HPS. There

were an adequate number of stools for student seating near the HPS. The monitor display was projected on an adjacent screen visible to all students. All students and instructors wore white laboratory coats. Other equipment and supplies were located adjacent to the HPS. These included a stethoscope, dental syringe packed in sterilized pouch and a defibrillator.

# B. Laboratory Cases:

The entire case narrative is read at once for scenario #2. For both scenarios, the table of values is filled in during the case. At the conclusion of the simulation sequence, the patient has recovered and presents with normal values.

# Scenario #2: Angina Pectoris

Mr. Denton is a 68 year-old Caucasian male. He is a retired middle school teacher and now leads a sedentary lifestyle, living at home with his wife.

Mr. Denton has a history of heart failure secondary to CAD (coronary artery disease) and high cholesterol. He was a smoker for over 30 years, but quit about 14 years ago. He is rated as ASA-II (can walk 1 flight of steps and two level blocks before becoming winded). He currently is taking $\beta$ -blockers, diuretics, and $\alpha$ -blockers for his condition. He is regularly taking aspirin, but stopped taking it 4 days prior to today’s appointment. He also has a prescription for Nitroglycerin to take as needed. You consulted Mr. Denton two weeks ago and referred him to his internist to get a medical clearance for dental treatment.

Mr. Denton has now come to you for a scheduled appointment for multiple tooth extractions with medical clearance.

# Objective:

T: $3 7 . 0 ^ { \mathrm { { \circ } C } }$ BP: 118/72 HR: 68 RR: 18

General:

Oral exam: General tooth decay, tooth mobility, upper and lower.

CVS: \*Will assess in lab.

Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally, no wheezing, rales, rhonchi.

Abdomen: Normal bowel sounds, soft, non-tender, no masses.

Extremities: no cyanosis or edema.

# Case Narrative:

Given the patient’s history, you, as a good UCLA graduate dentist, sent Mr. Denton to his internist to get cleared for dental treatment. Now that he has been cleared, you are ready to begin with his multiple extractions.

Mr. Denton is now under local anesthesia and you are extracting the first tooth. As you proceed, Mr. Denton begins to complain of moderate substernal chest pain which radiates to his jaw. An ECG shows PVC’s (and ST segment changes). If nitro is given, there is resolution of pain. Now you must decide what to do about the oral bleeding and partially extracted tooth.
