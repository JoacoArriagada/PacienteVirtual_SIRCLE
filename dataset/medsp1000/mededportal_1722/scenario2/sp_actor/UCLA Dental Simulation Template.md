# Scenario #2: Angina Pectoris

Mr. Denton is a 68 year-old Caucasian male. He is a retired middle school teacher and now leads a sedentary lifestyle, living at home with his wife.

Mr. Denton has a history of heart failure secondary to CAD (coronary artery disease) and high cholesterol. He was a smoker for over 30 years, but quit about 14 years ago. He is rated as ASA-II (can walk 1 flight of steps and two level blocks before becoming winded). He currently is taking $\beta$ -blockers, diuretics, and $\alpha$ -blockers for his condition. He is regularly taking aspirin, but stopped taking it 4 days prior to today’s appointment. He also has a prescription for Nitroglycerin to take as needed. You consulted Mr. Denton two weeks ago and referred him to his internist to get a medical clearance for dental treatment.

Mr. Denton has now come to you for a scheduled appointment for multiple tooth extractions with medical clearance.

# Case Narrative:

Given the patient’s history, you, as a good UCLA graduate dentist, sent Mr. Denton to his internist to get cleared for dental treatment. Now that he has been cleared, you are ready to begin with his multiple extractions.

Mr. Denton is now under local anesthesia and you are extracting the first tooth. As you proceed, Mr. Denton begins to complain of moderate substernal chest pain which radiates to his jaw. If nitro is given, there is resolution of pain.
