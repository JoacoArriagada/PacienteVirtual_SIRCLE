| MANAGEMENT OR DIAGNOSITIC PLAN | Diagnostic testing should include a lumbar puncture with routine cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) studies (CSF white blood cell count and differential; CSF gram stain; CSF glucose; CSF protein). Enterovirus and herpes simplex virus PCR testing of the CSF should be performed to look for these viruses (the former, because it is a common cause of meningoencephalitis in the summer months, and the latter, because it is potentially treatable), and empiric treatment with IV acyclovir (for HSV) should be considered.  Meningoencephalitis viral PCR panels are available and may be ordered to attempt to identify the etiological agent, though doing so may not alter the patient’s management, as most of these viruses only benefit from supportive care.  If West Nile virus in particular is suspected, testing should include serum and/or CSF testing for West Nile IgM for confirmation.  If rickettsial disease is suspected, empiric treatment with doxycycline should be initiated, due to the high mortality rate of these conditions (serological testing is not often performed, as it is often falsely negative early in the clinical course of these diseases). |
