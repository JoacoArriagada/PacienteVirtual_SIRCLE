# Simulation Interest Group Scenario Template

I. Title: Acute Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)

II. Target Audience: medical students, junior residents

III. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives

A. Primary Demonstrate appropriate assessment of patient from a potentially critically ill stand point (ABCs) Demonstrate recognition of CHF Vocalize potential causes of CHF Demonstrate appropriate management of CHF
B. Secondary –recognize historical cues that increase likelihood of diagnosis of CHF
C. Critical actions checklist:

1. Perform an assessment utilizing the ABC method administer oxygen establish IV access raise head of bed initiate appropriate pharmacological management of CHF

2 Optimal sequence of critical actions (performance in order $=$ higher scores in certain management areas) check patient, check airway, check breathing, recognize crackles, initiate O2, assess cardiac status including blood pressure and heart rate, initiate IV access, administer appropriate medication(s).

3 Duration to critical actions: assessment to recheck of patient after appropriate interventions within 10 minutes.

IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – Emergency Department
B. Manikin Set Up – high fidelity manikin (SimMan), moulage of lower extremity edema (can be a stocking filled with cotton or other soft, compressible material), IV line for IV access, syringe for administering IV medications
C. Props – a chest x-ray demonstrating cardiomegaly and pulmonary edema, an ECGs showing left ventricular enlargement, basic airway equipment and crash cart.
D. Distractors – none

V. Actors

A. Roles – facilitator doubles as bedside nurse to carry out physicians’ orders

VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants at initiation 1. Chief complaint: Shortness of breath

Triage note: This is a 69 year old female, brought in by daughter with complaint of shortness of breath. It started last night at a birthday party, and worsened throughout the night. Patient unable to sleep lying down. Patient can only speak in short phrases due to shortness of breath.

Past medical history: Significant for hypertension and high cholesterol Meds and allergies: Taking “Procardia” and lovastatin, no known drug allergies Family/social history: non contributory

B. Scenario conditions initially

1 History patient gives: unable to provide further history due to language barrier 2 Patients initial exam: BP 160/90, HR 110 and regular (normal sinus rhythm), RR 35, temperature: afebrile, $S a0 2 8 9 \%$ on room air, lung sounds crackles at moderate volume, 3 Patients physiology: mild to moderate respiratory distress

C. Scenario branch points

1 Changes in patient’s condition: This is a rather static case, After students arrive at the correct diagnosis, they are asked to cite causes of CHF, then appropriate management, including pharmacology and adjuncts, including respiratory support (BiPAP and CPAP). After supplying oxygen, a trend to increase saturations by $5 \%$ over 30 seconds is initiated.

2 Responses to treatments: After successful demonstration of knowledge required to manage this case, the scenario changes to 4 hours later, checking on the patient who has since been admitted to the telemetry unit, and has received appropriate therapy. At this time, the patient is much more comfortable, BP is 125/85, Heart rate is 75, RR is 18, Sa02 is $98 \%$ on 2 liters via nasal cannula, and lung exam shows low volume crackles at bases.

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer: Facilitator may go around the group sequentially, each participant stating a potential etiological possibility for CHF, then the order is reversed, asking for possible pharmacological and other adjunctive therapies. When the facilitator is satisfied the group has met this goal, the case advances to post admission state, and participants assess the changes.

1. Optimal management path: demonstrating knowledge of pharmacological and other treatment modalities for CHF
2. Potential errors path(s): not arriving at correct diagnosis

IX. Pilot Testing and Revisions

A. Numbers of participants ideally groups of 2-4 participants.
B. Performance expectations, anticipated management mistakes: It is expected groups will make correct diagnosis with little or no prompting. Facilitator can probe group asking for less common etiologies of CHF. Groups should know commonly used medication and adjuncts (avoid beta blockers in acute CHF)
