# Simulation Interest Group Scenario Template

I. Title: Status Asthmaticus

IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – Emergency Department
B. Manikin Set Up – high fidelity manikin (SimMan), nebulizer, oxygen mask, IV line for IV access, syringe for administering IV medications, medications including nebulized Beta 2 agonists, steroids, magnesium, Endotracheal tube intubation equipment.
C. Props – none other basic airway and crash cart
D. Distractors – none

V. Actors

A. Roles – bedside facilitator who also doubles as nurse to carry out physicians’ orders

VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants at initiation: 1 Chief complaint: shortness of breath

Triage note: This is a 16 y/o male with a Past Medical History of asthma, who ran out of his inhalers la week. Complaining of 2 days of shortness of breath. Has had previous hospitalizations for asthma wh "much younger." No other significant Past Medical History, and otherwise in good health.

2 Past medical history: see triage note
3 Meds and allergies: No known drug Allergies, medication: “inhalers”
4 Family/social history: non contributory

B. Scenario conditions initially

1 History patient gives; unable to provide additional information due to extreme dyspnea 2 Patients initial exam: BP 160/90, HR 145 normal sinus rhythm, RR 48, temperature afebrile, SaO2 $85 \%$ on room air, lung sounds wheezes at low volume 2-3 on an 9 point scale 3 Patient’s physiology: extreme respiratory distress.

C. Scenario branch points

1 Changes in patients condition: after application of oxygen, O2 saturations improve to $98 \%$ over 20 seconds, after application of initial beta 2 agonist therapy, patient’s lung sounds change to wheezes at volume of 4 on a 9 point scale (slightly louder than initially).
2 Responses to treatments: After second beta agonist nebulizer, patient’s breath sound increase to wheezes at maximum setting, over next two minutes: respiratory rate decreases to 28, heart increases by 14 beat per minutes, Blood pressure drops to 140/85, and two minutes after initiation of second nebulized treatment, patient announces “I feel better now”.

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer: May need to reinforce the concepts of ABC process, depending on level of participants, may need to prompt reassessing after an intervention (“Have breath sounds improved after treatment?”)

Potential complications path(s): none other than lack of improvement if not aggressive in therapy.
