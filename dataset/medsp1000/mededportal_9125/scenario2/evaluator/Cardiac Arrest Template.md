# Simulation Interest Group Scenario Template

I. Title: Cardiac Arrest

II. Target Audience: medical students, junior residents

III. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives

A. Primary Recognize an unstable patient Demonstrate the skills necessary to assess stability in a potentially unstable patient Demonstrate recognition and proper management of an unstable arrhythmia Demonstrate appropriate technique for assessing stability of a post resuscitation patient
B. Secondary Demonstrate the proper safety techniques while cardioverting/defibrillating Demonstrate proper team communication skills, including closed loop communication, clear concise orders
C. Critical actions checklist: 1. Properly assessing stability of a potentially malignant arrhythmia Checking for a pulse in ventricular tachycardia 2 Optimal sequence of critical actions (performance in order $=$ higher scores in certain management areas) Check patient, check for a pulse, perform cardioversion/defibrillation if indicated 3 Duration to critical actions: Recognizing arrhythmia within 15 seconds of onset, checking patient with 10 seconds of recognizing arrhythmia, assessing stability within 30 seconds of recognizing arrhythmia, performing cardioversion/fibrillation with one minute of arrhythmia onset

IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – Telemetry Department, Hospital Ward
B. Manikin Set Up – high fidelity manikin (SimMan), oxygen mask, bag-valve- mask, IV line for IV access, syringe for administering IV medications, typical crash cart
C. Props – none other basic airway and crash cart
D. Distractors – none

V. Actors

A. Roles – bedside facilitator who also doubles as nurse

VI. Case Narrative

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants at initiation

Case Preamble: This patient is a 63 year old male admitted into the hospital earlier today with an episode of chest pain. The nurse calls you to assess Mr. Nakamura because he says his chest pain has returned.

1 Past medical history: Hypertension
2 Meds and allergies ACE inhibitor, no known drug allergies
3 Family/social history non contributory

B. Scenario conditions initially

1 History patient gives: central substernal pressure-like chest pain for last 5 minutes
2 Patients initial exam: Awake, alert male in no apparent distress, Blood pressure 120/80. Heart rate 80 (normal sinus rhythm), respiratory rate 12, afebrile, pulse oxymetry at $98 \%$ on 2 liters oxygen via nasal cannula, lungs clear, heart with regular rate and rhythm with no murmurs, all pulse present and normal.
3 Patients physiology: normal at this time

C. Scenario branch points

1 Changes in patient’s condition: at one minute of scenario, patient goes into ventricular tachycardia at 180 bpm, becomes unresponsive, pulses weak, BP 70/50,RR of 12, Sa02 of $85 \%$ , patient remains in this condition until cardioversion or defibrillation, at which point goes into ventricular fibrillation, remains in this condition until defibrillation, at which point goes into asystole. Patient stay in this condition until 2 minutes after chest compressions are performed, at which time patient goes into PEA of normal sinus rhythm at rate of 85, with frequent PVCs. Patient stays in this condition until administration of inotropic agent (epinephirine or dopamine), 2 minutes after administration of these medications, patient has return of circulation, sinus rhythm of 120, Sa02 of $98 \%$ , pulses present, RR of 16, BP 120/80, case ends.

2 Responses to treatments: as above

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer: Students may require prompts, such as “How do you assess stability in an unresponsive patient?”, or “What is the correct treatment for this arrhythmia?” 1. Optimal management path: Assess responsiveness and stability in a patient with ventricular tachycardia, perform synchronized cardioversion, recognized ventricular fibrillation, perform defibrillation, recognize asystole, and reassess including checking patient and checking leads, perform chest compressions, recognize PEA, administer inotropes, reassess patient after resumption of spontaneous circulation. 2. potential complications path(s): none except lack of improvement in not managed properly 3. potential errors path(s): most common was performing chest compressions instead of defibrillation

IX. Pilot Testing and Revisions

A. Numbers of participants: ideally groups of 2-4 participants B. Performance expectations, anticipated management mistakes: This case requires the management of 4 causes of cardiac arrest, and requires the management skills to manage each of these entities.
