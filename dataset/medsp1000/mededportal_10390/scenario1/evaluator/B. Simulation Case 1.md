**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*As the students enter the room, they find the patient coughing intermittently, but in no apparent distress. The students verbalize for the nurse (the preceptor in the simulation control room would respond as the nurse) to connect the patient to the heart monitor and obtain a full set of vital signs. Upon identification of hypoxemia along with the complaint of being short of breath, the patient should be placed on supplemental oxygen (2 L NC). If the patient is placed on a high flow oxygen face mask, that is also acceptable; after a few minutes the patient can report that they are breathing easier and ask whether the students can try oxygen through the nose because they do not like the mask on their face. The students should continue to perform a focused H&P. A physical examination would reveal minimal accessory muscle use and rales on the right side. A chest radiograph is ordered, and it demonstrates a right lower lobe infiltrate. If laboratory studies are obtained: CBC – WBC 14.5, 76% segs, 5% bands, 18% lymphocytes, and a BMP – normal. ABG results: not performed/not available. Oxygen saturation 91% on room air increases to 97% on 2 L NC.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. *Difficulty with simulation: This exercise is designed for the pre-clinical student. Although some students at this stage have already experiences simulated patient encounters, most are still relatively new to this experience. Because of this, most students have difficulty multitasking and more often perform the evaluation and management in series. Meaning that they will perform many aspects of the history before they move on to the physical examination. It is less common for students at this early stage to recognize the need to apply supplemental oxygen to a patient who is hypoxemic and short of breath. At the conclusion of the experience, the sequence and approach to this type of case can be discussed.*
2. *Difficulty with examining the simulator: At times, we find that some students have difficulty identifying rales when listening to the simulator. This may at times be a limitation of the simulator. If the students report that they auscultate clear lungs, they are informed that the patient has rales. Other aspects of the physical examination will be provided when asked (skin exam, extremity exam, etc.).*
3. *Difficulty interpreting the chest radiograph: Students at this stage of their training have had an introduction to chest radiography; however they are still becoming familiar with radiographic terminology. Students will at times need assistance when describing the chest radiograph along with incorporating medical terminology.*
