**HIV: A Socio-Ecological Case Study**

Pre-Session Overview

**KEY PRINCIPLE**: The complex lives of our vulnerable patients demand equally complex strategies for their care.

**Objectives**:

1. Apply the socioecological model to persons with HIV/AIDS.
2. Discuss issues of cultural bias with marginalized patient populations (e.g. LGBT).
3. Identify approaches at the clinical, community, and policy levels that clinicians can use to help care and advocate for their patients.

**1. Setting the stage**

Since the recognition of the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) as the cause of acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) in the early 1980’s there have been many medical advances in the United States in the ability to diagnosis and treat those infected with HIV. However, despite these medical advances, 50,000 Americans each year continue to be infected with this virus. In addition, since more Americans have access to effective treatment, they are living longer, and the increasing number of persons with chronic HIV infection requires effective long-term coordinated chronic disease prevention and management. Continued work is needed on individual, community, and public policy levels to address the continued epidemic of HIV and chronic management of the complex psychosocial and medical needs of this vulnerable population. A socio-ecological model can help guide our thinking about how to address a formidable challenge of our time.

**2. Small group and large group session**

In today’s session, we turn our attention to a complex public health challenge that is no less a part of the current medical landscape: HIV/AIDS. How do we care for patients who may be marginalized or stigmatized, and who may receive fragmented care in our health system? How do race, ethnicity, income, and sexual orientation affect access to care? What are the barriers to HIV prevention, and why do they pose such an intractable challenge? What can we do as physicians to mitigate the effects of the virus in our communities?

For the first hour, you will gather in small groups to examine a case study. We will then convene as a large group – please sit together with your small group in the lecture hall.
