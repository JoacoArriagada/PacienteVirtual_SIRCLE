**Difficult Learning Behaviors Debriefing Guide**

**How do I deal with difficult learning behaviors *after* the session?**

*Take home point:*  In order to make meaningful change to the learner’s behavior, you need to figure out what the root of the problem is (aka. “Discover the learner’s frame” - figure out what their underlying thought process or feeling is that is driving this behavior).

1. **Conversation framework**
   1. The SOAP process
      1. S: subjective (how did you feel?) – annoyed, disappointed, angry (“I felt disrespected when…”)
      2. O: objective - what exactly did they do that made you feel that way?  (focus on specific actions, behaviors, such as “I noticed you interrupted me 5 times during that session”)
      3. A: assessment - what could be driving their undesired behavior? (“what was the reason for the many interruptions today?”) - you will not know the reason until you ask – many times the true reason for their behavior may surprise you!
      4. P: plan – make a plan together for improvement going forward (use implementation intentions, described below). Essential to get their input and buy-in on this, as solutions won’t work if they are not on board.
2. **Having the conversation**
   1. Establishing Educational Alliance
      1. Begin with “I’m on your side.” Emphasize motivation to help them improve. Often, this starts well before the conversation begins and extends to your entire relationship with the resident
      2. Do they see you as someone who has their best interests at heart?
   2. The advocacy - inquiry approach.
      1. Advocacy is stating something that you noticed. This is the “O” from the SOAP Process.
      2. Inquiry is telling the learner your concerns about it and asking for their thoughts.
         1. “I noticed that you were very eager to share your thoughts during the conference session today.  I do appreciate your willingness to participate, however it seemed that you were interrupting multiple times in order to do this.  I am concerned that asking that many questions may not be well received by speakers.  Tell me about what you were thinking about today when you were asking questions.”
   3. Discover the learner’s frames (this is the “A” from SOAP - why did they do what they did?)
      1. Be inquisitive. Either from a single advocacy inquiry statement or may require more “digging” to actually “get to the root of” why it is happening.
      2. “I am interested to hear more about why you feel like this.” You may be (very!) surprised by the answer!
   4. Collaborate to improve.
      1. Co-create a plan for future improvement.  Have the learner first suggest ways they think they could improve their behavior. Modify the plan as needed.  Learners are more likely to be successful if they came up with the solution.
      2. Use accountability and implementation intentions.
         1. Implementation intention is a self-regulatory [strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategy) in the form of an "[if-then](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indicative_conditional) plan" that can lead to better [goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal) attainment, as well as help in habit and [behavior modification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavior_modification).
         2. “Next time I want to interrupt, I will write down what I want to say and wait for an appropriate time to ask / say it”.

**Specific Cases**

1. **When there are gaps in medical knowledge (Learner Scenario 1, Appendix C)**
   1. Assessment. Clarify if resident truly is at the level perceived (are they covering for deficits?).
      1. Find out what they are currently doing to learn emergency medicine
      2. Emphasize the difference between clinical knowledge and boards/In-training exam knowledge (can be strong in one area and not the other)
   2. Collaborate to improve.
      1. Co-create a study plan.
         1. Have the learner first suggest ways they think they could improve their medical knowledge.
         2. Be specific. Have the learner pick a reading material and include chapters to cover.
         3. Create a deadline. Use a calendar to outline curriculum keeping in mind the rotations of the learner.
         4. Modify the plan as needed.  Learners are more likely to be successful if they came up with the solution.
         5. Accountability. Schedule time for check-ins and commit to placing in calendar.
         6. Evaluation. Use various modalities for assessment. (e.g. Rosh review, question banks, etc.)
         7. Celebrate mini-successes.
   3. Optimize for success.
      1. Testing effect/retrieval practice (for those already reading/listening to podcasts/etc.
         1. Creating flashcards then testing themselves
         2. Creating mini-lectures/1-minute presentations and encourage teaching junior learners.

**A Note On Reporting**

1. Do you tell the program director or CCC chair about difficult behavior in your didactic?
   1. In general, the answer should be “yes”.
   2. The PD (and maybe the CCC) need to know if patterns of behavior are emerging for any one resident - they are uniquely positioned to have this perspective.
      1. If this is an isolated incident, they will likely not do anything further with the information.
      2. If this is a pattern of behavior, or is an egregious act, the resident may need some more dedicated coaching from the program.
   3. If it is a personal issue (sick family member, etc), I encourage the learner to tell their PD, and offer to support them in any way they would like.
