| **Patient Name:** | Gregory Stewart |
| **Gender/Age:** | 82 year old man |
| **Goals for physician learner (fellow):** | * **Assess the patient’s thinking** using history taking and cognitive examination tools * **Determine the patient’s function** using geriatric screening tools * **Discuss treatment options and provide a recommendation** based upon the overall situation of the patient and his goals * **Determine the patient’s understanding** of the risks and benefits of treatment choices. |
| **Brief Summary:** | Mr. Stewart is an 82 year old man with a known history of hypertension, coronary artery disease (myocardial infarction/ “heart attack” at age 76 and history of 1 vessel coronary artery bypass surgery), atrial fibrillation (on Coumadin), diabetes type 2 (associated to nerve damage with resulting foot and leg pain, and chronic kidney disease), and non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma).   * His lung cancer has spread to the lymph nodes in his chest and the fluid around the lungs. * He has previously received chemotherapy with carboplatin and paclitaxel with improvement in his symptoms. Recently however, he was more tired and when he went to the doctor a restaging CT scan showed that the tumor has now spread to his liver. * He came to clinic with his daughter today to discuss possible second line treatment options. * The doctor may suggest: Pemetrexed (Alimta)—stronger chemotherapy   Erlotinib (Tarceva)—milder chemotherapy  Palliative care &/or hospice care  Mr. Stewart is a retired English teacher. Until recently, he was living alone in an independent senior apartment after his wife’s death one year ago. A few months ago he moved in with his daughter and her family due to difficulties taking his medications and remembering doctor appointments.  Mr. Stewart’s family reported that he was missing doses of medications at least 2-3 times per week, and had recurrent problems with hypoglycemia and insulin administration. They had noticed that he has been repeating statements and occasionally forgetting events and conversations. Currently, his daughter has been helping setting up a pill box and reminds him to take them on time. She is also responsible for managing his check book and medical appointments. |
| **Key “Findings” for the SP Patient to Portray:**  (We will go over this section with more detailed explanation at the SP training session) | **Patient demeanor:**   * Sitting in a chair next to the daughter * Appears tired, but has a pleasant attitude * Alert, maintaining appropriate eye contact, normal mood * When asked questions, he looks to his daughter for confirmation, and when she gives an answer about his health, he gets surprised saying things like “did that happen to me?” * When he is questioned regarding memory loss, he denies noticing any problems and states that he was an English teacher and never has problems with his memory. His daughter shakes her head “no”, letting the doctor know that she does not agree with his answer. * **Cognitive testing:**   + MoCA: 18/30     - Visuospatial/executive: unable to complete mini-trail B and put the arms of the clock in the right time (placed in 10 and 11)     - Language: unable to list 11 words that begin with the letter F (able to list only 4)     - Abstraction: concrete thinking. Answered “they have tires” and “they have numbers”     - Delayed recall: remembered 2 of 5 words     - Orientation: disoriented in time, but able to answer place and city   + Mini-Cog: abnormal     - Delayed recall: able to recall 1 word     - Clock draw test: able to draw a contour and put numbers in, but not the arms in the right place (placed them in 10 and 11)   **Patient/physician interaction content:**   * Patient states not having memory problems, but does make errors with cognitive testing * Patient reports not having any problems performing daily tasks ADLs and IADLs.   + ADLs (Activities of Daily Living: bathing, dressing, walking, eating, toileting)   + IADLs (Instrumental Activities of Daily Living: bill paying, pill taking, driving, cooking, laundry, phone use) * His daughter clarifies that he has difficulty with most IADLs (bills, pills, driving, cooking, laundry), but is able to use the phone. * Patient’s daughter agrees that he has no difficulty with ADLs.   **Patient goals of care:**  When the physician summarizes the patient’s current medical condition and initiates a discussion of goals of care and treatment options, he appears to pay attention to the conversation, but when questioned, he is not able to demonstrate understanding of his medical problems or possible plan of care.  He states his goal is to continue an active life, being able to participate in family activities and to “go for walk”. He is not able to provide a logical explanation regarding what is his perception of a “good quality of life”  When questioned, he is not able to make a choice regarding the plan of care, stating “do whatever you think is best”. |
| **Key “Findings” for SP daughter to Portray:** | **Background history**  **Father’s cognition:**   * She started noticing gradual presentation of memory loss (repeating himself or forgetting conversations) approximately 1-2 years before this visit * For the past year he is having more difficulties solving problems, including managing his medications and his check book * She has not noticed any symptoms suggesting depression or increased sadness * Her father occasionally complains of shortness of breath with activity, but when later forgets this symptom and when asked about it later he tells her that he is “not having any problems” (he is, in fact, forgetting his complaints)   **Father’s function/social situation:**   * **IADLs (Instrumental Activities of Daily Living):**   + Ability to use the telephone: Able to answer and use auto-dial, but has difficulty remembering numbers he used to know easily   + Shopping: Needs to be accompanied because he is not sure what he was trying to buy   + Food preparation: Heats and serves prepared meals (from microwave)   + Housekeeping: Performs light daily tasks with cues (dishwasher, bed making)   + Laundry: Needs assistance   + Transportation: Needs assistance   + Medication management: Needs reminders to take them and assistance organizing his pill-box   + Finances: He often manages day-to-day purchases if his daughter is with him, but needs help with banking and major purchases. * **ADLs (Activities of Daily Living):**   + Toileting: Independent   + Feeding: Independent   + Dressing: With minor assistance in lower body (socks and shoes) due to decreased mobility associated to lower back and hip arthritis   + Grooming: Independent   + Ambulation: Uses a cane without assistance   + Bathing: able to complete, but needs assistance to get in and out the tub |
