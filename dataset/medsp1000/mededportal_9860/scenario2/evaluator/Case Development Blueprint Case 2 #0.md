# Oncology/Geriatrics OSCE #2

# Case Development Blueprint

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Presenting Complaint:** | “I have been feeling tired” |
| **Gender and Age:** | 82 year old man |
| **Patient Name:** | Gregory Stewart |
| **Brief Summary:** | Mr. Stewart is an 82 year old man with known history of hypertension, coronary artery disease (AMI at age 76 and history of 1 vessel CABG), atrial fibrillation (on Coumadin), diabetes type 2 (associated to neuropathy and chronic kidney disease stage 3), and non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma).   * His adenocarcinoma is metastatic to the mediastinal lymph nodes, he also was found to have a metastatic plural effusion. * Past treatment includes: 6 cycles of Carboplatin and Taxol with good initial response. Subsequent staging CT scans show disease progression with a new solitary liver mass. * He presents today to the oncology ambulatory clinic with his daughter to discuss potential next therapies including Pemetrexed (Alimta), Erlotinib (Tarceva), or best supportive care (palliative care &/or * hospice care).   Mr. Stewart is a retired English teacher. His wife died one year ago, and since that time, he had been living alone in an independent senior apartment . He recently relocated to live with his daughter and her family, as he was having more difficulty taking his medications and remembering doctor appointments.  His family reported that Mr. Stewart was missing doses of medications at least 2-3 times per week, and had recurrent problems with hypoglycemia and insulin administration. Currently, her daughter has been helping setting up a pill box and reminds him to take them on time. She is also responsible for managing his check book and medical appointments. |
| **Case Objectives:** | * **Assess the patient’s cognition** using history taking and cognitive examination tools   + Take a cognitive history from the patient and daughter   + Mini-cog screening tool, MoCA * **Determine the patient’s functional status** using geriatric screening tools   + Take a functional history from the patient and daughter   + Barthel scale, ADLs/IADLs   + Gait and Balance exam/evaluation * **Discuss treatment options and provide a recommendation** based upon the overall assessment of the patient. * **Determine the patient’s understanding** of the risks and benefits of each option:   + Full chemotherapy, less intense chemotherapy regimen, supportive care |
| **Key “Findings” for SP patient to Portray:** | **Patient demeanor:**   * Sitting in a chair next to the daughter * Appears tired, but has a pleasant attitude * Alert, maintaining appropriate eye contact, normal mood and affect * When asked questions, he looks to his daughter for confirmation, and when she gives a report of his condition, he gets surprised saying things like “did that happen to me?” * When he is questioned regarding memory loss, he denies noticing any problems and states that he was an English teacher and never had problems with his memory. His daughter shakes her head “no” – indicating that this is not accurate. * Cognitive testing:   + Mini-Cog: abnormal     - Delayed recall: able to recall 1 word     - Clock draw test: able to draw a contour and put numbers in, but not the arms (placed in 10 and 11)   + MoCA: 18/30     - Visuospatial/executive: unable to complete mini-trail B and put the arms of the clock in the right time (placed in 10 and 11)     - Language: unable to list 11 words that begin with the letter F (able to list only 4)     - Abstraction: concrete thinking. Answered “they have tires” and “they have numbers”     - Delayed recall: remembered 2 of 5 words     - Orientation: disoriented in time, but able to answer place and city   **Patient/physician interaction content:**   * Patient denies having memory problems, but does make errors with cognitive testing * Patient reports not having any problems performing daily tasks ADLs and IADLs. * His daughter clarifies that he has difficulty with most IADLs (bills, pills, driving, cooking, laundry), but is able to use the phone. * Patient’s daughter agrees that he has no difficulty with ADLs.   **Patient goals of care:**  When the physician summarizes the patient’s current medical condition and initiates a discussion of goals of care and treatment options, the patient appears to pay attention to the conversation, but when questioned, he is not able to demonstrate understanding of his medical problems or possible plan of care.  He states his goal is to continue an active life, participating in family activities and to go “out for a walk”. He is not able to provide a logical explanation regarding what is his perception of a “good quality of life”  When questioned, he is not able to make a choice regarding the plan of care, stating “just do whatever you think is best”. |
| **Key Teaching Points of Case:** | * **Assess the patient’s cognition** using history taking and cognitive examination tools * **Determine the patient’s functional status** using geriatric screening tools * **Discuss treatment options and provide a recommendation** based upon the overall assessment of the patient. * **Determine the patient’s understanding** of the risks and benefits of each option. |
| **Exam Room Needs:** | Examination room  Chair or examining table for patient  Chair next to bedside or by patient’s chair for SP daughter  Chair for physician  Door sign with instructions for fellow  Pen  Paper for writing or note taking (if desired) |
| **Activities & Time Req:** | 20 minute session = 17 minute SP session, 3 minute SP debrief  If 2 fellows, 1st fellow is in the physician role, 2nd fellow observes/offers insights |
