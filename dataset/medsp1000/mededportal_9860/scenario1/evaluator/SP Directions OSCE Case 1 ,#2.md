| **Patient Name:** | Sarah Boland |
| **Gender/Age:** | 80 year old woman |
| **Goals for physician learner (fellow):** | * **Assess the patient’s thinking** using history taking and cognitive examination tools * **Determine the patient’s function** using geriatric screening tools * **Discuss treatment options and provide a recommendation** based upon the overall situation of the patient and her goals * **Determine the patient’s understanding** of the risks and benefits of treatment choices. |
| **Brief Summary:** | Mrs. Boland is an 80 year old woman with non-small cell lung cancer. She also has quite severe Parkinson’s disease, osteoporosis, and arthritis of the hips and knees.   * Her lung cancer has spread to the lymph nodes in her chest and the fluid around the lungs. * She has previously received chemotherapy with carboplatin and paclitaxel with improvement in her symptoms. Recently however, she was more tired and when she went to the doctor a restaging CT scan showed that the tumor has now spread to her liver; EGFR and ALK4 mutations were not identified. (in case the doctor asks, you can say that the doctors were checking some genetics of your cancer but it didn’t show anything) * She came to clinic with her daughter today to discuss possible second line treatment options. * The doctor may suggest: Pemetrexed (Alimta)—stronger chemotherapy   Erlotinib (Tarceva)—milder chemotherapy  Palliative care &/or hospice care  Mrs. Boland was hospitalized six months ago for treatment of pneumonia and she was treated in the emergency department two months ago for a fall that caused a dislocated shoulder. She has had more difficulty managing her activities over the past year. She needs help with bathing and dressing and occasionally needs help getting up from a chair. She is sometimes unsteady when she walks, but she doesn’t want to use a walker, and will only use her cane. Her Parkinson’s disease causes difficulties with tremors, and walking and balance problems. Her arthritis causes her pain when she moves and also makes it difficult to take care of herself and do those things she wants to do (she has shoulder, hip, knee, hand pain). She now moved in with her daughter to “make things easier”.  Her lung cancer and the treatments, in addition to her other comorbidities, have made her weak and tired. She has had more trouble with “getting things done” during the day, and needs to rely on others more than in the past. |
| **Key “Findings” for the SP Patient to Portray:**  (We will demonstrate these findings to you at the SP training session). | **Patient demeanor:**   * Sitting in a chair next to the daughter * Tired, and appears “worn out” * Alert, thinking is good, she is tired and her face does not show much expression. She has a resting, pill-rolling type tremor in one hand, and her movements are slow and seem stiff * She does not miss any answers in the Mini-cog screening or the MoCA cognitive testing. * Her voice is quiet, some words are mumbled together, as speech at the end of the sentence sometimes gets mumbled and is quieter in volume. * She has difficulty getting up from a chair and uses her arms to push herself up with the chair armrests to raise herself to a standing position. She leans over, and has some difficulty with balance on walking, and walks with short, shuffling gait, looking at the floor.   **Patient/physician interaction content:**   * She has no memory problems and does not make any mistakes on the memory testing. She has no difficulty with paying bills, taking pills, or using the phone, but does need some help with laundry (steps), cooking (tremor and hard to stand), and driving (tremor, slow movements) * She has difficulty with walking and balance, getting dressed and taking a shower. She is able to feed herself and use the toilet --occasional “accidents” if she can’t get to the toilet in time (arthritis + slow movements).   **Patient goals of care:**  She feels tired. When the physician summarizes the patient’s current medical condition and initiates a discussion of goals of care and treatment options, she is able to follow the discussion and options offered. She understands the risks and benefits of those options. The last chemotherapy treatments were tiring for her, and while she feels that she is not “ready to quit”, she is dreading the thought of another intensive chemotherapy treatment course. The milder chemotherapy regimen sounds like an option she would like to try. She is willing to hear about how palliative care or hospice may benefit her, but she is not ready to “sign on” right away. |
| **Key “Findings” for SP daughter to Portray:** | **Background history**  **Mom’s cognition:**   * Mom’s thinking has been “pretty good for someone her age”. She has some occasional trouble with remembering where she has placed things and it sometimes takes her a while to remember people’s names. She was able to learn how to use the basic functions of an iPad, which was a birthday gift.   **Mom’s function/social situation:**   * **IADLs (Instrumental Activities of Daily Living):** After Mom’s hospitalization she moved in with her daughter, but Mom is able to manage most of her own life details. She manages her own bills and takes her own pills without much trouble. Occasionally she misses one, but this is rare. She keeps track of her medical appointments on a calendar. She needs some help with laundry (basement stairs are a problem), and with driving (arthritis, Parkinson’s disease) and cooking (hard to stand and cook, tremor). * **ADLs (Activities of Daily Living):** Mom needs some help with bathing and dressing, (tremor, slow movements, poor balance are problems) and she sometimes needs help getting out of the chair (arthritis and Parkinson’s disease). She walks with a cane (doesn’t want to use the walker), and the daughter likes to be nearby as she worries that her mom may lose her balance and fall. |

| **SP Patient Opening Statement:** | “I am feeling so awful; my cancer must have gotten worse.” |
| **Elaboration of Symptoms:**  (if asked by MD) | * **Tell me more about how you are feeling?**   “I am tired all the time. It is hard to get up and do things.”  “I am short of breath at times, especially with walking around.”   * **What other problems are you having?**   “Sometimes I’m coughing a lot. Nothing comes up, but it’s really uncomfortable.”   * **Do you have any chest pain?**   “Only sometimes when I am coughing a lot, often more at night.”   * **How is your appetite? Have you lost any weight?**   “I am not too hungry these days; I have lost about 20 lbs over the past 6 months. It was hard to cook—with my shaking and it’s hard to stand that long. It’s been better since living with my daughter—she cooks for me—but I really am not hungry.”   * **If other questions are asked about symptoms**, just answer with “No.” |
| **Memory Questions** | * **Have you had any trouble with your memory or thinking?**    + “No, I think my memory is pretty good and I don’t have any problems with that, thankfully!”   + If you are asked any memory questions (day, date, month, year, president, remember 3 objects, etc) Just answer all questions correctly. |
| **Function Questions** | * **Do you have any trouble with:**   + **Walking?** “Yes, I have trouble with my balance and I am slow in my walking. I am sometimes worried about falling, so I use a cane.”   + **Bathing? Dressing?** “Yes, I have trouble because I am tired and I have trouble with my shaking hands and my Parkinson’s disease makes it tough to move my arms around.”   + **Driving? Grocery shopping? Laundry?** “Yes, but my daughter is able to drive me around and she helps with the groceries and laundry. It’s hard to stand for a long time and the washing machine is also in the basement.”   + **Bill paying? Taking your medications? Using the phone?** “No”   + **Are you spending much time in bed?** “Some. I am able to get around with my cane and I don’t like to be in bed all the time. It makes me feel like I am sick—so I try to sit in my favorite chair.”   + **Overall function?** “I have really been having more trouble over the last half year or so. Things are just getting tough for me to manage.” |
| **Family Medical History** | * Father died from “old age” in his 80s * Mother died from pneumonia in her 80s |
| **Social History** | * Retired for many years, worked as a librarian * She is widowed and was living alone until the last few months. After her most recent fall a couple months ago she decided to move in with her daughter “just in case” * She is a former smoker * She doesn’t use alcohol |
| **Patient’s Goals & Wishes for Care/ Questions**  (if asked by the physician) | * **How do you feel you are doing?**   “I think I am not getting better. I think that this cancer is starting to grow. I am so tired and just feel like I have no energy.”   * **What would you like to happen with your health?**   “I really would like to be getting better— to have more time to be with my family and friends. But I don’t want to be constantly miserable from the treatments, though.” “ That was really difficult last time.”  **If the MD recommends Pemetrexed (Alimta**), (this is the more intense chemotherapy regimen): You can say, “I really don’t know if I can manage that. I am so tired and that just sounds like too much to go through.”   * **If the MD recommends Erlotinib (Tarceva)**, (this is the less intense chemotherapy regimen): You can agree that this may be the best option for you, as you feel tired and “the last chemotherapy treatments were exhausting.” * **If the MD recommends hospice care or palliative care**   You can agree that it might be something to think about, but “I’m not sure I’m ready to sign up just yet,” and “are you sure there isn’t something else you can do to give me more time?” |
