# Oncology/Geriatrics OSCE #1

# Case Development Blueprint

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Presenting Complaint:** | “I am feeling so awful; my cancer must have gotten worse.” |
| **Gender and Age:** | 80 year old woman |
| **Patient Name:** | Sarah Boland |
| **Brief Summary:** | Mrs. Boland is an 80 year old woman with non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma). Additional co-morbidities include Parkinson’s disease, hypertension, osteoporosis and severe osteoarthritis of the hips and knees.   * Her adenocarcinoma is metastatic to the mediastinal lymph nodes, she also was found to have a metastatic plural effusion. * Past treatment includes: 6 cycles of Carboplatin and Taxol with good initial response. Subsequent staging CT scans show disease progression with a new solitary liver mass; EGFR and ALK4 mutations were not identified. * She presents today with her daughter to discuss potential next therapies including Pemetrexed (Alimta), Erlotinib (Tarceva), or best supportive care (palliative care &/or hospice care)   The patient was hospitalized six months ago for treatment of aspiration pneumonia, and she was treated in the emergency department two months ago for a fall that resulted in a shoulder dislocation. She has shown gradual functional decline within the past year and while she is still able to manage her own IADLs, she needs assistance with her ADLs, including bathing, dressing, transferring and ambulation (declines a walker and stand-by assist, uses a cane instead) due to her arthritis pain, tremors, and gait and balance problems. She now moved in with her daughter to “make things easier”.  Her malignancy and subsequent treatments, combined with the aspiration pneumonia and fairly recent fall, in addition to her general baseline of Parkinson’s disease and arthritis, have made her weak and tired. She has had more trouble with “getting things done” during the day, and needs to rely on others more than in the past. |
| **Case Objectives:** | * **Assess the patient’s cognition** using history taking and cognitive examination tools   + Take a cognitive history from the patient and daughter   + Mini-cog screening tool, MoCA * **Determine the patient’s functional status** using geriatric screening tools   + Take a functional history from the patient and daughter   + Barthel scale, ADLs/IADLs   + Gait and Balance exam/evaluation * **Discuss treatment options and provide a recommendation** based upon the overall assessment of the patient. * **Determine the patient’s understanding** of the risks and benefits of each option:   + Full chemotherapy, less intense chemotherapy regimen, supportive care |
| **Key “Findings” for SP patient to Portray:** | **Patient demeanor:**   * Sitting in a chair next to the daughter * Tired, and appears “worn out” * Alert, cognition is intact, affect is flat, her face does not have much expression. Patient has a resting, pill-rolling type tremor in one hand, and her movements are slow and seem stiff. * Does not miss any answers in the Mini-cog screening or in the MoCA, if it is done * Voice is quiet, some words are mumbled together, as speech at the end of the sentence sometimes gets mumbled and is quieter in volume. * Difficulty rising from a chair needs to use hands to push herself to a standing position. Leans over, some difficulty with balance on walking, and walks with short, shuffling gait, looking at the floor.   **Patient/physician interaction content:**   * Patient has no memory problems and does not make errors with cognitive testing * Patient has no difficulty with most IADLs (bills, pills, phone) but does need assistance with laundry (steps), cooking (tremor and hard to stand), and driving (tremor, slow movements) * Patient has difficulty with most ADLs (ambulation, dressing, bathing) but is able to feed and toilet herself (occasional “accidents” if can’t get to the toilet in time (arthritis + slow movements).   **Patient goals of care:**  The patient feels tired and fatigued. When the physician summarizes the patient’s current medical condition and initiates a discussion of goals of care and treatment options, she is able to follow the discussion and options offered. She understands the risks and benefits of those options. The last chemotherapy treatments were tiring for her, and while she feels that she is not “ready to quit”, she is dreading the thought of another intensive chemotherapy treatment course. She is willing to hear about how palliative care or hospice may benefit her, but she is not ready to “sign on” right away. The milder chemotherapy regimen sounds like an option she would like to try. |
| **Key “Findings” for SP daughter to Portray:** | **Background history**  **Mom’s cognition:**   * Mom’s thinking has been “pretty good for someone her age”. She has some occasional difficulty with remembering where she has placed things and it sometimes takes her a while to remember people’s names. She was able to learn how to use the basic functions of an iPad, which was a birthday gift.   **Mom’s function/social situation:**   * **IADLs (Instrumental Activities of Daily Living):** After Mom’s hospitalization she moved in with her daughter, but Mom is able to manage most of her own life details (IADLs). She manages her own bills and takes her own pills without much trouble. Occasionally she misses one, but this is rare. She keeps track of her medical appointments on a calendar. She needs some help with laundry (basement stairs are a problem), and with driving (arthritis, Parkinson’s disease) and cooking (hard to stand and cook, tremor). * **ADLs (Activities of Daily Living):** Mom needs more help with her ADLs including bathing and dressing, (tremor, slow movements, poor balance are problems) and she sometimes needs help getting out of the chair (arthritis and Parkinson’s disease). She walks with a cane (doesn’t want to use the walker), and the daughter likes to be nearby as she worries that her mom may lose her balance and fall. |
| **Key Teaching Points of Case:** | * **Assess the patient’s cognition** using history taking and cognitive examination tools * **Determine the patient’s functional status** using geriatric screening tools * **Discuss treatment options and provide a recommendation** based upon the overall assessment of the patient. * **Determine the patient’s understanding** of the risks and benefits of each option. |
| **Exam Room Needs:** | Examination room  Chair or examining table for patient  Chair next to bedside or by patient’s chair for SP daughter  Chair for physician  Door sign with instructions for fellow  Pen  Paper for writing or note taking (if desired)  Cane for SP |
| **Activities & Time Req:** | 20 minute session = 17 minute SP session, 3 minute SP debrief  If 2 fellows, 1st fellow is in the physician role, 2nd fellow observes/offers insights |
