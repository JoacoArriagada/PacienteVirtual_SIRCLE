1. **Page 10: Mr. Martin seems more confused now than he did earlier. What should I do?**

This patient has known reasons for altered mental status, including dementia and delirium. Delirium, (a waxing and waning of mental status, which can by hyperactive or hypoactive) in hospitalized patients is especially common, with medications (e.g. anticholinergics, benzodiazepines), infections (e.g., the UTI that he has), dehydration, substance use or withdrawal, unfamiliar environment, and many other things contributing. There are few good treatments other than attempting to maintain a normal sleep-wake cycle, reorienting the patient as able, and providing with familiar photographs, hearing aids, glasses, etc. Other things that can mimic delirium include alcohol withdrawal, non-convulsive status epilepticus, certain kinds of strokes, and psychiatric disorders, including depression and bipolar disorder. This is a good chance to ask the nurse how many times they have covered the patient. If this is their first time with the patient, this may just be their normal delirium. If they have covered the patient every night for the last week and this is different from what he did before, it may be worth looking further into. This would be an appropriate issue to at least discuss with your senior resident.
