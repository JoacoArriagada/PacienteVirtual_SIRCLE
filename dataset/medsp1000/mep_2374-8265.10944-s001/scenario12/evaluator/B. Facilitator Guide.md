**Cross-Cover Curriculum Facilitator's Guide**

1. **Page 12: Ms. Kent is looking uncomfortable breathing, used last prn morphine 1 hour ago. Can she get something?**

Acuity: Intermediate

Cross-cover to see patient: NoThis is a common page during end of life care. Air hunger is a common complaint that both causes distress to the patient as well as to the family and friends supporting them. Morphine and other opioids are excellent ways to reduce air hunger and make the patient more comfortable. Generally, this can be titrated up as needed as long as the goal is patient comfort. In addition, nonpharmacologic means, such as blowing a fan over the patient's face, can also relieve the sensation of air hunger. Other pharmacologic means, such as anti-muscarinic agents such as glycopyrrholate, can reduce secretions at end of life. Regardless, in response to this page you should increase the prn frequency of morphine that can be administered.
