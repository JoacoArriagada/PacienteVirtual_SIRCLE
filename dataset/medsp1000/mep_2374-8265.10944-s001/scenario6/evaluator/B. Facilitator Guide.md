**Cross-Cover Curriculum Facilitator's Guide**

1. **Page 6: Mr. Jones complaining of chest pain while getting up and going to the bathroom.**

Acuity: Very High

Cross-cover to see patient: Yes
Mr. Jones is very high risk from a cardiac perspective, and his chest pain started on exertion. As such, we need to rule out acute coronary syndrome.  This patient should be seen immediately, and you should order stat EKG and troponins along with checking updated vital signs and physical exam. Your senior resident should be notified about this page as soon as possible. Importantly, the first troponin can be negative if caught shortly after the initial event, so it is important to get 2-3 spaced several hours apart. He is on a heparin drip, which is some reassurance, but patient should still be evaluated for ACS. If his exam is concerning for ACS, and he has not received a full dose of aspirin, this should be considered as well. Other life-threatening conditions besides myocardial infarction to think about on your differential for severe chest pain, potentially with hemodynamic instability, would include: pulmonary embolism, aortic dissection, cardiac tamponade, Boerhaave syndrome, and tension pneumothorax.
