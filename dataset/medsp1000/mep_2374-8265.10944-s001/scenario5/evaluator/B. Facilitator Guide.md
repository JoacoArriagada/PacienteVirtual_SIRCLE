**Cross-Cover Curriculum Facilitator's Guide**

1. **Page 5: Ms. Smith’s last glucose is 120. Should we hold evening glargine?**

Acuity: Low

Cross-cover to see patient: No
Be careful about holding medications without knowing why they were started in the first place. This patient is type I diabetic, and as such needs a long-acting insulin on board. She is unlikely to go truly hypoglycemic overnight. If she were lower (say, 70), or was NPO for a procedure the next day, you could consider cutting glargine overnight but still providing her with some coverage.  This may be something that would be reasonable in the setting of her nausea, as her appetite is likely reduced from baseline. However, holding all insulin could push her into diabetic ketoacidosis. Whatever changes you make to the medication, remember to tell the day team about it when you sign the patient back out to them, as they may want to change it back. Many people write down each issue that comes up on their printed handoff. Don’t be fooled into thinking you will remember everything that happened overnight! If the day team doesn't know about the change, sometimes these changes can fall through the cracks with unfortunate results. It is advisable not to discontinue a medication unless you understand why it was started and the benefits and consequences of discontinuing it.
