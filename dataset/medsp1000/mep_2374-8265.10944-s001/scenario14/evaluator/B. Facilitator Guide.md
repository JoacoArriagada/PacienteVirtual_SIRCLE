**Cross-Cover Curriculum Facilitator's Guide**

1. **Page 14: Jones complaining of insomnia; can he get something for that?**

Acuity: Low

Cross-cover to see patient: No
Insomnia is another common complaint among inpatients, in part because the hospital is often a loud, disorienting environment. Insomnia can contribute to patient delirium. Nonpharmacologic measures such as discouraging napping during the day and maintaining a consistent day-night schedule can be helpful; try to avoid unnecessary labs or vital sign checks overnight. Many pharmacologic sleep aids can also have the effect of prolonging QTC intervals, including trazodone, doxepin, and quetiapine (even diphenhydramine can). If considering one of these agents, particularly in a patient with cardiac disease, you should check their most recent EKG to check for the QTC. Other agents that do not prolong QTC include melatonin (safe for use in almost all patients), benzodiazepines (though use with caution in older patients due to risk of delirium), and zolpidem (also a delirium risk).
