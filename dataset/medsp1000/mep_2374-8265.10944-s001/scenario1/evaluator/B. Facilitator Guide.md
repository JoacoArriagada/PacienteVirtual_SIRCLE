**Cross-Cover Curriculum Facilitator's Guide**

1. **Page 1: FYI: just got vitals on Ms. Jackson. T 38.4, P 77, BP 126/82, Pulse ox 98% on room air, RR 18.**

Acuity: Very High

Cross-cover to see patient: Yes
This is an “FYI page” that in actuality is anything but. We are told in handoff that the patient is neutropenic from chemotherapy. As such, this is a neutropenic fever, defined as one temperature over 38.3 or two measurements 1 hour apart of 38.0 in a patient with neutropenia (less than 1500 neutrophils/microliter blood. We are particularly concerned as this patient’s ANC (absolute neutrophil count) is below 500. This is an emergency, as the patient is without an immune system and is likely to go into septic shock. You should see this patient right away and perform a head-to-toe physical exam to evaluate for any possible sources of infection. In addition, this patient merits broad-spectrum antibiotics and cultures looking for potential source of infection. For laboratory evaluation, this would include CBC, LFTs, 2 sets of blood cultures from peripheral veins and/or any catheters, urinalysis and urine culture, and consideration of chest X-ray. For treatment, good broad-spectrum antibiotics would include piperacillin/tazobactam or cefepime (though cefepime will not cover anaerobes well). If there is concern for Gram positive infection, or if the patient is hemodynamically unstable, you should also add on vancomycin for MRSA coverage. While in most cases no clear source for the infection is found, prompt antibiotics and supportive care (e.g. fluids) are important to prevent patient from decompensating. The senior resident should be notified for anyone with neutropenic fever given that this can be life-threatening if prompt action is not taken.
