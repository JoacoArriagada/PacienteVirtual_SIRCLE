1. **Page 3: Smith having more nausea. She’s not written for anything—can she get something?**

Nausea is a very common complaint among inpatients. Unfortunately, however, many of the medications we would ordinarily give are QTC prolonging (e.g., ondansetron). Thus, they should be used very cautiously in patients with known cardiac disease/arrhythmias. This patient has HFpEF and atrial fibrillation. There are several anti-emetic agents that do not prolong QTC: aprepitant (though expensive), benzodiazepines (though you worry about delirium risk, particularly in the elderly), steroids (though take some time to work and concern for high blood glucoses and other problems), and trimethobenzamide. It is at least worth checking an EKG (or looking at their most recent EKG) to see what the QTC is before giving them a QTC prolonging agent. We want to avoid QTCs over 500.
