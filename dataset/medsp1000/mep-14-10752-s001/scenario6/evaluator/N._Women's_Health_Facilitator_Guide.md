# Women’s Health

#### Learning Objectives

1. Discuss current American parental leave policies and their impact on women’s health and the ability to access postpartum care.
2. Describe recent trends in American maternal mortality rates and contributing social, economic, and health factors.
3. List common barriers to contraception access and the impact of contraception access on women’s health.
4. Discuss how healthcare providers’ cognitive biases can lead to women being undertreated for pain and misdiagnosed for cardiac problems.

You are a third-year medical student on rotation in the emergency department. Your resident asks you to go speak with patient Sue Smith down the hall: “Pain case,” he mutters with mild annoyance.

Mrs. Smith is a petite, well-dressed 49-year-old woman who has been struggling with pain from an autoimmune disorder. At home she has been taking medication for her pain, but today it has not helped. She is timid and quiet, but clearly in extraordinary discomfort. She explains that the pain got out of hand last night and became “unbearable, a 10/10 pain.” You report this back to the resident and remark on how distressed she looks. The resident seems put out and responds, “Just give her a sedative for anxiety and send her home.”

Teaching and evaluation-relevant points retained from the source:

* Studies have demonstrated that, in many contexts, women are likely to be undertreated for their pain compared to men.
* Autoimmune disorders frequently have an intractable pain component, and women make up 78% of autoimmune cases.
* Social factors that may lead to men’s pain being addressed more aggressively include cognitive bias that women are more “hysterical” about their pain or have a greater tolerance for pain.
* Many women have been socialized to be less assertive about their needs and less willing to “make a fuss.”
* There are also differences in the diagnosis and treatment of heart disease between genders.
* The source recommends being aware of our own biases and actively combating them, and supporting increased research on pain and the way disease manifests differently in women.
