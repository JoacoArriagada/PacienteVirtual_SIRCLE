# Immigrant/Refugee Health and Language Barriers

#### Learning Objectives

1. Identify health disparities caused by differences in citizenship status.
2. Discuss the impact of health insurance on health outcomes.
3. Explain the importance of language translation services.

A 22-year old Latina woman, Ms. Garcia, is a recent immigrant to Houston. She primarily speaks Spanish and lives with her boyfriend, since most of her family is abroad. Ms. Garcia doesn’t have health insurance, so after a fight with her boyfriend, she is unsure where to go for her dislocated elbow. She ends up at a Harris Health county hospital, Ben Taub, where she receives treatment under EMTALA.

In the ED, the nurse notes the presence of multiple bruises on Ms. Garcia’s torso and arms, in addition to her dislocated elbow. The resident assigned to her care decides it would be faster to talk to the patient herself instead of calling language services since it is a busy night. As the resident attempts to take a history in broken Spanish, Ms. Garcia tearfully asks what her options might be when she returns home to her boyfriend. Not fully understanding what Ms. Garcia said, the resident smiles and nods. Ms. Garcia becomes uncomfortable. She senses that the resident doesn’t actually understand her and she doesn’t trust her physician enough to reveal how she hurt her elbow. She limits herself to one word answers and when asked what happened, Ms. Garcia says she “fell down the stairs.”

Teaching and scoring-relevant points retained from the source:

* Generally, patients who don’t speak English are less likely to have a regular source of medical care and access to preventative screenings.
* Language barriers are associated with decreased patient satisfaction, decreased patient compliance, and worsened disease severity.
* The best option for limited English speaking patients is the use of trained interpreters.
* Even with the availability of language services, research demonstrates these services are often underutilized.
* When physicians do not utilize interpreters, they often turn to patient family members or children for translation; these ad hoc interpreters lack training in medical terminology and confidentiality and may have conflicts of interest.
* This section is open for student interpretation. Possible suggestions: offering Ms. Garcia a professional interpreter, taking more notice of her bruises, or bringing in a social worker.
* Ms. Garcia has several signs of domestic abuse: multiple bruises and a dislocated elbow with a suspicious story.
* Her status as a recent immigrant may make it more difficult for her to leave or report her abuser.
* The fear of law enforcement and of being deported empowers abusers and pushes victims to stay in abusive relationships.
