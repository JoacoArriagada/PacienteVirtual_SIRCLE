# Women’s Health

#### Learning Objectives

1. Discuss current American parental leave policies and their impact on women’s health and the ability to access postpartum care.
2. Describe recent trends in American maternal mortality rates and contributing social, economic, and health factors.
3. List common barriers to contraception access and the impact of contraception access on women’s health.
4. Discuss how healthcare providers’ cognitive biases can lead to women being undertreated for pain and misdiagnosed for cardiac problems.

A few months pass, and you start an OBGYN outpatient rotation. As you enter the exam room for your first patient of the day, you recognize Mrs. Smith. She does not seem to be in pain, but looks stressed and weary. When you ask why she is here, she breaks down in tears and says she suspects her husband, who often travels for business, has been sleeping with prostitutes. She is concerned she may have contracted an STI and would like testing. She says between sobs, “I gave up my law career to raise our kids, I trusted him and thought he would change. Now I’m stuck and I can’t leave. I have no money of my own and everything is in his name. Nobody would believe what I put up with.”

The source gives this supportive response from the clinician: “It is really brave of you to tell me that, and I am so glad you are taking care of yourself by coming in today. I can’t imagine how hard this all must be for you.”

She then says, “He would go nuts if he knew I were here today and knew that I told someone my suspicions.” She also says, “If I didn’t have this autoimmune problem and all the expensive drugs and ER visits for my flares, I could just leave him. I could be without health insurance for a while until I get back on my feet.” She shakes her head and adds, “But everything is in his name and I’m sure he’d get the kids… I’m stuck.”

Teaching and evaluation-relevant points retained from the source:

* Warning signs suggesting an abusive relationship include language about feeling “trapped,” repeated mentions of dependency on her husband, language about him “going nuts” if he found out she was getting STI testing, and his willingness to expose her unknowingly to STIs.
* The source defines domestic violence as a pattern of abusive and threatening behaviors that may include physical, emotional, economic, and sexual violence, as well as intimidation, isolation, and coercion.
* Factors complicating attempts to leave can include isolation, children, fear, physical harm after trying to leave, threats, economic necessity, lack of resources or information, shelters being full, hope that the partner will change, failure of the criminal justice system, racism and homophobia in the criminal justice system, cultural or familial pressures, shame, and immigration status.
* The source specifically suggests asking, “Do you feel safe at home?”
* The source also says to offer resources and local referrals to professionals and organizations dedicated to helping women escape domestic abuse.
* It is not advisable for a healthcare provider to urge a woman to immediately leave her abuser because the period after leaving may be the most dangerous.
