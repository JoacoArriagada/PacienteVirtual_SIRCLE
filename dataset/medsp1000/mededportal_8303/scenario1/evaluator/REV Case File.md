Learning Module: Hypothermia

# Objectives:

1. Demonstrate recognition of critical hypothermia   
2. Perform a resuscitation including treatment of severe hypothermia   
3. Organize hospital resources to resuscitate patient effectively

Critical actions:

1. Gets pt out of wet clothes   
2. Tries naloxone (no change)   
3. Checks glucose (normal)   
4. Initiates non-invasive rewarming (minimal change)   
5. Initiates some form of invasive rewarming   
6. Participant must use rapid infuser due to lack of availability of warmed IV fluids.

Target Audience:

EM Residents (PGY I-III) at a large urban county hospital that will work together as part of a resuscitation team. We had residents perform as team members commensurate with their training (e.g., junior residents more procedure oriented; senior residents more resuscitation oriented).

Materials:

1. Rapid infuser   
2. Bair hugger (or available warming blanket)   
3. Chest tube tray   
4. DPL equipment   
5. Foley equipment (OPTIONAL: temperature sensing foley or equivalent lower temperature   
sensing device)   
6. ECG w/ bradycardia / Osborn waves (osborn)   
7. ECG w/ bradycardia / no Osborn waves (sinus brady)   
8. Intubation box

A 42 year old obtunded, disheveled man is brought into your emergency department. Past medical history, medications, allergies, and social history are unknown and unobtainable. He is unable to give you a clear history. EMS states they were called by a concerned bystander. He was found on the side walk.

Triage:

Chief Complaint: “altered mental status”

Vital Signs Temperature (unobtainable) Heart Rate 59 Blood Pressure 98/60 Respiratory Rate 12 Pulse Oximety $9 9 \%$ on room air

Temp if asked for rectally is 28 celsius

Exam

General Exam: The patient is obtunded, mumbles curse words occasionally, minimally withdraws from pain, opens eyes to pain.

Head: Atraumatic

Eyes: Pupils are symmetric.

Ears: Normal

Nose: Normal

Oropharynx/Mouth: Normal

Neck: Normal.

Lungs: Chest scattered wheeze, otherwise clear (no obvious respiratory distress)

Heart: Bradycardic, regular rhythm. No mumurs.

Abdomen: Nondistended, no obvious tenderness, scattered hemangiomas consistent with alcohol history.

Skin: Cool, clothing is drenched and cold.

Extremity: Toes are ruborous with some blistering. Otherwise atraumatic.

Play of Case

The patient is brought in cold with a temperature that must be obtained rectally with vital signs as outlined in state [Baseline Cold]. If the faculty preceptor determines that the learning group does not recognize the patient as critically ill or delays initiating treatment (warming, IV/O2/monitor), then the patient goes into cardiac arrest. If appropriate warming methods are started, the patient can be placed in the [Slightly Warmer] state. Of note, we do not have warmed IV fluids in our emergency department, so it was important for our case play to have the residents recognize that the rapid infuser needed to be used. If the residents performed some form of active rewarming (foley, peritoneal lavage, chest tubes) the patient was taken to the [Warm] state. Once the patient is warm, the learners are expected to disposition the patient to the ICU with appropriate handoff/communications. If there are significant delays in patient care, depending on preceptor preference, we have introduced distracters such as other patients, ECGs to be signed, etc. For cardiac arrest, we had both a pulseless electrical activity and aystolic arrest states

It’s important that the residents consider the differential diagnosis of hypothermia: environmental, septic, hypothyroid, hypoglycemic, drug induced. During debriefing, the historical use of bretyllium can be discussed. The timing and utility of ACLS drugs can also be discussed. We found it also helpful to discuss physiologic changes (diuresis, acid base, coagulation changes) common in hypothermia.

# OPTIONAL COMPONENTS:

1. Distractors – ECGs to sign, phone calls to take   
2. Include sign out in case   
3. Include walking wounded, urgent care patients to see while organizing resuscitation   
4. Include component on proper of treatment of frost-bite, which this pt has in addition to being   
critical ill.
