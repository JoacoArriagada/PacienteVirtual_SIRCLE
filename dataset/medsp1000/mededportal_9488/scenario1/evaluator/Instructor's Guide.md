# #14: Innocent Heart Murmur

A. Subject: Explaining a diagnosis to parents.

B. Question: A four month old male named John comes into your office for a well child visit. On exam you notice that he has a heart murmur. He has normal vital signs, an otherwise normal exam, and no significant past medical history; parents have no complaints and say that the John is doing well. You think he has an innocent murmur and decide to tell the parents about the murmur (which they were never told about before). Remember you are explaining the diagnosis to the parents.

C. Set-up: Interviewer acts as parent. Interviewer reads the question initially but can ask questions if the resident forgets to discuss any issues.

Possible questions: are you sure of the diagnosis; why wasn’t I told about it before by my previous pediatrician; what will happen to the child; should I beware of anything or look for anything, ...

# D. Correct Answer:

Resident should explain the diagnosis to the parents in an understandable manner, using lay terms, not medical terminology or abbreviations (unless he/she explains the abbreviations). Resident should start with positive statements (e.g. your baby is doing very well ...), should explain what a murmur means and what causes it. May be helpful if resident draws a picture while explaining the pathophysiology.

# E. Scoring:

5 - Resident explains diagnosis in an understandable and thoughtful manner. Resident does not use confusing medical terminology. Other suggestions may include: draws diagram, expalins other possibilities for comparison, ...
4 - Resident explains diagnosis in an understandable and thoughtful manner, but does not volunteer any other information unless prompted.

3 - Resident explains diagnosis in an understandable and thoughtful manner but occasionally uses confusing medical terminology. Does volunteer other information to help explain the diagnosis (e.g., draws diagram, gives other possibilities for comparison) 2 - Resident explains diagnosis in an understandable and thoughtful manner but occasionally uses confusing medical terminology. 1 - Resident does not explain the diagnosis in an understandable or thoughtful manner.
