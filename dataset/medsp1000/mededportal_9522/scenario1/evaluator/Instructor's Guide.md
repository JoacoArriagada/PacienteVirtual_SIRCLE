**Instructor’s Guide – Accidental Hypothermia**

B. Conceptual background for the case and goals

1. Conceptual background

a. The impetus behind this case was a patient encounter in a local ED in which the patient came in with hypothermia which went unrecognized initially as a low-reading temperature probe was not used. It is important for physicians to be familiar with equipment used in the EDs, including thermometers and their limitations.

b. There are many causes of hypothermia, including environmental and metabolic, and the ED physician should be able to formulate a differential diagnosis of potential causes and then know the appropriate management to rule in or out the different possibilities.

c. Hypothermia can complicate many disease processes and can occur in any season or region of the world. Providing emergency providers with practice in recognition and management of hypothermia will enable them to do so in actual patients.

2. Goals

a. To provide emergency medicine residents with a clinical experience that simulates diagnosis and treatment of hypothermia in a patient presenting with altered mental status

b. Analyze the differential diagnosis causing altered mental status

(AMS) and quickly rule in and out most-likely causes in the presenting patient.

c. Demonstrate initial stabilization of an altered patient with poor airway protection and hypotension.

d. Demonstrate a focused history from available sources.

e. Demonstrate an appropriate emergency department (ED) physical exam.

f. Demonstrate good team management skills.

g. Recognize hypothermia and initiate appropriate treatment.

i. Recognize bradycardia and why the usual advanced cardiac life support (ACLS) interventions may not be effective in hypothermia.

j. Demonstrate close patient monitoring with frequent reassessment.

k. Formulate an appropriate disposition for the patient

C. Limitations of the submission/ideas for improving the submission

1. Although the simulation patient is a mannequin and not really alive, simulation participants should approach the simulation patient the same way and with the same sense of urgency that they would a real-life patient.

2. Evidence to support particular treatments in hypothermia are lacking. The highest level of recommendation available is Level C, based on case reports and expert consensus.

3. Actual core temperature changes in real life will be gradual, however for the sake of expediting the case, once the appropriate treatment for hypothermia is ordered, consider more dramatic changes in core temperature.

4. Suggestions for mannequin set-up

a. Consider putting ice packs on the mannequin for a few minutes before participants start the cast, this will give them the tactile prompt to check a temperature if they forget initially.

c. If cold packs are used, remove them just before the case starts and out of site of the participant.

5. Consider having a “normal” EKG available if the participant asks for a repeat EKG after re-warming has begun.

6. This case should be used for residents in their second or third year of training.

7. Many residents do not know that not all thermometers read below 34˚C (93.2˚F). To make the case more realistic, if the participant does not ask for a core temperature or specifically request a low-reading thermometer, consider giving the initial temperature as 34˚C (93.2˚F). This temperature should not improve despite appropriate rewarming therapy because despite rewarming attempts, the temperature will still be below the thermometer’s lowest reading capability.
