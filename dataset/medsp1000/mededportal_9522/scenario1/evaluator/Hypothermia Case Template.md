# Simulation Scenario Template

I. Title: Accidental Hypothermia

II. Target Audience: Emergency Medicine Residents

III. Goals and Objectives

A. Goal: To provide residents with a clinical experience that simulates diagnosis and treatment of hypothermia in a patient presenting with altered mental status   
B. Learning Objectives: 1. Analyze the differential diagnosis causing altered mental status (AMS) and quickly rule in and out most-likely causes in the presenting patient. 2. Demonstrate initial stabilization of an altered patient with poor airway protection and hypotension. 3. Demonstrate a focused history from available sources. 4. Demonstrate an appropriate emergency department (ED) physical exam. 5. Demonstrate good team management skills. 6. Recognize hypothermia and initiate appropriate treatment. 7. Recognize bradycardia and why the usual advanced cardiac life support (ACLS) interventions may not be effective. 8. Demonstrate rapid and appropriate therapeutic interventions. 9. Demonstrate close patient monitoring with frequent reassessment. 10. Formulate an appropriate disposition for the patient   
C. Critical actions checklist 1. Instructs team to initiate key actions (Intravenous access (IV), Oxygen (O2), Monitor, etc) 2. Immediately performs primary survey on arrival 3. Obtains full set of vital signs including rectal temperature 4. Performs a focused history and physical (focused neurological exam with Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)) 5. Provides initial AMS workup/intervention (Dextrose, O2, Naloxone, Thiamine) 6. Intubates patient with rapid sequence intubation (RSI) upon recognition of airway compromise 7. Appropriately addresses unstable vitals (warmed fluids for hypotension and hypothermia, may try atropine for bradycardia but should not attempt pacing due to hypothermia) 8. Obtains appropriate laboratory studies for patient with altered mental status and unstable vitals (Complete blood count (CBC), comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP), troponin, coagulation studies (PT/INR), lactate, type and screen, urinalysis (UA), thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH), urine drug screen (UDS), acetaminophen (APAP), Aspirin (ASA), ethanol (EtOH))

9. Obtains other studies as indicated (chest x-ray (CXR), computed   
tomography (CT) head, electrocardiogram (ECG))   
10. Appropriately warms patient   
11. Reassesses patient following interventions   
12. Arrange for definitive care in the Intensive Care Unit (ICU)

IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – Emergency Department   
B. Manikin Set Up – SimMan with standard ED crash cart and medications, airway equipment   
C. Props – ECGs with bradycardia and Osborne waves, Normal CXR, Normal head CT   
D. Distractors – Patient with AMS and unable to cooperate with exam

V. Actors

A. Roles – Nurses and technicians played by other residents, consultants played by faculty   
B. Action Role – Each player performs activities as directed by the team leader to the best of their abilities. If the individual is playing the role of the nurse or technician they cannot complete tasks out of the scope of practice of that role.

VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants (given to participant to read) 1. CC: AMS 2. HPI: $8 0 \mathrm { y } / 0$ poorly nourished male brought in by EMS. En route he has been minimally responsive. Patient was staying with friends in a house with poor heat (this part of the history can be withheld for more advanced residents and only revealed if they ask EMS specific questions regarding the environment in which they found the patient). EMS was called when they couldn’t get him up off the couch this morning. Last seen normal last night.

3. PMH: Hypertension, osteoarthritis, hypothyroid

5. Allergies: No known drug allergies

6. Family History and Social History: smokes 1 pack per day, recently lost home and is staying with friends

Scenario conditions initially (participant must obtain this information)

1. Primary Survey a. General: Poorly nourished male, groans occasionally, opens eyes to painful stimulus but does not speak or follow commands appropriately. Weakly moves all extremities to pain. b. Airway: Poor gag reflex c. Breathing: Respiratory rate 10, no cyanosis d. Circulation: Pale and cold skin, delayed capillary refill

e. Disability: Pupils $4 \mathrm { m m }$ and sluggish f. Exposure: No medic alert tags or obvious injuries g. Finger: Heme negative, normal tone

2. Patient’s initial exam

a. Vitals: HR 38, BP 90/48, RR 10, O2 sat $9 4 \%$ on room air, Temperature $9 3 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { F }$ $( 3 4 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ axillary, rectal temperature $8 4 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { F }$ $( 2 9 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$   
b. HEENT: Normocephalic and atraumatic; Pupils $4 \mathrm { m m }$ and sluggish   
c. Neck: Supple; No jugular venous distension; normal thyroid   
d. Lungs: Bilateral coarse breath sounds; No wheezes, shallow breaths   
e. Heart: bradycardic; regular rhythm; no murmur, rub, or gallop   
f. Abdominal: Soft; not distended; no apparent tenderness; bowel sounds decreased   
g. Extremities: diminished pulses; weakly withdraws to pain with all extremities   
h. Neurologic: Opens eyes to pain; groans and incomprehensible sounds; weakly withdraws from pain all extremities (GCS 8)   
i. Skin: dry, pale, and cold; delayed capillary refill

3. Ancillary Studies

a. Labs:

i. CBC: white blood cell count (WBC), hemoglobin and hematocrit (H/H) and platelets within normal limits ii. PT/INR: slight elevation consistent with hypothermic coagulopathy iii. Accucheck: low-normal range iv. ABG: acidotic with borderline pO2 v. cardiac enzymes: troponin 0.014, CK 595, CK-MB 38.9 vi. LFTs, BNP, TSH, UA, Serum toxicologic screen, EtOH, Urine drug screen, Magnesium, Phosphorus, lactate within normal limits vii. BMP shows mild non-anion gap acidosis b. Imaging i. CXR: pre-intubation normal, post-intubation shows endotracheal tube in place and is otherwise normal ii. CT head: normal iii. ECG: Sinus bradycardia with Osborne waves.

C. Scenario branch points

1. Failure to obtain rectal temperature results in inadequate rewarming and deterioration of condition to ventricular fibrillation. Axillary and tympanic temperatures are often unreliable in hypothermic patients

2. Rewarming: a. If warming is attempted, temperature improves (to $3 3 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ $( 9 2 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { F } ) $ ) and HR improves to low-50s

i. Appropriate re-warming for a moderately hypothermic patient include forced air blanket, warmed IV fluids, warmed and humidified air, may try lavage through Foley catheter   
ii. Core re-warming with thoracic cavity lavage should not be attempted unless patient is in cardiac arrest or ventricular fibrillation. Rapid re-warming can cause lethal arrhythmias in hypothermic patients.   
iii. If dialysis or cardiopulmonary bypass is requested, they will be unavailable.

b. If blankets, warmed fluids, forced air blanket are not used, patient’s cardiac rhythm changes to ventricular fibrillation that does not respond to medications and/or defibrillation

3. Attempts to correct bradycardia with medications or electricity will be unsuccessful until patient is re-warmed.

4. Failure to address hypotension with IV fluid bolus results in worsening hypotension.   
5. Failure to intubate patient should result in decreased O2 saturation and respiratory rate and eventual respiratory arrest if failure to intubate continues.   
6. Examinee may give high dose corticosteroid without causing any adverse effect to the patient, however it will also not improve patient’s condition.   
7. Attempts to admit to the ICU prior to initiating fluids and rewarming should result in the intensivist not being available.

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing

1. Provide results of studies in an accelerated fashion (i.e. blood and imaging available about 5 minutes after vein is accessed and studies are ordered)   
2. Once appropriate re-warming has been initiated, temperature increases rapidly (ie. 1 degree every 1-2 minutes) with improvement in HR to low 50s

B. Scenario programming

1. Optimal management path

a. Mobilizes team to secure IV, O2, and monitor while he/she performs focused history and physical   
b. Obtains full set of vitals with rectal temperature   
c. Recognizes hypotension and starts IVF bolus   
d. Recognizes impending respiratory failure and intubates patient with RSI   
e. Recognizes hypothermia and attempts to correct it (warmed IV fluids, warm blankets, forced air blanket)   
f. Recognizes bradycardia as a side effect of hypothermia, may try a dose of atropine which will not improve or worsen condition, but does not attempt pacing   
g. Orders appropriate laboratory studies and ancillary testing   
h. Admit to ICU

2. Potential complications/errors path(s)

a. Failure to obtain rectal temp results in inadequate rewarming and deterioration of condition to ventricular fibrillation. Axillary and tympanic temperatures are often unreliable in hypothermic patients   
b. Overly-aggressive attempts at re-warming (ie core re-warming with thoracic cavity lavage) should not be attempted unless patient is in cardiac arrest or ventricular fibrillation. Rapid rewarming can cause lethal arrhythmias in hypothermic patients.   
c. Attempts to correct bradycardia with medications or electricity will be unsuccessful until patient is re-warmed.   
d. Failure to address hypotension with IV fluid bolus results in worsening hypotension.   
e. Failure to intubate patient results in decreased O2 saturation and respiratory rate and eventual respiratory arrest if failure to intubate continues.

VIII. Debriefing Plan

A. Method of Debriefing: Debriefing can occur as a group or one-on-one with the faculty. Debriefing should begin with the team leader reflecting on the case as a whole and his/her thought process. Next, review things that went well and things that could have been done better and review the team leader’s thought process leading to these decisions. Be sure to include commentary on the team leader’s communication with the rest of the team and performance as team leader. At the end, review the critical actions to reinforce the key elements and learning points of the case.   
B. Rules for Debriefing: Do not focus on success or failure of the case. Concentrate on actions, not people, to be sure the team leader is not made to feel like he/she is being criticized. Select key, relevant observations and avoid a “laundry list”. Faculty acts as moderator and keeps the debriefing focused, making sure one person speaks at a time.
