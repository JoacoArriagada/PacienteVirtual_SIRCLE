## **SP Training Notes**

## **Medical College of Wisconsin**

## **Geriatric Cardiology Session - Mr. John Smith**

**Patient Name:** Mr. John Smith

**Gender/Age:** 83 year old man

**Brief Summary:**

Mr. Smith is an elderly patient who has recently moved back to the area. His new primary care physician has referred him to the cardiologist for further help in managing his complex cardiac situation. The patient is now being seen by the cardiologist to review his care and to determine the next steps in management of his cardiac care. His last cardiologist told him that his defibrillator battery was “about to run out of juice”.

**Mr. Smith’s medical problems include:**

- High blood pressure
- High cholesterol
- Coronary artery disease with 2 heart surgeries in the past
- Atrial fibrillation (irregular heart rhythm) for which he takes a “blood thinner” (Coumadin)
- Severe heart failure. His heart does not pump blood well and he has been in the hospital 4 times in the last 6 months for heart failure exacerbations (trouble breathing, swollen ankles and legs)
- AICD (this is an implanted defibrillator in his chest that would “shock” his heart if his heart were to go into a fatal rhythm. The battery of this needs to be changed in a month and this is a surgical procedure)
- Severe kidney disease
- 2 falls over the last 6 months

**Mr. Smith has had four hospitalizations** due to worsening heart failure within the past 6 months. He takes his medications as prescribed and sticks to his diet from the physicians (not too much salt and not too much fluid). He has had a home care nurse come to his home to help him with his medications and heart failure for a few weeks after each hospitalization.

**Mr. Smith’s health is worsening** despite his efforts and his doctor’s care. Over the past 6 months his heart is getting weaker and he is becoming more short of breath. This is making it difficult for him to manage things at home. He is able to walk with a cane, but only for short distances and he gets quite short of breath. Getting dressed, showering, grocery shopping and driving are now very difficult due to his weakness, shortness of breath and swelling in his ankles and legs.

**When his doctor asks him about what he would like for his medical care** in the future (how to approach his care), he is able to come to the conclusion, albeit reluctantly, that his heart is in very bad condition. He is accepting of conservative measures that will continue to prolong his life (medications) but does not want measures to try to resuscitate him, should he decline suddenly or if his heart should stop (he declines CPR, ventilator). He is accepting that not replacing the AICD battery is an appropriate course of action. He is willing to hear about how a palliative care approach to care or using the Medicare hospice benefit may help him, but he is not ready to “sign on” right away.

**Key “Findings” for the SP Patient to Portray:**

**Patient’s demeanor:**

- Sitting in chair or on examination table wearing an oxygen nasal cannula (tube)
- Alert but tired, and appears worn out
- Mildly short of breath (about 1.5-2 times normal breaths per minute)
- Able to answer questions but answers are short and brief (due to shortness of breath)
- Occasionally taking deep inspirations to “catch your breath”

**Patient’s memory and functioning:**

- Patient has no memory problems
- Patient has difficulty with walking, bathing, dressing (due to shortness of breath and fatigue)
- Difficulty with driving, grocery shopping, laundry (due to shortness of breath), but takes care of bills, pills, phone with no difficulty

**SP Patient Opening Statement:**

“I was feeling well, but after two weeks of being at home I started to get short of breath again and my legs started to swell.”

**Elaboration of Symptoms:** (if asked by MD)

- **How did you decide to come in to see me?**
  “The home nurse thought I might be having more heart failure because of my breathing problems and my ankles were swelling. My doctor had wanted me to come and see you, also, so you could help me with my heart. He said my heart is in bad shape.”
- **Tell me more about your breathing?**
  “I went home from the hospital last month after I was in here for heart failure. I was doing better and had a home nurse checking on me, but now I am having more trouble breathing all the time. I have really noticed it being worse over the last 2 days.”
- “I am short of breath all the time, but it is worse with moving around or when I am sleeping.”
- “Sometimes I wake up at night and I can’t catch my breath”
- “I sleep so that I am almost sitting up. I use so many pillows. I can breathe better that way.”
- **What other problems are you having?**
  “I have swollen ankles. My ankles and legs are always swollen, but now they are even worse, and sometimes even seem to be leaking.”
- **Do you have any coughing? Wheezing? Nausea? Vomiting?**
  “No”
- **Do you have any chest pain? Racing heart beat? Lightheadedness?**
  “No”
- **Has your defibrillator (AICD) fired?**
  “No, and I’m sure I would know if it had!”
- **How is your appetite? Have you lost any weight?**
  “I am not too hungry these days; I have lost about 15 lbs over the past 6 months. It’s difficult to make food and so I sometimes get Meals On Wheels”

**Memory Questions**

- **Have you had any trouble with your memory or thinking?**
  “No, I think my memory is pretty good and I don’t have any problems with that, thankfully!”
- If you are asked any memory questions (day, date, month, year, president, remember 3 objects, etc) just answer all questions correctly.

**Function Questions**

- **Do you have any trouble with walking?**
  “Yes, I get very short of breath so I can only just go a few feet then I have to stop and rest. Catch my breath.”
- **Bathing? Dressing?**
  “Yes, I have trouble because I am tired and my breathing is bad.”
- **Driving? Grocery shopping? Laundry?**
  “Yes, I have a friend who is able to drive me around, and I have a neighbor who helps with the groceries and laundry. I get mobile meals sometimes, too.”
- **Bill paying? Taking your medications? Using the phone?**
  “No”
- **Are you spending much time in bed?**
  “Yes, I am in bed most of the time, or else I can walk a few steps to my favorite chair. It’s really hard to get around and staying in bed seems to be easiest some days”
- **Overall function?**
  “I have really been having more trouble over the last half year or so. Things are just getting really hard for me to manage.”

**Family Medical History**

- Father died from a stroke in his 90s
- Mother died from pneumonia in her 80s

**Social History**

- Retired administrator at a local business for many years
- Has a home care nurse who has been coming to help with medications and check on his heart failure (checks blood pressure, pulse), since his hospitalization for heart failure last month
- Lives in an apartment alone, as his wife died 5 years ago
- While he does not have any children he has good support from friends and several neighbors

**Patient’s Goals & Wishes for Care / Questions**

- **How do you feel you are doing?**
  “I think I am not getting better. It seems like each time I come in to the hospital I am getting a little better, but then when I go home again, I am just having more of the same breathing problems.”
- “It’s really difficult to keep up with all of these health problems. Sometimes they just seem to be in control, not me!”
- **What would you like to happen with your health?**
  “I really would like to be getting better, but it just doesn’t seem to be happening that way, even though I am doing everything I can. What do you think, doctor?”
- “I don’t want to be in the hospital any more.”
- **If the MD recommends medication changes**
  You agree to take additional medications or to change doses.
- **If the MD recommends changing the defibrillator (AICD) battery**
  “Is this going to help me? Will I be able to make it to 90 like my dad?”
- “Will I be able to walk around better without being so short-winded?”
- **If the MD recommends NOT replacing the defibrillator (AICD) battery**
  “Won’t I die right away? My other cardiologist told me that I needed this or I would die...”
- After further explanation by the MD about the functioning/role of the device, and in relation to your overall health and poor heart situation, you can be in agreement that it is ok not to change/replace the battery.
- **If the MD recommends hospice care or palliative care**
  You can agree that it might be something to think about, but “I’m not sure I’m ready to call it quits just yet.”
