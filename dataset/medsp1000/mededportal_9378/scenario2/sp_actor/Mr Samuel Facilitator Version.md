**Mr. Samuel**

**PART 1**

Mr. Samuel is a 27-year-old man who presents to your internal medicine clinic today as a new patient with a chief complaint of difficulty breathing. He moved to Miami three months ago from Boston. Shortly after moving here, Mr. Samuel started having difficulty breathing while he was running. He has been running since high school and typically runs about 20-25 miles per week. The month prior to developing difficulty breathing during exercise, he did not run at all because he had twisted an ankle. When he resumed running, he assumed the difficulty breathing was due to deconditioning. However, even as he has steadily increased his exercise back to his 20-25 miles per week, he was still having trouble breathing when he ran. He describes his breathing difficulty as chest tightness and “trouble moving air” in and out. He also experiences some coughing during these episodes. Initially these symptoms would subside within 15 minutes or so after completing his run. Now he seems to have intermittent difficulty breathing unrelated to exercise. Once or twice a week, he will experience shortness of breath, chest tightness and a dry cough that lasts for several hours. The symptoms appear to gradually resolve if he sits down and rests. Activity exacerbates his symptoms. He denies chest pain, palpitations, nausea, vomiting, visual changes or dizziness associated with these episodes.

Mr. Samuel has never had any surgeries or hospitalizations. He takes no medications and has no known drug allergies. He has never smoked or used illicit substances. He drinks alcohol occasionally (1-2 drinks on average a week) and denies binge drinking. He does not have pets. He has not travelled outside of the United States in over five years. He developed a positive PPD during college after travelling in Central America. He subsequently took 9 months of INH without difficulty. No one in his family has any medical problems. He has been in a mutually monogamous sexual relationship with one female partner for the last 3 years.

On review of systems he denies recent illnesses, fever, night sweats, or a cough productive of sputum. He does admit to occasional epigastric discomfort and a burning sensation in his throat, particularly after a large meal.

You ask Mr. Samuel if he is currently experiencing any symptoms and he states that he is having some trouble breathing that started just prior to arriving at your office.

Mr. Samuel is in your office today for his 1 month follow-up after his diagnosis of asthma. Two weeks ago he called you on the phone because he was still experiencing the same frequency of symptoms. He confirmed that he does experience relief of his symptoms when he uses his albuterol. You doubled his corticosteroid dose at that time.
