**Workshop Objectives:**

* Describe an effective strategy for leading a peer-debriefing session after a distressing patient care event.
* Apply the peer-debriefing model to learners’ own distressing patient care experiences.
* Formulate a plan for providing emotional support to a colleague experiencing emotional distress.
* Justify the importance of provider emotional well-being in providing safe, high-quality patient care.

**-Introduction of Debriefing:** Advancing to through slides 5 – 7, the facilitator introduces the technique for debriefing as a tool to use to begin emotional processing of distressing events.

* Rationale: This segment of the session introduces the concept of debriefing and the session objectives. An in-depth dive into the peer-debriefing model, broken down by component, allows the facilitator to provide the rationale for structuring a debriefing session in this way. This explanation of the peer-debriefing model structure provides for increased buy-in for the technique.
* Tips:
  + A script for the didactic slides are provided in the notes section of the slide presentation for facilitator use, as presented by the designing facilitator.
  + Slide 7, the debriefing model, should be given as much time as needed to ensure the participants understand the various sections of the model. The facilitator should pause and elicit questions regarding the model design. It should be emphasized that this model is a guide, not a substitute for a natural conversation, which can be modified by the participant as he or she sees fit.

**Mock Peer-Debriefing Demonstration (5 minutes):** After ensuring the participants are familiar with the peer-debriefing model, the facilitator and a pre-selected volunteer perform a demonstration of a mock peer-debriefing session utilizing a pre-scripted conversation (see Appendix). As the demonstration debrief occurs, the facilitator advances the slides of the presentation to correspond with the section of the debriefing model being discussed in the demonstration (slides 8 – 13).

* Rationale: After describing the various sections of the peer-debriefing model, the mock peer-debriefing demonstration allows participants to observe an example of the model in use in a representative clinical scenario.
* Tips:
  + Pre-select a volunteer who is a peer of the facilitator, if possible. If the facilitator is a faculty member, or other person with whom a peer or near-peer is not available, the facilitator may choose to pre-select 2 willing participants from the session to perform the mock debrief.
  + As the demonstration debrief occurs, facilitator should advance the slides along with the corresponding section of the debriefing demonstration.
  + At the close of the mock debrief, the volunteer(s) should be thanked for participation.
  + The facilitator should emphasize the time taken to perform the debrief (approximately 5-10 minutes), and how a debriefing session could fit into a busy clinical shift.
