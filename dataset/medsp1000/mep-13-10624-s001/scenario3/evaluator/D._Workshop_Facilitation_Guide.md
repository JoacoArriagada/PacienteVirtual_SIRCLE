**Workshop Objectives:**

* Describe an effective strategy for leading a peer-debriefing session after a distressing patient care event.
* Apply the peer-debriefing model to learners’ own distressing patient care experiences.
* Formulate a plan for providing emotional support to a colleague experiencing emotional distress.
* Justify the importance of provider emotional well-being in providing safe, high-quality patient care.

**Partner Role Play Peer-Debriefs (20 minutes):** The facilitator now introduces the role-play portion of the workshop utilizing slide 15 and the corresponding scripted notes as a guide. During this portion of the session, participants role-play the use of the peer-debriefing model in pairs, drawing from the patient care experiences they identified on the index card at the start of the workshop as a guide. This session will comprise two debrief role-plays, at approximately 5 minutes each with buffer time for transition into the activity and between roles. Each participant will have the opportunity to play the roles of debrief leader and distressed resident.

* Rationale: Participants directly engage in the peer-debriefing model using role-play, best simulating a recent distressing patient care event in a safe environment. Encouraging participants to draw on their own experiences as a basis for the “distressed resident” role improves the connection the participants develop with the activity, and the authenticity of the experience. Participants alternate between roles to gain experience leading a session and discussing their experiences with others, de-stigmatizing the emotional reaction physicians have in caring for sick and dying patients.
* Tips:
  + Participants should be encouraged to move about the room, if possible, and pair with someone with whom they feel comfortable sharing.
  + Once participants have paired up, advancing to slide 16 brings up a 5-minute timer. Clicking the light blue circle starts the timer, which gradually shades in a darker blue as time elapses. When 5 minutes have elapsed, the facilitator should encourage the pairs to finish up their conversations, and switch roles.
  + Slide 17 is a copy of slide 16 with a reset timer for use during the second peer-debrief role play.
* Modifications:
  + If a participant is unable, or finds it too difficult to emotionally engage in a prior distressing patient care experience, hypothetical patient scenarios are available for the participant to utilize as a guide (see Appendix). These scenarios include thought provoking questions, designed to improve the authenticity of the hypothetical scenario, and provide a basis for discussion of emotionally-distressing events. Those participants utilizing the hypothetical scenarios ought to read the scenario and the discussion question, prior to engaging with the debrief leader, to develop the character of the distressed physician.
