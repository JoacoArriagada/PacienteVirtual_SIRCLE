Read this scenario in full prior to engaging in the role-play debrief in the role of the distressed physician.

1. John is 10 year-old male, admitted to the PICU for hemorrhagic shock secondary to splenic rupture. He is stabilized with blood products, intubated and sedated on mechanical ventilation. Two days later, pathology revealed that splenic rupture was secondary to invasive leukemia. His parents are told about his new cancer diagnosis and opt for aggressive therapy despite a poor prognosis, hopeful for the best. John’s siblings draw a “Get Well Soon” card, showing John fighting off bad guys, which gets hung on the door of his ICU room.

One week into his chemotherapy, while still mechanically ventilated, John develops fever, abdominal distention, and shock. He is urgently taken to the OR where exploratory laparotomy reveals a gastric perforation and necrotic bowel. Pathology reveals disseminated murcormycosis (rhizopus) infection. In a family meeting, the infectious disease specialists tell John’s parents that without treatment, the infection will surely overwhelm him, but that the side effects of treatment are also likely to cause a high risk of death as well. The oncologist expresses similar concerns in regard to continuing John’s chemotherapy. John’s dad says, “It seems like no matter what we choose, John’s going to die. Soon. How can we possibly decide what to do next?”

You help stabilize John so that he can discuss with his family. You hear John say, “I’m not strong enough. I can’t fight anymore. Please let me die.”

John dies in the PICU 3 days later comfortably, surrounded by friends and family.

You are post-call, and find out about John’s death the next morning when his name is no longer on your daily patient list.

Sample Points for Discussion

* + - What parts of John’s story affect you most?
      * What part of his story is most difficult?
      * What aspect of his story is satisfying?
    - How would you feel since you, his primary resident, were not in the hospital when John passed away?
    - How could you begin to process John’s death?
      * Would you consider attending John’s funeral?
      * How else could you begin the grieving process if you didn’t have a chance to say goodbye?
