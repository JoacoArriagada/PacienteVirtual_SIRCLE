1. It is your first week of intern year. You admit Kayla, a 15 year old female admitted for evaluation of supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. After a thorough history and physical, you consider infections, HIV and malignancy highest on your differential. While awaiting further testing, Kayla is isolated with airborne precautions in case of TB. She undergoes lymph node biopsy on a Friday afternoon.

On Sunday morning rounds, you find out that Kayla and her family pray every night that Kayla has tuberculosis. The pathology returns Monday morning as lymphoma.

You, and your attending and the oncology fellow sit the family down to explain the results. They are tearful but resolute. You then accompany the family to Kayla’s room to tell her the results. On hearing the word cancer, she breaks down and shakes uncontrollably. She cries, supported by her family for several minutes. When she looks up, she locks eyes with you, her doctor, and asks “Am I going to die?”

Sample Points for Discussion:

* What was the most difficult aspect of caring for Kayla?
  + How do you balance honesty and hope when giving bad news?
* What was the most satisfying part of caring for Kayla?
* What did you learn by caring for her?
