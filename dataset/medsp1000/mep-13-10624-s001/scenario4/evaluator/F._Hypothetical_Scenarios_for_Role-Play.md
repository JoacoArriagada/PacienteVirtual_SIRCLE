1. Eduardo is an 11 year old male with acute alteration in mental status, who was admitted to your team several days ago by a colleague. You are the overnight resident taking care of him and many other patients. On signout, you are told that Eduardo is quite agitated, flails around, and requires an enclosed bed due to safety concerns. He is very difficult to examine due to his agitation and altered mental status. Testing for meningitis is negative, but anti-NMDAR antibody testing is still pending.

Two hours into your shift, you get a phone call from the bedside RN that Eduardo is “too wild” to keep the cardio-respiratory monitor leads on. You tell her, “Its ok. Let’s check his vitals every 4 hours then.”

Three hours later, a code blue is called from his room. You rush to the bedside and Eduardo blue, pulseless and apneic. You immediately begin chest compressions. Eduardo’s mother is frantic, shouting between wails that she was sleeping when she “heard a gurgling noise and looked over. His face was totally blue, and so I pressed the code button for help”.

The code team arrives, and you rotate with another resident performing chest compressions. After many rounds of chest compressions, and several doses of epinephrine, Eduardo remains pulseless, without detectable electrical activity. Your arms aching, you are instructed by the code captain to discontinue resuscitation. He is pronounced dead.

The patient’s mother is wailing in the doorway, supported by the Social Worker, “If only I hadn’t been asleep! I could’ve seen him getting worse! What happened to my son? Why didn’t anyone know this was going to happen?”

Sample Points for Discussion:

* What was the most difficult aspect of caring for Eduardo?
  + Do you think his difficult examination contributed to how he was cared for?
  + Should you feel guilty for taking off Eduardo’s monitor?
* What positive aspects can you take from caring for Eduardo?
* What did you learn by caring for Eduardo?
* Do you believe your future clinical practice will be changed by this experience?
