| **PATIENT NAME: Ashley Smith**  **PATIENT AGE: 22**  **CHIEF COMPLAINT: Rupture of membranes at term – breech presentation** | | | | | | | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | | Ashley is a 22-year-old G1 female at approximately 38 weeks gestation by last menstrual period who presents with rupture of membranes; cursory investigation by the obstetrician on call finds breech presentation without active labor, reassuring fetal heart tones, and glucose of 442. The obstetrician pushes for urgent cesarean section for breech presentation. After additional studies are made available, the patient’s condition of diabetic ketoacidosis becomes apparent. The learner requests that the non-emergent cesarean section be delayed for medical optimization, at which point the obstetrician requests help with medical management of diabetic ketoacidosis. After the learner begins appropriate therapy including fluid resuscitation, electrolyte replacement, insulin infusion, and dextrose supplementation, the glucose level falls to 175 with an anion gap of 19. The obstetrician again requests to go to cesarean section, to which the learner responds that resuscitation is not complete until the bicarbonate rises and the anion gap closes. However, during this discussion, the patient’s nurse informs the team of severe fetal bradycardia, after which the cesarean section is then deemed an emergency. The learner’s anesthesia plan is discussed (general anesthesia, rapid sequence induction, continued fluid resuscitation and insulin and electrolyte management), and the cesarean section proceeds uneventfully until the infant is delivered. The infant is found to be meconium-stained and have a low Apgar score; the learner is then asked to resuscitate the infant, which he or she does after quickly creating a plan to watch over the anesthetized mother. Despite appropriate airway management, the infant’s Apgar scores remain low. Upon investigation, the infant is found to be hypoglycemic; he is treated with dextrose and improves, triggering the end of the case. | | | | | |
