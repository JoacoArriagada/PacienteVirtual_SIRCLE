**Appendix C: Critical Actions Checklist \_ DKA in the Obstetric Population**

One route for optimal management of the simulated patient takes the following steps (check as completed):

□ Request additional information such as laboratory studies and pertinent history

□ Calculate an anion gap based on data provided

□ Diagnose longstanding diabetes based on Hb A1C of 11.3, anion-gap metabolic acidosis based on ABG, and DKA based on anion gap of 24 and B‑hydroxybutyrate of 3.3

□ Inform the obstetrician on call that the patient is not medically optimized for non-emergent surgery

□ Suggest strategies for DKA management including the following:

□ Two large bore IV lines, arterial line to guide resuscitation

□ Aggressive fluid resuscitation given the patient’s significant fluid deficit

□ Potassium replacement when the patient’s potassium is less than 5.5

□ Insulin infusion as long as the patient’s potassium is greater than 3.3 (insulin should be held for low potassium)

□ Addition of a dextrose source when the patient’s blood glucose is greater than 250

­□ Continued resuscitation until the patient’s anion gap closes

□ Inform the obstetrician that, even though the glucose is improved, resuscitation is not complete and the patient is not optimized for non-emergent surgery until the anion gap improves

□ Outline the plan for anesthetic management for the emergent cesarean section, including the following:

□ General anesthesia

□ Rapid sequence induction

□ Continued fluid resuscitation throughout the procedure

□ Continued management of insulin and electrolytes during the procedure

□ Create a plan for care of the anesthetized mother during resuscitation of the depressed neonate

□ Manage the airway in the meconium-stained neonate with poor Apgar score using ‘suction, intubate, suction, ventilate’ while avoiding stimulation

□ Assess for hypoglycemia in the neonate of a diabetic mother by checking a blood glucose level

□ Supplement for neonatal hypoglycemia with dextrose
