**Instructor’s Guide for OSCE Station: New HIV Diagnosis**

**Resource Files:**

The document titled “HIV Diagnosis Complete Case.docx” contains the following materials:

1. **Cover Sheet** – provides the objectives and checklist of materials needed
2. **Examiner instructions**

* To be given to the faculty examiner in advance of the OSCE session
* Provides explicit instructions for the faculty examiner regarding examiner tasks, structure of the OSCE station, and time breakdown of the components of the OSCE station
* Provides background material on the topic with references
* Provides a synopsis of the OSCE case and resident tasks

1. **Resident Instructions**

* To be placed on the door of the exam room for the resident to read before starting the OSCE station. Another copy can be given to the resident for use during the OSCE station
* Describes the resident’s role and tasks during the OSCE station
* Provides limited background information about the standardized patient
* Includes time breakdown of the components of the OSCE station

1. **Standardized Patient Script**

* To be given to the standardized patient and standardized patient trainer prior to the OSCE session for training
* Provides detailed background information about the standardized patient’s medical, social, sexual, and family history
* Provides detailed guidance on how to respond emotionally to news of positive HIV test and what questions to ask of resident

The document titled “HIV Diagnosis Checklists.docx” contains the following materials:

1. **Examiner checklist**

* To be given to the faculty examiner prior to the OSCE, and completed during or immediately after the OSCE
* Evaluates history-taking and counseling skills by assessment of directly observable behaviors
* Evaluates clinical decision-making by assessing ability to select appropriate baseline laboratory tests for patient with newly diagnosed HIV

1. **Standardized Patient checklist**

* To be given to standardized patient prior to OSCE, and completed immediately after OSCE
* Uses the “University of Illinois at Chicago Communication and Personal Skills Scale” to assess communication and interpersonal skills of residents during OSCE interaction

**Conceptual Background**

Receiving a new diagnosis of HIV is a traumatic experience for patients, who commonly exhibit shock, denial, anger, or despair as initial reactions. Delivering this diagnosis in a sensitive and compassionate way, assessing and validating the emotional response of the patient, and evaluating for adequacy of the patient's social support network are important skills for any primary care clinician. Patients also have varying pre-existing conceptions of the natural history and treatment options for HIV, as well as wide-ranging concerns regarding how this diagnosis will affect their lives, all of which need to be factored into the initial discussions about their new diagnosis. An understanding of an appropriate initial laboratory work-up is also important to clarify the degree of immunosuppression, assess for viral resistance or comorbidities which could influence decisions on antiretroviral therapy, and evaluate for sexually transmitted or blood-borne co-infections, such as syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia, or viral hepatitis.

Additionally, the task of relaying an HIV diagnosis is relevant to residents beyond internal medicine or providers involved in longitudinal care, given recent released screening guidelines and an incidence (albeit stable) of 50,000 new HIV infections each year.

**Development of Materials**

This OSCE station was designed as a component of a multi-station OSCE for residents in the HIV Primary Care Track in the Internal Medicine residency program at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center. The OSCE was exclusively intended for formative feedback. The station itself was written by the primary author, an instructor in infectious diseases and general medicine, as part of a medical education fellowship. The topic was chosen to align with the overall curricular objectives for the HIV Primary Care track, which are to train residents in providing primary care to HIV and LGBT patients. All elements of the case were reviewed and revised in an iterative fashion by the leadership committee of this track, which includes specialists in HIV primary care and an educator/hospitalist.

We selected the "University of Illinois at Chicago Communication and Personal Skills Scale" for the SP instrument upon review of multiple checklists to assess communication skills. This published and validated instrument by Dr. Rachel Yudkowski et al. was developed using 79 internal and family medicine residents participating in an OSCE designed to assess communication and interpersonal skills. The authors conducted reliability and validity testing, including factor analysis, and the scale was found to have a medial internal consistency (coefficient alpha) of 0.91.

**Implementation**

Training of the SP, lasting about four hours, was conducted by SP educators at the University of Massachusetts Standardized Patient Program.

The station was piloted with a faculty examiner and standardized patient, using a medical resident not involved in the HIV primary care track. This provided a good opportunity to clarify any confusion about the station, trouble-shoot any logistical problems, and fine-tune any aspects of the standardized patient performance.

Prior to implementing this OSCE station, the faculty examiner and standardized patient received their materials, each receiving opportunities to discuss the case, roles/functions of each person, general guidance on how to give effective feedback and how to complete their respective checklists. Specifically for faculty members, we provided a training session wherein we reviewed principles of effective feedback and advised faculty examiners to identify and discuss with the resident two or three aspects of the case in which they performed well, and two or three areas for improvement. We have adopted this stance based on our experiences that learners may have difficulty absorbing feedback on more than a small number of important points.

The cost of running this single case within the context of an 8-case OSCE was estimated at about $1800, which included 4 hours of SP training, SP educator time, fixed administrative costs (independent of the number of cases), travel, piloting and actual implementation.

Of note, this OSCE station also includes a faculty examiner physically present in the room as part of the interaction due to the fact that our SPs did not possess the clinical background knowledge of HIV care to give effective feedback on content issues. Furthermore, we held the OSCE in situ in actual clinic rooms to convey authenticity, without the infrastructure necessary for synchronous or asynchronous faculty observation. Variations on the case may entail the SP exclusively providing feedback on some components of the checklist, with faculty review of a videotape of the encounter or through a one-way mirror, as physical infrastructure permits.

**Materials**

* Stopwatch
* Pens/clipboards for resident, examiner, and standardized patient
* Hand sanitizer
* Resident instructions
* Standardized Patient Checklist
* Examiner Checklist

**Duration of OSCE Station:**

Disclosure of positive HIV result and counseling: 14 minutes

Selecting desired laboratory tests: 2 minutes

Feedback from Standardized Patient: 4 minutes

Feedback from Faculty Examiner: 5 minutes

**Total time:** 25 minutes
