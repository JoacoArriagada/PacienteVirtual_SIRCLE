# Summary of Case

Patient presented with history and ECG findings consistent with Wellens'syndrome. He is noted to have a positive troponin and can initially be treated as NSTEMi with consult to Cardiology for early PCl given deep anterior T wave inversion or Wellens'T waves.

· During his emergency department stay, his LAD lesion as suggested by anteriorTwave inversions progressed and he suffered aSTEMI and was taken to the cath lab.

·At time of catheterization he was found to have 10o% occlusion of the proximal-mid LAD and a drug eluding stent was placed.

# Critical Actions

Perform focused history and physical exam

Order STAT ECG and interpret appropriately

Recognize acute change in ECG #1 (Wellens' or TW inversions in anterior leads) in comparison to old ECG . Recognize need for repeat ECG in patients with changes in condition Recognize STEMl in ECG #2

·Order aspirin 325mg

Serial assessment of patient and repeat ECG when chest pain returns Activate Cath Lab and start appropriate anticoagulation

# Acute Coronary Syndrome

Definition: spectrum of acute cardiac disease including unstable angina, STEMI and NSTEMI

·NSTEMl: cardiac enzyme elevation with ischemic symptoms or ECG changes

·Etiology: decreased coronary blood flow

Ruptured atherosclerotic plaque (Type I)

·Demand ischemia (Type Il)

·Epidemiology:

·Median age 68

·Male to female ratio 3:2

# ACS Diagnostic Principles

ECG Within 10 minutes of ED arrival (AHA/ACC Recommendations Class I,Level C)

· Compare to prior ECG

ECG with any change in clinical status (Class l, Level C)

Laboratory evaluation including CBC, BMP, serial troponin (Class l, Level A)

With contemporary troponin values, CK-MB and myoglobin not useful (Class II,LevelA)

Chest XR to evaluate for other causes of chest pain

# ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction

Persistent ST elevation or anterior ST depression · New ST elevation at the J point in at least 2 contiguous leads of greater than or equal to 2mm in men or 1.5mm in women in leads V2-V3 and greater or equal to 1mm in other contiguous chest or limb leads

Emergent reperfusion-primary PCl recommended ·Goal of first medical contact to time of PCl is less than 90 minutes

If patient is at non-PCl-capable hospital and anticipated time to PCl facility exceeds 120 minutes fibrinolytic therapy should be used in the absence of contraindications

# Medical therapies

Aspirin 162-325mg at presentation,81-162mg daily (AHA/ACC Recommendations Class I,Level A)

·Reduced mortality and decreased Ml recurrence

Oxygen if oxygen saturation <90%, respiratory distress or high risk for hypoxemia (ClassI,v)

Nitroglycerine sublingual every 5 minutes x 3 (Class I,Level C) · IV NTG for persistent ischemia, HF or hypertension (Class I, Level B) ·Contraindicated if recent use of phosphodiesterase inhibitor (ClassIllHarm,Level B)

Morphine for persistent pain (Class Ilb, Level B)

Beta blockers within first 24 hours in the absence of HF (ClassI, Level A)

Calcium channel blocker if beta blocker contraindication and no HF (Class l, Level B)

High intensity statin therapy (Class I, Level A)

ACE should be started within hospitalization if hypertension, DM, stable CKD or LVEF less than 40%

# Anticoagulation and Antiplatelets

·Aspirin

P2Y12 inhibitor therapy (clopidogrel or ticagrelor) (AHA/ACC Recommendations ClassI,Level B)

Loading dose and maintenance with early invasive or ischemia-guided therapy P2Y12 inhibitor continued for 12 months in post-PCl coronary stents Ticagrelor in preference to clopidogrel for early invasive

SC enoxaparin for duration of hospitalization or until PCl performed (Class l, LevelA)

Alternatively IV unfractionated heparin 48hr or until PCl performed (Class l, LevelB)

If early invasive strategy may alternatively use bivalirudin

Thrombolytics should NOT be used in NSTEMI (ClassIl, Level A)

# Wellens' Diagnostic Criteria

Deeply inverted or biphasic T waves in V2-3 (can include V1-6)   
Isoelectric or minimally elevated ST segments < 1 mm   
No precordial Q waves   
Preserved R wave progression   
History of angina   
ECG changes present in pain free state   
Normal or slightly elevated cardiac markers

# Wellens treatment

Admit to telemetry monitored floor Early PCI If NSTEMl, begin anticoagulation Don't stress test a patient with Wellens'!
