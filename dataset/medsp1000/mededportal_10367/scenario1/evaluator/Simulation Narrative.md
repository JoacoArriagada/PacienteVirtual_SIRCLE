II. TARGET AUDIENCE: Emergency Medicine Residents, Internal Medicine Residents, Emergency Physician Assistants, Nurse Practitioners, Medical Students, and Emergency Medical Service Providers

# III. LEARNING OBJECTIVES:

# a. Primary Learning Objectives

i. Describe and identify typical ECG findings of ACS including STEMI and   
NSTEMI   
ii. Identify anterior t wave inversions as sign of critical LAD disease   
iii. Create an evaluation and treatment plan for patients with acute coronary   
syndrome   
iv. Order repeat ECGs for patients with suspected ACS   
v. Assess prior ECGs for patients with chest pain

# b. Secondary Learning Objectives

i. Communicate clearly with team members ii. Divide and assign tasks appropriately within the team iii. Perform a focused history and physical exams

# IV. Critical Actions

a. Perform focused history and physical exam   
b. Order STAT ECG and interpret appropriately i. Recognize acute change in ECG #1 (Wellens’ or TW inversions in anterior leads) in comparison to old ECG ii. Recognize need for repeat ECG in patients with changes in condition iii. Recognize STEMI in ECG #2   
c. Order aspirin $3 2 5 \mathrm { m g }$   
d. Serial assessment of patient and repeat ECG when chest pain returns   
e. Activate Cath Lab and start appropriate anticoagulation

V. ENVIRONMENT:

a. Lab set up – Emergency department bed in simulation center with cath lab, true emergency department setting b. Manikin set up – The manikin will be a clothed adult size manikin dressed like a middle aged man. At the beginning of the simulation, there are no monitor leads on the patient, no IV access has been obtained, and the patient is not on oxygen. Alternatively, an actor (a resident, nurse, or standardized patient) could role play as the patient. c. Props – Available for use will be a heart monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, and pulse oximeter. Supplemental oxygen by nasal cannula and face mask will be available. There will be a medicine cart or tray with a full complement of vasoactive agents, ACLS medications, and medicines necessary for sedation, rapid sequence intubation, and analgesia.

In addition, a fully stocked code cart with defibrillator will be available for use, along with a selection of direct laryngoscopes and intubation supplies and other emergent procedural equipment.

d. Audiovisual – (See Appendix A) Available for review when asked for by participants will be a Chest XR, ECGs, and laboratory studies.

e. Distractors – Distractors can be added at the facilitator’s discretion. Choices could include an intrusive or difficult family. However, as written, there is no need for distractors as the goal is simply to manage a patient with acute coronary syndrome, recognize deep t wave inversions as critical ECG findings, and recognize STEMI.

VI. ACTORS: Roles can vary depending on the number of participants in the simulation session and on potential actor availability. This case can be implemented using a single participant and one facilitator/operator who can provide oral feedback via overhead audio and play additional historical or consultant roles via voice only. Realism can be enhanced by using physical actors, such as nursing staff members, but these are not required to successfully implement this case. Roles may be played by resident or faculty physicians, nurses, or medical students.

a. Primary physician: The main scenario participant will act as the primary physician and   
perform the primary evaluation including obtaining a history, conducting a physical exam,   
and ordering any necessary medications or other interventions. The primary physician can   
perform any needed procedures, or can delegate these to other providers if multiple   
participants are present.   
b. Secondary physicians: Other participants in the scenario will serve as collaborators and   
assistants for any necessary procedures and consultants.   
c. Nursing staff: The role of the nurse will be to administer medications, verify orders, and perform other tasks as directed by the physicians. The nurse can also make observations as needed to stimulate case progression.

# Scenario Branch Points

a. Immediate Evaluation

i. ECG ii. IV, cardiac monitor iii. Obtain rapid focused history of present illness and risk factors iv. Obtain rapid focused physical examination v. Obtain CBC, chemistries, coagulation studies, troponin vi. Order Chest XR vii. Order aspirin $3 2 5 \mathrm { m g }$

b. Initial and Prior ECG Interpretation:

i. ECG #1

a. Normal sinus rhythm, Deep T wave inversions in anterior leads (V3-V6) b. Recognition of Deep T wave inversions in setting of recent chest pain with patient currently chest pain free as critical LAD stenosis consistent with Wellens’ Syndrome ii. Request previous ECG: Nonspecific T wave changes

c. Differential Diagnosis: The physician managing this case may consider the differential of chest pain, including acute coronary syndrome, unstable angina, pericarditis, pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax, aortic dissection, esophageal perforation, pneumonia, GERD, musculoskeletal.

d. Evaluation after ECG interpretation:

i. If Wellens’ deep T waves recognized, participant should consult cardiology and initiate anticoagulation with plan of early PCI

e. Changes in patient condition: Patient will initially present without chest pain. As evaluation and testing begin (basic labs, ECG, and chest XR) patient will develop new chest pain.

i. If participants have recognized Wellens’ anterior T waves, chest pain may develop after they report recognition and as they are calling Cardiology.   
ii. If the participants have not recognized Wellens’ Syndrome or t wave inversions in precordial leads as concerning finding a. During patient reassessment - patient will report worsening chest pain b. Patient is not reassessed - nursing may prompt participants by reporting that patient has new chest pain

f. Repeat ECG and interpretation:

i. If participant fails to order repeat ECG with development of chest pain the nurse will prompt the participant by asking, “Do you want a repeat ECG?”   
ii. Repeat ECG shows ST elevations in the anterior leads   
iii. Failure to recognize STEMI on ECG or failure to recommend cardiac catheterization or thrombolysis results in prompting regarding the ECG changes. For example, “What do you think the ECG shows, doctor?” and “Those look like ST Elevations. Do you want me to activate the cath lab?” When the simulation is over, the participants will learn that the patient has a decreased ejection fraction of $2 0 \%$ , demonstrating that potential delays in STEMI diagnosis result in worsening cardiac ischemia and function.

g. Administration of medication: The patient should be given aspirin $3 2 5 \mathrm { m g }$ . When chest pain returns, the patient should receive SL Nitroglycerin. When Wellens’ or STEMI are recognized, should give anticoagulants (i.e. enoxaparin, unfractionated heparin or bivalrudin, and consider loading dose of clopidogrel $6 0 0 \mathrm { m g } ,$ )

h. Consultation of specialist: Participant may consult cardiology after obtaining initial history/physical and ECG showing Wellens’ T waves (preferred) or at time of recognition of STEMI.

i. Non-recommended actions: The participant should not order stress testing on patient with Wellens’ T Waves. If stress testing is ordered, nurse may prompt participant by asking, “Are you sure you want a stress test with an ECG that looks like that?” If participant continues to order stress test, the simulation will end with report that patient went into ventricular fibrillation arrest.

# j. Case outcome:

In the Cath lab, a $1 0 0 \%$ occlusion of the mid LAD was found and a drug-eluting stent was placed.   
The patient recovered satisfactorily and was discharged home after a short hospital stay.

# VIII. DEBRIEFING:

A post-case debriefing conference is recommended immediately following the end of the scenario including the following elements:

Open-ended questions by facilitator – Begin the session with a question to the primary participant about what was learned during the scenario. This often leads to extensive participant-led discussion that will touch on many of the major issues in the case. Invite any secondary participants and/or observers to comment constructively about the case.

Brief didactic review – Materials for review after the scenario have been provided in Appendix B. They address several major teaching points related to the case. However, individual participants may raise additional questions.

Formal participant evaluation – A formal patient evaluation tool is attached.

a. Debriefing materials – See Appendix B

b. Rules for the debriefing – Often an open-ended question to the participants will provoke a discussion of aspects of the case management that were good, and those areas where improvement can be made. Encourage your participants to discuss the case management decisions in a non-judgmental way. When this case is used for experiential learning (without formal participant evaluation) discussions may vary as compared to situations where formal feedback or grading is anticipated. We have found that placing an emphasis on the learning that occurs from experiencing a case like this is very effective, rather than focusing on the “right answer” or whether the specific management decisions were precisely as the leader would have performed.

c. Questions to facilitate the debriefing. For answers, refer to Appendix B.

i. What did you learn?   
ii. What improvements would you make in the management of the case?   
iii. How did you interpret the ECG?   
iv. What is Wellens’ Syndrome and how does it relate to our patient?   
v. Explain briefly the physiology behind the ECG changes seen.   
vi. What are the two patterns that can be seen on ECG while the patient is without pain? vii. Where would reciprocal ECG changes be found during the STEMI?
