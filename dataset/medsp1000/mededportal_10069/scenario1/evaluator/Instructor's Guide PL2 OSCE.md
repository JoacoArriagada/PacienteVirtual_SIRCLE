# #3. Telephone advice

A. Subject: Telephone management of a child with petechiae.

B. Question: This will be a telephone triage. It is Monday night, midnight, and you are on-call for your private practice. The mother/father of an eleven-month old child calls you complaining that her/his child has a fever of 103 degrees. You may ask any questions you feel are appropriate.

C. Set-up: The resident will ask a series of questions to an interviewer (i.e. the mother/father of the child in question). The interviewer should not state that the child has a rash unless specifically asked. If asked, the child can be irritable but mildly consolable, sleepy but arousable, refusing to eat with some signs of mild dehydration, a very mild URI, no vomiting, no diarrhea. Do not describe the rash to the resident unless specifically asked, and even then, try to allow the resident to correctly describe petechiae to the parent. If 3-1/2 minutes have passed and the resident still has not asked about a possible rash, the interviewer can state that the child also has a “rash”. Finally, at the end of the call, the resident should correctly tell the parent what to do (i.e. come in or go to the nearest ER immediately). If the resident sends the child to the ER without asking about the rash, have the parent ask the resident what the possible diagnosis might be and then allude to the fact that the child also has a rash.

Provide the interviewer with these instructions and a timer to know when 3-1/2 minutes have passed. Also provide interviewer with the scoring instructions below, as well as a list of residents in order to record their score.

D. Correct Answer: The child has petechiae and possible meningococcemia, and needs to be seen immediately.

# E. Scoring:

5 - Resident asks correct series of questions, determines that the child has a rash, determines that the rash is petechial, and has the child seen immediately.   
4 - Resident asks correct series of questions, determines that the child has a rash, but then does not recognize that the child needs to been seen immediately.   
3 - Interviewer has to state that the child has a rash (because the resident does not ask), after which resident determines that the child has petechiae and needs to be seen.   
2 - Interviewer has to state that the child has a rash (because the resident does not ask), after which resident still does not determine that the child has petechiae but does realize that the child needs to be seen.   
1 - Resident asks incorrect questions and arrives at incorrect diagnosis and management plan.
