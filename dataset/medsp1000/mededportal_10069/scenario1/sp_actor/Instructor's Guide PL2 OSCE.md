# #3. Telephone advice

A. Subject: Telephone management of a child with petechiae.

B. Question: This will be a telephone triage. It is Monday night, midnight, and you are on-call for your private practice. A parent of an eleven-month old child calls you complaining that the child has a fever of 103 degrees.

C. Set-up: The resident will ask a series of questions to an interviewer (i.e. the parent of the child in question). The interviewer should not state that the child has a rash unless specifically asked. If asked, the child can be irritable but mildly consolable, sleepy but arousable, refusing to eat with some signs of mild dehydration, a very mild URI, no vomiting, no diarrhea. Do not describe the rash to the resident unless specifically asked, and even then, try to allow the resident to correctly describe petechiae to the parent. If 3-1/2 minutes have passed and the resident still has not asked about a possible rash, the interviewer can state that the child also has a “rash”. If the resident sends the child to the ER without asking about the rash, have the parent ask the resident what the possible diagnosis might be and then allude to the fact that the child also has a rash.
