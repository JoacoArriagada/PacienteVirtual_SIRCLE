# #5. Apparent life threatening event (ALTE)

A. Subject: Identification of ALTE and its possible etiologies

B. Question: A one-week old infant (born at 35 weeks gestation) became cyanotic while in mother’s arms. Mother rushed him to your office. - What else do you want to ask the mother? - List four possible etiologies for the child’s cyanosis.

C. Set-up: The resident will ask a series of questions to an interviewer (i.e. the parent of the child in question). The following answers will be supplied if these questions are specifically asked by the resident: apnea, duration [apneic for 20 seconds] seizure activity or tone [hypotonic] need for intervention [mouth-to-mouth, shaking] relation to feed [two hours after] family history of SIDS [none]

Provide the interviewee with these instructions. Also provide interviewer with the scoring instructions below, as well as a list of residents in order to record their score.

# D. Correct Answer:

- The resident must ask the above questions appropriately. - ALTE or “Near-miss SIDS” - sepsis, seizure, RSV, anemia, congenital heart disease, apnea of prematurity, pneumonia, metabolic disorder, pertussis, upper airway obstruction, lung disease, gastro-esophageal reflux, IVH, battered child syndrome, idiopathic.

E. Scoring: two and one-half points for asking correct history questions; one-half point for correct name of event; and two points for four correct possible etiologies (1/2 point for each).
