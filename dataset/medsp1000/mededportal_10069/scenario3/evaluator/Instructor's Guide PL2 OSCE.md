# #16. Henoch-Schonlein Purpura

A. Subject: Explaining a diagnosis to parents.

B. Question: A five year-old boy named John comes into your office with complaints of rash, abdominal pain, and joint pain. On exam he has normal vital signs, mild abdominal pain, mild arthritis in his knees, and purpura along his lower extremities and buttocks. You think he has Henoch-Schonlein Purpura (HSP) and can be managed as an outpatient. You must now explain this diagnosis to his parents, including its features, possible complications /sequelae, and treatment.

C. Set-up: Interviewer acts as parent. Interviewer reads the question initially and may prompt (e.g. what is the possible treatment) if the resident forgets to discuss any issues.

# D. Correct Answer:

Resident should explain the diagnosis to the parents in an understandable manner, using lay terms, not medical terminology or abbreviations (unless he/she explains the abbreviations). The explanation should include its presentation, possible sequelae, and treatment.

The clinical syndrome includes arthralgia or mild arthritis, abdominal pain, and (nonthrombocytopenic) purpura, although not all the symptoms have to be present simultaneously. It occurs primarily in children. Etiology is unclear. Complications include intussusception and renal disease. The latter includes possible hematuria, proteinuria, glomerulonephritis, or nephrotic syndrome. A very small percentage can go on to have end stage renal disease. Periodic evaluation of urinalysis and BP measurement are recommended for 6 months to a year. Steroids have been shown to be beneficial mostly for the gastrointestinal symptoms. The natural history of HSP is variable but most children are well within 4 weeks. Recurrences of the symptoms, especially the rash, are common for many months.

# E. Scoring:

5 - Resident explains diagnosis in an understandable and thoughtful manner. Resident does not use confusing medical terminology. Resident is familiar with and is able to discuss the presentation, possible sequelae, and treatment of HSP.   
4 - Resident explains diagnosis in an understandable and thoughtful manner. Resident does not use confusing medical terminology. However, resident is not completely familiar with presentation, possible sequelae, and treatment of HSP.   
3 - Resident explains diagnosis in an understandable and thoughtful manner but occasionally uses confusing medical terminology. Resident is familiar with and is able to discuss the possible sequelae, and treatment of HSP.   
2 - Resident explains diagnosis in an understandable and thoughtful manner but occasionally uses confusing medical terminology. Also, resident is not completely familiar with the possible sequelae, and treatment of HSP.   
1 - Resident does not explain the diagnosis in an understandable or thoughtful manner.
