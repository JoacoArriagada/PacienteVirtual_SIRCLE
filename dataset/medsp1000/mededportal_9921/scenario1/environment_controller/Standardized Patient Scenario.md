**Standardized Patient Scenario**

A 28 year old African American female, Mrs. Anna Amarillo, brings 3 day old infant boy, Aaron, to the Pediatric clinic for his newborn follow-up.

Mom’s only concern is that Aaron’s skin appears to be yellow.
Opening line: “I’m just really worried about my baby being yellow.”

Mom and infant were discharged from the hospital yesterday. The nursery doctor told mom that Aaron was “jaundiced,” or yellow, but that the bilirubin level that measures jaundice was not dangerously high. Mom feels the baby is significantly more yellow now than when they were discharged from the hospital. Aaron is breastfeeding every 2-3 hours (day and night), feeding 10-15 minutes on each side. Mom feels her milk has come in, and that Aaron is satisfied after feeds. Aaron has had no fever, and has been sleeping well. He sleeps much of the day, but is alert and active around feeds; Mom has not appreciated any change in his behavior or activity level. He has made 8 wet diapers since discharge yesterday, and 5 yellow, soft, seedy stools. Aaron has had no fever, no vomiting or large spit-ups, no rash, no cough, no runny nose, and no other associated symptoms. Mom is cleaning the umbilical stump with alcohol three times daily.

**Family History:** Aaron was mom’s 3rd pregnancy. Both of her other children are now healthy, but *both had jaundice as infants and required treatment with lights / phototherapy* (this information should be volunteered when student asks about other children OR family history). Dad and sister have a history of asthma. No other medical problems run in the family. Parents, grandparents, and siblings are otherwise healthy. No known family history of blood disorders or liver problems.

**Birth History:** Mom had no problems with her pregnancy, and has no history of sexually transmitted infections. She began prenatal care 10 weeks into her pregnancy. Aaron was delivered at 36 weeks and 2 days by vaginal delivery. Mom’s blood type is A+, and her GBS (Group B strep) status was negative. Aaron’s birth weight was 7 lbs, 8 oz. Today his weight is 7 lbs, 4 oz.

**Past Medical and Surgical History:** Aaron has no known medical problems, and is taking no medications. He has no known drug allergies. Mom is taking a multivitamin, but no other medications. Aaron was circumcised yesterday, prior to discharge from the hospital. He also received his Hepatitis B vaccine prior to hospital discharge.

**Social History:** Aaron lives with his mom, Dad (age 30), brother (age 3), and sister (age 5) in a townhome in [local city]. Dad works as a teacher, mom stays home with the children. There are no smokers or pets in the home. Aaron sleeps on his back in a basinet in Mom’s room.

**Additional information:** Mom reports feeling tired, but denies depressive symptoms. [Mom should act tired during the encounter, prompting the student to inquire about her wellbeing.] She states that she is excited about Aaron’s birth. Maternal grandmother is helping out.

Triggers: Questions Mom will consistently ask the learner during the encounter. (These questions may be asked at any time during the encounter at the standardized patient’s discretion, but all questions should be asked at some point during the encounter.)

“Is there anything we need to do for the jaundice?”

“Is the jaundice my fault because I’m breastfeeding?”

“How do I know if Aaron is getting too jaundiced?”

“Will my baby need to be admitted to the hospital?”
