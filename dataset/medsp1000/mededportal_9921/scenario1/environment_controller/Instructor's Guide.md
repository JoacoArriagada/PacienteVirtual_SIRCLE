**Pediatric Standardized Patient OSCE Case
Infant with Jaundice**

Instructor Guide

**Patient Complaint/Concern:** Mrs. Amarillo is a 28 year-old African American female who presents to the pediatric clinic today with her 3 day old infant for his routine newborn follow-up visit. Mom is concerned that the baby’s skin looks yellow.

**Setting**: Outpatient clinic

**Diagnosis**: Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia

**Length of encounter:** 15 minutes with caregiver, followed by 10 minutes for the post-encounter activity

**Methods:**

Environment: If each student is to have more than one patient encounter, they should be directed by a proctor in their rotation through examination rooms. CSE examination rooms should resemble a typical outpatient exam room with the exception of inconspicuous observation equipment such as a video camera. The room should be equipped with a clock to aid in time management, though a proctor announcement when five minutes remain in the encounter is appropriate.

Tools and Equipment: As no physical examination component is required for this CSE, no physical examination tools are required. Past CSE cases at BSOM have utilized a doll to simulate an infant in the exam room, however students provided feedback that this was disconcerting and detracted from the realism of case. As a result of this feedback, the authors have not utilized dolls in this CSE case, and do not feel that a simulated infant is required to successfully implement the case.

Post-encounter Activity for the Learner: Please see the post-encounter activity resource for more information. Immediately following the standardized patient encounter, each student should proceed to an assigned workstation in a proctored area to complete the post-encounter activity. Each workstation should be equipped with a copy of the post-encounter reference sheet that is not to be removed from the workstation. Students are allowed 10 minutes to complete the activity, with a 5 minute time warning provided by the proctor.
