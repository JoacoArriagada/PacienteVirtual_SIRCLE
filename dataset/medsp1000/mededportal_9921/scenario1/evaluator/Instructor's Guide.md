**Pediatric Standardized Patient OSCE Case
Infant with Jaundice**

Instructor Guide

Student performance for each case is evaluated in two ways: through standardized patient evaluation and through post-encounter activities. The standardized patient grades the student by using Yes or No checkboxes on a standardized patient evaluation form. The student is assigned a percentage grade for each of the graded cases; ex: 21 “Yes” responses out of 30 possible would give 70%. The evaluation items for each case are tailored to reflect necessary components of the history to be elicited and the psychosocial aspects of the case to be addressed. Interpersonal and communication skill attributes are also assessed for each case. These items are standardly employed by the Office of Clinical Skills, and are therefore utilized across all clerkships, with all standardized patient cases. Consistency of these items provides an additional resource for the Office of Student Affairs to assess for patterns of professional behavior if concern about a student arises during the third-year. The post-encounter activities for the different cases vary based on the knowledge-based goals for each case (examples: prescription-writing, order-writing, note-writing skills). Students are assigned a percentage for post-encounter activity items correctly completed. Students’ final OSCE grade is an average based on four components: the three graded standardized patient evaluations and the total post-encounter activity percentage, with each of these 4 percentages weighted equally.

**Goals and Objectives for this case** (with correlating LCME ED-2 Core Competencies). Goals 1-3 will be assessed by the standardized patient, while goals 4-6 will be assessed through the post-encounter activities.

1. Obtain and age- and complaint- specific focused history, including relevant PMHx, Fam Hx, Social Hx, Diet Hx, and ROS, in an efficient but through manner. (Interpersonal and Communication Skills, Medical Knowledge)
2. Engage with a pediatric patient’s caregiver in an empathetic and compassionate manner, addressing the caregiver’s concerns. (Professionalism, Interpersonal and Communication Skills, Patient Care)
3. Provide patient / caregiver education in a clearly understandable way. (Interpersonal and Communication Skills, Patient Care)
4. Use evidence-based resources by interpreting an algorithm and table based on medical literature in the context of patient care, and use this interpretation to develop a management / treatment plan. (Practice-Based Learning)
5. Developing a differential diagnosis for neonatal hyperbilirubinemia based on the patient history. (Medical knowledge)
6. Recognizing that kernicterus is the most concerning potential complication of untreated neonatal hyperbilirubinemia. (Medical Knowledge)

**Patient Complaint/Concern:** Mrs. Amarillo is a 28 year-old African American female who presents to the pediatric clinic today with her 3 day old infant for his routine newborn follow-up visit. Mom is concerned that the baby’s skin looks yellow.

**Setting**: Outpatient clinic

**Diagnosis**: Neonatal Hyperbilirubinemia

**Length of encounter:** 15 minutes with caregiver, followed by 10 minutes for the post-encounter activity

**Methods:**

Evaluation Tool for the Standardized Patient: Please see the standardized patient checklist resource for more information. Either a paper or computer-based assessment tool should be available to the standardized patient for completion immediately following the student encounter. Immediate completion of the evaluation allows for better recall. A checklist evaluation tool is utilized in for the CSE case, as there is evidence that these have greater inter-rater agreement than rating scales. (van der Vleuten & Swanson, 1990)

Post-encounter Activity for the Learner: Please see the post-encounter activity resource for more information. Immediately following the standardized patient encounter, each student should proceed to an assigned workstation in a proctored area to complete the post-encounter activity. Each workstation should be equipped with a copy of the post-encounter reference sheet that is not to be removed from the workstation. Students are allowed 10 minutes to complete the activity, with a 5 minute time warning provided by the proctor.
