**Simulation Scenario**

1. Title**: Increased intracranial pressure secondary to external ventricular drain obstruction in a teenager**
2. Target Audience: pediatric residents, pediatric intensive care nurses
3. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up
      1. Simulated hospital intensive care room with video and audio capture in suite, as available
   2. Manikin Set Up
      1. High-fidelity adult-sized mannequin (I.e., Laerdal © 3G SimMan) attached to monitor with automated non-invasive cuff pressure and arterial line tracing, rhythm strip electrocardiogram, PulseOx reading, heart rate, respiratory rate and intracranial bolt with intracranial pressure measurement, central line access
      2. External ventricular drain should be attached to the mannequin’s head with dressing to cover the catheter hub. Hang the external ventricular drain on an IV pole at 10cm above the midline of the ear with tubing attached.
      3. 1 peripheral intravenous catheter, central line at right femoral site
   3. Fully-stocked pediatric Code Blue cart and defibrillator with pediatric and adult pads
   4. Props
      1. “Patient chart”: binder with a history section, containing a brief admission note, including the chief complaint, history of present illness, past medical history, medications, and allergies.
4. Case Narrative (Please see below)

Requirements: Adult mannequin: 1 peripheral IV, 1 femoral central venous line, 1 intracranial pressure bolt, 1 external ventricular drain with drainage chamber on IV pole.

| **Simulation Lab – simulated hospital intensive care room where the scenario will be run; what participants will experience**  *Prior to entering Sim suite, participating residents and nurses receive the following from the patient’s chart:* “BL is a 16-year-old male with history of obstructive hydrocephalus status post ventriculoperitoneal shunt placement with multiple revisions in the past who is now status post externalization of ventriculoperitoneal shunt, post-operative day #1, secondary to shunt malfunction with contralateral intracranial bolt placement. He remains hospitalized awaiting internalization of his ventriculoperitoneal shunt later this week.  Overnight, his external ventricular drain drained 10cc per hour but had significantly decreased drainage over the last two hours.”  Past Medical History: obstructive hydrocephalus  Medications: none  Allergies: no known drug allergies”  BL is now complaining of headache and nausea.  (Patient weight = 55 kg)  Nurses are called to the patient’s room.  The residents are then called to the patient’s room.  BL is complaining of headache and nausea, becomes obtunded. |
