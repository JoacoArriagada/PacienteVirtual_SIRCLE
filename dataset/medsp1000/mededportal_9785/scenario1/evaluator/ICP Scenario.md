**Simulation Scenario**

1. Title**: Increased intracranial pressure secondary to external ventricular drain obstruction in a teenager**
2. Target Audience: pediatric residents, pediatric intensive care nurses
3. Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives
   1. Primary
      1. Recognize patients with increased intracranial pressure and understand the pathophysiology leading to Cushing’s triad
      2. Address underlying causes and activate tertiary measures for the treatment of elevated intracranial pressure
   2. Critical actions checklist
      1. State the patient has increased intracranial pressure physiology
      2. Start resuscitation by addressing the initial steps (Airway-Breathing-Circulation-Disability) from Pediatric Advanced Life Support guidelines
      3. Recognize that the external ventricular drain is not draining
      4. Lower the external ventricular drain to the floor
      5. Recognize that the external ventricular drain is obstructed
      6. Call for a STAT neurosurgical consultation
      7. Order a STAT head CT scan
      8. Provide measures to decrease intracranial hypertension:
         1. Raise the head of the bed
         2. Administer mannitol
         3. 3% normal saline bolus
         4. Administer a fluid bolus of normal saline
         5. Hyperventilate the patient with bag-mask ventilation
      9. Attempt to notify the family/guardian
4. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up
      1. Simulated hospital intensive care room with video and audio capture in suite, as available
   2. Manikin Set Up
      1. High-fidelity adult-sized mannequin (I.e., Laerdal © 3G SimMan) attached to monitor with automated non-invasive cuff pressure and arterial line tracing, rhythm strip electrocardiogram, PulseOx reading, heart rate, respiratory rate and intracranial bolt with intracranial pressure measurement, central line access
      2. External ventricular drain should be attached to the mannequin’s head with dressing to cover the catheter hub. Hang the external ventricular drain on an IV pole at 10cm above the midline of the ear with tubing attached; the tubing should be obstructed. Mannequin should also be lying supine with the head of the bed flat. On contralateral side, an intracranial bolt should be present, giving an intracranial pressure reading on the patient monitor.
      3. 1 peripheral intravenous catheter, central line at right femoral site
   3. Fully-stocked pediatric Code Blue cart and defibrillator with pediatric and adult pads
   4. Props
      1. Electrocardiogram showing sinus bradycardia
      2. “Patient chart”: binder with a history section, containing a brief admission note, including the chief complaint, history of present illness, past medical history, medications, and allergies.
5. Actors
   1. Roles: the voice of “BL” (the mannequin)
   2. Who may play them: facilitator (in control room)
6. Case Narrative (Please see below)

**PC** = Patient Care, **MK** = Medical Knowledge, **PBLI** = Practice Based Learning and Improvement, **SBP** = Systems-Based Practice, **P** = Professionalism, **ISC** = Interpersonal Skills and Communication

Requirements: Adult mannequin: 1 peripheral IV, 1 femoral central venous line, pupillary changes of eyes function “ON”, 1 intracranial pressure bolt, 1 external ventricular drain with drainage chamber on IV pole.

Overnight, his external ventricular drain drained 10cc per hour but had significantly decreased drainage over the last two hours.”  Past Medical History: obstructive hydrocephalus  Medications: none  Allergies: no known drug allergies”  BL is now complaining of headache and nausea. BL is complaining of headache and nausea, becomes obtunded.  Residents should do an initial exam, evaluate and declare patient status. Team leader should assign roles: airway, compressor, defibrillator, medications, recorder, person to read monitor, check pulse and confirm intravenous access (one peripheral IV, one central line).  **(MK, SBP, ISC)**  Team member should be assigned to inform guardian, if in the room.  **(ISC, P)**  Residents should recognize signs of increased intracranial pressure. Call Neurosurgery STAT. They should provide medical management with 3% normal saline, mannitol, etc. They can attempt to lower the EVD to see if it is working (it is clotted and so should not flow at all).  **(MK)**  Cardiopulmonary resuscitation should be initiated with two-person rescuer team, and code medications should be ordered.  **(MK, ISC)**  With vital sign improvement following medical management, residents should proceed with recovery treatment with non-rebreather face mask, begin discussing other possible underlying causes, send labs, call for repeat STAT CT scan, etc.  **(MK, SBP, ISC)** | **Control Room – where the simulation technician can manipulate the mannequin and/or vitals screen remotely**  ***Presentation:*** *External ventricular drain obstruction leading to increased intracranial pressure.*  *(approximately 20 min)*  *Monitor: Saturations, heart rate, electrocardiogram tracing (initially with sinus tachycardia, progressing to sinus bradycardia), blood pressure cuff pressures, respiratory rate, Pulse Ox, intracranial pressure monitor*  *Air compressor should be ON; ventilator tree should be screwed on oxygen port. External ventricular drain should be bandaged on left side of head. External ventricular drain should be attached to IV pole at 10 cm above the midline of the ear. Arm IV x1, central line x1 should be working.*  *Vitals: respiratory rate = 14, blood pressure = 115/72, heart rate = 110, O2 saturation = 99%. Pupils equally round and reactive. Intracranial pressure measurement 30. Unresponsive to questions/verbal commands.*  *Vitals change: Respiratory rate increases to 30. Blood pressure increases to 145/90, heart rate = 42, O2 sat = 98%. 1. Instructor’s Notes
   1. If the medical team fails to institute the appropriate interventions, proceed to worsen vitals, bradycardia, hypertension, tachypnea and promote signs and symptoms of uncal herniation.
   2. Do not provide “hints” along the way; this decreases the realism of the simulation and does not provide educational value.
   3. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer
      1. Monitor medical team interventions and prepare to adjust vitals “on the fly”.
   4. Scenario programming
      1. Follow script as written above.
   5. Optimal management path
      1. Follow script as written above, allow for impromptu changes during the simulation depending on the participants’ actions.
2. Debriefing Plan
   1. Method of debriefing
      1. Group, with video/audio replay, following debriefing models from Children’s Hospital of Boston and the Center for Medical Simulation, Massachusetts General Hospital (Rudolph, et al. Debriefing as Formative Assessment, Acad Emerg Med, Nov. 2008, Vol. 15, No. 11, p. 1010-1016.)
   2. Stages
      1. Reactions phase
         1. Allow participants chance to discuss feelings about the case, possibly one particular point in time to a specific event that was memorable and why
      2. Analysis phase
         1. Review of medical facts of the case: review the physiology of increased intracranial pressure, diagnosis and treatment regimens
         2. Video/audio replay: begin the video, facilitator should look for points to highlight Core Competencies and relate them to Topics of Crew Resource Management, including: Role Clarity, Closed-loop communication, Recognizing Support Systems and Resources and maintaining a Global Assessment
      3. Summary Phase
         1. Participants can review the lessons they have learned in the simulation, allowing for formative assessment
   3. Actual debriefing materials
      1. Consider a brief presentation of VP shunt malfunction for Analysis phase, handouts can include review articles:

* Birdsall C & Greif L. “How Do You Manage Extraventricular Drainage?” American Journal of Nursing. 1990; 90(11): 47-49.)
* Kochanek PM et al. “Guidelines for the Acute Management of Severe Traumatic Brain Injury in Infants, Children, and Adolescents.” Pediatric Critical Care Medicine. 2012; 13(p): S3-S6.
* Larsen GY & Goldstein B. “Consultation with the Specialist: Increased Intracranial Pressure.” Pediatrics in Review. 1999; 20(7): 234-9.
  1. Rules for the debriefing
     1. The facilitator must encourage participation from everyone
     2. Limit interruptions by others when someone is speaking
     3. Ensure a confidential and safe environment
     4. Allow time for participants’ responses
  2. Questions to facilitate the debriefing
     1. Reaction phase
        1. “Was there something in particular during this scenario you liked, did not like, would or would not repeat or think you or the team could have done differently?”
     2. Analysis phase
        1. “Can someone discuss what they think happened medically with this patient?”
        2. “How do you think the patient responded to your interventions?”
     3. During the video/audio replay: to point out particular events, consider asking:
        1. “I see here that \_\_\_ was done. I was wondering what you thought about this?”
        2. “At this moment, someone said \_\_\_. What was on your mind at this time?”
        3. “What do you think you or the team could have considered doing or doing differently here?”
  3. Numbers of participants
     1. Four residents with at least one senior resident
     2. Two pediatric intensive care nurses
  4. Performance expectations, anticipated management mistakes
     1. Facilitator can continue the scenario adapting to changes in medical team management
