**Simulation Scenario**

1. Title**: Increased intracranial pressure secondary to external ventricular drain obstruction in a teenager**
2. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up
      1. Simulated hospital intensive care room with video and audio capture in suite, as available
   2. Manikin Set Up
      1. High-fidelity adult-sized mannequin (I.e., Laerdal © 3G SimMan) attached to monitor with automated non-invasive cuff pressure and arterial line tracing, rhythm strip electrocardiogram, PulseOx reading, heart rate, respiratory rate and intracranial bolt with intracranial pressure measurement, central line access
      2. External ventricular drain should be attached to the mannequin’s head with dressing to cover the catheter hub. Hang the external ventricular drain on an IV pole at 10cm above the midline of the ear with tubing attached; the tubing should be obstructed. Mannequin should also be lying supine with the head of the bed flat. On contralateral side, an intracranial bolt should be present, giving an intracranial pressure reading on the patient monitor.
      3. 1 peripheral intravenous catheter, central line at right femoral site
   3. Fully-stocked pediatric Code Blue cart and defibrillator with pediatric and adult pads
   4. Props
      1. Electrocardiogram showing sinus bradycardia
      2. “Patient chart”: binder with a history section, containing a brief admission note, including the chief complaint, history of present illness, past medical history, medications, and allergies.
3. Actors
   1. Roles: the voice of “BL” (the mannequin)
   2. Who may play them: facilitator (in control room)
4. Case Narrative (Please see below)

Requirements: Adult mannequin: 1 peripheral IV, 1 femoral central venous line, pupillary changes of eyes function “ON”, 1 intracranial pressure bolt, 1 external ventricular drain with drainage chamber on IV pole.

| **Simulation Lab – simulated hospital intensive care room where the scenario will be run; what participants will experience**  *Prior to entering Sim suite, participating residents and nurses receive the following from the patient’s chart:* “BL is a 16-year-old male with history of obstructive hydrocephalus status post ventriculoperitoneal shunt placement with multiple revisions in the past who is now status post externalization of ventriculoperitoneal shunt, post-operative day #1, secondary to shunt malfunction with contralateral intracranial bolt placement. He remains hospitalized awaiting internalization of his ventriculoperitoneal shunt later this week.  Overnight, his external ventricular drain drained 10cc per hour but had significantly decreased drainage over the last two hours.”  Past Medical History: obstructive hydrocephalus  Medications: none  Allergies: no known drug allergies”  BL is now complaining of headache and nausea.  (Patient weight = 55 kg)  Nurses are called to the patient’s room.  The residents are then called to the patient’s room.  BL is complaining of headache and nausea, becomes obtunded. | **Control Room – where the simulation technician can manipulate the mannequin and/or vitals screen remotely**  ***Presentation:*** *External ventricular drain obstruction leading to increased intracranial pressure.*  *(approximately 20 min)*  *Monitor: Saturations, heart rate, electrocardiogram tracing (initially with sinus tachycardia, progressing to sinus bradycardia), blood pressure cuff pressures, respiratory rate, Pulse Ox, intracranial pressure monitor*  *Air compressor should be ON; ventilator tree should be screwed on oxygen port. External ventricular drain should be bandaged on left side of head. External ventricular drain should be attached to IV pole at 10 cm above the midline of the ear. Arm IV x1, central line x1 should be working.*  *Vitals: respiratory rate = 14, blood pressure = 115/72, heart rate = 110, O2 saturation = 99%. Pupils equally round and reactive. Intracranial pressure measurement 20.*  *NOTE: Nurses should be called to the bedside first, start initial assessment, should request physicians soon afterwards (i.e., call a Rapid Response).*  *Complaining of nausea and headache, beginning to wretch (vomitus on bed sheet).*  *Initial physical evaluation: assess airway, breathing, circulation, attach pads and assess IV status.*  *Over next 2 minutes, decrease heart rate to 65, increase blood pressure to 130/80, increase respiratory rate to 20. Intracranial pressure measurement 30. Unresponsive to questions/verbal commands.*  *Vitals change: Respiratory rate increases to 30. Blood pressure increases to 145/90, heart rate = 42, O2 sat = 98%. Pulses within normal limits, pupils sluggishly reactive. Intracranial pressure 35.*  *If residents lower external ventricular drain level or raise the head of the bed, there should be no change or drainage; vitals continue to worsen. Intracranial pressure increases to 40.*  *If residents do not provide medical management, at least two of the following: raising the head of the bed by 30 degrees, sedation, keeping head midline, hyperventilation, administration of 3% normal saline, and/or mannitol, the patient stops breathing, and pupils become dilated.*  *Residents to initiate bag mask ventilation with hyperventilation.*  *STOP scenario.* |

1. Instructor’s Notes
   1. If the medical team fails to institute the appropriate interventions, proceed to worsen vitals, bradycardia, hypertension, tachypnea and promote signs and symptoms of uncal herniation.
   2. Do not provide “hints” along the way; this decreases the realism of the simulation and does not provide educational value.
   3. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer
      1. Monitor medical team interventions and prepare to adjust vitals “on the fly”.
