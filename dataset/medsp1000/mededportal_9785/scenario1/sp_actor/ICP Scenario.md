**Simulation Scenario**

1. Title**: Increased intracranial pressure secondary to external ventricular drain obstruction in a teenager**
2. Actors
   1. Roles: the voice of “BL” (the mannequin)
   2. Who may play them: facilitator (in control room)
3. Environment
   1. Manikin Set Up
      1. High-fidelity adult-sized mannequin (I.e., Laerdal © 3G SimMan) attached to monitor with automated non-invasive cuff pressure and arterial line tracing, rhythm strip electrocardiogram, PulseOx reading, heart rate, respiratory rate and intracranial bolt with intracranial pressure measurement, central line access
      2. External ventricular drain should be attached to the mannequin’s head with dressing to cover the catheter hub.
4. Case Narrative (Please see below)

| **Simulation Lab – simulated hospital intensive care room where the scenario will be run; what participants will experience**  *Prior to entering Sim suite, participating residents and nurses receive the following from the patient’s chart:* “BL is a 16-year-old male with history of obstructive hydrocephalus status post ventriculoperitoneal shunt placement with multiple revisions in the past who is now status post externalization of ventriculoperitoneal shunt, post-operative day #1, secondary to shunt malfunction with contralateral intracranial bolt placement. He remains hospitalized awaiting internalization of his ventriculoperitoneal shunt later this week.  Overnight, his external ventricular drain drained 10cc per hour but had significantly decreased drainage over the last two hours.”  Past Medical History: obstructive hydrocephalus  Medications: none  Allergies: no known drug allergies”  BL is now complaining of headache and nausea.  (Patient weight = 55 kg)  BL is complaining of nausea and headache, beginning to wretch (vomitus on bed sheet).  Over next 2 minutes, BL becomes unresponsive to questions/verbal commands. |
