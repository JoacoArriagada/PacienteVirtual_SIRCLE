**Pediatric Pacifier Choking Scenario**

**Scenario Branch Points**

Optimal management path

* Group made aware that this patient has arrived
* Group quickly forms a “team” plan with a clear leader
* Participants recognize upper airway foreign body obstruction/ Severe respiratory distress
* Patient is left in a position of comfort, oxygen is provided by blow by
* IV is requested and placed by the ED nurse
* All necessary equipment is assembled at bedside for difficult airway – magill forceps, transtracheal jet ventilation set-up, glidescope, laryngoscope, 2 different endotracheal tube sizes based on broslow tape or weight indications
* Attempt is made to remove the pacifier – pt may be given ketamine for awake look
* The pacifier is not able to be removed – patient desaturates to 40%
* Patient is bagged, saturations range from 40-60%, heart rates falls after 2 minutes of hypoxia
* Patient deteriorates while in the ED, pOx2 starts to fall while the pt is on oxygen and the heart rate starts to fall as well – team decide to place transtracheal jet ventilation
* Anesthesiology, ENT is consulted for emergency airway management (over phone only)
* Transtracheal jet is placed, pOx2 rises to 95%
* The patient is then transferred to the OR with anesthesiology, pediatric ENT and pediatric surgery

Potential Complications

* Failure to form a team with a clear leader
* Placement of a mask, laying the patient down – the patient will desaturate to low 70’s, oxygen saturation will rise if pt is sat upright again and not agitated
* Failure to recognize severe hypoxia and implement treatment – oxygen, need for secure airway
* Failure to assemble the necessary equipment for transtracheal jet ventilation – if airway is not established quickly patient will arrest, pt will respond to one dose of epinephrine
* Failure to consult the appropriate services, pt will remained stable with the jet ventilation for 5 minutes and then start to desaturate again if a final disposition has not been made
