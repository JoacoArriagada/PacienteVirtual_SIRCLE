**Instructor’s Guide**

Pediatric Pacifier Foreign Body Simulation Case

**Purpose of this module:**

To teach health care professionals how to recognize the signs of upper airway obstruction and the methods of how to treat the obstruction. The educational objectives are below.

**Educational Objectives:**

Recognize and treat upper airway obstruction

1. Develop an approach to determine the cause of respiratory distress
2. Demonstrate an approach to managing a child with an upper airway foreign body
3. Demonstrate how to remove an upper airway foreign body
4. Demonstrate the use of the Magill forceps
5. Understand the importance of consulting ENT, surgery, anesthesiology early for upper airway foreign bodies
6. Create an algorithm for managing upper airway foreign bodies

Recognize and treat respiratory arrest

1. Demonstrate the ability to perform a pediatric resuscitation based on PALS algorithms

**Explanation of when to use resources:**

1. Advance preparation: No prerequisite preparation for its use. It is recommended that the instructor become familiar with the case scenario and the PowerPoint presentation.
2. How the authors implemented the module:

When the participants arrive they are split into groups of 3-4. The module is to be used in a simulation setting with an area able to gather to debrief. The module takes approximately 45 minutes to complete. Each group completes the simulation module (approximately 25 minutes). Once all of the groups have completed the module the case is discussed using the debriefing questions and the PowerPoint presentation is given. At the end of the discussion of the case each learner is evaluated on the evaluation sheet provided.

1. Purpose of each resource:
   1. Case outline provides a thorough description of the case flow, participant roles and potential pitfalls.
   2. Scenario template is an overall description of the intended audience, educational objectives, the H&P, the vitals signs changes based on the assumed critical changes, the planned progression of the scenario, the debriefing questions and the evaluation of the learner.
   3. History and Physical is the history that is given to the learners at the beginning of the simulation case, this includes the vital signs for the start of the case as well as the physical exam
   4. Triage document is the document that is in the simulation room when the learners arrive, this gives them some information about the case, the age of the patient, the vitals signs that have been obtained and where the patient is located within the hospital.
   5. Scenario branch points is the document that details how the case is intended to flow and what should happen in the case when the learners do not follow the intended sequence of events
   6. Chest radiograph showing a normal pediatric chest radiograph
   7. Laboratory results these are common labs that a learner may ask for in this scenario
   8. Debriefing questions are questions intended to cover the most important learning elements of this case
   9. Trainee evaluation is completed by the lead instructor as well as all other instructors present for the case, an evaluation form should be completed for all participants
   10. PowerPoint presentation is given after the discussion, this presentation is designed to solidify the knowledge that was gained by participating in this case scenario, there are notes for the lecturer for each slide

**Environment:**

1. Set- Up: An emergency room medical resuscitation bay. The learners may be notified that the patient will be arriving soon, and then the EMS providers arrive with the patient. The learners should identify that there is evidence of foreign body obstruction and instruct the nurse and other participants of what they need, including IV access, radiographs, labs and airway equipment.
2. Mannequin Set-up: High fidelity pediatric simulation mannequin with placement of piece of a pacifier (we used the green hospital pacifiers and cut it to fit into the airway) lodged just below the epiglottitis
3. Props: Laryngoscope, chest radiographs, laboratory results including a blood gas if requested, equipment necessary for placement of a transtracheal jet ventilation, magill foreceps.

Actors:

1. Child’s parent can be played by a case instructor, this person answers the participant’s questions about the history
2. ED nurse: typically played by one of our own peds ED nurses, may also be played by a case instructor. The nurse functions in a typical nursing role,
3. Any consults: the team may place a consult to any subspecialist; pediatric surgery, pediatric otolaryngology, pediatric anesthesiology. To place a consult they may call on the phone in the room, or say out loud to whom they want to consult. We have a phone in the simulation room that they can call a consult on. Typically the consulted team is not available forcing this team to perform the necessary procedures.

Instructors Notes:

1. Scenario programming: Optimal management path – the simulator is programmed to have decreased air entry bilaterally with stridor. It should be noted that it may be difficult for the learner to determine that the air entry is decreased bilaterally.
2. Major errors:
   1. Not suspecting foreign body: Instructor should be aware that this is likely going to happen and help direct the learners to the presence of a foreign body – may need to stress the sudden onset of difficulty breathing
   2. Not assembling standard intubation equipment and any difficult airway equipment that may be necessary when intubating a patient with an obstruction
   3. Inability to assembly the equipment needed for the placement of a transtracheal jet airway

**Past use of module:**

This module has been used with pediatric residents, emergency medicine residents, and pediatric emergency medicine fellows. Approximately, 40 learners have completed this module during 10 separate sessions.

**Lessons Learned:**

We have observed a few patterns between each group of learners:

1. Most groups initially thought that the “patient” had stridor from croup or reactive airway disease, they typically give “breathing treatments” to the patients but we indicate early that the there is no improvement during or after the treatments. The treatments may actually make the patient worse because they are upset getting the treatment.
2. Once the learner visualizes the foreign body obstruction and removes it with the Magill foreceps, the patient will not improve. We have found that the learners are confused as to why the patient is not improving. It is important to inform the learners right after they remove the FB that there is still more in there that they are not able to visualize. This will help steer them in the direction of doing a trans tracheal jet airway.
