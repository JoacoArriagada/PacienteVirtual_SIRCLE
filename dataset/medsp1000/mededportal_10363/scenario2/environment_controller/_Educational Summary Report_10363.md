# Methods

Within the approach to the PE, building a verbal rapport with the parent or caretaker is important for the toddler examination. The PE of the toddler might take place on the examination table but is often performed on the parent’s or caretaker’s lap. The PE of a toddler generally begins with the least invasive portions and then proceeds to the most invasive. It therefore starts with examination of the chest and ends with the head, eyes, ears, nose, and throat. There are several PE components that are unique to infants and toddlers, such as vital signs, palpation of the anterior fontanelle, measurement of head circumference, evaluation of the red reflex during the eye exam, examination of the ear and tympanic membrane, and rotation of the hips.

The class is divided into groups of 12 to 14 students. One facilitator is assigned to each student group.

There should be one toddler (13 to 24 months) with a parent assigned to each group. Parents are asked to bring the child’s most recent weight and length measurements to the workshop.

Our workshop takes place in our clinical skills center, a simulation space with multiple examination rooms. An exam room with enough space for an exam table and chairs for all students, the parent, and the faculty member is needed for each group.

Facilitators have 2 hours with the students. The first 15 minutes are used to set up the session with them.

The next 40 minutes are devoted to the examination of one toddler. When a parent and child enter the room, they are introduced to the group without revealing the age of the child.

Facilitators ask students to observe the child’s behaviors and demonstration of developmental milestones. Students can use the Denver Developmental Screening Test to identify milestones observed or reported by the parent when asked. At the end of the session, students will try to estimate the age of the child. The facilitator then examines the patient while discussing and describing the unique approach, techniques, and findings. Students may participate at the discretion of the facilitator and parent. The exam may be done on the exam table or the parent’s lap. Students may ask parents questions about the child. Facilitators ask the parent to report the child’s most recent weight and length.
