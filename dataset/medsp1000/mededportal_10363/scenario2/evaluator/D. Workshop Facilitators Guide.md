# Workshop	Facilitator’s	Guide

# Goal:

To	introduce	pre-clerkship medical	students	to	the	pediatric	physical	exam,	illustrating	the distinctions	from	the	adult	exam,	and	developmental	assessment	of	infants	and	young	children.

# Objectives:

By	the	end	of	this	session,	students	will	be	able	to:

1) Describe	the	different	approach	to	the	pediatric	physical	exam	based	on	the	child’s developmental	level.
2) Recognize	the	importance	of	observation	in	making	general	assessments	of	an	infant	and toddler.
3) Identify	unique	components	of	the	infant	and	toddler	physical	exam.
4) Perform a	basic	developmental	assessment	of	an	infant	and	toddler.
5) Interpret	growth	charts.

# Detailed	Workshop	Schedule:

2:30-2:45	pm			Setting-up	the	Session	with	Students Introduce	yourself	and	your	role	as	a	pediatrician

Discuss	with	the	group:

1. Differences	in	the	approach	to	the	pediatric	physical	exam,	in	infancy	and	toddlerhood
2. How	pediatricians	gather	important	information	from	observation	alone
3. Need	to	be	flexible	in	the	sequence	of	the	exam
4. The	distinctive	portions	of	the	exam	or	typical	physical	findings	in	infancy	and childhood-(fontanels, red	reflex,	rapid	HR,	hip	exam)
5. The	use	of	growth	charts
6. Use	of developmental	screening	and	surveillance	tools

# 4:10	– 4:30 pm			Post	Examination	Discussion	with	Students	and	Wrap-up

1. 		Discuss	observations	and	aspects	of	the	encounter
2. 		Reinforce	unique	approach	to	infants	and	toddlers	students	should	have	observed
3. 		Review	pediatric	specific	portions	of	the	exam
4. 		Guess	the	age	of	the	children	based	on	developmental	milestones
5. 		Plot	reported	measurements	on	the	growth	charts	and	assess	percentiles
