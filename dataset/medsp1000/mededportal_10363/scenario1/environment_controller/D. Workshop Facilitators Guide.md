# Workshop	Facilitator’s	Guide

# Description	of	Workshop	Activity:

This	half-day	“Examination	of	the	Infant”	Workshop	is	a	component	of	the	pre-clerkship medica students’	Introduction	to	Clinical	Medicine course.		Pediatric	faculty	facilitators	lead	an	interactive demonstration	of	the	infant	physical	exam,	including	growth	and	developmental assessments,	with	small	groups	of	students.

# The	Workshop	within	the	Context	of	the	Introduction	to	Clinical	Medicine	Course:

Differences	in	the	physical	examination	between	an	adult	and	an	infant	are	made	explicit	in this	workshop	and	include	elements,	such	as:

1) Approach	to	the	physical	examination – building	a	rapport	with	the	parent/s	or	caretaker	is	important for	the	infant	examination.	In	addition,	the	physical	examination	of	the	infant	might take	place	on	the	examination	table,	but	is	often	performed	on	the	parent’s	or	caretaker’s	lap.

2) Sequence - the	physical	examination	of	an	infant	generally	begins	with	examination	of	the	chest	and ends	with	the	HEENT.

3) Variations	of	the	physical	examination	techniques – There	are	several	physical	examination components	that	are	unique	to	infants	and	toddlers:

a. vital	signs	– different	parameters	for	heart	rate,	respiration	and	blood	pressure
b. palpation	of	the	anterior	fontanel
c. measurement	of	head	circumference
d. evaluation	of	the	red	reflex	during	the	eye	exam
e. examination	of	the	ear	and	tympanic	membrane
f. rotation	of	the	hips

# Detailed	Workshop	Schedule:

# 2:45	– 4:10	pm			Examination	of	the	Infant

1. 		Introduce	the	parent/s	and	infant	to	the	group.	Do	not	reveal	the	age	of	the child.

2. Ask	students	to	observe	the	child’s	behaviors	and	demonstration	of	developmental milestones-students	can	use	the	Denver	Developmental	Screening	Test	to	identify milestone	observed	or	reported	by	parent	when	asked.	At	the	end	of	the	session student	will	try	to	estimate	the	age	of	the	child	(explain	this	is	not	the	typical	use	of	a developmental	screening	tool).

3. Examine	the	patient,	while	discussing	and	describing	the	unique	techniques	and findings.	Students	may	participate	at	your	discretion	(feel	a	fontanelle,	measure	a	heart rate).

4. 		Students	may	ask	parents	questions	about	the	child.

5. 		Ask	parent	to	report	the	child’s most	recent	weight	and	length.	At	the	end	of	the	session, and	once	the	age	of	the	child	is	estimated	and	revealed,	students	should	plot	the measurements	on	the	growth	charts	to	determine	percentiles.
