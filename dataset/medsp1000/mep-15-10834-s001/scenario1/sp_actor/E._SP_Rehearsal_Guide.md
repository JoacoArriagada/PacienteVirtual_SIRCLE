**Appendix E: Standardized Patient Rehearsal Guide**

Instructions for use: These rehearsal guidelines are intended to help SP trainers, educators, and/or researchers prepare Standardized Patients; however, these statements are not intended to be uttered verbatim. Our strategy in developing this rehearsal guide was to help study team members (SPs included) refresh their memory following large gaps of time (weeks to months) between participants.

| **Standardized Patient Warm-up Questions** | |
| Opening Statement | PHYSICIAN  “Hi, I’m Dr. [Name], the nurse said you just fell, how are you doing?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“A little frustrated...I spent* ***ALL*** *night in the ER, and now this!”*  *------*  PHYSICIAN  “Can you tell me what happened?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *I was trying to get out of the bathroom and got tangled up in all this stuff.”* [gesture to IV pole/tubing] |
| Key Statements | PHYSICIAN  “Where is your pain? Can you point to it?  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“I have a lot of pain here in my chest* [guarding right side of chest]*... it hurts to take a full breath.”*  *------*  PHYSICIAN  “Were you lightheaded or dizzy at all before you fell?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“No, I don’t think so...I was trying to get out of the bathroom and got tangled up in all this stuff.”* [gesture to IV pole/tubing]  *-------*  PHYSICIAN  “Did you feel your heart racing before you fell?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“No.* [Frustrated]*Like I said...I was trying to get out of the bathroom and got tangled up.”* [gesture to IV pole/tubing]  *------*  PHYSICIAN  “Did you hit your head when you fell? Or anywhere else?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“No, I don’t think so...the side of my chest really took a hit.”*  *-------*  PHYSICIAN  “Do you know why you’re here?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“Yeah, I came to the hospital because of my foot, they told me I have an infection and I had to be admitted for antibiotics through the IV.”*  *-------*  PHYSICIAN  “Do you know what day it is?” [Or any other questions regarding mental status]  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“Yes”* [Respond appropriately]  -------  PHYSICIAN  “When was your last meal?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“I had a light lunch yesterday, I haven’t been eating much lately, I just haven’t been feeling well.”*  *-------*  PHYSICIAN  “How is your foot?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“It’s actually feeling a little better after they drained it.”*  ------  PHYSICIAN  “What did they do for you in the ER?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  *“Some procedure on my foot where they drained some fluid... Cellulitis, they said”*  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------  PATIENT WORSENS **[Starting around minute 5 through minute 10]**  At this point, it is getting harder and harder to breathe, the more you try to compensate (such as changing positions) you start to feel like each breath is harder than the one before until you eventually feel like you are trying to breathe through a straw. You are becoming scared for your life.    PHYSICIAN  “How are you doing now?”  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  [panting] *“I just feel really out of breath…I feel like I can’t... breathe...”*  *-----*  STANDARDIZED PATIENT  “*What’s wrong with me?”*  *“Am I going to be ok?”*  *-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------*  PATIENT DETERIORATES **[Minutes 10-15]**  At this point, you transition from being anxious and scared to becoming somnolent/lethargic and have decreased awareness and responsiveness. You should respond more slowly with brief responses.  *-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------*  PATIENT IMPROVES **[Post Needle or Tube Thoracostomy]**  In the event the participant performs a needle or tube thoracostomy you should start to improve quickly. You go from somnolent/lethargic to waking up and feeling much better and breathing much more easily. You should respond more normally if asked how you are doing or feeling. |
