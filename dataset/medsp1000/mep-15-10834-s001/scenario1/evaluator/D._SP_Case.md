Type and level of learner: First-Year Resident through Attending

Case Objectives:

1. Practice engagement in clinical behaviors in the inpatient setting, including gathering and analyzing information and evidence and interpretation of that evidence to formulate a differential diagnosis, a problem list and a leading diagnosis with supporting evidence.
2. Practice planning and performing a focused physical exam to further support clinical reasoning.
3. Provide immediate resuscitative care to a patient suffering from a tension pneumothorax and then practice developing and communicating a future management plan.
4. Reflect on one’s own diagnostic and management reasoning efforts and strategies by thinking aloud and observing one’s own video-recorded simulation.

| THE PATIENT STORY: | Carl Fisher is a male in his mid-50s who was just admitted for IV antibiotics for treatment of cellulitis in his left foot, complicated by a large abscess and history of diabetes. Shortly after arrival, he went to use the restroom unassisted and tripped and fell and hit his right lateral chest on the edge of the sink. He has significant pain in his chest and feels sharp pain in his chest wall with inspiration.  **Symptom Story:**  Mr. Fisher has been having pain, swelling, and redness of his left foot and ankle for about a week. It has been worsening over the past several days, making it uncomfortable to walk. He has also been feeling generally unwell for the past two days, with a fever, fatigue, and poor appetite. He went to the ER last night at the urging of his younger daughter after he stayed home from work yesterday (i.e., he cancelled visits with his parishioners). He spent most of the night in the ER being evaluated, told he has cellulitis, and then having the abscess drained while in the ER. He was expecting to be discharged home, but then the ER doctor informed him that he needed an antibiotic that could only be given through the IV, so he needed to be admitted. He then had to wait several hours for a hospital room to be ready for him. Finally, after arriving on the ward and getting settled into his hospital room, he was started on his Vancomycin. He then got up unassisted, still connected to his IV, to use the restroom. On his way out of the restroom, he tripped while trying to push his IV pole. He landed, hitting the right side of his chest on the sink. He has a great deal of sharp pain in that area, especially when trying to take a deep breath. As the scenario progresses, it becomes more and more difficult to breathe and Mr. Fisher becomes anxious and then unresponsive.  **Personal context:**  Mr. Fisher sees himself generally as a healthy, independent, and active person, so being hospitalized is something he had not expected and something that does not fit with how he sees himself.  **Emotional context:**  Mr. Fisher was very disappointed at the news that he had to be admitted and is really frustrated about having spent the whole night in the ER only to be admitted to the hospital.  As the scenario progresses, Mr. Fisher becomes anxious as his pneumothorax develops and he has more difficulty breathing and becomes oxygen deprived. |
