| **Scenario Start**  **0-3 min** | 1. After the participant receives their instructions and expectations for participants and indicates they have no further questions (see Appendix B) they will receive a phone call (using a designated phone) from the participant portraying the patient’s primary nurse. 2. **Opening Statement** [Read verbatim for continuity by SP Primary Nurse]:   *“Hi, this is [name of nurse participant] up on 4 Center, I’m the nurse taking care of your patient, Mr. Fisher, this morning. He just got to the floor from the ED about 30 minutes ago. I’m calling because I got his antibiotics hung and, then, when I had stepped out of the room, he got up to go to the bathroom and fell. He says he just tripped over his IV pole. He’s alert and oriented and says he didn’t hit his head...but he’s got some pain on his chest where he hit the sink.”*  If asked, his last set of vital signs before he fell were:  *Pulse: 98*  *BP: 130/80*  *Respirations: 18*  *SP02: 98% RA*  *Pain: 7/10 at the injury site*   1. The study participant may ask further questions of the primary nurse, such as, “how is he doing now?” or “What are his vital signs?” or “Can you get a 12 lead so I can see it when I get there?” If the participant asks for any of these, let the participant know you will work on getting them. 2. For the remainder of the scenario, the primary nurse assists the participant with assessing the patient, gathering findings and seeking additional help. Like the participant, this patient is new to the primary nurse who has had limited time with the patient because he’s just come up from the emergency room. | | |

| **Physician arrival at bedside**  **3-5 min** | Goal:  During this segment the patient will compensate to give the participant an opportunity to assess the patient and consider potential differential diagnoses.  Vital Signs:  *Pulse: 106*  *BP: 124/78*  *Respirations: 20*  *SP02: 97% RA* | 1. Patient is in the bed with staff having already obtained a set of vital signs showing on the device or given verbally by the primary nurse. 2. The patient appears uncomfortable and is in pain (5/10) without movement and is “*fearful*” of the sharper pain that comes with movement. 3. The patient is guarding his right chest during any movement - and braces *(holds area with arm or hand type gesture*) his injured side. The patient’s respiratory effort is mildly elevated; inspirations are becoming short rather than normal or deep because it makes the pain worse (8/10). 4. Increased pain and tenderness to the right lateral chest with palpation(8/10) 5. *SP should guard and try to deflect any attempt by the physician to palpate the injured area*) (8/10). | 1. Communicate with the patient (introduction, diagnostic questions). 2. Conduct an initial physical exam (focused). 3. Review/analyze initial set of vital signs. 4. May request repeat vital signs. 5. Request and review patient admission documents, diagnoses, prior treatment, and medications. 6. May request supplemental Oxygen, 7. May request pain management (e.g., Tylenol, Tylenol with a narcotic PO or medication by IV). 8. May request radiological studies, such as a chest x-ray or an ultrasound. |

| **Tension pneumothorax worsens.**  **5 - 10 min** | Goal:  During this time frame the patient will start to deteriorate which will further introduce increased acuity and increase patient anxiety.  V*itals (trending down over the next 10 minutes):*  *Pulse: 130*  *BP: 86/60*  *Respirations: 26*  *SP02: 91% with supplemental Oxygen.*  \*Decreased breath sounds on the right (injury site) noted verbally or by use of a simulated stethoscope if available. | 1. Pain may be decreased (6/10) if analgesia given - may vary depending on what is given. 2. The patient complains of worsening difficulty breathing (”*it’s getting harder for me to breathe”*) and should increase his respiratory rate. 3. Patient becomes increasingly anxious as he struggles to breathe. 4. At approximately minute 10 if the participant does not call for help or appears to be struggling, the primary nurse should tell the participant “*I’m concerned about him - I’m going to call a RRT so we can get some more help.*”[[1]](#footnote-1) 5. Near the end of this stage the patient will start to become somnolent. | 1. Review/analyze vital signs 2. Continue communication with the patient. 3. May request supplemental Oxygen. 4. May request subsequent vital signs checks. 5. May request moving crash cart into the room along with continuous 3 - lead ECG monitoring. 6. May request a stat chest x-ray 7. May request an ultrasound/ultrasound machine. 8. May request additional vascular access and start fluid resuscitation. 9. May choose to call rapid response or senior resident. 10. May decide to treat the tension pneumothorax and perform a need decompression and/or prepare to place a chest tube. |

Give handoff report to the arriving RRT team member. |

| **End Point Option 2**  **Needle decompress or chest tube placement or both.**  **10 – 20 min** | Goal:  If the participant performs a needle decompression or chest tube placement correctly the patient will begin to rapidly improve.  Vitals (trending upwards)[[2]](#footnote-2):  *Pulse: 120*  *BP: 94/70*  *Respirations: 22*  *SP02: 94% with supplemental Oxygen.* | 1. If the participant indicates they want to perform a needle decompression or place a chest tube the primary nurse should respond:  *“I’m going to get the crash cart”*  2. If the participant hasn’t yet requested help or a rapid response, the primary nurse can add:  *“..and call a RRT”*   1. If the participant decompresses or places a chest tube the patient will experience relief after placement and begin to rapidly improve and breathing will be easier and the patient will become more responsive/alert. 2. When the first member of the RRT team arrives [Approximately 5 minutes following the first call] they enter and introduce themselves and ask “*how can I help?*”[[3]](#footnote-3) | 1. Needle decompresses or place a chest tube. 2. May request a RRT – the primary nurse will cue this if not called and request to do a decompression/chest tube. 3. May request continuous vital signs assessments/updates. 4. Will give handoff report to the arriving RRT team member to allow for an opportunity to learn about the participant’s understanding of the situation. |
