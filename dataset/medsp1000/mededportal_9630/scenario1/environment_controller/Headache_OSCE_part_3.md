# Headache OSCE Part 3

Headache OSCE: A 22-year old woman presents to the emergency department with a severe headache—Part 3

Headache OSCE Instructor Packet

1. You will assess the examinee’s ability to take a focused, but complete headache history from the patient.   
2. You will read aloud the physical examination findings, then ask the examinee to generate an initial differential diagnosis.   
3. You will read aloud the results of basic labs and ask the examinee what diagnostic test he or she would order first.   
4. Regardless of whether non-contrast head CT was ordered, you will pull the head CT from the sheet protector, hand it to the examinee, and ask for an interpretation.   
5. Regardless of whether the CT was interpreted correctly, you will read aloud the correct interpretation.   
6. You will then ask the examinee if any further diagnostic test is necessary.   
7. Regardless of whether a lumbar puncture is ordered, you will remove the results from the sheet protector, hand it to the examinee, and ask for an interpretation.   
8. You will ask the examinee to correctly diagnose and defend this patient’s headache, by listing the pertinent positives.   
9. The OSCE will last approximately 15 minutes.   
10. Ideally, 5 additional minutes will be available for immediate feedback.

It is very important to read the instructions, verbatim.

Read this, verbatim: “You have 6 minutes to take a complete, but focused history on this patient with headaches. You may take notes. You will be given a warning when 2 minutes remain.”

![](images/f80a74b28ba1f81f394484391b21dbe5fd72e8f391d6a8c145c2e23faa67fdd2.jpg)

Set your stopwatch for 6 minutes. Give the examinee a warning when 2 minutes remain, and continue to the next section when the 6 minutes has elapsed. If the examinee stops before eliciting all of the information on the checklist, inform him or her that there is time remaining. The examinee must continue with the history until all of the required information has been elicited, or until 6 minutes has elapsed. Don’t give the examinee any hints about what questions to ask.

Read this, verbatim: “You examine the patient. She is in extreme pain and very sensitive to the light. Therefore, you cannot visualize her fundi or reliably test her visual fields. Otherwise, the complete neurological and head and neck examination is normal and nonfocal. You have 90 seconds to think about the differential diagnosis. You will then state the 4 most likely diagnoses, from most likely to least likely. Don’t list the pertinent positives and negatives at this time.” Don’t give any hints.

Set your timer for 2 minutes. After 90 seconds, the examinee must start listing the diagnoses. This section must be completed within 2 minutes.

Read this, verbatim: “Basic labs were already ordered, including CBC, platelets, chem 7, PT, PTT, and INR. All were normal. Urine pregnancy test was also negative. What is the first test you would order on this patient?” The examinee must respond immediately. If the examinee hesitates, prompt him or her for a response.

Regardless of what the examinee requested, tear out the next page (head CT) and hand it to the examinee. Read this. $" \mathsf { A }$ head non-contrast head CT was performed on this patient.”

Regardless of the accuracy of the examinee’s interpretation, read this, verbatim: “The head CT was read by the neuro-radiologist as normal.”

Read this, verbatim: “Is there any other diagnostic test you would like to order at this time, or is the evaluation complete?”

If more brain imaging is requested (such as an angiogram, MRI, MRA, or CTA) say that all of the radiology technologists have gone home for the day. We could call them, but it could take 2 hours for them to return. This would not count as an incorrect answer. However, ask the student “Is there any other diagnostic test you would like to order at this time, or is the evaluation complete?”

Whether the examinee ordered a lumbar puncture or not, read this, verbatim: $\cdot$ lumbar puncture was performed on this patient. You may think out loud, and then state your final interpretation.” No hints.

Read this to the examinee, verbatim: “You may take a moment to think about your final diagnosis, including the pertinent positives. As soon as you are ready, please state the final diagnosis, and list the pertinent positives. Do not list any pertinent negatives. Also state how the diagnostic tests support your diagnosis.”

![](images/0f534b7ba296f9f3797a01b3e32407f31141710b2081343601730946d0112c86.jpg)

Set your stopwatch for 3 minutes. Encourage the examinee to keep listing pertinent positives, but end the session after 3 minutes. You can give hints regarding the pertinent positives. For example, “Are there any other clinical features that support the diagnosis?” Or, “Are there any diagnostic evaluations that support the diagnosis?”

The OSCE is complete.
