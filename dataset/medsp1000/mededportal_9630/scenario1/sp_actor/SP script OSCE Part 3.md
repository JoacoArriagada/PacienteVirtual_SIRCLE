Headache OSCE Part 3: A 22-year old woman presents to the emergency department after waking up with the sudden onset of a severe headache

# Standardized Patient Script

Case Summary: The patient is a 22-year old woman who came to the emergency department with a severe headache. She is nauseated, and vomiting into an emesis basin. She is very concerned and wants to find out what is causing this.

Patient appearance: The patient should be a 20-something year-old overweight woman. If she is not overweight, she should wear a big shirt stuffed with a pillow, in order to appear obese. The patient should be well-groomed. Preferably, she should be wearing a hospital gown. However, conservative dress would also be acceptable. She is holding her head, rocking a little, and in excruciating pain. She tries to cooperate with examiner, but she is retching, holding an emesis basin (containing chicken baby food mixed with water), and shielding her eyes from the light.

Patient instructions: The challenge for the patient is to communicate with the physician as one would naturally, without giving away too much information. If the student uses technical terminology, ask for clarification.

Patient script:

The student will ask specific questions about the headache.

Onset: I don’t know when it started. I was taking a nap with my 1-year old daughter (we were sleeping for about 2 hours) and the headache woke me up. The pain was severe when I awoke. When it didn’t go away, I had my sister drive me to the emergency department. I woke up about 8 hours ago.

Location: right side of the head and neck.

Description of pain: 10/10, throbbing, worst pain in your life. Exacerbated by light, sound, and normal activity. Nothing relieves the pain.

Accompanying symptoms: You have sensitivity to light and sound, nausea and vomiting. No loss of vision, no double vision, not seeing flashing lights, no weakness, no numbness of extremities, no dizziness, no trouble speaking, no loss of consciousness.
