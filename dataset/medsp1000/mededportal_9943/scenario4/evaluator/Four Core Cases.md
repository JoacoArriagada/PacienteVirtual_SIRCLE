CASE FOUR

I. Title: Toxicologic Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA) Arrest With or Without Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) Arrest

III. Learning Objectives:

A. Goals:

o Ensure resident facility with Pediatric Advanced Life Support (PALS) algorithms for PEA arrest and VF arrest o Develop resident competency with bag-mask ventilation and chest compressions o Improve junior and senior residents' crisis resource management and code team leadership skills

B. Learning Objectives:

o Assess airway/breathing and initiate bag-mask ventilation in a timely manner
o Demonstrate correct bag-mask ventilation technique, including confirming adequate chest rise and breath sounds
o Identify cardio-respiratory arrest and initiate cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) within one minute
o Perform correct CPR technique, including: hand positioning, depth of compressions, compression-to-ventilation ratio, counting cycles of CPR and rechecking a pulse after 5 cycles of CPR
o Initiate isotonic IV fluids appropriately in the setting of PEA arrest.
o Administer correct dose (0.01 mg/kg = 0.1 cc/kg = 1 mL) of IV or IO epinephrine within 3 minutes of PEA arrest, and then every 3-5 minutes
o If superimposed VF arrest, recognize the change in cardiac rhythm and perform appropriate defibrillation technique, including dosing energy correctly, placing pads correctly, confirming that personnel is clear, and releasing charge
o For code leader, assume leadership of code, assign clear roles and demonstrate closed-loop communication
o For code leader, intermittently summarize and maintain a global view

C. Critical Actions Checklist: as listed in the scenario branchpoints and TRACS tool

VI. Case Narrative: The PICU day team resident is providing sign-out to the night team resident on a patient who just arrived to the unit.

A. Scenario:

o Chief Complaint: Opioid ingestion
o History of Present Illness: A 14 month-old healthy male with no past medical history presented to the Emergency Department with presumed opioid ingestion, after being found with a bottle of Grandma's pills. He arrived to the ED sleepy but arousable with a HR 105, RR 14-18 and miosis. His venous blood gas was remarkable for pH 7.3, PCO2 50, HCO3 17. His serum chemistries, lactate, glucose, osmolality, liver function tests were all unremarkable. His urine toxicology screen was positive for opioids. He has serum toxicology screens (aspirin level, Tylenol level, tricyclic antidepressant level) pending. He received 1 mEq/kg Naloxone, after which he was more alert and his respiratory rate was in the high teens. He was admitted to PICU for continuous monitoring ~1hr ago.
o Interval: In the last 15 minutes, he seems sleepier with HR 100s, RR mid-teens, O2 > 95% but occasional desaturations to 88% and bradypnea 8-10 breaths per minute when asleep. Placed on 2L nasal cannula, currently O2 > 95%.
o Past Medical History: None
o Medications: None
o Allergies: no known drug allergies
o Weight: 10kg

B. Initial Scenario Conditions: Sudden PEA Arrest (minutes 1-3); nurse notices that patient is unresponsive and not breathing; calls CODE!

Initial Physical Exam:

o T 96.5; HR 80 on monitor but no pulses (PEA), no spontaneous respirations, blood pressure not detectable, oxygen saturation too low to read on 2L NC, capillary refill 2-3 seconds
o Small pupils; 24 or 26 gauge IV in right upper extremity; on 2L nasal cannula

Critical Actions Checklist:

o Call Attending and Code Blue or equivalent
o Patient on monitors within 1 minute
o Effective bag-mask valve ventilation and CPR within 1 minute
o IV epinephrine within 3 minutes
o Normal saline bolus administered
o Labs ordered: o Venous blood gas: pH 7.29 / pCO2 55 / HCO3 17; lactate 2.1 o Serum toxicology screen now positive for tricyclic antidepressants
o +/- Repeat narcan and sodium bicarbonate administration

C. Scenario Branch Points:

Scenario A: after 3-4 minutes of CPR and 1-2 doses of epinephrine successful resuscitation.

Physical Exam: o T 96.5; HR 120 sinus rhythm; RR climbs from 10 to 22; BP 100/56; O2 92% with shallow breaths o 40 seconds after return to spontaneous rhythm, infant whimpers/cries

Scenario B: if team does not provide IV epinephrine within the first 3 minutes asystole.

Physical Exam: o T 96.5; HR none (asystole); RR no spontaneous breaths; BP not detectable; O2 not detectable

Scenario C: after 3 minutes of CPR and 1-2 doses of epinephrine VF Arrest.

Physical Exam: o T 96.5; HR VF; no spontaneous respirations; no BP; O2 95% with bag-mask valve ventilation

Critical Actions Checklist:

o Recognize change in rhythm (VF)
o Continue CPR
o Perform successful defibrillation once (2 Joules/kg)
o Call Cardiology

D. Conclusion of Scenario: if Scenario C's Critical Actions are successful, proceed to Scenario A (successful resuscitation). Otherwise, continue Scenario C until scenario completed and goals and objectives met or time runs out.

Instructor Guide:

a. Common toxidromes:

o Sympathomimetic:
o Vital signs - fever, tachycardia, hypertension
o Central nervous system - agitated, delirium, psychosis, mydriasis
o Distinguishes from anticholinergic: diaphoresis, increased bowel sound
o Cholinergic:
o Vital signs - bradycardia or tachycardia, tachypnea
o Central nervous system - confusion, drowsiness, coma, paralysis, miosis
o Other - diaphoresis, hypersalivation, bronchospasm, pulmonary edema
o Anticholinergic:
o Vital signs - fever, tachycardia, hypertension
o Central nervous system - delirium, psychosis, convulsions, coma, mydriasis, blurry vision
o Distinguishes from sympathomimetic: flushed, hot, dry, decreased bowel sounds, ileus
o Opioid:
o Vital signs - bradycardia, bradypnea, hypotension, hypothermia
o Central nervous system -- euphoria, CNS depression, hyporeflexia, pinpoint pupils
o Serotonin syndrome:
o Vital signs -- autonomic instability (hypertension, tachycardia, fever)
o Central nervous system -- altered mental status, neuromuscular hyperactivity (clonus, hyperreflexia), mydriasis
o Other -- Diaphoresis, increased bowel sounds

b. Additional interventions:

o Repeat Narcan every 30-60 minutes: 0.1mg/kg IV/IO/IM for <5yrs or <20 kg; 2mg for >5yrs or >20kg
o Sodium bicarbonate: 1MEq/kg IV/IO; 1cc/kg of 8.4% solution; 2cc/kg of 4.2% solution.

c. Reversible causes of PEA arrest: Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, Hydrogen ions (acidosis), Hypo/hyperkalemia, Hypoglycemia, Hypothermia, Toxins, Tamponade (cardiac), Tension pneumothorax, Thrombosis, Trauma

f. Bag- Mask Technique: review mask size selection, appropriate mask placement, hand positioning, jaw thrust, single-handed vs. two-person hold.

g. Ventilation:

o Adequacy of breaths should be judged by chest rise and auscultation.
o Extreme pressures can lead to barotraumas, pneumothorax, gaseous distention and increased risk for aspiration

h. Tips for patients who are difficult to bag-mask ventilate:

o Two-person technique
o "MRSOPA" mnemonic: mask size; reposition (head-tilt, chin-lift and jaw-thrust; only the latter is okay for cervical instability); suction; open mouth; increase pressures; airway adjuncts

i. Circulation Didactics:

o Compression-to-ventilation ratios:
o Neonatal CPR: 3:1 until airway secured
o Infant and child two-person: 15:2
o Infant and child one-person: 30:2
o Adult: 30:2
o Once airway is secured, continuous chest compressions at rate of 100/min with 8-10 breaths/minute (1 breath every 6-8 seconds)
o Non-shockable rhythms:
o Asystole, PEA Arrest
o Causes of PEA Arrest ("Hs and Ts"): hypoxia (most common), hypovolemia, acidosis, hypo/hyperkalemia; hypoglycemia; hypothermia; toxins; tamponade; tension pneumothorax; thrombosis; trauma.
o Primary treatment: 0.01 mg/kg or 0.1 mL/kg IV/IO (1:10,000 dilution) epinephrine every 3-5 minutes
o Additional treatment: normal saline bolus
o Shockable rhythms:
o VF and pulseless ventricular tachycardia (Vtach): 2-4 J/kg, then repeat with 4 J/kg and consider medications - lidocaine, amiodorone, mag sulfate
o Supraventricular tachycardia (unstable): cardioversion 0.5-1 J/kg; repeat up to 2 J/kg OR Adenosine 0.1mg/kg IV/IO (max 6mg)
o During resuscitation, remember to call out cycles of CPR (generally, the two minutes of CPR rule correlates to 5 cycles of adult CPR).
o Rhythm should be checked and compressors should switch after 5 cycles of CPR.
