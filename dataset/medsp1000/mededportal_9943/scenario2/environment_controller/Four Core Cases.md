CASE TWO

I. Title: Status Epilepticus

IV. Environment:

A. Lab Set Up: Simulation Suite
B. Mannequin Set Up: o SimBaby high-fidelity mannequin o Peripheral intravenous line (22-24 gauge) in antecubital fossa o Medications: normal saline, rectal diazepam (Diastat), lorazepam, fosphenytoin, phenobarbital, Keppra, etomidate, rocuronium, propofol o Results of: blood glucose level 86; venous blood gas pH 7.25, pCO2 56, HCO3 19, Na 132, K+ 4.5, Cl 100, CO2 26, lactate 2.4; serum electrolytes, liver function tests, urine toxicology screen and Keppra levels pending o Result of chest x-ray (if ordered): nonfocal lungs with endotracheal tube appropriately in place

C. Props: nonrebreather; bag-mask device; oral airways; nasopharyngeal airways; suction; tape; 4.0 cuffed and 4.5 uncuffed endotracheal tubes; laryngoscope handle, Miller and Macintosh 1 blades, stylet; end-tidal capnography

D. Distractors: none

V. Actors:

A. Roles: Attending (played by simulation instructor) providing case history to residents; team leader; airway manager, circulation manager. May also include nurses (bedside and medication), and additional roles like recorder, airway assistant and order entrant.

VI. Case Narrative: Attending in the Emergency Department is asking the resident to see the following patient.

A. Scenario:

o Chief Complaint: Seizures
o History of Present Illness: A 1 year-old girl with past medical history of seizures on maintenance Keppra at home just arrived to the Emergency Department following a 7-minute generalized tonic clonic seizure at home, where parents administered rectal Diastat and called Emergency Medical Services (EMS). The family endorses having missed her last 5 Keppra doses, as their prescription ran out. By EMS arrival, the seizure had resolved and the patient was postictal. An intravenous line was placed and normal saline bolus administered in the ambulance. Triage vital signs were: T 98.2; HR 110; BP 80/45; RR 16; O2 98% RA. The patient was sleepy but arousable, in no acute distress.
o Past Medical History: 3 prior complex febrile seizures and 2 afebrile generalized tonic clonic seizures; initiated on Keppra 6 months prior with no further seizures until today
o Medications: Keppra 300 mg twice per day
o Allergies: no known drug allergies
o Weight: 10 kg

B. Initial Scenario Conditions: As you get ready to see this patient, the nurse says that the patient is seizing and presses the "Staff Assist" button.

Initial Physical Exam:

o Vitals: T 98.2; HR 152; BP unable to obtain due to seizure activity; RR 12; O2 92% in room air (increases to 95% once nonrebreather is placed)
o Activity: seizing
o CV: tachycardic but otherwise normal S1, S2, regular rate and rhythm; capillary refill brisk
o Lungs: shallow breaths but good air entry bilaterally

C. Scenario Branch Point: After lorazepam, patient becomes hypopneic, increasingly hypoxemic, and is still having seizures at minute 8 (may simulate time lapse).

Physical Exam:

o T 98.5; HR 148; RR 5-7; BP unable to obtain due to seizure activity; O2 with desaturations to 71-80% on nonrebreather, + oral secretions
o Activity: seizing
o CV: tachycardic but otherwise normal S1, S2, regular rate and rhythm; capillary refill brisk
o Lungs: very shallow breaths

D. Conclusion of Scenario: Seizures cease after administering paralytic, fosphenytoin OR Keppra (whichever occurs first). Patient is successfully intubated. Heart rate subsequently decreases to 118, oxygen saturation increases to 98%; end-tidal CO2 is 52.
