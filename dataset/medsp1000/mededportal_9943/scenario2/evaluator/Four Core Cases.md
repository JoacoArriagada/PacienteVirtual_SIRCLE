CASE TWO

I. Title: Status Epilepticus

III. Goals and Objectives:

A. Goals:

o Ensure resident facility with the management of status epilepticus o Develop resident procedural competency with endotracheal intubation o Improve junior and senior residents' crisis resource management and code team leadership skills

B. Learning Objectives:

o Recognize and appropriately manage status epilepticus
o Anticipate respiratory depression as a complication of benzodiazepine use
o State the indications and advantages of nasopharyngeal and oropharyngeal airways
o Demonstrate appropriate selection of medications for rapid sequence intubation
o Demonstrate proper technique for endotracheal intubation, including preoxygenation, appropriate endotracheal tube and blade size selection, and confirmation of tube placement
o For team leader, assume leadership of code, assign clear roles and demonstrate closed-loop communication
o For team leader, intermittently summarize and maintain a global view

C. Critical Actions Checklist: as listed in the scenario branch points and TRACS tool

VI. Case Narrative: Attending in the Emergency Department is asking the resident to see the following patient.

A. Scenario:

o Chief Complaint: Seizures
o History of Present Illness: A 1 year-old girl with past medical history of seizures on maintenance Keppra at home just arrived to the Emergency Department following a 7-minute generalized tonic clonic seizure at home, where parents administered rectal Diastat and called Emergency Medical Services (EMS). The family endorses having missed her last 5 Keppra doses, as their prescription ran out. By EMS arrival, the seizure had resolved and the patient was postictal. An intravenous line was placed and normal saline bolus administered in the ambulance. Triage vital signs were: T 98.2; HR 110; BP 80/45; RR 16; O2 98% RA. The patient was sleepy but arousable, in no acute distress.
o Past Medical History: 3 prior complex febrile seizures and 2 afebrile generalized tonic clonic seizures; initiated on Keppra 6 months prior with no further seizures until today
o Medications: Keppra 300 mg twice per day
o Allergies: no known drug allergies
o Weight: 10 kg

B. Initial Scenario Conditions: As you get ready to see this patient, the nurse says that the patient is seizing and presses the "Staff Assist" button.

Initial Physical Exam:

o Vitals: T 98.2; HR 152; BP unable to obtain due to seizure activity; RR 12; O2 92% in room air (increases to 95% once nonrebreather is placed)
o Activity: seizing
o CV: tachycardic but otherwise normal S1, S2, regular rate and rhythm; capillary refill brisk
o Lungs: shallow breaths but good air entry bilaterally

Critical Actions Checklist:

o Place patient on side, and on monitors within 1 minute
o Administer supplemental oxygen (ideally via nonrebreather) within 1 minute
o Obtain key labs (glucose level, Keppra level, venous blood gas, lactate, serum chemistries +/- complete blood count, liver function tests and urine toxicology screen)
o Administer either rectal diazepam (0.3-0.5 mg/kg = 3-5 mg) or IV/IM lorazepam (0.1 mg/kg = 1 mg) over 3 minutes (may simulate time lapse)
o Additional behaviors (helpful but not necessary): page Neurology, call for backup

C. Scenario Branch Point: After lorazepam, patient becomes hypopneic, increasingly hypoxemic, and is still having seizures at minute 8 (may simulate time lapse).

Physical Exam:

o T 98.5; HR 148; RR 5-7; BP unable to obtain due to seizure activity; O2 with desaturations to 71-80% on nonrebreather, + oral secretions
o Activity: seizing
o CV: tachycardic but otherwise normal S1, S2, regular rate and rhythm; capillary refill brisk
o Lungs: very shallow breaths

Critical Actions Checklist:

o Suction, change nonrebreather to bag-mask ventilation +/- nasopharyngeal airway
o Administer second dose of benzodiazepine (lorazepam) 0.1mg/kg IV
o Administer 20 MEq/kg of fosphenytoin over 7 minutes (will also accept 30mg/kg Keppra 300mg based on history)
o Administer premedication and appropriate rapid sequence intubation medications
o Endotracheal intubation with confirmation by auscultation, chest x-ray and/or end-tidal carbon dioxide monitor

D. Conclusion of Scenario: Seizures cease after administering paralytic, fosphenytoin OR Keppra (whichever occurs first). Patient is successfully intubated. Heart rate subsequently decreases to 118, oxygen saturation increases to 98%; end-tidal CO2 is 52.

Instructor Guide:

a. Algorithm for status epilepticus management:

o "ABCDD" mnemonic: Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Dextrose-stick (blood glucose)
o IV, oxygen, monitors
o If no IV access:
o Diazepam 0.3-0.5 mg/kg per rectum (PR) (max 20mg)
o Midazolam 0.2 mg/kg IM or buccal OR lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IM
o Fosphenytoin 20 PE/kg IM
o With IV or intraosseous (IO) access:
o Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV/IM over 3 minutes (max 4 mg); may repeat every 5 min PRN
o If seizure persists 10-15 min: repeat lorazepam and give fosphenytoin 20mg PE/kg IV/IM (max 1000mg PE) over 7 min; side effects include decreased blood pressure and respiratory rate
o If seizure persists 15-20 minutes: administer phenobarbital 20 mg/kg IV over 15-20 minutes or consider Keppra to avoid further respiratory compromise
o Subsequent options include fosphenytoin, Keppra, valproic acid or pentobarbital coma in consultation with Neurology
o Remember to notify Neurology

d. Bag- Mask Technique: review mask size selection, appropriate mask placement, hand positioning, jaw thrust, single-person vs. two-person hold.

e. Ventilation:

o Adequacy of breaths should be judged by chest rise and auscultation
o Extreme pressures can lead to barotrauma, pneumothorax, gaseous distention and aspiration

f. Tips for patients who are difficult to bag-mask ventilate:

o Two-person technique
o "MRSOPA" mnemonic: mask size; reposition (head-tilt, chin-lift and jaw-thrust; only the latter is okay for cervical instability); suction; open mouth; increase pressures; airway adjuncts

g. Rapid Sequence Intubation:

o Equipment: "STATICS" mnemonic - scope, tube, adjuncts, tape, introducer, connector, suction
o Preoxygenate: 100% oxygen x 2 minutes
o Premedicate (2-5 minutes prior): normal saline bolus, <5yrs - Atropine 0.02mg/kg/dose IV (min 0.1mg, max 0.5mg)
o Sedation, Analgesia, Amnesia: Appropriate agents in this case, as they may decrease intracranial pressure; thiopental also has anti-convulsant properties. Seizing patients with profound respiratory depression may have received sufficient benzodiazepines to obviate the need for amnestic anesthetic agents. Neuromuscular blockade:
