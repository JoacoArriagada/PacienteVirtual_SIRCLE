CASE THREE

I. Title: Septic Shock

III. Goals and Objectives:

A. Goals:

o Ensure resident facility with the recognition and management of septic shock o Develop resident procedural competency with intraosseous access o Improve junior and senior residents' crisis resource management and code team leadership skills

B. Learning Objectives:

o Recognize the signs of septic shock by history, vital sign changes and physical exam findings
o Demonstrate proper technique for intraosseous needle placement
o Initiate appropriate fluid resuscitation (volume and rate) for septic shock
o Recognize the need to start vasopressors
o Obtain key labs (complete blood count, venous blood gas, serum electrolytes, lactate, blood cultures, urinalysis, urine cultures, liver function tests, type and screen, coagulation studies) and broaden antibiotic coverage for septic shock
o For code leader, assume leadership of code, assign clear roles and demonstrate closed-loop communication
o For code leader, intermittently summarize and maintain a global view

C. Critical Actions Checklist: as listed in the scenario branch points and TRACS tool

VI. Case Narrative: day team residents are receiving sign-out on the pediatric inpatient floor from the overnight team.

A. Scenario:

o Chief Complaint: Cellulitis
o History of Present Illness: This is an 8 year-old girl on hospital day two, admitted with left upper extremity cellulitis, in the context of 2 days of fevers, decreased oral intake and decreased urinary output. Preliminary blood cultures are no growth to date x1 day. She was admitted to the pediatrics floor for IV antibiotics. Admission labs included a white blood cell count of 15, with 50% neutrophils and 4% bands.
o Overnight History: She had low-grow fevers overnight and now has been febrile (101-104 degrees Fahrenheit) and tachycardic (hear rate 130s-140s) for 4 hours despite Tylenol, Motrin, tepid towels and a 20cc/kg normal saline bolus. Her blood pressure is low/normal. She just lost the IV that was in her right arm, and we were unsuccessful at obtaining a new one, so paged the IV team. Her left upper extremity is slightly more erythematous and tender, though not woody today (it is outlined from admission).
o Past Medical History: Two prior abscesses treated with Clindamycin s/p incision and drainage
o Medications: IV Clindamycin; IV maintenance fluids with D5 normal saline plus 20MEq of potassium per liter
o Allergies: no known drug allergies
o Weight: 25kg

B. Initial Scenario Conditions: after signout, the nurse comes in saying that patient's heart rate is consistently 150s-160s for the last 45 minutes, she has had no urine output for the last 6 hours, and her blood pressure is now 75/45.

Initial Physical Exam:

o Vitals: T 101.2; HR 166; BP 75/40; RR 30; O2 in room air; capillary refill <2 seconds (flash)
o Mental status: sleepy and whimpering but not talking
o CV: tachycardic but otherwise normal S1, S2, regular rate and rhythm; hyperdynamic peripheral pulses; flash capillary refill
o Lungs: shallow tachypnea, clear bilaterally
o Left upper extremity: moulage (4cm x 5cm red cellulitis, extending 0.5 cm beyond its outline); no IV access.

Critical Actions Checklist:

o Provide supplemental oxygen (ideally via nonrebreather)
o Obtain intraosseous access (if IV access is attempted, instructors should say that the veins blow each time)
o Administer 60cc/kg normal saline (ideally over 15 minutes; may simulate time lapse)
o Draw key labs (i.e., venous blood gas, lactate, complete blood count, blood cultures, serum electrolytes, liver function tests, type and screen, coagulation studies,)
o Call for backup (i.e., pediatric intensive care unit, rapid response or equivalent)
o Broaden antibiotic coverage - change to Vancomycin plus Nafcillin for a critically ill patient with skin and soft tissue infection
o May also perform the following actions (not necessary):
o Insert Foley catheter
o Cooling interventions: undress patient, turn down heat in room
o Call Surgical consult (concern for possible necrotizing fasciitis)

C. Scenario Branch Point: after the above interventions, simulate a 35-minute time lapse with minimal change in patient's status.

Of note, if the PICU or Rapid Response was called, they agreed with management and are getting ready to transfer patient to the PICU, where they will place a central line. If the surgical team was called, they thought this was unlikely to be necrotizing fasciitis given minimal spread, and suggested imaging (x-ray and magnetic resonance imaging) once the patient is stabilized.

Physical Exam:

o T 100.8; HR 160; RR 32; BP 75/50; O2 92% on oxygen support (ideally nonrebreather); patient increasingly sleepy; only 10cc of urine (from Foley, if placed; otherwise, minimal urine output)
o No change in remainder of physical exam from prior
o Lab results: o Complete blood count: 24 > 27 < 190; 52% neutrophils; 5% bands; 40% lymphocytes; 2% eosinophils o Venous blood gas: pH 7.2 / pCO2 40 / HCO3 11; lactate 4.5 o Serum electrolytes: Na 132; K+ 4; Cl 110; HCO3 11; BUN 20; Cr 0.8; Mag/Phos/Ca within normal limits o Pending laboratories: liver function tests, coagulation studies; repeat blood cultures; no urine output for urinalysis and urine culture.

Critical Actions Checklist: verbalize need for or initiate pressors, +/- transfusion.

D. Conclusion of Scenario: Ultimately, patient goes to PICU, requires 2 pressors (dopamine and norepinephrine), speciates methicillin sensitive staph aureas (MSSA) from blood cultures and is found to have staphylococcal osteomyelitis; treated appropriately and recovers.

b. Recognizing septic shock:

o Tachycardia: compensation for low systemic venous return (SVR) to maintain cardiac output
o Warm, bounding pulses: may occur early on due to decreased SVR
o Decreased peripheral perfusion (delayed capillary refill, cool extremities, weak pulses, narrow pulse pressure): SVR increases peripherally to shunt blood to vital organs
o Decreased urine output: splanchnic vasoconstriction to shunt blood to vital organs
o Altered mental status: decreased oxygen and nutrients to brain

d. Hypoxia in septic shock:

o All patients should receive high-flow oxygen
o Consider positive pressure ventilation (CPAP, BIPAP, intubation)
o Consider blood transfusion if anemic

e. Circulatory management of septic shock:

o Large bore IVs if possible (allows for pressurized bags); low threshold to place intraosseous line
o 20cc/kg normal saline bolus (repeat as needed to 60cc/kg or more, titrating to adequate perfusion, heart rate, mental status and urine output while monitoring for side effects)
o Consider packed red blood cells
o Pressors:
o Dopamine (can safely give 5 mcg/kg/min through a peripheral IV, and can increase once central or IO access is obtained): low dose predominantly increases contractility; alpha effects and vasoconstriction with higher doses
o Epinephrine: low dose has vasodilatory effects; higher doses have alpha effects and vasoconstriction; is a good choice for "cold shock" and generally as a second agent
o Norepinephrine: systemic vasoconstrictor with increased SVR; good for florid warm shock
