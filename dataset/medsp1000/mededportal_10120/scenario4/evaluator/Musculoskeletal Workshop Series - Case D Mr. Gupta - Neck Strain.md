**Key Objectives**: Review the differential diagnosis of **neck/shoulder pain**.

Recognize the typical history and physical findings for a patient with a **neck strain**, as well as appropriate diagnostic tests and treatments.

**Patient Name**: Mr. Gupta, 56 years old

**Setting:** Outpatient clinic – exam room

**Complaint**: Acute neck pain

**HPI**: Mr. Gupta is a school teacher who reports 2 days of severe **right** shoulder pain that he noticed when waking up. Patient states that he competed in a tennis tournament three days ago but the pain is “terrible”. He rates the pain 9/10 and says he isn’t able to fully flex or rotate his neck due to the pain. He describes the pain as “knife” like and is non-radiating. The course is intermittent and exacerbated when turning his head to the left. He states he has been taking Vicodin he had left over from a tooth extraction with relief. He is requesting more today. Denies fevers, chills or prior episodes. States no loss of sensation or strength. **His right neck pain is presently a 6 out of 10.**

**PMHx**: HTN, Paroxysmal A. Fib, HLD

**Meds**: Chlorthalidone, Dronedarone, Simvastatin

**Social**: Smokes ½ pack per day. Occasional alcohol, denies drugs. Teaches math at local middle school. States regular activity including tennis on the weekends and jogging during the weekdays.

**Exam:** The patient appears with neck mildly laterally flexed to right. Normal gait and pleasant affect.

Vital signs stable, afebrile. The cardiac, pulmonary, GI, and skin exams are unremarkable.

Neck and Shoulder:

* Gross Inspection: Symmetric. No kyphosis or scoliosis. No muscular atrophy
* Neck ROM: Rotation to L limited to 30 degrees, limited lateral flexion to L due to R neck/shoulder pain, forward flexion to 80 degrees, full extension to 75 degrees
* Tenderness to palpation of R trapezius, no tenderness over paraspinal, or upper arm muscles
* Strength 5/5 bilat upper extremities.
* Reflexes 2+ bilat
* Spurling test negative
* Shoulder abduction relief sign negative
* Cervical distraction test is negative
* Hoffman’s negative. Lhermitte’s test is negative
* Strength and reflex testing in LE’s is within normal limits

**Differential Diagnosis** (not comprehensive):

* + **Muscle strain**
  + Cervical Disk Herniation, Cervical Spondylosis with impingement on C5 or C6 nerve roots
  + Rotator cuff pathology
  + Drug related myopathy
  + Malignancy (intra and extraspinal tumors)
  + Cardiovascular (Angina)
  + Other (Thoracic outlet obstruction, Reflex Sympathetic Dystrophy, Fracture)

**Management Options include:**

* + Acetaminophen, NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, and mild opioids (tramadol) are all reasonable short term options. Also recommend heat/cold pads and postural changes while sleeping.
  + Plain films would be of minimal utility in this case as this is a muscle strain, in addition 1) they cannot diagnose disk herniation, 2) age-related spondylosis would be an expected finding regardless of his symptoms, 3) radiography is poorly sensitive for detection of c-spine malignancies, and 4) fracture is not high on the differential.
  + Given the presence of no neurologic findings a CT is unwarranted.
  + PT should be recommended once pain has subsided. Spinal manipulation has also been shown to reduce symptoms.
  + No follow up necessary unless pain persists or neurologic findings.
