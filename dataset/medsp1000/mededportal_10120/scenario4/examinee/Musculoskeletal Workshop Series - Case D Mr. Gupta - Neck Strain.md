# Case D: Mr. Gupta

**General Instructions**: The focus of this workshop is on the exam itself, and the interpretation of one’s findings, rather than history-taking. The provided history is presumed to be accurate and complete.

**Patient Name**: Mr. Gupta, 56 years old

**Setting:** Outpatient clinic – exam room

**Complaint**: Acute neck pain

**HPI**: Mr. Gupta is a school teacher who reports 2 days of severe **right** shoulder pain that he noticed when waking up. Patient states that he competed in a tennis tournament three days ago but the pain is “terrible”. He rates the pain as 9 out of 10 and says he isn’t able to fully flex or rotate his neck due to the pain. He describes the pain as “knife” like and is non-radiating. The course is intermittent and exacerbated when turning his head to the left. He states he has been taking Vicodin that was left over from a tooth extraction with relief. He is requesting more today. Denies fevers, chills or prior episodes. States no loss of sensation or strength. **His right neck pain is presently a 6 out of 10.**

**PMHx**: HTN, Paroxysmal A. Fib, HLD

**Meds**: Chlorthalidone, Dronedarone, Simvastatin

**Social**: Smokes ½ pack per day. Occasional alcohol, denies drugs. Teaches math at local middle school. States regular activity including tennis on the weekends and jogging during the weekdays.

**Exam:** The patient appears with neck mildly laterally flexed to right. Normal gait and pleasant affect.

Vital signs stable, afebrile. The cardiac, pulmonary, GI, and skin exams are unremarkable.
