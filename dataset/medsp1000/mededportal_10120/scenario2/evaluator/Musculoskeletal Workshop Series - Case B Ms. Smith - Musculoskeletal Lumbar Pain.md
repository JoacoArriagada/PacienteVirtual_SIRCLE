**Key Objectives**: Review the differential diagnosis of **acute lumbar back pain**.

Recognize the typical history and physical findings for a patient with **lumbar musculoskeletal strain,** and recommend appropriate diagnostic tests and treatments

**Patient Name**: Ms. Smith, 45 years old

**Setting:** Outpatient clinic – exam room

**Complaint**: Lower back pain

**HPI**: Ms. Smith states she has had pain in her lower back for the past 2 days. She began a new pilates work out routine 1 week prior. She awoke with lower back pain yesterday and states that it has gradually worsened. Tylenol ES has not helped and she spent most of the day in bed. She is stooling and urinating normally. She states that she works as a CNA which involves some lifting of patients. She desires an MRI and something strong for the pain so she can get back to work tomorrow.

**PMHx**: Type 2 diabetes, Obesity; there is no family history of malignancy.

**Meds**: Metformin

**Social**: Occasional alcohol use. Married with 2 children. Works as CNA.

**Exam:** Gait is cautious with minimal twisting with direction change

Vital signs stable, afebrile. The cardiac, pulmonary, skin, and GI exams are unremarkable. Likewise, examination of the cervical and thoracic spine is benign.

Back Exam:

* Gross Inspection: loss of natural lumbar lordosis
* Palpation: no vertebral TTP or step off, iliac crests are symmetric. The left lumbar paraspinal muscles are tender and more prominent than on the right.
* ROM: flexion of lumbar spine limited by pain at 30 degrees. There is pain with lateral bending to the right elicits left lumbar pain. Extension and lateral bending to the left are normal.
* Neurologic: strength, sensation, and reflexes in the lower extremities are intact. Students should test: Strength: hip flexion (L2), knee extension (L3), foot dorsiflexion (L4) and plantar flexion (S1), and extension of the great toe (L5); Reflexes: Patellar (L4) and Achilles (S1) reflexes;
* Specialty testing: Absent babinski, No clonus. Straight leg and cross leg raise tests negative, Reverse Lasegue also negative.

**Differential Diagnosis** (not comprehensive):

* + **Acute paraspinal muscle strain**
  + Disk herniation
  + Vertebral compression fracture
  + Spondylosis

**Management Options include:**

* + Return to normal physical activity, avoid bed rest and overexertion (heavy lifting, twisting)
  + NSAIDS
  + Consider muscle relaxants
  + Warm compresses
  + Consider Physical Therapy and/or stretching exercises
  + A note to employer requesting she avoid heavy lifting and twisting activities
  + Follow up if not improving in 3-4 weeks
  + Imaging and narcotics would not be justified at this time
