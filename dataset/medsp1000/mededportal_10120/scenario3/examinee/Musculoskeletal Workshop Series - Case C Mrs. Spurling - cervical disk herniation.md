# Case C: Mrs. Spurling

**General Instructions:** The focus of this workshop is on the exam itself, and the interpretation of one’s findings, rather than history-taking. The provided history is presumed to be accurate and complete.

**Patient Name**: Mrs. Spurling, 63 years old

**Setting:** Outpatient clinic – exam room

**Complaint**: Acute neck and shoulder pain

**HPI**: Mrs. Spurling is a retired accountant who reports sharp pain in her **left** shoulder that began suddenly after getting out of bed two weeks ago. Over the past week she has developed concomitant left neck pain which radiates down her arm to her fingertips.. The course is intermittent and Mrs. Spurling states the pain is the worst when she is driving. Otherwise there is no temporal association or other aggravating/alleviating factors. She has taken over the counter medication with minimal relief, stating ibuprofen has worked the best. Denies fevers, chills or prior episodes. Reports occasional numbness over upper arm. Also states she feels a bit weaker when lifting up her grandson over past week. Otherwise ROS is negative. **Her left neck/shoulder pain is presently a 4 out of 10 and radiates down her arm**.

**PMHx**: HTN, DM, L knee arthroplasty 5 years ago

**Meds**: Metformin 500 BID, HCTZ 12.5 QD

**Social**: Smokes ½ pack per day for 20 years. No hx of alcohol or drugs. Worked in Miami as accountant. Recently moved to Connecticut to enjoy the winters.

**Exam:** The patient appears with L arm adducted to side. Normal gait and pleasant affect.

Vital signs stable, afebrile. The cardiac, pulmonary, GI, and skin exams are unremarkable.
