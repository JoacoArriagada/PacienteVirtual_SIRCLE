**Musculoskeletal Workshop Instructor’s Guide:**

**Submission Resource Files:**

There are four standardized patient cases regarding commonly encountered neck and spine musculoskeletal conditions. Each case file includes three components. 1) An instructor copy of the case to which the standardized patient and instructor have access. This includes all the details of the case presentation, including the pertinent exam, differential diagnosis and management options. 2) Instructor Evaluation form with instructions for standardized grading of the exam. 3) Learner copy of the case with relevant history and non-musculoskeletal physical exam.

**Practical Implementation:**

Each workshop consists of an interactive sixty to ninety minute lecture which reviews relevant anatomy, history, physical exam findings, work up and treatment for each relevant musculoskeletal disorder. The interactive portion of the workshop consists of three to four OSCE stations through which residents rotate over a ninety-minute period. Volunteer second year medical students serve as standardized patients, after receiving forty minutes of training by the workshop director prior to the workshops. Insofar as case history is provided to workshop participants, SP training is limited to the physical exam. Thus, SP training involves a review of relevant anatomy and practice with responding to components of the exam (e.g. restricting range of movement or expressions of pain during certain maneuvers). The medical student’s performance as a standardized patient is further assessed and perfected by feedback from faculty members during the workshop itself.

As described above, the nature of the case scenarios, which emphasize physical examination skills rather than history-taking, make both the training of SPs and the portrayal of the cases fairly straightforward. During the brief forty minute training session, medical student SPs learned which maneuvers should elicit a response. Student SPs were advised to respond to maneuvers as if they were being performed correctly, in order to avoid confounding the residents’ creation of a post-examination differential diagnosis. Faculty instructors were present throughout the workshops to give the medical student SPs real-time feedback on their portrayal of the cases.
