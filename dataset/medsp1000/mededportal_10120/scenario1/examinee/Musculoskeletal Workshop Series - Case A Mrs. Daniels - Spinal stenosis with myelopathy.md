# Case A: Mrs. Daniels

**General Instructions**: The focus of this workshop is on the exam itself, and the interpretation of one’s findings, rather than history-taking. The provided history is presumed to be accurate and complete.

**Patient Name**: Mrs. Daniels, 72 years old

**Setting:** Outpatient clinic – exam room

**Complaint**: Increasing low back pain

**HPI**: Mrs. Daniels states she has had low back pain for a number of years, but feels the pain has gotten much worse over the past several weeks. She reports her pain is worst after walking for more than 5 minutes, and she tends to get pain in her right buttock and leg at the same time. She feels she has been less steady on her feet of late, and finds it more difficult to get out of a chair. She denies fevers, chills, night sweats, or weight loss. She has no problems with pain or weakness in her arms or neck. She takes 5-6 extra-strength Tylenol every day with mild pain relief. No history of trauma.

**PMHx**: HTN

**Meds**: Chlorthalidone

**Social**: No hx of alcohol, tobacco or drug use.

**Exam:** The patient is sitting on the examination table, in no acute distress, somewhat slumped forward.

Vitals: Temp 98.4 HR 60 BP 128/78 RR 15

The cardiac, pulmonary, GI and skin exams are unremarkable. Likewise, examination of the neck and upper extremities are benign. Her gait is mildly unsteady.
