# Instructor Guide

# Title: Interprofessional Geriatric Assessment Elective for Health Professional Students: A Standardized Patient Case Study and Patient Script

# Use of Resource:

This case study is designed to be used with a team of any mix of interprofessional healthcare students and 1 standardized patient as a standalone activity or as a part of a larger interprofessional course. The standardized patient encounter utilizes three documents. The Case Study 1: Standardized Patient Script is presented to the standardized patient as part of preencounter training. The script is reviewed with faculty to clarify any questions related to the role. Immediately before the encounter, students receive one document, Case Study 1: Introduction; the only document available to guide the student team assessments. The instructor may choose to have a guided small group discussion using the provided questions prior to the students meeting the standardized patient. It is up to the student groups to decide how to go about doing team interviews (how to divide up the time, what to cover, how to handle logistics of a team interview etc.) Fifty minutes is allotted for the team assessment. Following the SP encounter, teams receive Case Study 1: Physical Exam and laboratory findings. This document provides additional information about the patient e.g., laboratory results that would be reasonable for teams to have requested as part of the assessment. Developing a team- oriented care plan is based on the 2 documents and the 50-minute assessment.

# Purpose of Resource:

This case study encompasses a wide range of geriatric assessment issues and requires attention to topics including but not limited to: common health conditions of older age, pain, cultural considerations, cognitive concerns, issues related to safety and function, family concerns, and ethics.

Moreover, the case study presents an opportunity for students to develop skill in geriatric assessment and interprofessional teamwork, working towards the following educational objectives. The faculty members evaluate the students using the McMaster-Ottawa Team

Observed Structured Clinical Encounter (TOSCE)1 . Each objective is linked to a TOSCE core objective as indicated in parentheses.

1. Actively communicate as an effective member of an interprofessional team (Communication)   
2. Demonstrate emerging collaborative skills while developing a patient-centered care plan (Collaboration)   
3. Describe the unique perspective, value, contributions, and responsibilities that healthcare professionals bring to the geriatric interprofessional team. (Roles and Responsibilities)   
4. Demonstrate the ability to collaborate with the patient and family as well as develop an understanding of the potential barriers to communication with geriatric patients including health literacy, cultural barriers, cognitive, psychosocial, and physical impairments. (Collaborative Patient-Family Centered Approach)   
5. Explore and develop an understanding of the ethical dilemmas that arise in the care of older adults and manage conflicts that may occur from different perspectives of team members. (Conflict Management/Resolution)   
6. Identify and develop effective teamwork skills and strategies essential for interprofessional geriatric care. (Team Function) In addition, the faculty utilized the “Oral Case Presentation Assessment Tool” and the “Written Case Presentation Assessment Tool” to assess the teams’ written and oral patient presentations after the OSCE. The oral and written assessment forms were developed by the faculty through a consensus process, with a focus on the teaching goals of developing a team assessment and problem-solving approach, presentation/documentation in a standard   
subjective/objective/assessment/plan (SOAP) format, and the coordinated approach of the interprofessional student team.

# Implementation advice:

In order to successfully implement this case study as a team observed structured clinical exam, a number of logistics need to be considered. This section describes how the authors implemented this case.

On the day of the simulation, to prepare for the encounter, student teams are given one hour to develop a team plan that addresses how to use their team assessment time, how to divide assessment tasks, and what types of assessments to use. They are then given 50-minutes to interview and examine the standardized patient in an outpatient office setting. Encounter props include an examination table. In addition, the student teams should have access to standard physical assessment equipment.

Prior to the encounter, standardized patients (SPs), were selected from a pool of potential candidates maintained at the health sciences center based on approximate age of the SPs. If a pool of SPs is not maintained at your institution, consider seeking volunteer actors from community theater groups or senior centers that might be interested in supporting educational endeavors. The case was sent to the SPs for review prior to the day of the encounter. The SPs met with one of the faculty members for about an hour prior to the encounter to review the details of the case and expectations of their roles, including how they should respond to various questions from the team.

We have at least 2 faculty members from different professions observing each OSCE and also video record the sessions for other faculty to observe later if needed. Faculty members observe the assessment from behind one-way mirrors. If a separate observation room is not available, then faculty members may choose to observe from the corner of the room. After the encounter is over, the standardized patients give the students verbal feedback, and then the faculty members give verbal feedback as well, and time is spent debriefing the experience. The faculty members also evaluate the students using the McMaster-Ottawa Team Observed Structured Clinical Encounter (TOSCE)5 . The TOSCE tool is used to evaluate each student and team in the areas of communication, collaboration, roles and responsibilities, collaborative patient-family centered approach, conflict management/resolution, and team functioning. The results from the TOSCE are shared with each team verbally after the OSCE and are also entered into our electronic course management system for the teams to view.

Finally, the student teams are required to write and orally present a comprehensive, coordinated care plan describing findings and recommendations. An example note is available as a separate document. Students are instructed to create care plans that are client-centered, and that reflect all of the team members’ expertise in an integrated manner. After the case study is concluded, students complete a team effectiveness assessment about their group. The faculty facilitators use the “Oral Case Presentation Tool” to assess the oral team presentations and the “Written Case Presentation Assessment Tool” to evaluate the teams’ written notes.
