**Case 4:** A 28-year-old woman with a history of headaches presents with a typical headache though worse than usual. She has had headaches for several years, though she normally does not have to come to the ED for these. This headache was gradual in onset and slowly progressing but more severe than her typical headaches. The headache is unilateral and throbbing in nature, with phonophobia and photophobia. She is afebrile with normal vital signs, a normal detailed neurologic examination, and an otherwise unremarkable physical exam.

1. How would you discuss your decision-making with this patient in terms of your diagnostic work-up for her headache?
2. How would you describe the therapeutic interventions you plan for this this headache?

(Facilitators: Discuss how to communicate differential diagnosis to patients, indications and modalities for neuroimaging for headaches, and how to discuss performing a lumbar puncture when necessary.)
