**Case 2**

PATIENT: You are a healthy 30-year-old patient who saw your primary care physician (PCP) this morning for 1 week of back pain, and he recommended you go to the ED for an MRI. You are not having any “red flag” symptoms such as fever, numbness, weakness, urinary or bowel incontinence or perineal anesthesia. (Really try to have the doctor order the MRI. “My doctor told me...” etc.)

DOCTOR: Your patient is young and otherwise healthy, and they were sent by their PCP to have an MRI of their spine due to back pain. However, they have no “red flag” signs or symptoms.

1. How do you approach this patient who is expecting advanced imaging per her doctor?
2. You assess that no emergent workup needs to be done today. How do you ensure your patient understands they are receiving high quality of care?
3. What barriers exist to communication in this encounter, and what approaches can you take to overcome them?

(Facilitators: Explain how reviewing vital signs, exam findings, risk factors and differential diagnosis may be beneficial in reassuring patients and preventing unnecessary or invasive testing.)
