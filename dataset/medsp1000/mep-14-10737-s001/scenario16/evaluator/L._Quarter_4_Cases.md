**Case 3**

PATIENT: You are in the ED for acute abdominal pain. Your labs and CT scan are concerning for appendicitis. You have been given pain medicine, which is helping some. It is time for shift change, and your doctor tells you that a new doctor is coming in to take over your care. He has also called the surgeon to come and evaluate you.

DOCTOR: Your patient has CT-proven appendicitis. It is shift change, and you are signing this out to the oncoming provider. Her pain is still 7/10 after IV analgesia.

1. How do you approach “coaching up” the oncoming team to (or with) your patient?
2. How do you approach “coaching up” the surgeon or surgical team to you patient?
3. What other measures can be taken to ensure a smooth transition of care?

(Facilitators: Discuss the importance of a good and safe patient hand-off and the potential for errors during transitions of care. Talk about the importance of a “bedside” hand-off or sign-out and how that might improve patient satisfaction. Talk about the importance of praising our consultants for their expertise in front of patients.)
