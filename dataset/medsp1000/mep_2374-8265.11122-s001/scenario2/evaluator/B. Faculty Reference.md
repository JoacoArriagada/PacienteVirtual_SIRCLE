**Faculty facilitators:**

The faculty facilitators are select group of clinicians who are experienced in serious illness communication and simulation-based teaching. These faculty members have either taught communication skills in similar settings and/or have received specialized training in serious illness communication. The chosen faculty are required to attend an annual faculty development meeting with video review of prior sessions in order to standardize teaching and evaluation. They act as facilitators in the scenario rooms to guide the conversations, call timeouts and provide appropriate and timely feedback. The facilitators also complete evaluations of residents’ performance at the end of the simulation trainings and Mini-CEX encounters.

**Faculty facilitator tasks before SP encounters:**

* Huddle with learners outside the encounter room and address any questions and clarifications about the scenario.
* Elicit learning goals and specific skills to practice from each learner before the encounter (sitting with emotion, using clear language without jargon, using silence, sharing prognosis, or other)
* Plan with learners’ regarding encounter (i.e. who will go first, second) based on the learning goals and how they match with the flow of the SICG.

**Faculty facilitator tasks during encounters:**

* Try to steer the learners away from their comfort zone of “medical management” i.e. getting into discussions of LVAD, inotropes.
* Keep eye on the watch while listening to the conversation during encounter to call time-outs at appropriate time intervals; take copious notes with specific language used by learners and SPs as you observe and target your feedback towards learner goals.
* Solicit short feedback from all the participants (SP’s and both residents) at each time-out and clearly articulate a skill to work on for the learner.
* Invite the learner to redo certain parts of conversation when needed for practice.

**Guidelines for faculty feedback:**

* Call timeouts at moments of learning opportunities that link to residents’ goals (usually every 5-6 min) for debriefing and feedback
* The feedback should be tailored to residents’ stage of development; remember that the residents have only finished one year of their training.
* Faculty should notice and facilitate feedback on the residents’ interaction with both the patient and family member.
* Since your time for feedback is limited, prioritize items based on specific, observed behaviors.
* Use the Learner Evaluation and Content and communication skills document as a guide to what feedback is most appropriate.
* Use a Plus/Delta approach:
  + What did the interviewer do well (to reinforce the good behavior)?
  + What could be changed to be more effective?
  + Would it be appropriate to suggest different words or strategies that others have used and that have worked well?
* The faculty facilitators can include these questions in debrief-
  + What went well?
  + Where did you feel stuck?
  + Is there something you would specifically want to redo?

* **Scenario B description** (Time allotted 35 min): Discussing next steps in care and discharge plan (hospice if appropriate). 2 residents/learners; 1 Faculty Facilitator; 2 SPs - 1 patient, 1 adult child/family member (S*cenario Room)*
  + Outside the room: Team Huddle (5 min) with learners, faculty facilitator
  + The simulation center staff member announces overhead the start of the session and learners enter room.
  + Interview and feedback with SPs (30 min)
  + The simulation center staff member announces overhead when 5 minutes left in session so as to guide closing the conversation and again at the end.

**After completing session B, the learners will be able to:**

* Provide empathic presence and sit with strong emotion
* Describe eligibility and services of hospice care
* Make a recommendation about hospice care
* Recognize care transitions as integral part of patient care and recognize case manager as part of interdisciplinary team
