Case Study: Anh Kha Smith is Mr. Kha’s sister and was the first of the Kha family to come to the US. She is 65 years of age. She and her husband, David, who is 60 years of age, have been married for 40 years and live in a 3 bedroom patio home just outside of the city. Mrs. Smith has a close relationship to her brother and provides assistance to her niece, Van Tran with her father’s care.

One month ago, Mrs. Smith, who is right hand dominant, tripped on an uneven piece of cracked sidewalk, and fell, landing on the heel of her right hand. Her neighbor witnessed the incident and called Anh’s husband who took her to the ED. Anh was diagnosed with a Colle’s fracture of her right wrist. Because her past medical history included hypertension and mild chronic obstructive pulmonary disease due to second-hand smoke exposure (both well controlled), the orthopedic surgeon performed a nonsurgical closed reduction of the fracture in the ED. The post-reduction radiographs revealed good alignment with approximately 5 degrees of dorsal angulation. Mrs. Smith’s wrist was placed in a volar splint with elastic wrap, and she was admitted to the general orthopedic unit for observation and pain management. An occupational therapy consult was obtained so that a customized volar splint could be fabricated after the edema in her hand and wrist resolved. While in the hospital, a DEXA scan was performed to rule out osteoporosis. The DEXA scan revealed a T score of -2.6 on the lumbar spine and hip. Mrs. Smith was started on oral Calcium with Vitamin D, 600 mg/200 IU PO TID and Percocet 5/500 one tab PO Q 6 hours PRN pain. She was informed that she would be started on a bisphosphonate at her one month follow-up visit. She was discharged home with her husband 3 days later without complications with a customized right wrist volar splint. She was instructed to return for a follow-up appointment in 1 month.

Mrs. Smith is being seen today for her four-week recheck of her right wrist fracture. She states that her pain is well managed with the Percocet. She is taking one tab with food in the morning and one tab at bedtime to help her to sleep. She occasionally takes a dose in the afternoon if needed but this is rare. She denies nausea or vomiting. She says that she has a very firm BM every 3 days. Mrs. Smith states this is a change from her normal routine which was one formed/soft BM every morning since she was hospitalized.
