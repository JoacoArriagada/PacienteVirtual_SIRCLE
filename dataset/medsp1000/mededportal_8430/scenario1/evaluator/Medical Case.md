1. Learning Objectives

9. **Case Narrative**
10. Scenario Background Given to Participants
11. Chief complaint, triage note, medic report - 24 year old healthy, but anxious, female with vaginal bleeding. The patient complains of lower abdominal pain for the last 3 days, worse in the last 6 hours.
12. Past medical history
    Illnesses: mild asthma
    Surgeries: none
13. Meds and allergies
    Meds: albuterol inhaler as needed
    Allergies: NKDA
    Habits: denies smoking and illicit drug use, drinks alcohol 4 times per week, 3-4 drinks at a time.
14. OB/Gyn Hx: never been pregnant, has regular periods but isn’t sure when her last period was. Has no history of sexually transmitted diseases. Never had a Pap smear.
15. Physical Exam
    Thin, fit appearing female, anxious and in mild distress
    Wt : 53 kg T : 37.2 HR : 95 BP : 100/60 RR : 20
    Lungs: clear
    Heart: normal

Abdomen: slightly distended, tender to palpation in bilateral lower quadrants with questionable rebound tenderness. No masses, HSM, or hernia.

PV: not done

1. Pelvic Ultrasound

Uterus: 7.3 cm x 3.5 cm x 4.4 cm

Endometrium 2.9 mm with no intrauterine pregnancy

Left ovary: 2.2 x 3.8 x 3.4 cm

Right ovary: 3.3 x 1.5 x 3.8 cm

Free fluid in cul-de-sac: minimal

1. Labs
   Urine Pregnancy Test: positive

Hematocrit: 35
White blood cell count: 10.8
Electrolytes, Glucose, Blood Urea Nitrogen, creatinine : normal

1. NPO Status
   Last ate 4 hours ago
2. State: Arrive
3. History patient gives: The patient is uncomfortable but complies with all requests.
4. Exam: Abdomen is slightly distended, tender to palpation in bilateral lower quadrants with questionable rebound tenderness without masses, hepatospenomegaly, or hernia.
5. Visual speculum exam: Nulliparous cervix with small amount of bleeding from the cervical os.
6. Message given if Palpation of the Uterus/Adnexa: It is not appropriate to perform a pelvic exam at this time.
7. Phys: HR 105, 90/ 60
8. What Next: Perform quick history and physical exam. Do not perform brisk palpation of the uterus/adnexa at this time. Establish IV access and administer fluids. Order pregnancy test and pelvic ultrasound.

## Pilot Testing and Revisions

1. Numbers of participants – this scenario is modeled from the simulations used by the anesthesia residents over the last 2 decades at the University of Washington. This clinical tool uses a modification, making it applicable to OB/Gyn trainees. It has now been incorporated in the training for the first year OB/Gyn residents. Their surveys reveal that this is a worthwhile activity that allows them to be better prepared for similar clinical scenarios.
2. Performance expectations, anticipated management mistakes:
3. Many junior residents take 5 or more minutes to diagnose ectopic pregnancy.
4. Many residents fail to act to definitely diagnose ectopic pregnancy and fluid resuscitate while they are making management decisions.
5. Evaluation form for participants: Participants receive a detailed printed record of the case simulation and printed debriefing of their case management.
