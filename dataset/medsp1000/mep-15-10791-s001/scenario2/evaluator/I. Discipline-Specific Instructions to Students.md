***Instructions to Nursing Student***

**Background:**

You are the nurse taking care of Ms. Dunn, an 80 y/o woman with hypertension, diabetes, coronary artery disease with previous myocardial infarction, and renal insufficiency. She presented to the ER for chest pain and was admitted to your unit for further work-up due to her risk of acute coronary syndrome (ACS). She is a retired school teacher, widowed, and lives alone with one adult child who lives close by. You did not meet the patient’s son/daughter before your shift, but you know they will be coming back shortly to stay with the patient.

The physician has ordered anticoagulation for the patient due to her high risk of ACS. Because of her renal insufficiency (Cr 2.8), an order is placed for heparin. The patient weights 60-kg. The order you are given is for 36,000 units of heparin.

Approximately 30 minutes later, the pharmacy resident calls you to verify the order. You call the physician to verify the order and s/he states that the order is correct. You call pharmacy to confirm.

Approximately 15 minutes later, the heparin arrives and you notice that there are more vials than normal. You again call the physician to verify and s/he again verifies the order.

You give the medication as ordered.

Two hours later, a cardiology fellow comes up to the floor to see the patient. She realizes the mistake and asks how it happened. You tell her that pharmacy called to verify the order and you called the physician twice to confirm. The fellow states that the patient needs to be transferred STAT to the CCU for closer monitoring and orders protamine to reverse the heparin. The patient is otherwise doing well and has no signs of bleeding or other complications. The fellow tells you there must be a family meeting right away to discuss the error. She contacts the responsible physician and pharmacist, but is paged to a patient with ventricular tachycardia in the ED. She instructs you and the physician and pharmacy resident involved to discuss the error with the patient’s family.

**Instructions to the Nursing Student**:

You will now have approximately 10 minutes to discuss this case with your colleagues before meeting with the patient’s son/daughter to inform them of what has happened. They have only been told that the patient was transferred to the ICU and was stable.

***Instructions to Medical Student***

**Background:**

You are the intern taking care of Ms. Dunn, an 80 y/o woman with hypertension, diabetes, coronary artery disease with previous myocardial infarction, and renal insufficiency. She presented to the ER for chest pain and was admitted to your unit for further work-up due to her risk of acute coronary syndrome (ACS). She is a retired school teacher, widowed, and lives alone with one adult child who lives close by. Approximately 15 minutes later, the nurse again calls you to verify the order. You again state that the order is correct and ask that she please complete your orders as written.

Two hours later, a cardiology fellow calls you about the patient. She informs you about the mistake in dosing and asks how it happened. You tell her that you calculated the dose, but must have ordered it incorrectly. You also verify that the nurse called you twice to verify the order and both times you instructed to her to give the medication as ordered. The fellow states that the patient needs to be transferred STAT to the CCU for closer monitoring and orders protamine to reverse the heparin. The patient is otherwise doing well and has no signs of bleeding or other complications. The fellow tells you there must be a family meeting right away to discuss the error. She contacts the responsible pharmacy resident and nurse, but is paged to a patient with ventricular tachycardia in the ED. She instructs you, the nurse, and pharmacy resident involved to discuss the error with the patient’s family.

**Instructions to the Medical Student**:

You will now have approximately 10 minutes to discuss this case with your colleagues before meeting with the patient’s son/daughter to inform them of what has happened. They have only been told that the patient was transferred to the ICU and was stable.
