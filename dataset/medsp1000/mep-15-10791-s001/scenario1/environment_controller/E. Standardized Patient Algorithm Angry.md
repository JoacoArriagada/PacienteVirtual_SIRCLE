| The team will tell you that they made a medication error that resulted in the need to transfer your mom to an intensive care unit (ICU) setting for careful monitoring. You are furious. You had just left the hospital and were to return almost immediately. During that short time at home, these people make a mistake that could’ve killed your mother. You are angry and you want answers. Whether you stay ANGRY or begin to de-escalate to agitation and to calmer concern depends on how the team responds. | ***Student Team Behavior:***  *The group will introduce themselves (hopefully) and explain that a medical error was committed.*  **SP Anger level = Very high; SP Vocal tone = low level yelling, “spitting words.”**  **SP Action to a failure to introduce all team members:** “Wait a minute – I want to know who each of you are! Who are you?” (Students should introduce everyone.)  **SP Action AFTER Introductions:** “How could you all make this mistake? Who is responsible?” | Students should try to answer your last two questions.  **SP Anger level = Very high; SP Vocal tone = low level yelling, “spitting words.”**  SP Action if only one person responds for team:  “What do you have to say about this? How could have let this happen?” (directed to those not speaking)  **NOTE: Teams may respond differently. Two general response patterns are:**  **RESPONSIVE:**   * Takes responsibility for mistake * Is calm in voice and demeanor * Is empathetic to your anger and concern   **DISMISSIVE and/or ESCALATED:**   * Blames individual team members or fails to take responsibility as a team * Voice and demeanor are raised, agitated or dismissive of your concerns in response * Does not acknowledge your anger in a genuine way | SP ACTION will depend on student response pattern. | Students should try to answer your last questions.  SP ACTION will depend on student response pattern. | Students should try to answer your last questions.  SP DISPOSITION will depend on student response pattern OVER THE COURSE OF THE ENCOUNTER. |
| IF STUDENT TEAM RESPONSE PATTERN IS: RESPONSIVE  **SP Anger level = Slightly lower; SP Vocal tone = low level yelling but clearer articulation**  SP Action to Responses:   * Is my mother going to die? * Will this affect her heart? * How long does she have to be in the ICU? | IF STUDENT TEAM RESPONSE PATTERN IS: RESPONSIVE  **SP Anger level = Slightly lower;**  **SP Vocal tone = Calmer delivery at lower voice, though still agitated. You are beginning to calm down.**  SP Action to Responses:  Gather information   * How are you going to make sure this doesn’t happen again? | IF STUDENT TEAM RESPONSE PATTERN IS: RESPONSIVE  **SP’s anger should have dissipated to just slight agitation and concern for mother. SP’s tone should be calm, but worried.**  Student team may or may not attempt to close the encounter, depending on how the exchanges go and the students’ use of time. |
| IF STUDENT TEAM RESPONSE PATTERN IS: DISMISSIVE, ESCALATED  **SP Anger level = Remains high;**  **SP Vocal tone = remains at low level yelling and still “spitting words.”**  SP Action to Responses:   * Is my mother going to die? * Will this affect her heart? * How long does she have to be in the ICU? | IF STUDENT TEAM RESPONSE PATTERN IS: DISMISSIVE, ESCALATED; NON-EMPATHETIC  **SP Anger level = Remains high;**  **SP Vocal tone = remains at low level yelling still “spitting words.”**  SP Action to Responses:  Gather information   * How are you going to make sure this doesn’t happen again? * Ask to have your parent transferred or given another team. | IF STUDENT TEAM RESPONSE PATTERN IS: DISMISSIVE, ESCALATED, NON-EMPATHETIC, overall  **SP’s anger will not dissipate very much, and SP will want another team caring for her. May shift to concern for mother. SP’s tone should be coldly angry.**  Student team may or may not attempt to close the encounter, depending on how the exchanges go and the students’ use of time. |
