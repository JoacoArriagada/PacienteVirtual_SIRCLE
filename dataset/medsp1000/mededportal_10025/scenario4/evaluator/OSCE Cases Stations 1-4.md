**Station 4**

**Clinical Scenario:**

Terry Phillips (age 40-60) is in the outpatient clinic today for a work physical.

The patient who is overweight recently attended a community health screening and was told the patient has HBP.

Today, the nurse took the patient’s BP and patient again has elevated BP of 170/108.

**Instructions:**

Please discuss with the patient the need for taking HBP medication.

Do not perform any part of the Physical Examination.

**Type of SP Case:** Formative, Communication, Interpersonal, Counseling, Health Disparities, (No Physical Exam)

**Length of Patient Encounter:** 9 minutes

**Setting:** Outpatient Clinic

**Rating form:** Kalamazoo, patient-centered feedback

**Learner Level:** Post-Graduate (Residency) – Years 1 - 4/ Medical & Surgical Specialties

**Learner Objective:** Learners will demonstrate ability to discuss the recommended therapy (to prescribe HBP medication and NOT anti-anxiety medication) with the patient, eliciting the patient’s hesitations about having the surgery while respectfully negotiating the options with the patient.

**Case overview:** Terry Phillips is a middle-aged (age range 40’s – 60’s), overweight patient and is in the office for work (Department of Transportation) PE. Terry is under a great deal of stress and recently attended a community health screening where the patient was told the patient had high blood pressure. The patient feels the need for medication to help reduce stress levels and reports that the patient is managing the HBP with garlic tabs. The patient does not believe the patient has much control over hithe patientr weight as the patient thinks a “fat gene” runs in hithe patientr family.

**STANDARIZED PATIENT SCENARIO BRANCHING:**

**Goal for** **Final Outcome:** Patient feels the resident has respectfully listened to her concerns and feels informed enough to give consideration to taking HBP medication as well as the dangers associated with taking anti-anxiety medication. However, this is going to be achieved based on the communication skills of the resident.

**Cue for SP:**

If the resident probes and discovers your rationale for the medications you should or should not be taking, and respectfully offers you alternative considerations, then you can begin to focus and, at the very least, consider the options presented by the resident.

If the resident simply dismisses your rationale, and/or comes across as judgmental or disrespectful, then you should remain firm in your requests for those therapies (meds) YOU feel are appropriate (continuing on the garlic tabs, insisting that you have inherited a “fat gene”, and continue to request anti-anxiety medications).
