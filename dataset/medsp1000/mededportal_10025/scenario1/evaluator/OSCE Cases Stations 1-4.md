**Station 1**

**Clinical Scenario:**

Ida Creswell, 51, female has come into the ER because of recurring dizziness.

Mrs. Creswell is perimenopausal, with a history of heavy menstrual flow. Her doctor has prescribed iron in the past, for which she has been intermittently compliant.

When tested, her hemoglobin came back at 5.4.

When seated, patient pulse is 95; BP is 110/60. Upon standing, pulse is 120; BP is 90/40

**Instructions:**

Please discuss the need for a transfusion with the patient, covering the basic components of an informed consent (i.e., risks, benefits, etc.).

Do not perform any physical exam maneuvers.

**Type of SP Case:** Formative, Communication, Interpersonal, Counseling, Difficult Encounter (No Physical Exam)

**Length of Patient Encounter:** 9 minutes

**Setting:** Emergency Room

**Rating form:** Kalamazoo, patient-centered feedback

**Learner Level:** Post-Graduate (Residency) – Years 1 - 4/ Medical & Surgical Specialties

**Learner Objective:** Learners will demonstrate ability to complete the informed consent process ensuring the patient understands all risks and benefits therein.

**Case overview:** Ida Creswell is a perimenopausal patient, 51, who has been admitted to ER because of dizziness. Patient has orthostatic hypotension (dizziness). When tested, her hemoglobin is 5.4, ***which is dangerously low.*** The patient discloses that her OB has prescribed iron to her in the past, but that she doesn’t usually take it. The physician explains what anemia is, how serious it can be and how it can be treated; that recommended treatment for this level of severity is transfusion. The patient expresses concern about needing a transfusion, with a belief that it could be risky. The resident should cover all aspects of the informed consent, including information on safety and risks associated with transfusions, that her particular situation is not complicated, and the transfusion would supply her body with what it needed so that she could feel better more quickly and go home.

| Resident Task: | **Counseling Points**   * Resident should tell you that blood tests show that your red blood cell level is low, which creates a condition called anemia. * Should ask: *Has anyone ever told you that before?* * Resident will explain the degree of anemia, using numbers to explain relative severity. Specifically:   + Your hemoglobin level is 5.4.   + Normal menstruating female is 12-15.   + Milder anemia (8-10) is treated with iron supplements.   + Anything lower than 7 is usually treated with a recommendation for transfusion * Therefore, transfusion is recommended for your level of severity. * Resident will explain that severe anemia can cause problems beyond dizziness like stroke, heart attack and kidney damage * Resident should acknowledge that this is scary for you, - and certainly isn’t what you expected today when you came here * Resident should explain blood loss vs. how fast your body could make blood; that because of your heavy vaginal bleeding, your body could not keep supplying what your body needed; that the iron your doctor prescribed would help, but that it takes a very long time to work. With a transfusion, you will feel much better very quickly.   Resident should stop intermittently if they don’t the patient can be confused. |
