**Station 4**

**Clinical Scenario:**

Casey Denham (age 40-60) is in for a surgical consult for a gastrectomy.

Patient had presented to the primary care physician with complaints of loss of appetite, nausea and heartburn.

An upper GI series and gastroscopy/biopsy showed stage 2 adenocarcinoma of the stomach.

The patient does not want the surgery.

**Instructions:**

Please elicit the patient’s explanation of why the patient does not want surgery and negotiate with the patient to agree to surgery.

Do not perform any part of the Physical Examination.

**Type of SP Case:** Formative, Communication, Interpersonal, Counseling, Health Disparities, (No Physical Exam)

**Length of Patient Encounter:** 9 minutes

**Setting:** Consult Room

**Rating form:** Kalamazoo, patient-centered feedback

**Learner Level:** Post-Graduate (Residency) – Years 1 - 4/ Medical & Surgical Specialties

**Learner Objective:** Learners will demonstrate ability to discuss the recommended surgery with the patient, eliciting the patient’s hesitations about having the surgery while respectfully negotiating the options with the patient.

**Case overview:** Casey Denham is a middle-aged patient, (age 40-60), and is in the office for a surgical consult for a gastrectomy. Casey had seen the primary care physician previously with complaints of loss of appetite, nausea and heartburn. An upper GI series and gastroscopy/biopsy showed stage 2 adenocarcinoma of the stomach.

The patient does not want the surgery due to the patient's mother having died from stomach cancer; Casey believes that the surgery the patient's mother underwent for the stomach cancer exposed the cancer to air, which caused the cancer to grow more quickly and spread. The patient expresses concern about needing this surgery, maintaining that it (the surgery) is more dangerous than the cancer itself. The resident should share the need for the surgery with Casey, respectfully listen to the patient’s concerns and attempt to negotiate with the patient to have the surgery.

**STANDARIZED PATIENT SCENARIO BRANCHING:**

**Goal for** **Final Outcome:** Patient feels the resident has respectfully listened to her concerns and feels informed enough to give consideration to having the surgery. However, this is going to be achieved based on the communication skills of the resident.

**Cue for SP:**

If the resident probes and discovers the anxiety source and reassures you that you will be safe, then you begin to calm and at least consider surgery as an option.

If the resident says that the surgery is needed, but does not probe or reassure your fears, then you should refuse the surgery and continue to say no.
