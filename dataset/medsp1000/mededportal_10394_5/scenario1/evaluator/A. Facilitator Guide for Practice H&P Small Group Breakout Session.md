# Facilitator Guide for Practice History and Physical (H&P) Small Group Breakout Session

# Special Note:

This instructor guide focuses on detailed instructions for the “Ivan Topee” UTI example H&P.   
The supplemental H&Ps provided in this curriculum can be used for additional practice.   
Feedback and common pitfalls will parallel this example.

# Group set-up:

Small groups with 2-3 medical students/facilitator.

# Timeline for simulated practice:

Allow for about 30-35 minutes for the small group practice.

First two minutes are for introductions of group members and to review expectations of practice.

o Assign each medical student a section of the H&P to present.

If two students: First student will present until the physical exam. Second student will present from the physical exam and through the assessment and plan.  If three students: First student will present through the review of systems. Second student will present until the end of the labs/imaging. The final student will present the assessment and plan.

Allow the students 5-7 minutes to read the entire H&P to familiarize themselves with the sections they will be presenting.

Spend the remainder of the time having the students present their sections and to get directed feedback.

o Have the students present in the appropriate order. Feedback should occur immediately after each student presents.   
o Instruct the student(s) that are not presenting that they should be prepared to give the presenting student feedback based on the FCR principles that were just reviewed.   
o Once the students provide feedback to one another, the instructor should supplement with any additional pertinent points.

Allow 2-3 minutes at the end for any final questions from the students regarding expectations for oral presentations.

# Expectations to outline prior to practice:

The practice H&P provides a good example for a complete written H&P with all the necessary components and details. Emphasize that oral presentation for FCR is geared towards the pertinent positive and negatives relevant to the chief complaint.

o Acknowledge that this means that some aspects of the written H&P will simply not be said (e.g. no need to restate the entire Review of Systems [ROS]) if all the pertinent positive and negative symptoms will have already been mentioned in the History of Present Illness [HPI]).

Normalize difficulty with presenting an H&P on a patient they have not personally obtained the H&P from. However, also stress this is a skill that they need to develop as they progress to residency.   
Emphasize that the students should ask for any particular expectations from their senior residents and attendings at the start of their rotation, especially in the setting of more complex patients or sub-specialty services.

Facilitator Feedback and Common Pitfalls to be Addressed:

- NOTE: The feedback below is recommended based on the expectations of attendings and senior residents at our institution. Please tailor your feedback to include your institution-specific expectations.

HPI

o Many students will provide a detailed fever history during their oral presentation. Instruct student to simplify history to persistent fevers at home over several days despite Tylenol administration.   
o Many students will provide detailed intake and output history. Instruct student to simply state if intake and output were appropriate or below goals for age.   
o Remind students of importance of discussing other pertinent positive and negative symptoms.   
o Many students will either not present the ED course at all or present almost verbatim what is written.   
Explain which aspects are important to include and how to say them in a FCR  
appropriate way.  Quick summary of initial examination (well-appearing, signs of focal infection, etc). Explanation that laboratory examination performed was consistent with a urinary tract infection. Explain that all the details of the labs do not need to be given. Explain that infant was given antibiotics through the IV for the urinary tract infection. Explain importance of stating why the infant was admitted to the hospital.

o Common medical jargon is often used by medical students in this section: febrile, cath urine specimen, urinalysis, leukocyte esterase, nitrite, WBC, CBC, hemoglobin, platelets, IV, ceftriaxone.

If students use this jargon, ask the group whether or not they felt it was necessary to use the term for the presentation. If they feel it is necessary, then discuss more FCR appropriate ways to explain them.

E.g. Urinalysis with leukocyte esterase, nitrites and WBCs can simply be stated as “the urine sample obtained showed signs that the infant had an infection in the urine.”

ROS

o Many students will present a list of symptoms for every system or will present the systems that they did not talk about in the HPI section of the presentation.

Explain to students that a full ROS does not need to be presented, though it is important to document on the H&P. All pertinent positive and negatives should have already been mentioned in the HPI.

Remainder of History

o Many students will include information from every aspect of the remaining parts of the history (e.g. Birth History, Past Medical History, Past Surgical History, etc).

TIP: Ask students what aspect of the history is important in a 7 month old male with a fever and potential UTI. Typical answers discussed: abnormal fetal ultrasounds, circumcision (as patient is male), family history of genitourinary abnormalities, immunization status, sick contacts.

Emphasize to students that many times the pertinent aspects of these other history components may be already noted in the HPI portion of the presentation and therefore do not need to be repeated.

Physical Examination (PE)

o Many students will provide a complete PE.

TIP: Ask students what aspects of the PE are pertinent to present in this setting. Typical answers discussed: overall clinical appearance (well-appearing vs illappearing vs toxic), evidence of perfusion to evaluate for shock/sepsis, findings that may suggest focal infection, etc.   
Emphasize to students that it is important to solicit from attendings and senior residents preferences for providing a focused examination with only pertinent positives and negatives, as some educators will prefer hearing a complete examination from a medical student.

Labs/Imaging

o Many students will give all the labs without avoiding medical jargon

TIP: Discuss with students if labs were necessary to obtain. Highlight labs that have been discussed in HPI already. Many will discuss that the most pertinent result would be the gram stain from the urine culture as that was not discussed earlier. Discuss how this can be said in a FCR-appropriate way.

Assessment

o Many students will include some aspects of the plan in the assessment (e.g. discuss need for ultrasound).   
o Discuss with students that it is important to have a definite statement that these symptoms are from a UTI. Discuss it is acceptable to include a differential diagnosis

during the initial H&P presentation but that this differential diagnosis should be succinct and to the point.

Plan o Many students will provide very specific details about medication dosage and frequency. Discuss the need to state that infant is on appropriate antibiotic coverage and that antibiotics are currently intravenous. o Many students will provide a plan for every problem. Discuss that even though there needs to be a written plan for each of the problems, only active problems need to be discussed during the presentation (e.g. fever).

Disposition

o Many students will either not state the disposition at all or will read this directly from written documentation. Discuss importance of highlighting specific medicallyappropriate discharge goals to set expectations for the family, including an anticipated timeline (e.g. after one dose of oral antibiotic and when drinking well, which we hope will occur tomorrow).
