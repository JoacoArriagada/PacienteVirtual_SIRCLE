# Facilitator Guide for Workshop Videos

# Group set-up:

Large group discussion with 1-2 facilitator(s)   
Ensure a PowerPoint capable computer and AV equipment (video, audio) are available and powered on.

# Timeline:

Allow 10-15 minutes for discussion of videos.   
Use the first 2-3 minutes to provide the case background as outlined in the “Medical Student Presentation Workshop” as well as to state expectations for discussion.   
Show History of Present Illness_weak video and then immediately show History of Present Illness_strong video. Facilitate discussion based on the talking points below.   
Show Assessment and Plan_weak video and then immediately show Assessment and   
Plan_strong video. Facilitate discussion based on the talking points below.   
OPTIONAL: If time permits, you may show each of the four video examples separately and have discussions following each of them.   
Finish presenting the “Medical Student Presentation Workshop” as a summary of the videos jus shown.   
Allow 2-3 minutes at the end for any final questions prior to small group practice.

# Expectations and Outline for Video Discussion:

Explain to students that there are two sets of videos. The first set will review the History of Present Illness (HPI) portion of the admission History and Physical (H&P). The second set will review the assessment and plan portion of the admission H&P.

o The other components of the presentation are left out due to time constraints.

Explain that not all aspects of the remainder of the history need to be presented   
– the only aspects to be presented should be pertinent positives and negatives   
that relate to the chief complaint. For example, this is an opportune time to   
explain that the Review of Systems (ROS) does not need to be verbally stated, as   
all pertinent positive and negatives should have already been addressed in the   
HPI.   
Similarly, explain that only pertinent positives and negatives for the physical   
exam need to be stated as well.  Please note: At our institution, the expectation is that only pertinent aspects of the physical exam need to be stated. If at your institution the expectation is for a complete physical examination for the presentation, please explain appropriately.

 OPTIONAL: If time permits, facilitators may use a few minutes to review what aspects of the physical exam would be considered pertinent and why.

Explain to students that although one of the videos will demonstrate a stronger FCR presentation compared to the other video, there are still aspects needing improvement in the “strong” videos and aspects of the “weak” videos that are to be highlighted as done well. Explain team set up for videos. Presenter is the intern or medical student. Patient’s father is sitting in the chair. Senior resident is between the presenter and patient’s father. The rest of the team is in the room around the patient’s bed.

o Please note: At our institution, the interns usually have a computer on wheels during rounds so this video was set up to mimic that situation. The medical students will present from paper notes. Both of these presentation adjuncts will add potential complexities to the presentation (especially from the standpoint of organization and interaction with family). If the learners at your institution use similar adjuncts to the presentation, it will be important to highlight these concepts.

Stress that no presentation will ever be perfect so there is always room for growth and feedback.

Facilitator Discussion Points for Each Video:

Please note: The below discussion points are examples of the elements of the presentation that are typically highlighted for each video. There may be more aspects that you want to highlight as well. As noted above, the different videos are compared to one another. However, facilitators may opt to talk about each video separately. Therefore, the discussion points below are listed for each video so they can be adapted to either type of conversation.

Talking points for History of Present Illness_weak

o Appropriate aspects

Some members of the group may discuss that this version of the HPI presentation is at times more concise despite the inappropriate aspects noted below.

For example, the presenter discusses that the patient had a fever at home that was not responsive to medications without given all the details of the fever.  As noted below, many will also comment that there was superfluous information included in this presentation as well.

o Inappropriate aspects

Presenter never addresses the father or welcomes him to presentation.   
 Presenter always looks at computer/papers and never makes eye contact with father. Opening statement does not describe the presenting symptoms and goes directly to the diagnosis.

Presenter gives extensive ROS in the HPI portion of the presentation. Discussion can focus on what aspects of the ROS should be considered pertinent positives or negatives and therefore explained.

Extensive use of medical jargon.

Examples: Pneumonia, tachypnea, sputum, anti-pyretics, PO intake, hypoxic, right lower lobe infiltrate, elevated white blood cell count, fluid bolus

 Does not explain reason for admission to hospital.

Talking points for History of Present Illness_strong

o Appropriate aspects

Presenter addressed and welcomed the father at the start of the presentation and instructed the father to add to the presentation if details needed clarification.   
 Presenter maintained good eye contact with the father throughout the presentation.   
 The presenter paused intermittently to ensure the father agreed with what was being reported about the patient’s presenting problem.

o Inappropriate aspects

 Use of medical jargon, especially during discussion of the emergency room visit.

Examples: oxygen saturations, nasal cannula, crackles, infiltrate, complete blood count (CBC), white blood cells, segs, IV fluids You may opt to have a discussion regarding whether or not it is important to discuss the CBC and the specifics for this presentation. If group decides it is important to discuss the CBC, continue discussion on how to present the CBC in simplified FCR terms.

Talking points for Assessment and Plan_weak

o Appropriate aspects

Some members of the group may discuss that this version of the assessment and plan is simple and potentially easier to follow at times (e.g. fewer specifics about the type of antibiotics). However, the focus of the conversation will be on the issues noted below.

o Inappropriate aspects

Unorganized assessment and plan: Information was basically combined into one statement without a definitive assessment statement or updated status for the patient.

Plan described was very basic and did not give specifics as to when aspects of the plan would occur.

Example: The presenter stated that the patient needed to be transitioned to oral antibiotics before discharge but never indicated when this would occur.

The presenter gave hints to the discharge criteria for the patient but did not describe the patient’s current status for each of those elements.   
Use of medical jargon.   
 Examples: tachypnea and PO   
No eye contact with the father.   
Does not include the father in the discussion or assess his understanding of the plan.

Talking points for Assessment and Plan_strong

o Appropriate aspects

Gives assessment statement that describes presenting symptoms and most likely primary diagnosis. Also describes the secondary problem of dehydration. Gives status of patient both at the time of admission and how this has changed based on interventions since admission. Gives a plan for both the primary and secondary problems. Describes the goals for discharge home so that the father of the patient knows when to expect discharge. Ends the presentation by asking the father if he has any questions.  Discussion may also focus on how to ask the family if they understand the plan and to repeat the key aspects of the patient’s problem and plan.

o Inappropriate aspects

Does not give differential diagnosis in assessment. Discussion on this topic should include if the differential is needed given the known diagnosis. Also discuss that the differential diagnosis does not need to be presented in subsequent daily presentations when there is a known diagnosis as well.

 Some use of medical jargon.

Examples: Pneumonia (explain that many families may not know what pneumonia really is), IV
