Title Acute Respiratory Failure in an Infant
Target Audience Residents, Nurses, Paramedics, Medical Students

Learning Objectives

A. Primary

1. Participants will recognize respiratory distress and failure in a pediatric patient 2. Participants will initiate treatment and stabilization of a pediatric patient in respiratory failure 3. Participants will be able to list causes of respiratory decompensation in a pediatric patient, before and after intubation B. Secondary

1. Participants will demonstrate the proper use of a bag-valve mask

2. Participants will be able to establish intraosseous access

3. Participants will be able to intubate the patient

4. Participants will be able to perform needle decompression of a tension pneumothorax

5. Participant acting as team leader will demonstrate leadership skills necessary to care for the patient effectively, such as verbalizing goals of treatment, facilitating input from other members, clarifying roles of team

6. Participant acting as team members will demonstrate closed loop communication techniques to assist team while performing role C. Critical Actions Checklist 1. See attached standard event evaluation with facilitator guide

Environment

A. Set Up – adjust based on participants   
B. Mannikin Set Up – high fidelity infant simulator, no moulage needed, no priming of IV arm tubing as successful IV placement will not be possible   
C. Props a. Bag Valve mask b. Oxygen mask c. Nebulizer treatments d. IV catheters (for unsuccessful attempts) and supplies e. IO needle and supplies f. Intubation supplies

g. Access to resuscitation cart or transport bag pending on participants

D. Distractors – None

Actors

A. Roles – at a minimum one health care provider and an assistant. They serve as team leader and airway assistant in scenario. A second assistant for access could be added as well as a nurse role depending on how scenario is being utilized.   
B. Roles may be played by paramedics, nurses, residents, medical students depending on educational objectives

Case Narrative

A. Scenario Background

 4 month old male who has had 2 days of congestion and rhinorrhea and 1 day of increased work of breathing, prompting mother to bring infant in to hospital. This information is offered at the scenario start.

. If asked:

o Child is a full term infant, normal spontaneous vaginal delivery   
o No other medical problems   
o Never hospitalized   
o No surgeries   
o Child has been growing and developing normally   
o Child has had tactile fever for the past 2 days   
o Child has been drinking but less than usual and noted to be having fewer wet diapers today   
o Child was noted to be less active today   
o Child has no siblings but there is a sick contact in the home.

B. Scenario Conditions

Initial presentation: vital signs – pulse 168, respirations 72, blood pressure 83/60 pulse oximetry 94% in room air. On physical exam infant has retractions, grunting and wheezing. If asked: o Capillary refill is about 3 seconds o Infant is just lying there not active o All other physical findings are normal.

C. Scenario Branch Points, Progression:

Infant begins to desaturate by 20 points over 1 minute unless intervention is made with bag mask ventilation, causing saturation to rapidly improve in 15 seconds. Oxygen alone does not help. In approximately a minute regardless of prior interventions infant becomes apneic. If bag mask ventilation has not yet been started the heart rate begins to drop by 75 points over 1 minute Expectations at this point before further progression of scenario are:

o Participants will recognize apnea and prepare to intubate.   
o Participants will consider whether or not rapid sequence medications are needed and verbalize what medications might be used (choice may vary by institution, Fentanyl, Midazolam, with consideration of Atropine and a paralytic like Vecuronium would be our suggested choice).   
o Participants will evaluate whether or not the infant has IV access. There is no IV in place and no success at attempts at IV placement. Participants will need to recognize the need for intraosseous access and establish such access.

After intubation:

o Vital signs: pulse 140, respirations with bagging, blood pressure 80/50, saturation 98%.   
o Breath sounds are equal bilaterally, when assessed, no change in remainder of exam.   
o Infant becomes more difficult to bag mask ventilate (lung resistance increased on simulator).

After 45 seconds:

o Infant develops left sided pneumothorax (programmed in simulator). Pulse drops over 20 seconds reaching 65, saturation decreases over 20 seconds reaching 70, blood pressure decreases over 20 seconds to 55/30.

Expectation of participant is that:

o Differential causes of decompensation in an intubated patient will be verbalized (tube malposition, tube obstruction, equipment failure and pneumothorax)   
o Participants will perform needle decompression. If needle decompression successfully performed, pulse, oxygen saturation and blood pressure will recover over 15 seconds to a pulse of 138, saturation of 100%, blood pressure of 82/53. The infant lung resistance returns to normal with the infant being easy to bag ventilate with equal chest rise and the scenario should end.

Instructor Notes

A. Tips for scenario flow a. Scenario programming tips are outlined in the scenario progression b. Facilitator needs to anticipate the possibility that participants do not recognize that the infant has become apneic. Suggest asking participants to stop bag mask ventilation for a minute and re-evaluate the infant’s respiratory status. This should be discussed in the debrief c. Facilitator needs to anticipate the possibility that the pneumothorax is unrecognized. Prompting participants to name the causes of decompensation in an intubated patient or to look carefully at the chest rise – i.e. is it symmetric, may be necessary steps to move the scenario forward.

Debriefing Plan

A. Method will vary based on participants. At times we only have one paramedic participating when this scenario is used for annual competencies with a helper assigned, other times it is a group of three participants together. We debrief all participants together and use video only if helpful to show a specific behavior as an adjunct.

B. Key debriefing points are:

a. The recognition of severe respiratory distress in a pediatric patient b. The recognition of apnea in a pediatric patient, with emphasis on the importance of reassessing the patient, not just relying on the monitor c. The advantages and disadvantages of using rapid sequence medications in this scenario d. The recognition of an acute change in respiratory status after intubation and the differential diagnosis, emphasizing again the importance of continual reassessment e. Skills review if there were technical challenges with the intubation, bag mask ventilation, intraosseous placement, or needle decompression. These skills should be reviewed at the end of the scenario, not during the scenario. f. Communication among team members if there are multiple participants in a hospital setting (i.e. closed loop communication, assigning roles) g. If the event is in the pre-hospital setting, the importance of potentially stopping the ambulance and notifying medical command should also be addressed   
C. Rules of the debriefing a. Standard rules – events that occur in a simulation session are not discussed outside of the simulation session with non participants. It is a safe, non judgmental environment with learning as the focus.   
D. Questions to facilitate debriefing a. Refer to initial learning objectives as those points should be discussed in debrief as well as the key debriefing points listed above.

Pilot Testing and Revisions

Our initial usage was with just the transport team and one actor as an emergency medical technician assistant. It worked well to evaluate essential skills and equipment from the ambulance. If using for competency evaluation it is helpful for two observers to be present to verify what may occur

Scenario is now used with other trainees for educational purposes, not competency with good success, just adjustments of location of case based on participants (i.e. ambulance vs emergency department
