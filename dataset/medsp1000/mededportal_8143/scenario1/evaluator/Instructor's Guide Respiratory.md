# Instructor Guide

# Directions for Use and Purpose:

1. Scenario Flow Sheet

a. This resource should be utilized first. This resource contains the learning objectives, the flow of the case to allow the simulator to be programmed and the instructions to the facilitator with respect to expectations of performance during the scenario and debriefing.   
b. The educational objectives outlined in the flowsheet are: i. To recognize respiratory distress and failure in a pediatric patient ii. To initiate treatment and stabilization of a pediatric patient in respiratory failure iii. To list causes of deterioration in a pediatric patient before and after intubation iv. To demonstrate medical knowledge, communication, and patient care skills in a medical emergency

2. Event Evaluation

a. This resource is used while conducting the scenario. It provides a standardized means of evaluating participants in the scenario. It may be used to evaluate participants by their roles or the event as a whole. If the scenario is being utilized to evaluate annual competencies for prehospital providers it provides a standardized record of performance and would be used just for that individual.

# Background:

This case was initially designed for our transport team. Our simulation education team met with the leadership for the transport team to explore what skills were important to know and what type of scenario they might encounter. We developed clear learning objectives for the individual team members and also for the team as a whole, for example utilizing their own equipment to make sure the department was checking it properly. We then developed a scenario that achieved these objectives. It served as a way to standardize their annual competency evaluation and make sure that they could demonstrate the skills that they are expected to be proficient in a realistic way. Both the psychomotor aspects of performing the skill and the critical thinking skills to recognize the need to intervene were effectively evaluated. Events were evaluated in a consistent way so that there is documentation of performance available and areas for educational improvement for that department were identified. Its usage expanded with minimal modifications to other learners.

# Practical Implementation Advice:

1. Materials – a. One infant human patient simulator. We utilized Laerdaal SimBaby but the scenario should be applicable to other simulators. b. Emergency supplies – we utilize the actual transport bag our team utilizes or the code cart. Supplies that will be utilized are an oxygen mask, a bag valve mask or flow inflating bag, a laryngoscope and Miller 1 blade, a 4.0 endotracheal tube with stylet, tape, suction, an end tidal detector, an intraosseous needle, intravenous fluids, and an intravenous catheter to perform needle decompression.

2. Length of session – 10 minutes for scenario, 15 for debriefing

3. Faculty/facilitator needs – If being done simply as a teaching exercise with 1- 3 individuals only one facilitator is needed. If being done for annual competency evaluation then at a minimum two facilitators is helpful. If only one individual is participating a confederate (for example the ambulance driver) may be needed to assist with bag mask ventilation, etc.

4. Preparation – No special preparation is required of the simulator. Any bed space can be used.
