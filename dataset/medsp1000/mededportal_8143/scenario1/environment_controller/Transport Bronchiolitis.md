Environment

B. Mannikin Set Up – high fidelity infant simulator, no moulage needed, no priming of IV arm tubing as successful IV placement will not be possible   
C. Props a. Bag Valve mask b. Oxygen mask c. Nebulizer treatments d. IV catheters (for unsuccessful attempts) and supplies e. IO needle and supplies f. Intubation supplies

D. Distractors – None

Actors

A. Roles – at a minimum one health care provider and an assistant. They serve as team leader and airway assistant in scenario.

Case Narrative

A. Scenario Background

4 month old male who has had 2 days of congestion and rhinorrhea and 1 day of increased work of breathing, prompting mother to bring infant in to hospital. This information is offered at the scenario start.

If asked:

- Child is a full term infant, normal spontaneous vaginal delivery
- No other medical problems
- Never hospitalized
- No surgeries
- Child has been growing and developing normally
- Child has had tactile fever for the past 2 days
- Child has been drinking but less than usual and noted to be having fewer wet diapers today
- Child was noted to be less active today
- Child has no siblings but there is a sick contact in the home.

B. Scenario Conditions

Initial presentation: vital signs – pulse 168, respirations 72, blood pressure 83/60 pulse oximetry 94% in room air. On physical exam infant has retractions, grunting and wheezing.

If asked:

- Capillary refill is about 3 seconds
- Infant is just lying there not active
- All other physical findings are normal.

C. Scenario Branch Points, Progression:

Infant begins to desaturate by 20 points over 1 minute unless intervention is made with bag mask ventilation, causing saturation to rapidly improve in 15 seconds. Oxygen alone does not help. In approximately a minute regardless of prior interventions infant becomes apneic. If bag mask ventilation has not yet been started the heart rate begins to drop by 75 points over 1 minute.

There is no IV in place and no success at attempts at IV placement.

After intubation:

- Vital signs: pulse 140, respirations with bagging, blood pressure 80/50, saturation 98%.
- Breath sounds are equal bilaterally, when assessed, no change in remainder of exam.
- Infant becomes more difficult to bag mask ventilate.

After 45 seconds:

- Infant develops left sided pneumothorax. Pulse drops over 20 seconds reaching 65, saturation decreases over 20 seconds reaching 70, blood pressure decreases over 20 seconds to 55/30.
- If needle decompression successfully performed, pulse, oxygen saturation and blood pressure will recover over 15 seconds to a pulse of 138, saturation of 100%, blood pressure of 82/53.
- The infant lung resistance returns to normal with the infant being easy to bag ventilate with equal chest rise and the scenario should end.

Instructor Notes

A. Tips for scenario flow a. Scenario programming tips are outlined in the scenario progression b. Facilitator needs to anticipate the possibility that participants do not recognize that the infant has become apneic. Suggest asking participants to stop bag mask ventilation for a minute and re-evaluate the infant’s respiratory status. c. Facilitator needs to anticipate the possibility that the pneumothorax is unrecognized. Prompting participants to look carefully at the chest rise, i.e. is it symmetric, may be necessary steps to move the scenario forward.
