**Explanation of Resources**

The simulation should first begin by the operators and facilitators opening the simulation outline and familiarizing themselves with the beginning scenario as well as the entire case. The participants will then begin with a general explanation of the case scenario by reading the text located in italics on page one of the outline. The goal of the outline is to allow for an easily observable flowchart showing the operators how the case should be run. Participants should then work their way through the simulation as shown in the outline with lab values and chest x-ray being shown to them when asked for. The goals of the labs are to indicate decompensation of an acute asthmatic, with an increased pCO2. The goal of the chest x-ray is to indicate that there are no other causes of the patient's symptoms, i.e. pneumothorax or pneumonia. Following the scenario the PowerPoint will be used to review the current management for acute asthmatics as well as the correct ventilator settings for such patients. It will be used to fill in any knowledge gaps that were apparent in the case scenario.

**Background of Creating this Case**

This case was created to discuss an ever more recurring event in today's emergency rooms, acute asthma exacerbations. Although management of acute exacerbations may be well known to many, ventilator settings are extremely unique in these patients and if not correct can cause considerable harm. This simulation will help solidify these settings and ensure a safe post-intubation environment for the patient. The scenario and simulation was created by using personal experience with a large asthma population, expert opinion, and the latest research in this area of medicine.

**Practical Implementation**

This case is designed to be run for a wide variety of medical professionals, including medical students, through resident level and faculty trained physicians. Ideally, the simulation requires a simulation room and mannequin, one actor as a nurse and one or more actors acting as a parent(s) as well as one facilitator running the simulation. It is understood that there is a wide range of simulation environments, however, actual monitors, intubating equipment, and x-ray capabilities are ideal in order to make the experience as realistic as possible. The simulation can be run however on as little as two actors, a facilitator and imaginative learners. The simulation should last approximately 15-30 minutes depending on the skill levels involved. Preparation should include a run through by the facilitator and actors prior to the actual simulation experience by learners.

**Limitations**

There is obviously no perfect simulation and nothing can take the place of the real patient scenario. This simulation however does try and have the physician more prepared and comfortable for these events when they do occur. The reality of the scenario can be a severe limitation and it is encouraged that every possible measure be taken to make the experience as 'life-like' as possible. The scenario could also be expanded to include further complications of acute asthma, i.e. pneumothorax, and include chest tube placement as well as its implications. At this time, however, I chose to focus on ventilator management and its possible complications.
