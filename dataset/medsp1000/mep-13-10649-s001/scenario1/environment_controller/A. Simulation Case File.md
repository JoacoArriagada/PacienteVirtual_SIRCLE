| **Appendix A: MedEdPORTAL Simulation Case**  **SIMULATION CASE TITLE: Alcohol Withdrawal and Lithium Toxicity**  **AUTHORS:** **Ish P. Bhalla MD, Kirsten M. Wilkins MD, Tiffany Moadel MD, Ambrose H. Wong MD MSHPEd, Louis A. Trevisan MD, Brian Fuehrlein MD PhD** | |
| **PATIENT NAME: Steven Crane**  **PATIENT AGE: 40 years old**  **CHIEF COMPLAINT: “I don’t have any injuries and feel fine. The paramedics brought me here as a precaution.”** | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case**  *Include the presenting patient chief complaint and overall learner goals for this case* | Steven Crane is a 40-year-old male truck driver with a history of bipolar disorder and has been on the same dose of lithium for years. He presents to the emergency room as a precaution after an accident, and his breathalyzer on arrival was 0.120. He initially has signs and symptoms of alcohol withdrawal, and symptoms remit with the administration of benzodiazepines. However, he has other unexplained symptoms including polydipsia, polyuria, and dizziness that does not improve. Laboratory results reveal lithium toxicity. |
| **Learner Preparation**  *What information should the learners be given prior to initiation of the case?* | A triage nursing note is given to the participants before the case begins: “The patient is a truck driver, and was driving his 18-wheeler when he lost control of the vehicle, but fortunately did not hit another car. The paramedics brought him to the ED as a precaution. A trauma workup has already been completed. The patient’s breathalyzer on arrival was 0.120.” |
