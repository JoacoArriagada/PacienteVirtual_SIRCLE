**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

*The learners receive information from the triage nurse that the patient was brought to the emergency room as a precaution following a truck accident, and sustained no trauma. His breathalyzer was 0.120. Participants enter the room to find a patient lying in an emergency room bed connected to telemetry monitoring. They begin a discussion with the patient to gather a history regarding what happened and quickly notice that the heart rate and blood pressure are elevated. The patient also reports anxiety and a tremor. They ask the patient about alcohol use and discover that he has been drinking daily for the past several months and is likely in acute alcohol withdrawal. He has no history of medically complicated withdrawal. The team orders benzodiazepines and the patient responds with an improved heart rate and blood pressure. However, he continues to feel tremulous, tired, and thirsty. The patient complains of right-sided rib pain and tells a story of falling off a ladder 2 weeks ago and then being prescribed naproxen, which he has been taking. Since then, he has also been noticing increased thirst and urination as well as a tremor and general malaise. The team orders labs and discovers that the patient has lithium toxicity, acute kidney injury, and elevated TSH level. The team obtains collateral information from the patient’s wife with his permission, and discovers that he has been minimizing his alcohol use. The patient asks if the team thinks he has a problem with alcohol, and the team administers a brief intervention to address his alcohol use. The case ends, and the faculty facilitator playing the part of the patient then enters the room and begins a debriefing session.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. *Uncertainty deciding about an Initial dose of benzodiazepines for alcohol withdrawal: During the case, we found that students were able to recognize that the patient is in alcohol withdrawal and make the decision to start a benzodiazepine. However, students often are unsure about the starting dose, especially if they have not elicited a thorough history to risk stratify the patient. The in-room faculty facilitator often pauses the case to provide guidance on an approach to determining an initial dose.*
2. *Failure to follow up with unfamiliar jargon from patients: The patient states in the case that he drinks on average of “a fifth” of vodka every night. We have found that students generally do not know what this means and do not ask the patient to explain in other words what this means. We included a teaching point during the debriefing session to not be afraid to ask a patient to clarify jargon that he or she may use, which is particularly common among patients who abuse substances.*
3. *Ordering labs without considering a broad differential: Participants often jump to a diagnosis of alcohol withdrawal and forget to probe further to consider other comorbid problems. When this happens, they order labs prematurely and fail to also order lithium levels. The in-room facilitator pauses the case and asks participants to come up with a differential and pushes them to probe for other comorbidities before ordering labs all at once.*
4. *Failure to understand the lab abnormality significance: Students occasionally fail to recognize laboratory abnormalities or the significance of the abnormalities. The in-room facilitator guides the students to identify the abnormalities and discuss the ramifications of the abnormalities.*
5. *Administering inappropriate medications: Students occasionally discuss administering medications that may not be indicated. For example, attempting to treat the tachycardia and hypertension with antihypertensive medications is not indicated as the students should recognize that this is from alcohol withdrawal. The in-room facilitator discusses this with the students.*
