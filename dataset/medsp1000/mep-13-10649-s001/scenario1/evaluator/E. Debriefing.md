Appendix E: Debriefing Session

After the case ends, the patient-voice facilitator joins the in-room facilitator and students in the simulation room. Facilitators begin with an open-ended inquiry of the students’ perspectives of the simulation experience in general. Common themes that have emerged include the benefits and challenges of working in a group setting and the ability to treat the mannequin as a real patient. Facilitators then provide both reinforcing and constructive feedback on the students’ teamwork, interview, and clinical reasoning, as well as highlight key teaching points including:

* Management of alcohol withdrawal
* Alcohol use disorders including a review of general information on alcohol such as units of measure for alcohol, definitions of a standard drink, interpretation of breathalyzer results, and alcohol metabolism
* Lithium toxicity and complications of lithium use including nephrogenic diabetes insipidus
* Counseling of alcohol cessation in the emergency setting, bringing to the patient’s attention the clear vocational and potentially legal consequences of his drinking
* Discuss long term strategies for sobriety including pharmacologic (e.g. naltrexone, acamprosate, and disulfiram) and therapeutic interventions (e.g. Alcoholics Anonymous)
* Legal/ethical considerations

Additionally, facilitators highlight the importance of using deductive reasoning to consider alternative or additional diagnoses and to avoid premature closure after discovering an initial obvious diagnosis (alcohol withdrawal) 13. Facilitators engage students in a discussion of the pharmacokinetics of lithium and naproxen, and review the importance of obtaining a thorough history before prescribing even a seemingly benign medication such as naproxen. Students discuss the renal complications that resulted from the additive effect of afferent constriction of glomerular arterioles. The case highlights that patients with mental health problems often present with concurrent medical illnesses or complaints.

Although it does not come up in the case, the facilitators challenge students during the debriefing to consider whether they would report the patient to his licensing agency. The ethical principles of confidentiality versus protecting public safety (i.e., the patient’s driving while under the influence can lead to traffic accidents) are discussed. Students are educated about the common nature of substance use disorders among licensed medical professionals and that licensing agencies generally assist in obtaining treatment rather than punish.
