# Pediatric Scenario – Apnea due to Intoxication

Scenario: 6 year old is brought to the emergency department by an ambulance having been found unresponsive at his grandmother’s house. He is stable en route.

Further history if asked: No history of trauma. Grandmother is his normal after-school babysitter. (If subject asks specifically about drugs in home, respond with “An uncle in the home is newly in a methadone treatment program.”)

Past medical history is unremarkable.   
Meds: None   
No allergies.   
Family and social history: as above, otherwise non-contributory Development: normal

# Environment

1. ED setting – bed, monitor, crash cart, IV pole.   
2. Child on bed without any monitors on, “patient just placed in room”.   
3. ECG available: sinus bradycardia with a rate of 80. No specific lab sheets are needed as labs would all be normal. Urine drug screen is negative.   
4. Distractors – none.

# Actors

1. Roles – nurse required   
2. Nurse provides support – no specific prompts and is a “new nurse” so not very helpful. When asked for bag-mask, for example, he/she hand the subject all the bag-masks in the drawer rather than selecting the correct one.   
3. Parent (optional) – to provide history – otherwise nurse provides history and states grandmother is unavailable. Grandmother is still at home.

Say the following: “Child is comatose and has slight perioral cyanosis – no response to deep, painful stimulus.”

Physical Exam: Offer the general appearance and only offer additional clarification if asked.

T 37.5 P 80 RR 8 BP per monitor $S _ { \mathrm { a } } O _ { 2 }$ : $9 2 \%$ on room air

Wt: estimated $2 5 \mathrm { k g }$ (only if asked)

Airway: Patent Breathing: Shallow, hypopneic respirations. Circulation: normal, slightly delayed capillary refill Pinpoint pupils noted (eyes closed)

# Instructors Notes

Case evolution is limited – Child starts with vitals given above.

If naloxone is given at ${ > } 1 0 ^ { }$ -fold normal dose, patient should have a transient improvement and then return to apnea after ${ \sim } 3 { - } 4$ minutes. Alternately, naloxone may simply not work.
