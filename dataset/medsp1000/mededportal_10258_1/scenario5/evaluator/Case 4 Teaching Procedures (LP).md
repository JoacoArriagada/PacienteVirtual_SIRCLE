Your name is John/Jennifer Kimball.Your name is John/Jennifer Kimball. You are an intern on the Tisch wards. You were called to the ER to admit a patient. Your resident is busy in clinic.

Mr. Thomas is an 80-year-old man who was transferred from a nursing home. He has a history of dementia. He is nonverbal and unable to care for himself. According to the nursing home records he developed a fever two days ago and has become less responsive. He has a history of hypertension. He does not have a bleeding disorder. He has no history of head trauma.

In the ER he was found to have a temp of 101.4 BP 146/82 HR 88 RR 18. His lungs were clear, heart regular, no murmur, he has no rashes, and he did not have a stiff neck or papilledema. His labs were significant for WBC 13 with a slight left shift. BUN/Cr 32/1.1 (baseline of 24/0.9). His UA was neg for infection. His chest x-ray was clear.

Head CT was negative for bleed or mass effect. You don’t know why the ER did the head CT. You think they wanted to rule out a bleed.

Due to his deteriorated mental status, his fever, and the lack of an infection source, the decision was made to do an LP.

You have seen two LPs done since you started your residency. One was performed by your resident in July, and the other one by a neurology resident. You feel like you know the basic steps but do not feel comfortable performing an LP. You are eager to learn.

You ask your attending to assist/teach you since your resident is busy in clinic.

You would like your attending to help you perform an LP.

You know how to set up the procedure and a sterile field.   
You can find landmarks.   
You do not feel comfortable inserting the needle or obtaining an opening pressure.

# Personality

You are slowly beginning to feel more confident as an intern. You feel you do well coming up with differential diagnoses but you get nervous learning hands-on skills. Many of your peers have done LPs; you feel this is something you should know how to do, and you are eager to learn.

# Teaching Challenge

Assess learner’s knowledge and comfort performing procedure.   
Review procedure prior to starting.   
Guide learner though performing an LP.   
Demonstrate sensitivity toward patient. Attends to patient comfort.   
Assess learning.

# Timing

# Beginning

Greet attending and introduce him/her to the patient. Say the patient is nonverbal. Let the attending take the lead. You will have all materials set up by the patient. The patient will be prepped and draped. Tell the attending you already gave him Lidocaine (5 min ago).

The attending may want to know what you did. If so, say:

Positioned patient   
Identified landmarks and marked where you thought the   
needle should go – between L3/L4 or L4L5   
Set up LP tray   
Sterilized with betadine   
Draped patient and set up sterile field   
Anesthetized with Lidocaine

If asked, you have questions about the procedure: Say “Yes I have a few questions”

• How do you angle the needle when you insert it? • How do you know when you are in the right place? How do you obtain an opening pressure?

# If asked, you are not sure what goes in the tubes. Say:

• I’m not sure if it is cell count first, or culture. I think the other tubes are cytology, chemistry (protein), TB, but I’m not sure.

# Middle

# When you perform the procedure:

Let the patient know what you are doing.   
Identify landmarks   
Attempt the LP: Go in smoothly, do not jiggle the needle, and do not make small adjustments from side to side.   
If you don’t get fluid back immediately, ask the attending if s/he wants to take over.   
If asked to pull back, do so very slowly, about $2 { \cdot } 3 ~ \mathrm { m m }$ at a time.   
Pull the stylus out fully to check for fluid.   
Make two attempts.   
If you get fluid back start, colleting it in the tubes.   
If you have made two good attempts and still have no fluid, the attending may improvise and say you have fluid, so just go with it.   
Ask for help obtaining an opening pressure. Have the   
attending guide you.   
Collect fluid in one tube.   
Remove the needle and clean the field.

If the attending takes over the procedure: Observe what s/he does, listen to what s/he says, and if given the opportunity, ask questions:

• How did you know where to direct the needle? • How did you know when to remove the stylus? • Can you show me how to obtain an opening pressure?

# End

After finishing the procedure, wait to hear what the attending has to say.

If you feel s/he did a good job showing you how to do the LP so you now feel more confident, thank her/him and say how helpful this was.

If you feel s/he was too rushed or was confusing, simply ask her/him if there is anything else to do and say you will make sure the specimens get to the lab.
