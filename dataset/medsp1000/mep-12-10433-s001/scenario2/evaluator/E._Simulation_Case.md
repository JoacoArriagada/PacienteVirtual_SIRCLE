| **Appendix E: Ebola Preparedness Simulation Case**  **SIMULATION CASE TITLE: Supportive care for EVD patient with sepsis**  **AUTHORS: DA O’Keeffe, C Pozner, R Akineni.** | |
| **PATIENT NAME: Jane Grant**  **PATIENT AGE: 42**  **CHIEF COMPLAINT: Sepsis secondary to acute infection with Ebola Virus Disease** | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | This case is designed to teach and assess the performance of already known basic patient care clinical skills within the confines of the PPE. Participants will be expected to adhere to all procedures for infection control as they attach the patient to monitors, start IV fluids and other supportive measures, cleaning the soiled patient and disposing of the soiled materials in the correct way. The participants will be observed for their communication with the patient and their colleague in the room and their performance of these clinical tasks. Faculty may choose to add additional tasks to the scenario such as insertion of an IV line or managing a fluid spill on the ground. |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | 1. Perform clinical procedures correctly while donned in PPE, exhibiting appropriate management of physical and environmental challenges including dexterity, heat, stress and communication. 2. Demonstrate the enhanced collaboration necessary to safely care for a patient with EVD. 3. Demonstrate the safe management of body-fluid spills and hospital waste in the setting of managing a patient with EVD. |
| **Critical Actions** | * Place patient on monitor by attaching leads to chest * Place patient on supplementary oxygen via nasal prongs or facemask * Perform limited physical examination of patient * Prepare equipment and insert IV line * Commence IV fluid infusion using pump * Administer medications as required * Roll patient to clean soiling and dispose of soiled material appropriately * Communicate clearly and consistently with colleague and patient |
| **Learner Preparation** | Learners are given history and presenting complaint as outlined below. They are instructed to don their PPE prior to entering room and then to enter room to assess and care for patient as per their usual clinical practice. |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*The learners enter the room to find a patient alert but hypotensive. They immediately place the patient on bedside monitors and recognize that the patient is hypoxic, mildly tachycardic, and hypotensive. Supplemental oxygen is provided and the need for IV access and IV fluids is discussed. The patient’s oxygen saturation responds to the supplemental oxygen. IV access is obtained and the IV fluids are started as wide open. The blood pressure responds. More thorough physical examination may be carried out and the results will be told to the participants due to the limitations of the PPE and mannequin. The soiling is noticed under the patient and the two learners roll and clean the patient. They then dispose of the soiled material in the correct manner. During the whole scenario, the learners are communicating openly with each other and the patient despite the confines of the PPE.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. *Because of the PPE, learners will find it difficult to hear and be heard through the head gear and with the noise of the air filter. This may cause them to reduce communication, especially with the patient.*
2. *Full infection control protocol may not be adhered to, especially while disposing of the soiled materials.*
3. *Learners will find it difficult to perform some tasks because of the gloves and PPE and therefore may cut corners with things like sterility for IV line insertion.*
4. *The learners will be unable to auscultate breath sounds or heart sounds as they will be wearing PAPRs.*
