Ideal Scenario Flow

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

The learners enter the patient room to find the patient pacing anxiously. In a prolonged ramble, he states that he doesn’t know why he can’t just have his bupropion, that he doesn’t know why he is admitted, and that he can’t fall asleep in the noisy inpatient setting, especially with frequent neuro checks. He feels that there is no reason to have his psychiatric medications changed, especially since they were working well for him.

The learners should approach calmly and ask the patient to describe his concerns, employing open body language with minimal interruptions. Successful use of de-escalation techniques will persuade the patient to stop pacing and listen to the learners.

The learners should explain that bupropion can lower seizure threshold and make the patient more prone to seizures, so this medication must be avoided for now. They can offer a repeat dose of his prn clonazepam, or they can offer reasonable sleep aids, such as melatonin, diphenhydramine, or zolpidem. They can also remind the patient that psychiatry will see him in the morning to re-establish his care. These interventions will persuade the patient to lie in bed and go to sleep.

Anticipated Management Mistakes

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. Learner may not recognize escalating behaviors
2. Learner may not recognize nonverbal cues
3. Learner may lack knowledge around de-escalation techniques
4. Learner may fail to listen to the patients’ needs
5. Learner may lack medical knowledge around insomnia and anxiety pharmacotherapy

Debriefing Guide

1. Reactions and reflections

Start with an open ended question: How did you feel about this case? What went well?

1. Learner summary of the scenario to clarify facts of the case
2. Learning points1
   1. Approach to the agitated patient
      1. Ensure safety of yourself and your colleagues: scene safety and exit strategy
      2. Self-monitor your emotions
   2. Body language
      1. Most emotional communication is through body language
      2. Open versus closed body language
         1. Knees slightly bent
         2. Face the patient at a slight angle
         3. Calm facial expression
         4. Avoid crossed arms or standing above the patient
   3. De-escalation domains
      1. Respect personal space
      2. Do not be provocative
      3. Establish verbal contact
      4. Be concise
      5. Identify wants and feelings
      6. Listen closely to what the patient is saying
      7. Agree to agree or disagree
      8. Lay down the law and set clear limits
      9. Offer choices and optimism
      10. Debrief the patient and staff
3. Pharmacotherapy for acute mild anxiety or insomnia that are relevant to the case 2,3
   1. Benzodiazepines
      1. Examples: lorazepam, clonazepam
      2. Desired effect: anxiolysis
      3. Potential side effects: drowsiness, sedation, hypotension, respiratory depression
   2. Antihistamines
      1. Examples: hydroxyzine, diphenhydramine
      2. Desired effects: anxiolysis, sleepiness
      3. Potential side effects: drowsiness, sedation (hypotension and respiratory depression at high doses)
   3. Sedative hypnotics
      1. Example: zolpidem
      2. Desired effects: alleviate insomnia
      3. Potential side effects: complex sleep behaviors, dizziness, drowsiness
