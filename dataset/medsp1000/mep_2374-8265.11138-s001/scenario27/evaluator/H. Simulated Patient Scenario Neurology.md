Ideal Scenario Flow

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

The learners arrive at the patient’s bedside and discover new neurologic deficits compared to the baseline described to them at handoff, including new altered mental status and left-sided hemiplegia. They verbalize their thought process to the nurse confidant or the facilitator operating the scenario throughout the case.

Learners take immediate steps to diagnose and stabilize the patient. Ideal initial interventions would include obtaining emergent head CT, recognizing hemorrhagic conversion of right MCA stroke, and controlling hypertension to goal systolic blood pressure of ~140 mm Hg.

Based on imaging and physical examination findings, learners should call for a neurosurgery consultation, and advocate for the patient to be transferred to ICU level of care. They should update their senior resident or attending as well.

Anticipated Management Mistakes

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. Learners are sometimes unable to prioritize interventions for a sick patient in clear distress. They may verbalize an appropriate degree of concern for hemorrhagic transformation, but forget basic interventions, such as placing the patient on monitor, obtaining repeat imaging, or calling for help from a senior resident or neurosurgical consultant.

1. Conversely, learners may not recognize the patient’s deterioration, due to failure to perform a comprehensive physical examination, or inadequate understanding of the patient’s baseline neurologic examination at time of simulated handoff.

1. Rarely, learners may downplay the patient’s condition and simply focus on pain management.

1. Rarely, learners may lack medical knowledge to diagnose hemorrhagic transformation.
2. Learners sometimes consult the neurosurgery service simply based on the patient’s neurologic examination, without a specific question or concern. The surgical service will give the learner a difficult time about the consult unless the learner specifically provides results of head CT imaging.

Debriefing Guide

1. Reactions and reflections

Start with an open ended question: How did you feel about this case? What went well?

1. Learner summary of the scenario to clarify facts of the case
2. Learning points
   1. Approach to initial resuscitation techniques
      1. Airway, Breathing, Circulation assessment
         1. Airway - assess if the patient can speak or airway obstructions
         2. Breathing- breath sounds, secondary indicators such as cyanosis
         3. Circulation - pulse assessment, skin color, capillary refill
      2. IV, O2, Monitor
         1. IV placement - is the IV access sufficient at this time?
         2. O2- does the patient require oxygen therapy at this time?
         3. Monitor - obtain the vital signs and monitoring required
      3. Identify abnormal neurologic findings
      4. Differential diagnosis
         1. Altered mental status ddx: hypoglycemia vs. intoxication vs. delirium (from other metabolic/infectious etiology) vs. CNS etiology
         2. Diagnosis of exclusion: post-stroke symptoms
         3. Abnormal neurologic findings point strongly towards CNS etiology
         4. Have students communicate with each other and justify diagnosis
      5. Identify central etiology of neurologic deficits
   2. Approach to suspected CNS etiology of altered mental status
      1. Head CT
      2. MRI (if brainstem/posterior fossa)
      3. EEG (if status epilepticus suspected)
      4. Discuss speediness of each study
   3. Interventions
      1. Head CT - demonstrating post-tPA hemorrhage
         1. occurs in 2-7% of patients
         2. risk factors: larger CVA/high NIHSS, older age, diabetes
      2. Blood pressure control
      3. consider cryoprecipitate administration in conjunction with consultants - reversal agent controversial
      4. Emergent neurosurgical consultation
      5. ICU readmission
