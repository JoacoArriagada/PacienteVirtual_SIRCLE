| **Brief narrative description of case** | A 68-year-old male suffers a pulmonary embolism one month after a total hip replacement (THR). The learner will assess the patient and start anticoagulation as well as identify the patient’s hypotension and hypoxia. The patient will become progressively unstable prompting the consideration of systemic thrombolysis. The team will have to collaborate with the nursing and orthopedic surgery teams to provide care. |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | By the end of the simulation activity, learners will be able to:   1. Have increased confidence collaborating effectively with an interprofessional team in the management of a massive pulmonary embolism. Includes shared decision making, delegation of tasks and conflict resolution. 2. Identify core concepts required for effective collaboration in a complex medical case. 3. Critically analyze collaboration performance. 4. Lead an interprofessional team in providing emergency care for a patient with a massive pulmonary embolism. |
| **Critical Actions** | Medical Expert Role:  Prior to patient decompensation   * Performs focused history and physical examination. Includes inquiring about bleeding risk factors and ensuring that there is no active bleeding from the surgical site. * Contact the orthopedic surgery team to review the case and discuss the risks and benefits of anticoagulation. * Recommends anticoagulation and provides instructions for dosing/ formulation.   Following patient decompensation:   * Calls for help at the bedside and attempts to contact the attending physician. * Assesses for indications and contraindications to systemic thrombolysis. * Recognize that an IVC filter is not indicated * Order systemic thrombolysis   *Examples of acceptable systemic thrombolysis orders –*   1. *t-PA Bolus: 0.6-1.0 mg/kg/5-15 min (max 50 mg),* 2. *t-PA Infusion: 100 mg/2 hrs,* 3. *t-PA may be given as a 50 mg bolus during CPR*   Following decision regarding systemic thrombolysis   * Recommends patient is transferred to the ICU * Provides recommendations for ongoing anticoagulant use   Collaborator Role:   * Maintains good eye contact with embedded participants * Reflects back embedded participant statements * Uses closed loop communication * Clarifies team goals and defines roles * Encourages input from embedded participants * Responds to non-verbal cues * Respectful and responsive to feedback from embedded participants * Elicits help in managing the case from multidisciplinary team (e.g. nurse, orthopedic surgery team, ICU team) |
| **Learner Preparation or Prework** | Learners should arrive to the scenario with at least a basic understanding of adverse effects associated with cancer treatment (e.g. chemotherapy, immunotherapy). At our center this was accomplished in part through dedicated lectures but could also be achieved by distributing pre-reading. It is expected that learners will have some hand on experience in supervising immunotherapy treatments although this would likely have been done in conjunction with an attending physician.  At arrival at the simulation lab, the learner should be given a 5-minute overview of the simulation room. This includes an overview of the monitors, phone, mannequin and embedded participants. In addition, the learner should be reminded that what occurs in the simulation lab is confidential and the performance of other learners should not be discussed without consent outside of dedicated debrief sessions.  The learner should be told that the simulation is intended to be a formative assessment focused on medical expert and collaboration skills. |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

The learner is asked to see a patient with a new pulmonary embolism following orthopedic surgery. The learner enters the room (surgery ward) and finds a patient complaining of pleuritic chest pain and shortness of breath. The learner immediately performs a focused history and physical with emphasis on bleeding risk factors. They should then proceed to contact the orthopedic surgery team (by phone) to discuss risks and benefits of anticoagulation. The learner uses conflict resolution skills to collaborate with the surgery team. After discussing the management plan, the learner starts the patient on anticoagulation and IV fluid. The patient does not respond and becomes progressively more hemodynamically unstable. The learner immediately calls for help and tries to contact their attending physician, beginning to review the indications/contraindications for thrombolytics. After collaboratively discussing the case and plan with the ICU team, they reach a consensus and administer systemic thrombolytics. The learner should then recommend that the patient is transferred to ICU, and provide recommendations for ongoing anticoagulant use.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. Failure to apply best principles in collaboration with an interprofessional team. The residents may fail to do one or more of the following; Introduce self to nursing team, engage in shared decision making, delegate responsibilities, define roles, use closed loop communication. The nurse embedded participants will demonstrate good collaboration between each other as an example to the learner. The learner will also be met with resistance if they do not incorporate the principles listed above. Good collaboration is also a major focus of the debrief.
2. Delayed elicitation of support in managing the patient. This includes discussions with the surgery team and contact ICU/ rapid response. The nurse embedded participants will prompt the learner in both situations and ultimately contact the collaborating services independently if not done by the learner.
3. Difficulty in balancing risks of bleeding and thrombosis in a complex patient. The embedded participants will prompt the learner to proceed with anticoagulation and provide direction on administration and dosing of systemic thrombolytics if required.
