| **Appendix A**  **SIMULATION CASE TITLE: Rituximab Infusion Reaction**  **AUTHORS: Martina Trinkaus, Zachary Liederman**  **LEARNER AUDIENCE: Hematology Residents (PGY4 and PGY5)** | |
| **PATIENT NAME: Mr. Smith**  **PATIENT AGE: 72**  **CHIEF COMPLAINT: Increased shortness of breath one hour after initiation of a rituximab infusion**  **PHYSICAL SETTING: Chemotherapy treatment unit in a cancer center** | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | A 72 year old male with Stage 4A mantle cell lymphoma is receiving his first infusion of rituximab as part of a planned 6 cycle course of bendamustine and rituximab. Prior to the infusion he is generally well with the exception of mild cytopenias and splenomegaly. The learner (e.g. hematology resident) is called to assess the patient after the patient develops shortness of breath 1 hour into the infusion. The learner must collaborate with the nursing team to identify a rituximab reaction, stabilize the patient and provide appropriate treatment. |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | By the end of the simulation activity, learners will be able to:   1. Have increased confidence collaborating effectively with an interprofessional team in the management of acute chemotherapy reactions (i.e. rituximab infusion reaction). Includes shared decision making, delegation of tasks and conflict resolution. 2. Identify core concepts required for effective collaboration in a complex medical case. 3. Critically analyze collaboration performance. 4. Lead an interprofessional team in providing emergency care for a patient with a rituximab infusion |
| **Critical Actions** | Medical Expert Role:  Prior to patient decompensation   * Obtains history and physical examination with focus on allergic, cardiac and infectious symptoms. * Clarifies timing of pre-medication (e.g. diphenhydramine, acetaminophen, corticosteroids) and if other pre-medications such as ranitidine were given * Determines at what rituximab infusion IV rate symptoms started and if the iv has been clamped * Clamps rituximab infusion   Following patient decompensation   * Immediate review of airway/ breathing/ circulation with stabilization as required * Positions patient with legs elevated * Orders 50 mg IV diphenhydramine, 50 mg IV ranitidine, 1000 mg acetaminophen, corticosteroids IV (e.g. Hydrocortisone 50 mg)   Following onset of rigors   * Requests demerol 50 mg, identifies a suitable IV line and pushes the first 25 mg while watching the rigors dissipate. * If rigors persist after 30 seconds a second dose of demerol 25 mg is administered * Identifies causes of low oxygen saturation including poor oximetry reading secondary to rigors and attempts to trouble shoot (e.g. moves oxygen probe, locates alternative oxygen monitor) * Provides reassurance to patient and coaches them with breathing * Clearly indicates to nursing when to be called again (i.e. any hypotension, hypoxia, fever, rigors) * Recommends that patient can resume rituximab infusion at reduced rate after period of recovery (e.g. after 30 minutes resume at 50 ml/ hour so long as vitals are stable)   Collaborator Role:   * Maintains good eye contact with nurse embedded participants * Reflects back nurse embedded participant statements * Uses closed loop communication * Clarifies team goals and defines roles * Encourages input from nurse embedded participants * Responds to non-verbal cues * Respectful and responsive to feedback from nurse embedded participants * Elicits help in managing the case from interprofessional team (e.g. nurses, supervising hematologist) |
| **Learner Preparation or Prework** | Learners should arrive to the scenario with at least a basic understanding of adverse effects associated with cancer treatment (e.g. chemotherapy, immunotherapy). At our center this was accomplished in part through dedicated lectures but could also be achieved by distributing pre-reading. It is expected that learners will have some hand on experience in supervising immunotherapy treatments although this would likely have been done in conjunction with an attending physician.  At arrival at the simulation lab, the learner should be given a 5-minute overview of the simulation room. This includes an overview of the monitors, phone, mannequin and embedded participants. In addition, the learner should be reminded that what occurs in the simulation lab is confidential and the performance of other learners should not be discussed without consent outside of dedicated debrief sessions.  The learner should be told that the simulation is intended to be a formative assessment focused on medical expert and collaboration skills. |

**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.*

The learner is provided handover for carrying the chemotherapy unit pager and then is promptly called to assess a patient with shortness of breath. The learner enters the room (chemotherapy unit) to find a patient experiencing shortness of breath during his rituximab infusion. Immediate provision of diphenhydramine, acetaminophen and IV corticosteroid is administered and rituximab infusion is clamped. The learner then quickly examines the patient for tongue swelling as well as allergic and cardiac symptoms. No rash, flushing or other signs of a hypersensitivity reaction are present. Cardiac examination is normal with no significant peripheral edema. Within two minutes, the patient decompensates. The learner calls for help from the attending physician to find that they are unavailable. The resident then initiates treatment for a suspected rituximab infusion reaction: 50 mg iv diphenhydramine, 50 mg ranitidine, 1000 mg acetaminophen, and iv corticosteroid is ordered. Within 30 seconds of getting medications assembled, the patient starts to have full body rigors with subsequent drop in oxygen saturation to 60%. Learner requests Demerol 50 mg and pushes the first 25 mg while watching for the rigors dissipate. After 30 seconds another 25 mg is required. To achieve a proper oxygen saturation the resident may, for example, check for different O2 monitors and get a face mask on the patient. Post Demerol initiation, HR returns to 80/min, BP normalizes to 120/60 and oxygen saturation improves to 96%. Learner recommends that the patient may continue rituximab infusion at a reduced rate with close monitoring.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

*Provide a list of management errors or difficulties that are commonly encountered when using this simulation case.*

1. Failure to apply best principles in collaboration with nursing team. The learner may fail to do one or more of the following; Introduce self to nursing team, engage in shared decision making, delegate responsibilities, define roles, use closed loop communication. The nurse embedded participants will demonstrate good collaboration between each other as an example to the learner. The learner will also be met with resistance if they do not incorporate the principles listed above. Good collaboration is also a major focus of the debrief.
2. Difficulty in diagnosing a rituximab infusion reaction. Given the learners likely limited experience with cancer therapy early in their training, we expect many of them to first consider more common causes of shortness of breath such as infection and classical allergic reactions. The nurse embedded participants as well as the patient themselves will prompt the learner to consider a rituximab infusion reaction in their differential.
3. Lack of comfort with treating rigors. We anticipate that the learner will be unfamiliar with the correct dose and administration of demerol as well as be weary of adverse effects. The nurse embedded participants will reassure the learner and use examples of previous rigor treatment orders (from other physicians) to guide the learner.
