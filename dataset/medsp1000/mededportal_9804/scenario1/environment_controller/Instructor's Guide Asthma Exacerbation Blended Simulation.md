# Instructor Guide- Asthma Exacerbation Blended Simulation with SimMan3G/Standardized Patient/Patient Care Assistant

# Definition of Roles

In addition to the SimMan3G with well-equipped simulation lab, the case requires an operator and voice for the SM3G, an SP support person/ spouse of the SM3G, and a patient care assistant. The SM3G will be programmed with initial vital signs that will be displayed on the monitor. The operator and voice will adjust the monitor findings based on the student’s actions and will briefly speak to the students in short answers. The SP spousal /support will provide most of the history and ask questions as scripted. The patient care assistant will carry out student instructions, if and when directed, for immediate patient care (e.g. applying oxygen, starting neb treatments, etc.)

Document 1 is the case development form or case blueprint. This is for the instructor’s use and forms the basis for the case and other supporting documents.

Document 2 is the standardized patient script. This training document should be memorized by the SP to ensure consistency and accuracy of case portrayal. This document is used by the SP educators during the training sessions. Patient care assistant challenge statements or questions are also included. The SM3G operator has very little to say and the scenario has to be flexible enough to allow appropriate response to the students without changing the intent of the case.

Document 5 is the student instruction sheet for this case. This is placed outside the simulation lab for the student to read prior to entering the room. The same form is also positioned in an accessible place inside the room for the student to reference as needed. Note that more general OSCE protocol instructions are presented before the multi-station OSCE begins.

Document 6 is the examiner evaluation of the standardized patient. This form is used by the examiner or other quality control monitor to document the SP performance for accuracy and consistency. Document 7 is the equipment/supply list.

Document 8 is the sample timeline illustrating how the experience unfolds. It includes specifics for monitor settings and physiologic response of the SM3G to treatment rendered.

# Practical Implementation

The case is designed to be given to a student at the end of the third year of medical school. Students were assigned to work in pairs. This encounter was given extended time for completion at 25 minutes instead of the typical 15 minutes. The extra time was given to allow for the interaction between the students and the case participants. Based on the students’ therapeutic choices, the monitor settings for the SimMan3G were manually adjusted from the simulation control room according to predetermined parameters.

Students were given advance notice that one of the OSCE cases would include the SimMan3G. This notice was initially given during the formal OSCE orientation session held three weeks before the actual examination. They were reminded again via email and in person during the general briefing session before the start of the OSCE. Students were not informed of their partners for this case in advance. This information was revealed on the day of the examination when they received their case flow information bookmark.

While waiting outside the simulation laboratory, students read their instructions for the case. They entered the room at the audio announcement to “begin case.” The case instructions are also posted in the simulation laboratory. The SP, SimMan3G, and patient care assistant are already in the lab. The operator and voice of the SimMan3G is located in the simulation control room and can monitor activity via the one way glass. The students were given 25 minutes to complete this encounter ending either with time expiring or arriving at a disposition decision for the patient before time expired. Students were notified at the 20 minute mark that 5 minutes remained if they had not already completed the case. There was no SOAP note requirement for this case.

Case participants are trained under the direction of the OSCE director and members of the OSCE committee who are charged with SP training. The committee includes clinicians, director of evaluation and assessment, simulation center staff, and support staff. A minimum of 2 training sessions is scheduled; one for script training and software training and one for practical training with repeated software training including completion of the evaluation forms. Attendance at training sessions is mandatory for participation.
