# Standardized Patient Script - Asthma Exacerbation (Blended Simulation)

Participants: High fidelity simulator (SM3G); Standardized Patient (SP); Patient Care Assistant (PCA)/Nurse

Age range: Age 26 for SM3G, 22 to 40 for SP significant other; SP age range: same; PCA age range: any

Gender: Male or female

Name: SM3G – Sam Brown; SP significant other – Kelly Walsh

# Affect (including facial expression):

Sam will be in obvious respiratory distress.

Kelly will be quite concerned and serious; almost frightened. Kelly should be positioned close to the gurney.

# Chief complaint:

SM3G/Sam: “hard to breathe, short of breath”

# History of Present Illness:

Kelly:

Sam caught a cold and for the past 3 days had a stuffy nose, sneezing, and a dry cough. He has used his rescue inhaler a lot, up to 6 times a day. His neb machine is broken. This morning was the worst. He woke up very short of breath and his inhaler did not help. I asked him if he wanted to come to the ER and he said yes.   
Usually his asthma is under good control. He sees the lung specialist once a year. He takes his allergy medication daily. Current medications: albuterol inhaler as needed, typically 3 times a week; Claritin once a day. He does not use a daily steroid inhaler because he has been doing so well. Once last winter he took prednisone when he had bronchitis. He has not been hospitalized.

# Past Medical/Social History:

Kelly:

Sam works as a school teacher (theater/drama) and does some acting in community productions during the summer.   
The two of you have been together for 6 years and are totally committed.   
Neither of you smokes.   
Sam has a glass of wine with dinner 3-4 times a week; perhaps 20 oz. weekly.   
Sam has not had any operations or major injuries.

# Family History:

Kelly:

Sam’s parents are in their late 50’s and have no medical problems that you are aware of. His grandfather died of a stroke a year ago (dad’s father).

# SP Challenge Question(s) :

 Kelly may ask what caused Sam to become so short of breath.
