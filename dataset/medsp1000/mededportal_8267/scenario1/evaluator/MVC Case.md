# Single Car MVC CRM Instructor Highlights

This case is designed to introduce the first two CRM principles, Communication and Global Assessment. It also serves as an introduction to the goals for both leaders and followers on the trauma team.

# CRM topic: Communication

Introduce themselves and address people directly

Use names or at least roles when communicating. Cite specific examples during the debrief.

Establish communication paths

. Communication should flow back and forth between the trauma leader and the rest of the team. Depends on introductions and verbal and non-verbal communication.

Use non-judgmental comments

Self-explanatory, point out any memorable comments.

Close the loop and give feedback

Vital signs called out by a team member and acknowledged by the Team Leader, along with a plan to act on them. Team members acknowledge assignments (infusing blood, placing chest tube, etc…) and update the Team Leader on progress.

Use clear and standard language

 Avoid generic terms like “tube”, instead using specific terms like “chest tube” or “endotracheal tube” Specific directions by the team leader: volume of fluid to be infused, size of IVs to be placed, frequency of vital signs

# CRM topic: Global Assessment

eam leader summarizes patient history, data, differential, and current situation  Performed prior to patient arrival and at least once more during the case

Avoid fixation errors

Team Leader cannot focus in on laryngoscopy or chest tube, must continue leading the resuscitation while procedures are ongoing If the forearm fracture is identified during the primary or secondary survey, it should be quickly splinted and left to be dealt with after the more pressing issues

Generate new ideas and provide clarity of established ideas  Team members can and should suggest intubation, chest tube placement, FAST, or OR  Suggestions should be acknowledged and acted upon by the Team Leader

He was restrained, there was a prolonged extrication, and he is awake but confused and combative with a large laceration on his forehead from where he struck the windshield. His vital signs are: a respiratory rate of 22, heart rate of 115, BP of 80/60, and O2 sat of $9 0 \%$ on a non-rebreather. He is boarded and collared and we’re attempting to obtain IV access en route. Our ETA is 3 minutes. Any questions?”

# Medic Report on arrival:

“Is everyone here? Okay. This guy is 70 years old and was noted to be driving erratically at a high rate of speed by other motorists. He crossed over into oncoming traffic and then swerved off the road and hit a tree. His latest vital signs are a BP of 78/54, a pulse of 120, and he’s breathing at 28 with an O2 sat of $9 0 \%$ on a nonrebreather. We still haven’t been able to get an IV but did put in a tibial IO and have given him 500 ccs of saline through it. We haven’t gotten to look for other injuries because we were focusing on getting access. Any questions?”
