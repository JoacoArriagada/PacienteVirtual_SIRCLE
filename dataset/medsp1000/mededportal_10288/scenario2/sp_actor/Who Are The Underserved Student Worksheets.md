Student Worksheets: Who Are the Underserved

## Case 2, Part 1:

A 45-year-old man comes in for a routine check up of his type 2 diabetes. He feels well but has not been able to check blood sugars because he ran out of test strips and could not afford more. He's been trying to make healthy meals from what he gets from the food bank. His past medical history include diabetes and high blood pressure for 10 years. He's never had a surgery. He quit smoking about 5 years ago and drinks a beer with friends for special occasions. He's single, finds work as a day laborer, and lives in low-income housing. His Dad died from kidney failure related to diabetes, his two sisters also have diabetes. He denies increased thirst or urination.

## Case 2, Part 2:

This patient is African American and considers himself lucky to have had the same doctor for 10 years at a community health center 3 blocks from his apartment. He sings in a Barbershop quarter for fun with some friends he's had since high school. He has been working with a diabetes team at the clinic to improve his self-management skills. He does not want to end up on dialysis like his Dad. He wants to walk more but worried about his inner-city neighborhood at night. He's also worried about recent state budget cuts - he may lose his low-income housing subsidy.

## Case 2, Part 3:

Your patient plans to work with nutritionist regarding healthy food choices and to start walking on days he does not have labor jobs. Unfortunately, he comes back to clinic a few weeks later with a sore on his right big toe from a pair of work boots a friend let him borrow.
