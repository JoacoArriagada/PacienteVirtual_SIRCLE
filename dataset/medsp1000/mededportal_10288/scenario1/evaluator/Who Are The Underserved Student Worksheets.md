Student Worksheets: Who Are the Underserved

## Case 1, Part 1:

You are seeing a 37-year-old woman who is a new patient to your clinic with "heavy period that smells bad for one month". She's had heavier periods over the past few years and has tried ibuprofen to help with cramps. No abdominal pain. No change in vaginal discharge. No other medical problems. She has been pregnant twice with two deliveries. An abnormal pap smear complicated her second pregnancy. She also had a c-section at age 27 for this pregnancy. She smokes 2-3 cig per day, is sexually active with her husband of 15 years who works for the Department of Natural Resources and she works as a Sunday school teacher in her church. Her Dad has heart disease, but her Mom is healthy. The only other symptom she notes is fatigue.

## Case 1, Part 2:

This patient is white, comfortably middle class with her husband's government job, and has lived in Montana her whole life. She had 4 sexual partners before her husband while she was in college. As you dig deeper you discover she had a very traumatic experience with her second birth (prolapsed cord that resulted in emergency c-section) and she did not feel comfortable with male doctors after that experience but the closest female provider was 100 miles away and so she has not seen a doctor for about 10 years. She was also told that the pap smear abnormality would probably get better after the birth so she was not as worried about it.

## Case 1, Part 3:

Your general exam is remarkable for a pale woman and a pelvic exam reveals a necrotic, friable cervix. A Pap smear shows cervical squamous cell carcinoma. At her follow up visit you give your patient her diagnosis of cervical cancer and refer her to the closest gynecologist-oncologist 200 miles away for discussion of treatment options.
