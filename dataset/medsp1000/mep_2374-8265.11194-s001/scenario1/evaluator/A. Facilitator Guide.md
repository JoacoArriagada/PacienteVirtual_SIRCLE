**Medical Student Documentation**

**Facilitator Guide**

**Group Didactic – 30 minutes**

***Introduction (2 minutes):***

* Purpose of this session is to review basics of documentation in emergency medicine
* Informal show of hands: How many have received formal training in documentation? In billing and coding considerations?

***3 goals of a patient note (10 minutes):***

Why do we write patient notes at all?

* Continuity of care
* Medicolegal
* Billing

The most important part for ALL THREE is that your note is **ACCURATE** and **HONEST**

Continuity of care

* Coherent history: For patients who decompensate, end up getting intubated, etc., this may be the only history guiding a patient’s entire hospital course
* ED course: Narrative of events in the ED, particularly if patient’s status is very dynamic

Medicolegal

* Appropriately consider dangerous differential diagnoses
* Explain why something was NOT done (e.g. CT head for head trauma, antibiotics for sore throat)

Billing

* Based on checklist of required items
* ED notes are classified as Level 3, 4, or 5 based on acuity and complexity
* Most ED pts = level 5, “urgent care” type encounter = level 4, “primary care” type encounter = level 3
* Higher level note requires more included information: See Appendix B (documentation reference card) for minimum required components for each note level

***Billing specifics (5 minutes):***

Required components of a Level 5 note

* History of Present Illness (HPI): 4+ qualifiers
* Past, Family, and Social History (PFSH): 1 element from each of three categories (“non-contributory” or “no results found” doesn’t count)
* Review of Systems (ROS): 10+ systems (individually documented or sometimes can use a standardized phrase attesting that at least 10 systems have been reviewed)
* Can state “History limited due to \_\_\_\_” for patients who are unresponsive, confused, or otherwise unable to give you an accurate history themselves
* Physical Exam (PE): 8+ systems (1 component per system, see documentation reference card for what counts as a system)
* Medical Decision Making (MDM): Should accurately demonstrate acuity and complexity
  + Interpret labs (e.g. “electrolytes normal” or “no leukocytosis”)
  + Interpret imaging (summarize radiology read)
  + Individually interpret ECG
  + “Case discussed with \_\_\_” (every admission, every consult)
  + Additional history gathered from someone else (e.g. EMS, family member, nursing home)
  + IV meds and prescriptions

***MDM and Impression (5 minutes):***

MDM format

* Narrative format while incorporating differential diagnoses
* Particularly important if ED course is dynamic
* Different from inpatient H&P or progress notes, where the assessment/plan is a snapshot of the whole day

Impression

* Single word or phrase
* Specific if diagnosis is confirmed, otherwise it’s okay for the impression to be generic (e.g. “headache” or “right lower quadrant abdominal pain”). Also okay for impression to be the same as chief complaint.
* Complex cases can require multiple impressions (e.g. “right lower lobe pneumonia”, “sepsis”, “hypoxia” for the same case)

Sign notes with name and “Medical Student” designation

***Review Appendix B – Documentation Reference Card (3 minutes)***

* Helpful to reference what counts as a separate body system (e.g. “allergy/immune”, “lymphatic”)
* Can keep handy while writing notes on shift
* Critical care time: not used now as a med student, but useful to reference as a future resident or attending

***Review Appendix C – Sample ED Note (3 minutes)***

* No single right way to write a note, but MDM format shown works quite well

**Standardized Patient Activity – 60 minutes**

***Briefing (10 minutes):***

Once all students are seated in the classroom, explain the goals of the next activity

* Practice documentation skills with simulated patient encounter
* Introduce students to common chief complaint in emergency medicine

Explain the flow of the session

* Students will split into two groups. Each group will jointly perform a history and physical exam on their standardized patient who is being evaluated in the emergency department setting.
* Enough information should be gathered to formulate a plan and write a complete note.
* Each group can split up their duties in the room however they wish.
* Students will have about 15 minutes in the room to interact with the SP.
* Students and facilitator will then reconvene and talk through the rest of the case together.
* Students will complete a sample patient note based on this simulated encounter.

Open “Appendix E” PowerPoint file

* Show Slide 1 and read out loud
* “When a patient arrives in the emergency department with a chief complaint of chest pain, they will often have an ECG performed immediately after triage.”
* Show Slide 2 and allow students to look over ECG for 1-2 minutes
* Ask 1-2 student volunteers to read the ECG. ECG shows normal sinus rhythm with no acute ST or T wave changes.

***Standardized Patient Encounter (15 minutes):***

* Divide students into two groups, and lead each student to the door of each exam room.
* Give each group a “5-minute warning” about 5 minutes prior to the 15-minute mark.
* When both groups have finished and exited the exam room, reconvene in the classroom.

***Discussion (20 minutes):***

As a group, discuss the emergent differential diagnosis of chest pain

* Acute coronary syndrome: ACS comprises STEMI, NSTEMI, and unstable angina. ECG non-ischemic but should still be worked up given age and comorbidities.
* Pulmonary embolism: Can’t use PERC criteria since age is over 50. However, PE is unlikely given lack of shortness of breath, tachycardia, hypoxia, or signs or symptoms of DVT
* Aortic dissection: Unlikely since pain is not ripping or tearing or radiating to the upper back, and patient is not uncomfortable appearing or excessively hypertensive.
* Pneumothorax: Unlikely given normal lung exam and lack of shortness of breath or hypoxia. Could obtain chest x-ray to definitively rule out.
* Esophageal rupture: Unlikely given lack of vomiting or retching. Important to consider as a non-cardiopulmonary dangerous etiology of chest pain.

Review initial plan and ED course

* Initial plan: BMP, CBC, troponin, chest x-ray
* Show Slide 3 (lab results) and Slide 4 (chest x-ray). Ask student volunteers to interpret labs and read chest x-ray.
* What is an appropriate next step given these results? Repeat troponin (2 or 3 hours after initial) given recent onset of chest pain.
* Show Slide 5 (repeat troponin result). What would you do next?
* If there is still concern for acute coronary syndrome, HEART score can help risk-stratify.
* If there is moderate to high concern for acute coronary syndrome, the patient should be administered a full-dose aspirin.
* This patient can be admitted to the observation unit for a next-day cardiac stress test. Observation units can be helpful for patients who are not quite ready for discharge but aren’t likely to need admission for more than 24 hours.

***Wrap-Up (5 minutes):***

* Each student will write their own sample note due midnight tonight
* Hand out copies of Appendix F (ED record) and Appendix G (ECG) to each student for reference
* Email Appendix H (note template) to all students
* Students will receive individualized feedback on their sample note
