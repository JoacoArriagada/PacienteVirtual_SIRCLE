| **Brief narrative description of case** | This case describes a 31-year-old male patient presenting unresponsive after an opioid overdose. He responds well to naloxone, but he quickly develops dyspnea secondary to flash pulmonary edema. The learner goals are to recognize this potential complication of naloxone and to appropriately treat it. |
| **Primary Learning Objectives** | * Describe 1 mechanism of opioid induced pulmonary edema and 3 mechanisms of naloxone induced pulmonary edema * Execute appropriate pharmacological and airway management of an opioid overdose patient using equipment available * Execute the best management for a patient in flash pulmonary edema that is difficult to oxygenate * Demonstrate team work and communication skills by: * Appointing a team leader * Team leader assigns clear roles and tasks to available team members * Team utilizes closed-loop communication as appropriate * Team communicates in an open and respectful manner |
| **Critical Actions** | 1. Recognize that the patient is unresponsive and in respiratory failure due to opioid overdose 2. Appropriately treat the patient with naloxone 3. Recognize the progressive dyspnea after the naloxone administration 4. Reassess the patient and diagnose pulmonary edema 5. Attempt to oxygenate and ventilate the patient using either a non-rebreather oxygen mask or NIPPV. 6. Recognize that the patient’s O2% saturation is not improving by any means, and perform rapid sequence intubation or awake intubation. 7. Establish lung-protective ventilator settings (e.g., tidal volumes 6-8 mL/kg or even as low as 4-6 mL/kg; rate 14-18; FiO2 100% initially; plateau pressure <30 mmH2O; PEEP 5 mmH2O initially)10 8. Recognize that oxygenation is still poor despite maximizing ventilator settings 9. Initiate advanced ICU management (e.g., prone positioning, advanced ventilator modes, neuromuscular blockage), ECMO procedures per local protocol, or consider transfer to a higher level of care8 |
| **Learner Preparation** | The patient is a 31 year-old male who presents to the ER by EMS after he was found unresponsive in a nearby park by his friends. He is only producing agonal respirations. |

Ideal Scenario Flow

Learners discover from EMS that the patient was found unresponsive after injecting heroin. They should administer naloxone to reverse the opioid overdose. The patient will respond to the antidote and will regain consciousness. The learner should complete a history and physical exam. However, the patient develops progressive dyspnea and his O2% saturation holds in the low 60s%. The learner should recognize this and reassess the patient. They should discover rales on exam. The learners should order a chest x-ray at some point, which will show pulmonary edema. They may attempt simple oxygen administration or noninvasive positive pressure ventilation to improve oxygenation. However, there will be no improvement and the patient will gradually decline. The learner should recognize the need for intubation. Oxygenation does not improve, despite intubation. The learner should recognize the need for ECMO in this patient. They must either begin ECMO themselves, or call the respective team responsive for ECMO.

Anticipated Management Mistakes

1. Failure to recognize failure of the patient to oxygenate: Learners may not recognize that the patient is dyspneic and that their O2% sat is low after naloxone administration. If they do not notice, a “nurse” can enter and ask if the learners want to do anything for the patient’s vitals. The SP can also further exacerbate his dyspnea.
2. Failure to recognize need for ECMO or advanced ICU management: Learners may not recognize the need for ECMO or advanced ICU management in this patient, especially if they have not encountered a patient like this before. If the learners are struggling, the case can be stopped at this point and management options can be discussed further in the debrief.
