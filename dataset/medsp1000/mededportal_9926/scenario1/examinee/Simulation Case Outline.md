1. Title: Respiratory distress in the recovery room following general anesthesia for total thyroidectomy
2. Environment
   1. Simulation lab set up to be: Post-anesthetic recovery room
   2. Equipment
      1. Airway equipment: Ambu-bag, laryngoscope, video laryngoscope, fiberoptic bronchoscope, scalpel, capnogram, laryngeal mask airway
      2. Monitoring: Pulse oximeter, non-invasive blood pressure cuff on arm, 5 lead electrocardiogram (EKG)
      3. Code cart with defibrillator
      4. Paperwork: Pre-operative anesthesia history and physical, intra-operative anesthetic record
3. Case Narrative
   1. Scenario background given to participants:
      1. You are the anesthesia resident assigned to the recovery room. You are called by one of the recovery room nurses to evaluate his/her patient, who is complaining of difficulty breathing. The patient is a 57-year-old man who just underwent a total thyroidectomy for goiter with substernal extension. Sternotomy was not performed. He arrived 10 minutes ago in the recovery room, extubated, receiving 6 liters supplemental oxygen via face mask, hemodynamically stable and was initially not complaining of pain. Over the past 5 minutes he is now stating that it is becoming increasingly difficult for him to take a breath. He has never experienced this sensation before. He is also now complaining of neck pain that he is rating 6/10. Past medical history is significant for hypertension treated with hydrochlorathiazide; obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), using continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) 8 cm H2O at night; goiter causing tracheal compression – euthryoid status by preoperative labs. No known drug allergies. No significant surgical history. Denies social history. Meds: hydrochlorothiazide, aspirin 81mg (held one week ago). EKG: normal sinus rhythm, with left axis deviation and left ventricular hypertrophy. His operative course is notable for the following. He was intubated via awake fiberoptic due to concerns for tracheal compression without difficulty. A direct laryngoscopy following intubation was not performed. Anesthesia was maintained via total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) with propofol and remifentanil due to severe postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV). Long-acting muscle relaxants were not given due to use of recurrent laryngeal nerve monitoring intraoperatively (no events noted during monitoring). Emergence was uneventful and the patient was extubated in the operating room after meeting all criteria for extubation.
   2. Baseline State
      1. Vitals: HR: 70; BP: 120’s/70’s; RR: 35; SpO2: 96%
      2. Neuro: Somnolent, but arousable; Pupils 5mm
      3. Respiratory: 6L oxygen delivered by face mask
      4. Cardiovascular: Stable at baseline
      5. Environmental: Bloody surgical dressing on anterior neck; packing under one side of neck to make neck look unequal
