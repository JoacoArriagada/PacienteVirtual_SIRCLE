1. Title: Respiratory distress in the recovery room following general anesthesia for total thyroidectomy
2. Environment
   1. Simulation lab set up to be: Post-anesthetic recovery room
   2. Manikin Set Up
      1. Type of simulator: High-fidelity, adult simulator
      2. Lines: 1 peripheral IV, Oxygen via face mask
      3. Moulage: Bandage over midline neck, gauze underneath skin to appear as hematoma
      4. Drugs: maintenance IV fluid, propofol, succinylcholine, midazolam, phenylephrine, albuterol, naloxone, calcium chloride or calcium gluconate
      5. Miscellaneous: manikin covered with hospital gown
   3. Equipment
      1. Airway equipment: Ambu-bag, laryngoscope, video laryngoscope, fiberoptic bronchoscope, scalpel, capnogram, laryngeal mask airway
      2. Monitoring: Pulse oximeter, non-invasive blood pressure cuff on arm, 5 lead electrocardiogram (EKG)
      3. Code cart with defibrillator
      4. Paperwork: Pre-operative anesthesia history and physical, intra-operative anesthetic record
3. Actors
   1. Recovery room nurse
      1. No specific training required to play this role
      2. In this scenario, the nurse is new to their role in the recovery and feels very overwhelmed by the current status of the patient. They are also responsible for a second patient. The nurse will intermittently disappear to attend to the other patient. When returns to this scenario will be quite flustered and not very helpful.
   2. Surgeon
      1. No specific training required to play this role
      2. The surgeon will at first not be immediately available to evaluate the patient as they are in the operating room taking care of another patient. If participant requests their presence a second time, the surgeon will appear and be very upset that their surgery was interrupted. They will deny any bleeding or complications and refuse to open the incision.
4. Case Narrative
   1. Scenario background given to participants:
      1. You are the anesthesia resident assigned to the recovery room. You are called by one of the recovery room nurses to evaluate his/her patient, who is complaining of difficulty breathing. The patient is a 57-year-old man who just underwent a total thyroidectomy for goiter with substernal extension. Sternotomy was not performed. He arrived 10 minutes ago in the recovery room, extubated, receiving 6 liters supplemental oxygen via face mask, hemodynamically stable and was initially not complaining of pain. Over the past 5 minutes he is now stating that it is becoming increasingly difficult for him to take a breath. He has never experienced this sensation before. He is also now complaining of neck pain that he is rating 6/10. Past medical history is significant for hypertension treated with hydrochlorathiazide; obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), using continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) 8 cm H2O at night; goiter causing tracheal compression – euthryoid status by preoperative labs. No known drug allergies. No significant surgical history. Denies social history. Meds: hydrochlorothiazide, aspirin 81mg (held one week ago). EKG: normal sinus rhythm, with left axis deviation and left ventricular hypertrophy. His operative course is notable for the following. He was intubated via awake fiberoptic due to concerns for tracheal compression without difficulty. A direct laryngoscopy following intubation was not performed. Anesthesia was maintained via total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) with propofol and remifentanil due to severe postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV). Long-acting muscle relaxants were not given due to use of recurrent laryngeal nerve monitoring intraoperatively (no events noted during monitoring). Emergence was uneventful and the patient was extubated in the operating room after meeting all criteria for extubation.
   2. Baseline State
      1. Vitals: HR: 70; BP: 120’s/70’s; RR: 35; SpO2: 96%
      2. Neuro: Somnolent, but arousable; Pupils 5mm
      3. Respiratory: Upper airway obstruction due to OSA and tracheal obstruction; 6L oxygen delivered by face mask
      4. Cardiovascular: Stable at baseline
      5. Environmental: Bloody surgical dressing on anterior neck; packing under one side of neck to make neck look unequal
   3. Scenario development
      1. Phase 2: Increasing Stridor
         1. Patient status: Patient complains of increasing respiratory distress
            1. RR: 45, stridor; BP: 160/90; HR: 112; O2 Sat: 93%
         2. No improvement in oxygenation or respiratory status if face mask changed to non-rebreather mask or bag-valve mask or if oral/nasal airway placed.
      2. Phase 3: Inadvertent remifentanil bolus given by nurse when flushing IV
         1. Patient status: Apnea; RR: 0; BP: 100/65; HR: 96; SpO2: 85%
      3. Phase 4: Decision to secure airway
         1. Mask ventilation attempted initially while preparing for intubation. Difficult effective ventilation even with 2 person technique and oral/nasal airway due to obstruction. SpO2 will continue to decrease.
         2. Patient status if successful intubation- 100% Fio2; SpO2 remains 85%, patient becomes hypotensive to 80’s/40’s following induction and will progress to cardiac arrest. Learner needs to consider alternate etiologies for respiratory insufficiency and hemodynamic instability.
         3. Patient status if unsuccessful intubation following induction – cardiac arrest, patient continues to desaturate, participant should prepare to open surgical incision and consider cricothyroidotomy
      4. Phase 5: Cardiac arrest
         1. Patient status: Pulseless ventricular tachycardia
         2. Advanced cardiac life support (ACLS) initiated. Crash cart brought to bedside when requested. Learner should proceed to deliver defibrillation. Patient will return to sinus tachycardia with premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) after first defibrillation of 300 Joules or higher.
      5. Phase 6: Participant should prepare for return to operating room for neck exploration
         1. Patient status: Airway secured; SpO2 90%; HR 110’s with PVCs; BP 90’s/50’s
5. Instructor Notes
   1. Notes for actors
      1. Given this patient’s presentation, the main concern is for expanding neck hematoma.
         1. Event: Participant should make preparations to open the surgical incision.
      2. The recovery room nurse is new, and has limited experience managing unstable patients.
         1. Event: States the IV has not been flushed since arrival, going to do that now.
         2. Preparation: The recovery room nurse will not be able to make suggestions for management. He/she will be able to get supplies or push medications.
         3. Impact: The participant can ask for more assistance.
      3. The operating surgeon is available and present in the scenario after 2-3 minutes, and after a second attempt to contact. Surgeon is hesitant to open the neck, stating they do not feel the patients’ condition is due to hematoma.
         1. Event: Does the learner feel comfortable opening the incision without the surgeon present?
         2. Impact: Encourage discussion between participant and surgeon.
      4. The patient is complaining of 6/10 neck pain. He has not received any pain medication since arriving in the recovery room. He received 10mg Morphine intra-operatively.
         1. Impact: Intended to make the participant consider giving additional pain medications versus withhold medications due to difficulty breathing.
   2. Notes for operator
      1. Phase 1: After participant performs brief assessment, move to phase 2
      2. Phase 2: As participant is considering need for emergent re-intubation, the recovery room nurse will state that they are going to flush the IV (resulting in remifentanil bolus; patient will become unresponsive
      3. Phase 3: Patient will continue to desaturate and mask ventilation will be difficult
      4. Phase 4: Manikin airway will be programmed to all difficult settings; if participant uses IV induction agents, patient will become hypotensive and progress to cardiac arrest regardless of whether or not intubation is successful or not;
      5. Phase 5: Code cart is brought to patient bedside; participant should prepare for defibrillation. Following defibrillation of 300 Joules or greater, patient will return to sinus tachycardia with PVCs.
      6. Phase 6: Saturations will remain ~90% despite secured airway
