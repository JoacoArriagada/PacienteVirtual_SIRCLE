# STEPHANIE GIAMATTO SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED DISEASES STANDARDIZED PATIENT TRAINING MATERIALS

# CASE SUMMARY

Stephanie Giamatto is a 22-26 year-old female. She presents to the office at this time because: “I’m having a bit of discharge and I am concerned about it.” She is somewhat embarrassed to talk about this and has some concern of a possible sexually transmitted disease.

She has a lifetime history of 10 sexual partners and has been in the same heterosexual relationship for the past 6 months. She last had intercourse approximately 5 or 6 days ago and is concerned, because of the symptoms, that her boyfriend might not be monogamous. She is unconcerned that her behavior (not using condoms and binge drinking) puts her at high risk for STDS or pregnancy. She considers her behavior “normal.” She is aware of the health risks in her lifestyle but is not fearful about possible consequences.

Patient is casual in approach to her health and somewhat vague in response to questions but is not reluctant to answer specific questions. She should not seem evasive. The patient’s symptoms will remain vague because the symptoms themselves are not acute; therefore, she will really not have a lot of symptoms to relate. (Chlamydia sometimes is mild with very few symptoms.) She just knows that something is “not right,” or “different” (referring to the discharge).

Patient is also embarrassed about discussing the topic. She does not have a forceful personality; she is laid back and somewhat of a free spirit. She is initially uncomfortable discussing her lifestyle, including her sexual history, diet, and drinking, unless the learner takes time to build rapport and gain trust. She becomes uncomfortable if the learner confronts her, unless done so skillfully after building rapport and by using motivational interviewing skills.

Stephanie is uncomfortable discussing the topic but would not be considered to have low health literacy.
