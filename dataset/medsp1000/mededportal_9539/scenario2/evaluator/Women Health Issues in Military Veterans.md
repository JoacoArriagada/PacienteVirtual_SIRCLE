# CASE 2

# Stephanie Giamatto, Sexually Transmitted Diseases

# SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED DISEASES PATIENT: STEPHANIE GIAMATTO

LEARNER INFORMATION(to be shared with learner prior to role–play)

Patient’s Name: Stephanie Giamatto is a 22-26-year-old female.

Chief complaint: Vaginal discharge for one week

Location: VA Physician’s Office

Learner Skill Set: Eliciting data in an objective and non-judgmental manner; accurate recording of symptoms; sensitivity; motivational interviewing (MI) skills; self-awareness

# FACILITATOR’S GUIDE (NOT to be shared with learner prior to role-play)

Note to Facilitator: If a Sexually Transmitted Disease (STD) is suspected, it is necessary to ask questions regarding all possible STDs. The patient will remain vague and embarrassed, but will discuss behavior change if motivational interviewing skills are utilized.

This scenario is purposely written with vague complaints that may be interpreted in many different ways. The teaching point is that these symptoms can point to several disease processes. In addition to using motivational interviewing to prompt behavior change specifically in regards to tobacco and alcohol use, the interviewer should be able to identify that this could be a Sexually Transmitted Disease (STD), and the patient should be recommended to get testing for multiple STDs.

# Learner skills to which the patient would respond positively

□ Acknowledging the wisdom of the patient to seek help (validating)
□ Allowing any emotion to open and then effectively moving to different areas □ Allowing time to listen—this may be the first time this person has sought assistance. It is worth taking the time to listen.
□ Encouraging self-awareness and self-change talk
□ Reflecting your understanding of the situation to the patient
□ Identifying changeable behaviors (don’t ding a patient for personality)

# STEPHANIE GIAMATTO SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED DISEASES STANDARDIZED PATIENT TRAINING MATERIALS

# CASE SUMMARY

Stephanie Giamatto is a 22-26 year-old female. She presents to the office at this time because: “I’m having a bit of discharge and I am concerned about it.” She is somewhat embarrassed to talk about this and has some concern of a possible sexually transmitted disease.

She has a lifetime history of 10 sexual partners and has been in the same heterosexual relationship for the past 6 months. She last had intercourse approximately 5 or 6 days ago and is concerned, because of the symptoms, that her boyfriend might not be monogamous. She is unconcerned that her behavior (not using condoms and binge drinking) puts her at high risk for STDS or pregnancy. She considers her behavior “normal.” She is aware of the health risks in her lifestyle but is not fearful about possible consequences.

Your challenge as the standardized patient (SP) is threefold □ To appropriately and accurately reveal facts and emotional concerns about Ms. Giamatto’s health, medical, sexual, and social history To observe any changes in approach made by the interviewer and adjust your portrayal and responses accordingly To accurately report in verbal feedback the impact that the interviewer had on Ms. Giamatto and reflect how the observable skills were used by the learner

Patient is casual in approach to her health and somewhat vague in response to questions but is not reluctant to answer specific questions. She should not seem evasive. The patient’s symptoms will remain vague because the symptoms themselves are not acute; therefore, she will really not have a lot of symptoms to relate. (Chlamydia sometimes is mild with very few symptoms.) She just knows that something is “not right,” or “different” (referring to the discharge).

Patient is also embarrassed about discussing the topic. She does not have a forceful personality; she is laid back and somewhat of a free spirit. She is initially uncomfortable discussing her lifestyle, including her sexual history, diet, and drinking, unless the learner takes time to build rapport and gain trust. She becomes uncomfortable if the learner confronts her, unless done so skillfully after building rapport and by using motivational interviewing skills.

Stephanie is uncomfortable discussing the topic but would not be considered to have low health literacy.

Military History: Stephanie took ROTC in high school and enjoyed it. She liked feeling like she belonged to a group. She was told about the perks of joining in the military and decided to enlist in the US Navy after graduation. She also hoped that the GI Bill would help to pay for college. (She was able to take a few college credits through the Navy while enlisted.)

While in the Navy, Stephanie attended basic training at the Great Lakes Naval Base (north of Chicago on Lake Michigan), followed by tech school on the base, where she was trained in administrative functions (typing, filing, etc.). Boot camp involved a lot of getting yelled at, which she disliked.

Her one and only station was in Norfolk, VA, where she did office work. She was never deployed overseas, though she asked about it. Stephanie disliked the rigidity of the military, along with the stress of getting paperwork ready for those deploying. The job entailed working with people at all levels of the Navy. People always made mistakes on their paper work or filled it out incorrectly, which made more work for her and was very annoying. The hours ran from 7:30 AM to 4:30 PM, with occasional work on the weekends when she was inundated with paperwork.

Once realizing that the Navy was so different from ROTC, she decided the military was not for her. She does not have a very strong personality and did not take to a regimented lifestyle, and was disappointed that she was not getting the opportunity to travel. She did not like getting yelled at or being told what to do. She was able to get out of the military because both of her parents have health problems.

# MILITARY INTERVIEW THIS INTERVIEW IS TO BE USED AS REFERENCE FOR MILITARY DETAILS THAT A CIVILIAN MAY NOT KNOW. IT IS NOT NECESSARY TO MEMORIZE THIS INFORMATION BUT WOULD BE HELPFUL TO BE FAMILIAR WITH THE CONTENTS.

1. What branch of the military did you serve in? US Navy.

2. How long? 4 years. What rank? E-4 Petty Officer third class. Who would you report to? Chief Petty Officer. Who reported to you? Was just getting ready to be a supervisor when I got out. Where were you stationed? Basic training, Great Lakes Naval base; tech school was also there; then stationed in Norfolk, Virginia.

3. Describe the physical work environment. Office, doing paper work, computer work.

4. Please describe a typical day (is there travel, hours worked, tasks given, etc.) Mainly worked Monday-Friday 0730-1630, and an occasional weekend, to catch up on paperwork.

5. How did this profession affect your ability to live a healthy lifestyle (exercise, sleep, nutrition)? I was able to work out when I need to, to pass my PT (physical fitness test).

6. Are there over the counter medications, alternative medications that are typically used in your profession (weight loss, energy drinks, tobacco, steroids, etc.)? Smokes 1 pack of cigarettes per day since age 16. Has tried to

quit 3 times unsuccessfully.

7. What are things that your friends find interesting about military service (like how often do you shoot your gun)? What types of questions do you get asked by the public? I get asked about travel and being deployed (stationed at overseas locations). Unfortunately I was not able to get deployed and travel.

8. How did you get interested in Military Service? I was interested in getting the GI bill to help pay for school.

9. What did you like most about your profession? Getting to work with different people.

10. What did you dislike most about your job? Too much structure, had trouble adjusting to the rigidity of the military.

11. Educational background: High school, Graduated high school. Vocational school or College, Education Benefits? I was able to get in some basic math and English college courses while in the military.

12. Special training, Boot camp? Boot camp was a couple of months long; they just yell a lot and try to break you down and reform you in their image of a team player.

13. What are the physical and psychological qualifications/testing required? To get into the military you have to pass a written test. Each job has its own cutoff score to be eligible. The higher your score the better job you can get. You have to pass a basic background check.

14. What positions have you had since you started this profession? The assignment at Norfolk was my one and only after basic training and tech school (trained on administrative functions, typing, filing etc.).

15. Can you get promoted? If so, to what position and how? When are you done with your service? You can get promoted in the military. It is based on how long you have been in your present rank, for enlisted folks they are listed as E1 through E9. They are also based on test scores you achieve showing job proficiency. I have recently gotten out of the military and am trying situated to get into school.

16. What are the "perks" of military service? Steady paycheck, good insurance, 30 days of vacation per year, opportunities to travel, tuition assistance to go to school. The GI bill to pay for school.

17. How did you get paid (contract, per mile, per hour, etc.)? How could you make more money? You get paid a monthly salary, money for food—basic allowance for subsistence (BAS), a housing allowance if living off base or use of a house on base if you have a family or a dorm room if you live by yourself.

18.What was the most stressful part of your job? Getting all the reports in on time. Getting paperwork ready for those deploying and knowing you might have to deploy. Not many people look forward to being deployed.

19. How free were you to discuss your political views and opinions? You have the right to speak your view and opinion. You cannot give speeches or campaign for candidates while in your uniform.

20. What emotional problems were common in people in your profession?

Similar to the civilian counterparts.

21. What type of physical activity was required in your position? Normal office work. But to pass the PT test I had to work out and stay within my weight limit. I ran, lifted weights, and did yoga.

22. What things happened that really made you angry? People who did not read instructions to fill out their paper work and then wanted me to do their work for them.

23. What other professions did you work with, and how did they affect you (make your job easier or harder)? I worked with just everyone in the military as most office workers do at their companies.

24. What was it like to re-enter civilian life? It was a little scary. Even though the structure was a little hard to deal with, it kept me focused.

25. What "health risk" behaviors were common in your profession (tobacco, alcohol, specific drugs, sex, stress, marital or family discord, etc.)? I smoked a little more at times due to the deadlines. Drugs were not an option due to the random urinalysis and they didn't interest me anyway.

26. How did your vocation affect the personal life of you or people you knew? Well, in the military you have to be ready to leave everyone and everything at a moment’s notice. Sometimes you don't know how long you will be gone and sometimes you cannot tell people where you are going or what you are going to be doing.

27. What activities and/or hobbies did people in your profession like to do? Most of the people on the military were younger and involved in everything from sports, to camping, to school, to taking care of families for the married folks.

28. Are there any items of clothing or props that someone portraying a person in your profession would use consistently (hats, keys, stuff you’d keep in your pockets or bags, day planners, work boots, particular “costume,” or types of clothing…)? No specific props needed.

29. If a person in your position were to get discharged, what would they most likely have done to justify involuntary termination and what would the steps involve? To get separated involuntarily is fairly hard to do in most cases. Sometimes though there are cutbacks like in any job and anyone can be let go. If you break rules, there are rules in place—kind of like when you have a union job—that must be followed to let you go. If you break laws then you can be dealt with just like in the civilian world with fines, probation, jail time.

30. What type of jargon would be common for someone in this line of service? Are there words they use that are “lingo” specific to the profession? Every branch of the military has its use of acronyms. Basic training is boot camp. Then there is your job training (tech school for Navy and Air Force, AIT for Army). DOS is date of separation. For this scenario with a young person in an administrative-type job, there is no specific lingo to use.
