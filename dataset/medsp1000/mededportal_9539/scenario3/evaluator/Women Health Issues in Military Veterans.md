Learner Skill Set: Eliciting data in an objective and non-judgmental manner; accurate recording of symptoms; sensitivity; motivational interviewing (MI) skills; self-awareness

# FACILITATOR’S GUIDE (NOT to be shared with learner prior to role-play)

Judy is having pain with intercourse. She is postmenopausal and the symptoms started happening a couple years after menopause. Her husband has had difficulty with erections for the last two years or so. She thinks her problem is causing his problem. She has never liked sex as much as her husband and has long-standing guilt about this issue. She is also embarrassed about having to talk about it. Though she is glad her husband is around more, she avoids him because of her problem.

Note to Facilitator: This scenario is purposely written to provide the opportunity to actively counsel Mrs. Wilson. The provider’s focus for this encounter is trying to understand how her sexual dysfunction is impacting her current functioning.

It is worth taking the time to listen.
□ Encouraging self-awareness and self-change talk
□ Reflecting your understanding of the situation to the patient
□ Identifying changeable behaviors (don’t ding a patient for personality)

# JUDY WILSON SEXUAL DYSFUNCTION STANDARDIZED PATIENT TRAINING MATERIALS

# CASE SUMMARY

Judy Wilson is a married woman over 50. She is coming in at this time because over the past $1 { - } 1 \%$ years, sexual intercourse has become very painful for her. This has significantly influenced the couple’s relationship. She has been seeing her doctor who has told her about hormone treatments, but she is afraid to try them with all the stories she’s heard about the risks of cancer. She doesn’t like to take pills and she doesn’t see how that would help the problem anyway. Her husband thinks she should try and told her she should come into the doctor’s office to discuss it with him.

Her husband John is a truck driver and has spent most of their married life away from home doing hauls. Recently, he’s been home a lot more, which has been a change for her. She started working for a cleaning agency part-time about five years ago, because she was tired of having no extra cash.

Judy would say that she devoted herself to making a good home for the family. Since their child is grown, she sees herself as being able to pay more attention to her husband when he’s home now, while working part-time to have some extra cash and helping to save money for their retirement.

The patient reports that she thinks her husband knows that sex has become painful for her, although they have not discussed the problem much. He seems to be having “trouble” too (he has been having trouble maintaining erections), but she thinks it’s just because he knows that she’s in pain. Neither one of them really knows why the problem has developed or what to do about it.

# PRESENTATION/EMOTIONAL TONE

Judy is neatly dressed, though comfort is more important in her eyes than fashion. She does not consider herself to be an attractive woman, physically. She has always been uncomfortable talking about sex, even with friends. She would find it hard to describe her sexual relationship with her spouse and how it has changed recently. She would remain somewhat uncomfortable throughout the interview, although a straightforward, concerned, and caring interviewer would relieve some of her embarrassment. As indicated, this discomfort in talking about sex also exists between her and her husband. This new, big “problem” has escalated the distress of the situation, because she thinks that she is causing the problem; she wants to broach the subject, but can’t, and will think of lots of reasons why she can’t. She is very frustrated, embarrassed, and feels guilty about the situation.

The behavior change that may be helpful for this patient could include regular health maintenance, screening exams, and self care.

# SCENARIO DEVELOPMENT

# Judy would be vague about the symptoms at the beginning of the encounter.

She would share that her husband thinks she should ask about hormone replacement therapy, but Judy would be vague about why. Initially she would say things like “we’ve been having trouble,” “we aren’t as comfortable together as we used to be,” or “Things aren’t going so well between us.”

She is uncomfortable talking about sex or the sexual aspects of her relationship with her husband. She would say that she came to the doctor for her husband more than for herself. She has never discussed sex with a doctor before.

It would take some effort on the part of the interviewer to relieve her feelings of embarrassment in talking about sex. She has some long-standing guilt about the sexual relationship. He has always wanted more sex than she was comfortable with and thinks he may have some disappointment about this. Now, she thinks her pain during intercourse may be turning him off and causing his inability to become aroused. She wishes she were different …. Why can’t she enjoy sex like everyone else? She feels guilty, but ambivalent.

# MEDICAL HISTORY

She would report, with hesitation, that sexual intercourse is now very painful. If asked for further information she would describe the sensation as one of being ripped and torn during sex. It has become so painful that she has been avoiding sex, without telling her husband what is wrong. This has caused a change in their sex life because until this problem started, she thought things were okay. (Though her husband has always wanted more sex than she did.)

She has been very healthy and has no major medical issues to speak of. She is frequently tired, but attributes that to the long hours often spent on the job. She would admit that she has a tendency to put other people’s needs first and neglect her own health as a result.

# Description of the symptoms that Judy is experiencing (should the learner ask)

□ A vaginal burning feeling….It comes and goes.
□ Vaginal dryness
□ Light bleeding after sex, not like my period, just slight spotting for a couple of hours
□ No previous pain with sex
□ No previous treatments
□ Pain at entry, a dry feeling, friction, and irritation, also deep pain
□ Difficulty with penetration (made worse by husband’s poor erection)
□ More difficult to get aroused; decreased lubrication; little interest
□ She has tried KY jelly and other lubricants, but nothing has helped.

# PAST MEDICAL HISTORY

General health: Excellent, though she does wish she weren’t so tired. She’s had a few colds this year and gets occasional headaches.

# Menopausal Symptoms that you experienced

□ Overall she experienced mild menopausal symptoms (compared to friends) between ages 48 and 50. She skipped periods on and off for about a year, and had a lighter flow during that time . They seem somewhat better if she drank or splashed with cool water; they often seem worse when she drank alcohol.

# HOSPITALIZATION

Past Surgeries (if appropriate for SP): She has one child and was hospitalized for five days afterward because of a Cesarean. She does not remember exactly why the Cesarean happened…The doctor said the baby’s heart rate was dropping at contractions and didn’t come back up quickly enough.

# MEDICATIONS

Prescription drugs: None.

Over-the-Counter (OTC) drugs: Tylenol, or Advil for headaches. She takes Tums occasionally for heartburn (once a month). She takes $5 0 0 \mathrm { m g }$ of calcium twice a day and a multivitamin, hoping they will help with her energy level. She tends to lean on caffeine and sugar for energy.

Illicit/street drugs: None.

# SEXUAL HISTORY

She didn’t really know what sex entailed until after marriage. She was raised in the rural west  and those things just weren’t discussed in her family. Mother never talked about sex or about menstrual cycles. She learned from friends in high school, though when she got married she realized that her knowledge was incomplete.

Her husband is her only lifetime sexual partner. At first, she was uncomfortable with what he wanted (like oral sex), but he backed off when she expressed her hesitancy. She has tried to enjoy sex because she saw how important it was to her husband, but she was never very enthusiastic about it. Eventually, he stopped bugging her about it so much, so she thought maybe he just didn’t want it as much anymore. He was away so much that there was not much opportunity anyway. She is not sure what an orgasm is and if she’s ever had one. Over the years she learned what to expect from sex and felt that they had a “normal” sex life. She would “give in” about once a month to keep him happy.

Since her husband has been home more, she’s tried to make sure he’s satisfied, even though it’s caused a great deal of discomfort. She sees it as her duty as a wife. Recently, she’s noticed that her husband is not having a full erection and is afraid that maybe he is not aroused because he knows about her pain. The last couple of times they’ve attempted to have sex he was not able to “do it” at all. It was very embarrassing for both. She wanted to talk about it, but just didn’t know how to start.

# LIFESTYLE/HABITS

Alcohol: Occasional wine cooler with a nice dinner. She likes the sweet white wines but doesn’t buy it for herself very often. (once every 3 months).

Tobacco: None.

Caffeine: She has two cups of coffee in the mornings and sometimes one or more in the afternoon.

Diet: She used to cook more often. She still makes casseroles ahead of time. She likes to snack at night, which she knows she shouldn’t do.

Exercise: She would report that she gets plenty of exercise doing the cleaning work, but that’s about it.

OTC/Herbal: Black cohosh/Soy products (to help with Menopause).

Stress: Since her husband has been home more, life has been a bit more stressful. She was used to her own routine without him around and sometimes avoids being around him now for fear that he’ll want to have sex. Life is manageable. She is content with life.

Hobbies: She likes to watch soaps. She also has a card club that meets in rotation at friends’ houses. Her friends are mostly women she met in the neighborhood over the years and when her son played soccer. She’s been getting together with the same people for years. She’s relied on friendship since John was away from home so much. She just started scrap-booking every Tuesday night and is excited about it.

# PERSONAL/FAMILY HISTORY

She went into the Army reserves when she was 18, right out of high school. Didn’t like the Army and got out quickly, then got married to her high school sweetheart. This all happened over the course of a year when she was 19. Her husband has been employed at B & G Trucking Company for about 30 years. She would say that she has been devoted to making a good home for the family. Work is not very important to her; she’d quit if John wanted her to.

Although she does not volunteer the information, if asked in a supportive manner, she would express concern about the influence of this problem on their marriage. She feels that she is failing in a critical aspect of her role as a wife. She is someone who has been satisfied to devote herself to others. She thinks there may be something wrong with her because she’s never really enjoyed sex anyway.

She wanted to have more children but was never able to get pregnant again. She thought about getting it checked but just never did. She adores her son. John sometimes accused her of babying him. She thinks he was just jealous!

Marriage: She was married at 19. She had been thinking about going to college but wanted a family and children, and did not think that both were reasonable.

Children: She has one wonderful child. He is all grown, and dating a rather possessive young woman (in her opinion). She doesn’t see him as much as she’d like. She never talks with her son about sex. If asked, she would say that she assumes John talked to him about sex.

Education: High School

Occupation: She works for a cleaning company that a friend owns. She started working when her son moved away. She didn’t feel comfortable trying to get into a complicated career at that age, and this kind of fell into her lap. Her friend is glad because she is reliable and thorough. She doesn’t mind the work. Usually it is quiet because she cleans small office buildings or churches after hours. She gets paid by the job, rather than by the hour, so if things are done quickly she can get home early. She tries not to work more than three days a week, but has helped out more on occasion.

Typical day: She used to have a nice routine before John was home so much, but now she’s had to re-adjust. She spends much of the day doing things at home or running errands. She and John often have lunch together before she leaves to do her cleaning.

Lately, work hours have been in the evening, from $6 { : } 0 0 \mathrm { p m }$ to $1 1 { : } 0 0 \mathrm { p m }$ , after the offices are closed. It is mostly light cleaning (wiping down the glass doors, taking out garbage, vacuuming, dusting, bathrooms, replenishing supplies…). Twice a month there is more extensive cleaning (shampooing rugs, washing walls, cleaning windows inside…). She doesn’t like these evening hours and she is planning to be placed on another job. There is a church job available that she’d much rather do.

# MILITARY INTERVIEW THIS INTERVIEW IS TO BE USED AS A SUPPLEMENT TO THE DETAILS ALREADY PROVIDED IN THE CASE. IT IS NOT MEANT TO BE MEMORIZED

1. What branch of the military did you serve in? US Army Reserves.

2. How long? 1 year right out of high school. What rank? E-1. Who would you report to? Staff Sergeant Scott was my boss. Who reports to you? No one. Where were you stationed? Basic training, Great Fort Leonard Wood; tech school was also there; then returned home for the monthly training.

3. Describe the physical work environment. Kitchen—hot, wet, slippery conditions.

4. Please describe a typical day (travel, hours worked, tasks given, etc). One weekend a month we had drill and would work in the kitchen on base.

5. How did this profession affect your ability to live a healthy lifestyle (exercise, sleep, nutrition)? It helped me learn about foods and cooking.

6. Are there over the counter medications, alternative medications that were typically used in your profession (weight loss, energy drinks, tobacco, steroids, etc.)? Multivitamins, calcium $5 0 0 \mathrm { m g }$ twice daily, Tylenol or

Advil for headaches, Tums for heartburn, caffeine/sugar for energy boosts.

7. What are things that your friends find interesting about military service (like how often did you shoot your gun?)? What types of questions do you get asked by the public? I don’t really talk about it much. It was so long ago and for such a short time that most of my friends don't even know that I was in the military.

8. How did you get interested in Military Service? I was interested in getting out of my small town and seeing what was out there, but ended up feeling too uncomfortable in the military.

9. What did you like most about your profession? It taught me about healthy eating.

10. What did you dislike most about your job? Trying to make meals in large enough quantities to feed the entire unit but still making them healthy.

11. Educational background: Graduated high school. Vocational school or College, Education Benefits? None.

12. Special training, Boot camp? Boot camp was a couple of months long; they just yell a lot and try to break you down and reform you in their image of a team player

13. What were the physical and psychological qualifications/testing required? To get into the military you have to pass a written test. Each job has its own cutoff score to be eligible. The higher your score the better job you can get. You have to pass a basic background check

14. What positions have you had since you started this profession? After finishing the Army cooking school, I went back home to serve my one weekend a month, but got out soon after meeting my husband.

15. Can you get promoted? If so, to what position and how? When are you done with your service? You can get promoted in the military. It is based on how long you have been in your present rank. For enlisted folks they are listed as E1 through E9. Promotions are also based on test scores you achieve showing job proficiency.

16. What are the "perks" of military service? I was in the reserves and got paid for my one weekend per month.

17. How did you get paid (contract, per mile, per hour, etc.)? How could you make more money? You get paid a monthly salary, money for food—basic allowance for subsistence (BAS), a housing allowance if living off base or use of a house on base if you have a family or a dorm room if you live by yourself. In the reserves your pay is prorated for the few days you work a month.

18. What was the most stressful part of your job? Trying to get all the meals ready on time for a big group.

19. How was it taxing psychologically? Just being around all the men was intimidating.

20. How free were you to discuss your political views and opinions? You have the right to speak your view and opinion. You cannot give speeches or campaign for candidates while in your uniform.

21. What emotional problems were common in people in your profession?

Similar to the civilian counterparts, interpersonal conflicts, work fatigue, motivation.

22. What type of physical activity was required in your position? Lifting boxes of food, large pots/pans. But to pass the PT test I had to work out—running, doing sit ups and push-ups and I really did not like that.

23. What things happened that really made you angry? I don't really get angry very often.

24. What other professions did you work with, and how did they affect your (make your job easier or harder)? I worked with just about everyone in the military as most people wanted to be friends with those that fixed their food.

25. What was it like to re-enter civilian life? It was a relief to be out of the military.

26. What "health risk" behaviors were common in your profession (tobacco, alcohol, specific drugs, sex, stress, marital or family discord, etc.)? A lot of the folks smoked and drank when I joined the military, but that wasn't for me. The biggest concern was eating so much with all that food around.

27. How did your vocation affect the personal life of you or people you know? Well in the military you have to be ready to leave everyone and everything at a moment’s notice. Sometimes you don't know how long you will be gone and sometimes you cannot tell people where you are going or what you are going to be doing.

28. What activities and/or hobbies did people in your profession like to do? Most of the people are younger and involved in everything from sports, to camping, to school, to taking care of families for the married folks.

29. Are there any items of clothing, or props that someone portraying a person in your profession would use consistently (hats, keys, stuff you’d keep in your pockets or bags, day planners, work boots, particular “costume”, or types of clothing…)? No specific props needed.

30. If a person in your position were to get discharged, what would they most likely have done to justify involuntary termination and what would the steps involve? To get separated involuntarily is fairly hard to do in most cases. Sometimes though there are cutbacks like in any job and anyone can be let go. If you break rules, there are rules in place—kind of like when you have a union job—which must be followed to let you go. If you break laws, then you can be dealt with just like in the civilian world with fines, probation, and jail time.

31. What type of jargon would be common for someone in this line of service? …words they use that are “lingo” specific to the profession. Every branch of the military has its use of acronyms. Basic training is boot camp. Then there is your job training (tech school for Navy and Air Force, AIT for Army). DOS is date of separation. Chow hall is the dining facility.
