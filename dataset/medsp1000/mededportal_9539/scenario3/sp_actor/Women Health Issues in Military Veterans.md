# JUDY WILSON SEXUAL DYSFUNCTION STANDARDIZED PATIENT TRAINING MATERIALS

# CASE SUMMARY

Judy Wilson is a married woman over 50. She is coming in at this time because over the past $1 { - } 1 \%$ years, sexual intercourse has become very painful for her. This has significantly influenced the couple’s relationship. She has been seeing her doctor who has told her about hormone treatments, but she is afraid to try them with all the stories she’s heard about the risks of cancer. She doesn’t like to take pills and she doesn’t see how that would help the problem anyway. Her husband thinks she should try and told her she should come into the doctor’s office to discuss it with him.

Her husband John is a truck driver and has spent most of their married life away from home doing hauls. Recently, he’s been home a lot more, which has been a change for her. She started working for a cleaning agency part-time about five years ago, because she was tired of having no extra cash.

Judy would say that she devoted herself to making a good home for the family. Since their child is grown, she sees herself as being able to pay more attention to her husband when he’s home now, while working part-time to have some extra cash and helping to save money for their retirement.

The patient reports that she thinks her husband knows that sex has become painful for her, although they have not discussed the problem much. He seems to be having “trouble” too (he has been having trouble maintaining erections), but she thinks it’s just because he knows that she’s in pain. Neither one of them really knows why the problem has developed or what to do about it.

(More in Sexual History)

# PRESENTATION/EMOTIONAL TONE

Judy is neatly dressed, though comfort is more important in her eyes than fashion. She does not consider herself to be an attractive woman, physically. She has always been uncomfortable talking about sex, even with friends. She would find it hard to describe her sexual relationship with her spouse and how it has changed recently. She would remain somewhat uncomfortable throughout the interview, although a straightforward, concerned, and caring interviewer would relieve some of her embarrassment. As indicated, this discomfort in talking about sex also exists between her and her husband. This new, big “problem” has escalated the distress of the situation, because she thinks that she is causing the problem; she wants to broach the subject, but can’t, and will think of lots of reasons why she can’t. She is very frustrated, embarrassed, and feels guilty about the situation.

The behavior change that may be helpful for this patient could include regular health maintenance, screening exams, and self care.

# SCENARIO DEVELOPMENT

# Judy would be vague about the symptoms at the beginning of the encounter.

She would share that her husband thinks she should ask about hormone replacement therapy, but Judy would be vague about why. Initially she would say things like “we’ve been having trouble,” “we aren’t as comfortable together as we used to be,” or “Things aren’t going so well between us.”

She is uncomfortable talking about sex or the sexual aspects of her relationship with her husband. She would say that she came to the doctor for her husband more than for herself. She has never discussed sex with a doctor before.

It would take some effort on the part of the interviewer to relieve her feelings of embarrassment in talking about sex. She has some long-standing guilt about the sexual relationship. He has always wanted more sex than she was comfortable with and thinks he may have some disappointment about this. Now, she thinks her pain during intercourse may be turning him off and causing his inability to become aroused. She wishes she were different …. Why can’t she enjoy sex like everyone else? She feels guilty, but ambivalent.

# MEDICAL HISTORY

This has caused a change in their sex life because until this problem started, she thought things were okay. (Though her husband has always wanted more sex than she did.)

She has been very healthy and has no major medical issues to speak of. She is frequently tired, but attributes that to the long hours often spent on the job. She would admit that she has a tendency to put other people’s needs first and neglect her own health as a result.

# SEXUAL HISTORY

She didn’t really know what sex entailed until after marriage. She was raised in the rural west and those things just weren’t discussed in her family. Mother never talked about sex or about menstrual cycles. She learned from friends in high school, though when she got married she realized that her knowledge was incomplete.

Her husband is her only lifetime sexual partner. At first, she was uncomfortable with what he wanted (like oral sex), but he backed off when she expressed her hesitancy. She has tried to enjoy sex because she saw how important it was to her husband, but she was never very enthusiastic about it. Eventually, he stopped bugging her about it so much, so she thought maybe he just didn’t want it as much anymore. He was away so much that there was not much opportunity anyway. She is not sure what an orgasm is and if she’s ever had one. Over the years she learned what to expect from sex and felt that they had a “normal” sex life. She would “give in” about once a month to keep him happy.

Since her husband has been home more, she’s tried to make sure he’s satisfied, even though it’s caused a great deal of discomfort. She sees it as her duty as a wife. Recently, she’s noticed that her husband is not having a full erection and is afraid that maybe he is not aroused because he knows about her pain.
