**Document 1** is the actual case document and should be studied by the standardized patient, encounter facilitator, and encounter grader prior to the encounter taking place. This document should be used for all standardized patient training as a way to ensure accuracy of case portrayal. **Document 2** is the medical chart which should be presented to the student immediately prior to entering the exam room for the encounter. The standardized patient should have a blood pressure finding card, prescription medication card, and over the counter medication card on their person during the exam. If the student asks for any of that information during the exam, the Standardized Patient is to hand the student the card with the information on it. These cards are listed as **Documents 3, 4, and 5** to be downloaded for this case. **Document 6** is the documentation or SOAP note sheet. This should be used by the student following the exam room encounter for SOAP note documentation. The individual assigned to grade this case will use **Documents 7 and 8** to grade the student’s performance on the case in the exam room and on the SOAP note. Finally, **Document 9** will be used by the standardized patient to grade the student’s performance from a humanistic perspective after the encounter has ended.

**Purpose**

The purpose of the Henry Corbett standardized patient case is to challenge students to manage a common geriatric presentation as part of a capstone examination. This encounter is one of several encounters constituting the Clinical Skills Competency Exam. Students conduct a focused history and physical exam to assess contributing causes of lightheadedness in an elderly patient and document the encounter using SOAP note format. The educational objectives of the encounter are to measure students’ ability to do a focused history & physical exam on a patient presenting with lightheadedness, to assess students' ability to identify causes of lightheadedness, and to assess students' ability to recognize orthostatic hypotension secondary to medications.

**Reflection / Relative Value**

The Henry Corbett standardized patient case holds great value in medical education. With the growing number of geriatric patients seeking health care, it is imperative that medical students gain the necessary knowledge to recognize, understand and treat common geriatric ailments. On account of lightheadedness being so common in a geriatric patient, this case provides a strong foundation for students to build on. Providing a focused history and physical exam, coupled with the presenting issue in this case, will no doubt be something that these students face later on as medical professionals.

**Conceptual Background**

The Henry Corbett standardized patient case was developed at the University of Medicine and Dentistry of New Jersey’s –School of Osteopathic Medicine (UMDNJ-SOM) to be 1 of 6 stations in the Clinical Skills Comprehensive Examination (CSCE). The CSCE takes place at the end of medical students’ Year III course work. Students must earn an overall passing grade on the exam to go on to their fourth year (MS IV) of training. The Henry Corbett case was developed by faculty members from the New Jersey Institute for Successful Aging (NJISA) at UMDNJ-SOM, and Sima C. Bennett, MPH, RD., Director of the Clinical Education & Assessment Center at UMDNJ-SOM. The infusion of a geriatric based case into the exam is of great importance for the growing need for improved care of the elderly. Experts in the field of geriatrics and standardized patient case development played a major role from initial planning to completion of the project. Numerous planning, brainstorming, case development, and implementation meetings took place prior to the completion and implementation of the Henry Corbett case.

**Practical Implementation**

The Henry Corbett standardized patient encounter is limited to 14 minutes in the actual exam room. Students then have an additional 9 minutes to work on their SOAP note following the encounter. The standardized patient should have a blood pressure finding card, prescription medication card, and over the counter medication card on their person during the exam. If the student asks for any of that information during the exam, the standardized patient is to hand the student the card with the information on it. The case is recorded and later graded by a member of the University with expertise in the topic area. In situations where the recording equipment is not available, the faculty evaluator needs to be able to view the case interaction in real time from the outside of the exam room. The standardized patient is required to fill out the “Standardized Patient Checklist” immediately after their encounter with the student doctor only.

**Successful deployment**

The case has been used in both the 2011 and 2012 Clinical Skills Competency Exams at UMDNJ-SOM. In both exams, no major pitfalls can be reported. A helpful suggestion when carrying out this case is to make sure the standardized patient has been trained well, has memorized their role, and that practice runs are conducted multiple times prior to the beginning of the usage of the case. **Limitations**

There are few inherent limitations that arise with the delivery of this case. Due to the age requirement of the standardized patient for this case, it must be mentioned that the SP may be unavailable at certain times, or indefinitely, due to their own age related health issues. Also, in a situation where large numbers of students must go through a standardized patient case, the geriatric SP may need longer amounts of rest in between sessions. There is a need to train multiple SPs to portray the same roll accurately in case there is a circumstance where the main SP portraying the case is unavailable. Careful attention must be paid to training all SPs to portray the case in the same way to ensure reliability. Leading up to the delivery of this case all SPs are required to attend multiple training sessions/refresher courses about portrayals of their particular case. There is continued quality control measures put in place by the clinical skills lab to ensure the case is portrayed correctly and that the SPs are able to accurately do their job. The faculty evaluators, skills lab director, and departmental researchers all review the cases to ensure validity and reliability prior to the case running again in the following year’s exam. Currently, the Henry Corbett standardized patient case will run again in the up and coming Clinical Skills Comprehensive Exam in the same format presented in 2013.
