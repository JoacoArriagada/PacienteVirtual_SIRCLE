# Acting Intern On Call Simulations: Simulation Set-up and Preparation

This document provides content based on the MedEdPortal simulation template.

# Human Patient Simulation

1. Title: “Acting Intern On Call”

2. Target Audience: $4 ^ { \mathrm { t h } }$ year medical students, Acting Interns

3. Learning Objectives

1. Primary

1. The student will experience and demonstrate increased assumption of clinical responsibility for patient care, similar to those responsibilities of an intern on call.   
2. The student will demonstrate advanced clinical thinking, including the independent development of differential diagnoses, evaluation and management plans appropriate to the clinical scenarios.   
3. The student will appraise his/her decision-making skills in the initial management of patients with acute-onset undifferentiated problems.

2. Secondary

1. Experience on call stress through the simulation   
2. Recognize the importance of good communication during patient hand-off at sign-out   
3. Properly evaluate the acute issue by obtaining an adequate history, performing targeted physical exam, reviewing the sign-out sheet, and correctly interpreting vital signs, lab results, EKG and chest x-ray.   
4. Prioritize diagnoses, develop action plan, and commit to a course of action to stabilize the patient

3. Critical actions checklist

1. Conduct brief history and physical exam, with re-evaluation as indicated   
2. Determine level of stability   
3. Properly order and interpret studies   
4. Correctly diagnose the underlying problems   
5. Give appropriate orders to the Nurse

4. Environment

1. Lab Set Up: hospital floor bed

3. Props: EGKs (provided with documents), x-rays (not provided), sign-out sheet (provided), nursing response cards (provided), folders (not provided)

i. We have 3 folders, one for each simulation (see “Photos of folders”) ii. The baseline information is featured on the front of the folder iii. The folder contains a handout with the Nurse’s response for that case (see “Nurses responses for all three cases”) iv. The folder contains a copy of the EKG (laminated) v. We use actual x-ray film that the students place on a viewer.

Distractors: monitor alarms as appropriate based on vital signs

5. Actors—the scenarios are run in groups of 1-3 medical students

1. Roles: Intern on call, nurse, patient, observer (if available)

2. Who may play them:

i. Intern on call—medical student #1   
ii. Nurse—medical student #2 or faculty or simulation technician; iii. Patient—simulation technician;   
iv. Observer—medical student #3 (if available)

3. Action Role:

i. Intern—decision-maker   
ii. Nurse—relays patient-related information to intern   
iii. Patient—relays information to intern, may increase stress level of intern;   
iv. Observer—provides feedback during debriefing

# Case Narratives

We present three cases sequentially. Scenarios are limited to 10 minutes. In a group of three medical students, each student will assume the role of intern on call. Students are instructed that they are to approach this situation in the role of a medical intern on call in the hospital. While a senior resident is “in house” they will be required to make decisions regarding the problem to which they are called on their own until back-up arrives. If they have medical reference tools available they may use them. They are not permitted to receive suggestions or recommendations from other students. The intern may order labs, tests, medications or therapies. Some results may return before the 10 minute scenario ends. They will be provided these results as they become available. They are told that certain studies, such as CT scans, would not be available before the end of the 10 minutes, however.

Students are introduced to the mannequin and its capabilities. They are given opportunity to listen to normal breath sounds, heart sounds, and palpate pulses.
D. Debriefing Plan a. Method of debriefing

i. Group debriefing. After the scenarios the instructor meets with the medical students to discuss how the scenarios made them feel, what they did well and could have done better, and what they wished they had known or had available to them. The Instructor provides feedback about the students’ performances and makes recommendations for how to consider approaching a similar scenario in the future.

1. Rules for the debriefing. Students are asked to critique their own performance first, then other students provide feedback, ending with faculty feedback. Students grades (e.g. Honors, High Pass, Pass, Fail) are not affected by their performance. Refusal or lack of participation is potential criteria for course failure, however. Students are required to complete the simulation.

2. Questions to facilitate the debriefing.

a. How do you think you did?   
b. What did you do well or right?   
c. What do you think you could have done better?   
d. What do you think the underlying problem was?   
e. What was your rationale or thought processes at this point?

ii. Individual debriefing. Medical students are required to complete a typed 3- question post-simulation debriefing survey:

1. Which simulation did you experience?   
2. How did the simulation affect you (e.g. emotionally)? What impact did it have on you?   
3. As you consider the simulation experience, identify which pieces of knowledge, skills, or attitudes you would like to acquire for when you encounter this scenario in residency. Be as specific as possible.   
a. Number of participants. These scenarios have been embedded as part of the fourth year family medicine acting internship since 2007. 95 students have participated in the simulations.   
b. Performance expectations, anticipated managements mistakes. i. In our experience we expect a group of students to perform along a bell-shaped curve. The majority of students will make no serious mistakes while arriving at the correct diagnosis, and some of those will also providing correct medication management. A smaller number of students bring the scenario to resolution confidently. A smaller number on the other side of the curve do not know what to do and require prompting on even basic management items such as interviewing the Patient or Nurse, or make a decision that is fatal in the case. ii. We anticipate that as participants are further along in their fourth year of medical school they will be able to handle the scenarios in a more confident manner as compared to their peers who took the course earlier in the year. This has not been the case, however. In fact, we have seen some students toward the latter half of their fourth year who struggled greatly with the scenarios.

c. Evaluation. Since 2007 the majority of participating medical students have consistently rated the simulation experience among the most valuable aspects of the acting internship.

i. 2008-2009: 16 $( 8 0 \% )$ out of 20 students rated it as among MOST valuable. 0 rated it among LEAST useful   
ii. 2009-2010: 12 $( 7 1 \% )$ out of 17 students rated it as among MOST valuable. 1 rated it as among LEAST valuable.   
iii. 2010-2011: 13 out of 19 $( 6 8 \% )$ students rated it as among the MOST valuable. 1 student rated it among the LEAST useful.

# Simulation #3: Hypoglycemic Seizure

Scenario Background Information: for faculty and simulation technician only

This is a case of a 79 year old man admitted for a right hip fracture. He has a history of diabetes for which he takes oral hypoglycemic, but this is not noted on the sign-out sheet and is not conveyed during sign-out. Sliding scale insulin is noted on the sign-out sheet. The patient starts to become drowsy and notifies the nurse by call button. The nurse has recently changed shifts and finds him in a seizure when she enters the room. The prior nurse had given the patient his sliding scale insulin but at a much higher dose than was supposed to be administered. The patient is having a hypoglycemic seizure. The patient is not on a monitor and the Intern or Nurse will be required to place the leads if a heart monitor is desired.

Ideal outcomes: The Intern will order a glucose level as one of the first steps, determine hypoglycemia as the cause of the seizure, and provide medications to increase the serum glucose. The Intern provides an Amp of D50 and begins D5 in IV fluids to maintain blood glucose.

Special need: Locate a normal chest x-ray.

C. Instructors Notes

a. Tips to keep the scenario flowing. Remind Intern that the patient continues to have a seizure if the mannequin does not simulate a seizure.

b. Tips to direct actors.

i. Remind Nurse & Observer that they are not allowed to provide tips or suggestions.

ii. When Intern orders a medication class, the instructor asks the Intern to provide the name of a medication, strength, and route of delivery. Use of electronic or written references is encouraged, if Intern has brought these.

iii. Instructor provides the x-ray or prompts the Nurse to provide the EKG (or other lab results) if Nurse is not providing them upon request.

iv. If Intern asks for EKG the instructor asks the Intern and Nurse to place the leads in proper position on the mannequin, providing guidance if needed.

v. Scenario programming.

1. Optimal management path.

a. Intern reviews sign-out and recognizes that the patient is admitted for hip fracture. Intern may also notice that the patient is receiving sliding scale insulin (SSI) and NPH insulin.   
b. Intern interviews Patient and learns that he is unconscious.   
c. Intern interviews the Nurse and is provided with current vitals and documentation from prior notes in the chart that do not provide any indication for current seizure.   
d. Intern conducts brief physical exam and auscultates a regular rhythm as well as clear chest.   
e. Intern calls senior resident for help. Places order to move patient to Intermediate Care Unit. Places order to increase oxygen flow.
