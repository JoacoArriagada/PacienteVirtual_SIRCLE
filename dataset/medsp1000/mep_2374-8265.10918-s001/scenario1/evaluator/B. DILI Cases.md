# Case 1

**HPI:** A 60-year-old woman is brought in by her family with jaundice and confusion. She had been well until 3 days prior to presentation, when she complained of nausea and abdominal pain. Her family assumed the nausea was a side effect of a medication she was recently prescribed for a shoulder injury. The injury occurred when she fell at home while intoxicated. The family notes that she drinks alcohol regularly, usually 4-5 glasses of wine daily, but sometimes more on special occasions.

Past medical/surgical history: unknown

Medications: Her family brings in her pill bottles. She was prescribed 120 tablets of hydrocodone 5mg/acetaminophen 325mg one week ago, and only 2 tablets are still in the bottle. She also has prescriptions for Lisinopril and metoprolol.

**Exam:**

General - she is confused and drowsy, unable to respond to questions.

Vitals - Her temperature is 37 C, heart rate 90 bpm, respiratory rate 23 bpm, BP 100/70, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.

HEENT - She is jaundiced, with scleral icterus.

Heart and lung exam are normal.

Abdomen - good bowel sounds, nondistended, moderately tender to palpation in the right upper quadrant.

Laboratory testing reveals an AST of 5000 IU/L, ALT 4500 IU/L, bilirubin 7 mg/dL, INR 1.9. Her acetaminophen level is 20 micrograms per liter.
