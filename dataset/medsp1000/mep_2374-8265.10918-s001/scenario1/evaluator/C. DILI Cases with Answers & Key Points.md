# Case 1

**HPI: A 60-year-old woman is brought in by her family with jaundice and confusion. She had been well until 3 days prior to presentation, when she complained of nausea and abdominal pain. Her family assumed the nausea was a side effect of a medication she was recently prescribed for a shoulder injury. The injury occurred when she fell at home while intoxicated. The family notes that she drinks alcohol regularly, usually 4-5 glasses of wine daily, but sometimes more on special occasions.**

**Past medical/surgical history: unknown**

**Medications: Her family brings in her pill bottles. She was prescribed 120 tablets of hydrocodone 5mg/acetaminophen 325mg one week ago, and only 2 tablets are still in the bottle. She also has prescriptions for Lisinopril and metoprolol.**

**Exam:**

**General - she is confused and drowsy, unable to respond to questions.**

**Vitals - Her temperature is 37 C, heart rate 90 bpm, respiratory rate 23 bpm, BP 100/70, oxygen saturation 96% on room air.**

**HEENT - She is jaundiced, with scleral icterus.**

**Heart and lung exam are normal.**

**Abdomen - good bowel sounds, nondistended, moderately tender to palpation in the right upper quadrant.**

**Laboratory testing reveals an AST of 5000 IU/L, ALT 4500 IU/L, bilirubin 7 mg/dL, INR 1.9. Her acetaminophen level is 20 micrograms per liter.**

**Question 1: Which of her prescription medications is most responsible for her liver failure?**

The acetaminophen is the most likely cause of her acute liver failure.

**Question 2: On a biochemical level, how does chronic alcohol use make acetaminophen more toxic?**

People who regularly consume alcohol tend to have depleted glutathione stores. They also tend to have induced P450 enzymes (particularly CYP2E1) that lead to accelerated development of NAPQI and toxic metabolites as acetaminophen is metabolized.

**Question 3: What medication might help avoid transplant, and how does it help?** N-Acetylcysteine is used to provide the cysteine that liver cells need to make glutathione. The glutathione “detoxifies” NAPQI by converting it to mercapturic acid.

Unfortunately, in this case the patient may have presented too late for the NAC to be effective. Students should appreciate that this patient has fallen outside of the window where her acetaminophen level is no longer high, and the bulk of the ingestion likely happened prior to three days ago, when she started experiencing symptoms. Although the NAC may no longer be effective, most of us would still give it. In addition, she should be transferred to a transplant center where her acute liver failure can be managed as carefully as possible.

This case represents acute liver failure as a consequence of a drug-induced liver injury. When we categorize the severity of this injury, it would be 5+, which includes mortality or need for liver transplant. Not all drugs cause acute liver failure, as we will discuss in the next cases.
