# Case 2

**HPI:** A 25-year-old bodybuilder presents to your outpatient clinic with jaundice. This has been gradually progressive over the last several weeks. He denies any recent viral illness symptoms. Denies abdominal pain. In addition, He complains that he has lost 10 lbs in the past three months.

**PMH:** none - He has been in good health most of his life.

**PSHx:** none

**Social history:** No new sexual contacts. He works out six days a week at a small neighborhood gym. He does not drink alcohol, smoke cigarettes or use recreational drugs.

**Medications:** He takes no medications regularly. Denies acetaminophen. He has been using a steroid sold to him by a friend from his gym for the past six months.

**Dietary:** He drinks two protein shakes daily, prepared with a commercial powder he purchases from a major nutritional supplement store.

**Exam:**

**General - Appears healthy but jaundiced.**

**Vitals - Temp 36.9 C, HR 65, RR 16, BP 130/80.**

**His heart and lung exam are normal.**

**Abdomen - soft, nondistended, good bowel sounds, nontender. Liver appears to be of normal size and contour. Spleen is not palpable.**

**Laboratory testing reveals a normal complete blood count and basic metabolic panel. His AST and ALT are normal, but bilirubin is 25 mg/dL. His INR is 1.1.**

**Question 1: Use the website** [**https://livertox.nlm.nih.gov**](https://livertox.nlm.nih.gov) **(using generic names of drugs) to learn more about causes of bland cholestasis. What is the typical pattern of liver test abnormalities with anabolic steroids like this?**

Much information is available on the LiverTox website. According to the website, the diagnostic criteria for bland cholestasis are:

**Criteria for Definition.** Elements important in diagnosis of bland cholestasis due to medications include:

1. Minimal elevations in ALT (<200 U/L or <5 times ULN) and alkaline phosphatase (<230 U/L or <2 times ULN)
2. Latency of 4 to 24 weeks
3. Insidious onset of symptoms of pruritus and jaundice
4. Bilirubin >2.5 mg/dL
5. Slow recovery of more than 4 weeks
6. Exposure to an agent known to cause bland cholestasis (anabolic steroids, estrogens)
7. If liver biopsy is obtained, changes of intrahepatic cholestasis with minimal inflammation or hepatocellular necrosis.

**Question 2: Describe the clinical course for this type of liver injury after the drug is withdrawn.**

If the drug is withdrawn, most patients’ cholestasis slowly resolves. It can take weeks to months to fully resolve.

**Question 3: What other liver problems can this drug cause? (list at least two)**

Peliosis hepatis (or chronic vascular injury), induction of hepatic tumors (adenoma and hepatocellular carcinoma), and a mild acute hepatitis. Rarely they can cause nodular regenerative hyperplasia or vanishing bile duct syndrome.

**Question 4: How would you counsel this patient regarding supplement use in the future?**

He should avoid not only unlabeled medications sold at the gym, but also most dietary supplements. It’s important to point out that many supplements contain controlled substances, but this is difficult to predict, as they are unregulated. People commonly believe that supplements sold in a health food store or prescribed by an herbalist or nutritionist are safe, but they are unregulated and commonly implicated in DILI.
