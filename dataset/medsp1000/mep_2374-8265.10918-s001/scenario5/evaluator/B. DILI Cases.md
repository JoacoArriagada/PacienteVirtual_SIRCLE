# Case 5

**HPI: A 27-year-old man presented with itching followed by jaundice, anorexia, and nausea that has been worsening for two weeks. He has had two similar presentations in the past year. Each time, he has recovered without treatment.**

**PMHx:** history of hepatitis C that was treated successfully

**Social history: Occasionally uses recreational marijuana, cocaine, and methamphetamine. Does not drink alcohol. Is sexually active with male and female partners.**

**Family history:** no liver diseases

**Medications:** Occasional over-the-counter ibuprofen use. He admits to using multiple supplements purchased from friends at the gym, including “fat burners,” "testosterone boosters,” and “diet pills.” He admits to taking these substances intermittently over the past year, although he is uncertain of the timeline. He does recall taking them prior to each episode of jaundice and stopping them when he didn’t feel well, then starting to use them again at some point after he felt well. In addition, he has been taking Unisom for a month, and he started varenicline three weeks prior to admission; he also had taken varenicline for a short time 5 months prior to presentation, which was just prior to his last admission with similar symptoms.

**Exam:**

General - he appears jaundiced and mildly uncomfortable

Vitals - Temp 37, HR 75, RR 20, BP 125/85

HEENT - scleral icterus present

CV - normal.

Lungs – clear to auscultation bilaterally.

Abdomen – Soft, nondistended, normal bowel sounds, mild right upper quadrant tenderness, no organomegaly.

Neuro – no asterixis, mental status exam normal

Laboratory testing reveals a normal complete blood count, creatinine of 1.2 mg/dL, AST 400 IU/L, ALT 450 IU/L, bilirubin 8.0 mg/dL, alkaline phosphatase 250 IU/L. INR is normal.

Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) is normal, with no biliary dilation, stones, or strictures. Serologic testing for autoimmune hepatitis, syphilis, Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), and hepatitis A, B, and C are negative. Ultrasound with doppler shows no vascular thrombi. His liver tests improve gradually over 7 days.
