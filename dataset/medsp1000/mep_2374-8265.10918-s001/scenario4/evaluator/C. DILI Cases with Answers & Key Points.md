# Case 4

**HPI: A 58-year-old man presents with muscle pain and dark urine. He was in his usual state of health until a week prior to presentation, when he started taking a medicine for Candida esophagitis. His odynophagia has improved.**

**PMHx:** He has high cholesterol and mild chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

PSHx: none

**Social History**: no alcohol or illicit drugs. Quit smoking 3 years ago.

**Family history**: noncontributory

**Medications:** metoprolol, aspirin 81mg daily, simvastatin 80mg daily, inhaled budesonide, and allopurinol. Just started fluconazole 200mg daily.

**Exam:**

**Genera - he appears mildly uncomfortable.**

**Vitals - Temp 37.5, HR 89, RR 20, BP 130/80.**

**HEENT - no scleral icterus, some oral thrush.**

**CV - normal.**

**Lungs - decreased breath sounds bilaterally with a prolonged expiratory phase.**

**Abdomen - nondistended abdomen, normal bowel sounds, mild right upper quadrant tenderness, no organomegaly.**

**Laboratory testing reveals a normal complete blood count, creatinine of 1.7 mg/dL (baseline 1.0), AST 400 IU/L, ALT 450 IU/L, bilirubin 1.0 mg/dL, alkaline phosphatase 150 IU/L. INR is normal. Urinalysis shows elevated hemoglobin without red blood cells.**

**Use an online drug-drug interaction checker to search his list of medications for potential interactions. You can use any resource you choose. Lexicomp is available through Up-to-Date. Medscape has a free interaction checker as well.**

**Question 1: Which drug-drug interaction is most likely to have caused this problem?**

The interaction of concern is between simvastatin and fluconazole. While other interactions may be found that are less severe, it is important that students can differentiate the most likely culprits from less severe interactions.

**Question 2: What is the mechanism of the drug-drug interaction?**

Fluconazole inhibits CYP3A4. This inhibits the metabolism of simvastatin, increasing the risk of side effects (such as hepatotoxicity and rhabdomyolysis).

In general, for idiosynchratic DILI, the dose of the drug is not very important, although hepatotoxicity may be more common for drugs administered at doses greater than 50mg per day.3 However, this is not true for statins, and the dose and half-life of the drug is important when considering drug-drug interactions. In this example, when fluconazole inhibits CYP3A4, the half-life of simvastatin is prolonged, so the cumulative dose is increased every day when the drug is taken. This effect is not as pronounced for drugs with shorter half-lives.

**Question 3: What is the etiology of his dark urine, elevated creatinine, and urinalysis findings?**

He has rhabdomyolysis. His statin drug reached toxic levels when he started the fluconazole. The presence of hemoglobin without red cells in the urine should lead students to suspect rhabdomyolysis.

In the immediate setting, we treat rhabdomyolysis with copious IV fluids. We correct any acid/base or electrolyte derangements. We would hold the simvastatin and fluconazole while he recovers.

In the longer-term, fluconazole could again be used for his Candida esophagitis. However, his simvastatin should be held for the duration of the treatment. To prevent future Candida infections, he should avoid swallowing the steroid from his inhaler (should be educated on proper technique, perhaps using a spacer).
