**APPENDIX C**

**Practice Care of the Dying Older Adult**

**Hybrid Simulation – Standardized Patient/SIM Man**

**Debriefing Guide**

Introduction:

1. Team members present (learners, faculty, SP wives and nurse) introduction
2. Safe space assurance
3. Recognize the emotional intensity of the encounter
4. Purpose
   1. Feedback from learners to faculty and SPs
   2. Formative from faculty, wife and nurse

Question guide:

1. How did you feel? Was this hard for you?
2. Have you gone through similar experiences? If so, what was your role?
3. When did you recognize he was dying? Why?
4. Reflect on what you learned
5. What would you do differently if in a similar situation in the future?

Discussion points for faculty and SPs to cover:

* Pain meds
  + Less concern about causing respiratory depression and more duty to give appropriate amounts of pain medications as he has been on chronic opioids.
  + Second intent: With goal of comfort measures only, if high doses of opioids are necessary for pain relief, the side effect of respiratory depression is ethically acceptable.
  + Do not make the wife feel guilty regarding the possibility of pain medications causing respiratory depression.
  + Given his inability to swallow, IV or buccal meds are best… has an IV so use it
* Confirmation of goals, including DNAR
  + The expectation of hospice enrollment is the patient will die at home without coming back to the hospital. If the patient presents to the Emergency Department, it is appropriate to ask if they have changed their mind about hospice (sometimes they have and sometimes they have not.)
  + The utility of clinical testing is limited if the goal is comfort
* Body language
  + Physical presence is important in emotional situations--it does not have to be touch though many people respond well to a gentle touch on the arm or back.
  + Recommend
    - Mirror the wife’s body language
    - Be on the same side of the bed when you give her serious news.
* Word choice
  + Things not to say:
    - He doesn’t want anything done, we aren’t going to do anything
  + We **ARE** doing things—we are treating his symptoms
* Teamwork
  + Incorporate your nurse and other health care team members. We do best when we work together.
* Jargon
  + Watch out. You may say things that are code for medical professionals such as, “his blood pressure is dropping” or “he is not doing well” but the family may not understand. Even when they understand he is not doing well, to them, that may not mean dying.
* Saying the “D” word (death or dying)
  + Patients and families need to know what’s happening in ways they can understand
  + Suggest saying
    - “This looks like the dying process”
    - Hope/fear, e.g., “I hope he will make it home but I’m afraid that he may be actively dying now.”
  + Family report they knew he was going to die, they just need for you to declare that it appears the time is now. It is a gift so they can say good-bye/prepare.
* Death pronouncement
  + Review how to discreetly perform key exam findings, when they can be completed and the need to tell family that the patient has died
