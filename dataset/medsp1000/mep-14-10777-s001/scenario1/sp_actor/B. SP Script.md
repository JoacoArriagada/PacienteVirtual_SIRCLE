APPENDIX D

Practice Care of the Dying Older Adult

Hybrid Simulation - Standardized Patient/SIM Man

Standardized Patient Script

Name: Anne Smith

Husband: Barney Smith (will be played by SimMan)

Barney is 70 years old and was diagnosed with Multiple Myeloma 8 years ago. He was treated with chemotherapy but when it no longer was benefitting him, he (and you) agreed to focus on palliative care. He has been enrolled in home hospice for the last 1 1/2 years. At the time of hospice enrollment, he signed a Do Not Resuscitate Order and made it very clear that he wanted the end of his life to be focused on comfort and he did NOT want aggressive medical care.

The hospice nurse visits about once a week but there have not been many issues other than a very gradual decline in his function and need for pain medications. You are aware that he will die of the Multiple Myeloma but as his condition has only worsened very gradually, you are not aware of imminent death. In fact, your family (including sons and their families) is planning a much-anticipated trip to the lake this weekend as the whole family enjoys fishing and boating.

Barney was an architect and his son Tom now runs the firm here in town after he retired about 5 years ago. You two have 2 more sons who live in the St. Louis area (about 4 hours away.)

The hospice nurse saw Mr. Smith at home yesterday morning and there were no changes in his status at that time. However, by the afternoon and evening, he became groggy and unable to speak coherently. He did not eat or take his medications. During the night he kept moaning and thrashing and seemed very uncomfortable. He pushed you away when you attempted to move him. You called 911 and he is now in the Emergency Department in distress. You are clear that you want him to be made comfortable.

Learner (to Mrs. Smith): What prompted you to bring Barney to the Emergency Room?

Wife: "He was becoming more lethargic, not speaking coherently, moaning, thrashing and appearing uncomfortable. The hospice nurse had visited yesterday morning but by evening, things changed. I didn't think there was anything else to do in the middle of the night."

Wife: "We still want to be on hospice and our goal is that you help him get more comfortable so I can take him back home. My son can bring his van."

Learner: What kinds of symptoms is he having?

Wife: "He started to get worse yesterday afternoon and then just started moaning and mumbling. He seems irritated when I move him around at all, but I'm just trying to make him comfortable. He has never pushed me away! Please just help him get more comfortable so I can take him home!" (Wife is teary and upset so that learner can demonstrate words of comfort or reassurance.)

Learner: What medications was Mr. Smith taking?

Wife pulls list out of purse and hands to the learner.

Learner: How often was he taking them?

Wife: "He usually took the morphine controlled release tablets every day but he has not had anything for 24 hours because he has been so confused and refusing medications. He doesn't take the morphine oral concentrate very often."

Wife: "Please don't do anything else except just make him comfortable."

Wife: "We have been planning a trip to the lake this weekend with the kids and grandkids. My husband just loves the lake and going fishing." (She is not recognizing that he is actively dying.)

You or the nurse may bring up calling your son who lives in town. If it does not come up, the nurse may ask about calling a pastor or chaplain.

Wife: "I want to say some words to him. Can he hear me now?"

The wife will be tearful but her anxiety decreases tremendously as soon as she gets good clear communication about what is happening.

Wife will ask for time alone and the learner leaves the room.
