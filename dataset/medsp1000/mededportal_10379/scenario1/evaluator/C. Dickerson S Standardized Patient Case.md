**Standardized Patient Training Summary**

**S. Dickerson**

**Opening Statement: *“OK, so what’s going on? What do I need to do once I get home?”***

**First Position:** Sitting up in a hospital bed

**Affect/Physical Symptoms:** Concerned but in no acute distress without any symptoms.

**Current Condition**:

The patient is 45-60 year old S. Dickerson. About one month ago, the patient underwent a total knee arthroplasty on the right knee. This was performed because of a prior injury sustained in a car accident ten years ago complicated by progressive osteoarthritis of the knee. Symptoms progressed until they began interfering with the patient’s job and not allowing participation in activities like hiking and bowling. The patient was referred to Dr. Harris, an orthopedic surgeon, who recommended knee replacement. The surgery was successful and there were no immediate complications.

About two weeks ago, the patient developed acute right knee pain and swelling with subjective fevers. The patient returned to Dr. Harris about one week prior to this encounter. Laboratory studies and an x-ray were obtained, and an aspiration of the right knee was performed. The patient was admitted and taken to the OR for irrigation and debridement with liner exchange. The original hardware was left in place.

The patient thinks the fellow is coming to talk about the plan for discharge. When told about the MRSA infection, the patient is very concerned because of hearing about MRSA in a TV news report about wrestlers and worries it may be life threatening.

**Past Medical History:**

* High cholesterol (diagnosed four years ago, on treatment)
* Gastroesophageal reflux disorder (GERD) (diagnosed one year ago, on treatment)
* Osteoarthritis
* Depression (diagnosed 1.5 years ago, doing well on therapy)
* Atrial fibrillation (diagnosed four years ago, on warfarin, followed in anticoagulation clinic for monitoring with INR consistently therapeutic on therapy)

**Allergies:**

* Clindamycin phosphate (rash)

**Medications (may be written down on paper for learner):**

* oxycodone 5 mg by mouth every 4 hours as needed (for pain)
* pravastatin 40 mg by mouth at bedtime (for high cholesterol)
* Prozac 20 mg by mouth daily (for depression)
* Prilosec 40 mg by mouth daily (for GERD)
* Warfarin 4 mg by mouth at bedtime (anticoagulant for atrial fibrillation )

**Social History:**

* Occupation: Real estate agent for RE/MAX
* Marital status: Married 19 years
* Sexual Status: active with spouse only; two partners before patient met spouse.
* Children: Daniel,18 years old, in college at Ohio State University.
* Residence: Nashville
* Diet: Tries to eat well but occasionally has red meat and fried foods.
* Caffeine: 1-2 cups of coffee in the morning
* Smoking: Quit 7 years ago. Smoked 1/2 ppd for 10 years prior to that time.
* Alcohol use: None.
* Illegal drug use: None
* Exercise: Not regularly.
* Travel: No significant travel history.

**What to expect the fellows to cover:**

* Explanation of current treatment plan of IV antibiotics plus oral rifampin for an extended course (i.e. six weeks).
* Potential side effects of the recommended antibiotics.
* Potential need for possible long-term antibiotic suppression after end of therapeutic antibiotic therapy due to retained hardware.
* Potential drug-drug interaction between warfarin and rifampin and discussion of need for close anticoagulation monitoring.
* Specifics of outpatient parenteral antibiotic therapy as well as line care and laboratory monitoring.

**Patient prompts (must ask all questions unless given in the fellow’s explanation):**

1. “So how do we treat this infection?”
2. “What happens at the end of treatment?”
3. “Will the infection come back?”
4. “What side effects do I have to be aware of?”
5. “Do any of my medications interact with the antibiotics you are starting me on?”
6. “Can I be around my infant nephew? I’ve heard staph is very contagious.”
