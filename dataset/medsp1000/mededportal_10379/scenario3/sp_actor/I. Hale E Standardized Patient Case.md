**Standardized Patient Training Summary**

**E. Hale (HIV notification)**

**Opening Statement:** “(I found out recently that I’m HIV positive.) So what happens now?

What does this mean for me for the rest of my life?”

**First Position/Affect:** Sitting in chair. Slightly tearful at times but appropriate and inquisitive.

**Current Condition:**

The patient is 35-45 year old E. Hale. The patient is presenting to the HIV clinic today to receive counseling for a recent diagnosis of HIV. The patient was previously unaware of the infection and only found out because of a recent surgery. The patient is understandably upset and struggling to figure out how this will impact the rest of life.

The patient had surgery to remove the gallbladder because of gallstones six weeks ago. During perioperative evaluation for that surgery, the patient was told that the team was going to check an HIV antibody test and the patient assented. The next day before discharge, the patient was informed of the surprising positive result. The doctor met briefly with the patient and stated that the patient would need to be seen in an HIV clinic and provided the contact information for the clinic. The doctor said that if medication was started, the patient would likely remain healthy, but did not explain more specifics related to the new diagnosis.

The first person the patient told was the best friend Anna. The patient is scared to tell anyone but really needed the support of a friend. The patient has known Anna since moving to town and beginning to attend 2nd Presbyterian Church. Anna encouraged the patient to tell family. The patient recently met with the mother one week ago for lunch and told her the diagnosis then. The patient’s mother has agreed to let the patient tell the sister and father when ready.

**Past Medical History:**

* Recently diagnosed HIV
* Gallstones, now status post cholecystectomy which was performed six weeks ago
* Gonorrhea, diagnosed and treated when in college
* Wisdom teeth removal at age 22

**Allergies:** Penicillin (rash)

**Medications:** Flonase inhaled daily as needed for seasonal allergies

**Social History:**

* Occupation: Concert pianist.
* Marital status: Single.
* Children: None.
* Residence: Lives alone in a house in town.
* Diet: Generally healthy diet (mostly fish, chicken and vegetables).
* Caffeine: 1-2 cups of coffee in the morning.
* Smoking: Never
* Alcohol use: Will have 1-2 drinks on weekends with friends, but not more than this.
* Illegal drug use: Smoked marijuana occasionally in college.
* Exercise: Works out 2-3 times per week lifting weights or running.

**Family History:**

* Mother: 72; has mitral valve prolapse but otherwise healthy.
* Father: 75; has high cholesterol and is in treatment for prostate cancer, stage II, undergoing radiation therapy.
* Siblings: Sister Julie; three years older than the patient; married; has 3 kids (2 boys and 1 girl). Healthy.
* Maternal and Paternal Grandmothers: Died in their 80’s from natural causes.
* Maternal Grandfather: Died at age 60 from a heart attack.
* Paternal Grandfather: Died before the patient was born in a car accident.

**Sexual History:**

The patient became sexually active in college and has had a total of 12 prior sexual partners. The last HIV test was seven years ago as part of a life insurance exam and came back negative. The patient was last sexually active 9 months ago and did not use a condom. The patient has not been sexually active since then and is not dating anyone. The patient does not discuss personal relationships with the father. The patient’s mother is aware of the HIV status but not the father or sister.

**Mental Status:**

The patient is having some difficulty coping with the diagnosis but has done some research and has already gotten in touch with the local HIV/AIDS service organization to look into support groups.

There are no financial issues with access to healthcare or the cost of medications.

The patient has not thought about suicide or homicide.

The patient has told the best friend Anna and the mother, and both have been supportive. The patient is concerned about telling the father given his health right now. The patient is also concerned about telling the sister. While they were close growing up, the patient and Julie have grown apart some in recent years. They did not have a falling out; they just do not see each other that much. The patient is concerned about Julie’s husband’s reaction.

The patient is worried that people at work, at church, and other friends in the social circle will find out.

Although the patient has a friend from college who was diagnosed with HIV four years ago, the patient has not really talked with him about it that much. He lives in Boston so the patient does not see him that often and when they talk on the phone they catch up about jobs and relationships more than discussing general health issues.

**Primary Patient prompts (must ask all questions unless given in the fellow’s explanation):**

1. “So am I going to die? I don’t feel sick.”
2. “Do I need to take medications?” What will the virus do to my body if I don’t take medications?
3. “Will I be able to have sex again?”
4. “What do my lab results mean? Do I have full-blown AIDS?”

**Secondary concerns (may ask these questions if appropriate):**

1. “When my numbers get better can I stop taking the medication?”
2. “What happens if I don’t take the medications?”
3. “In general, what are some of the side effects of HIV meds?”
4. “Should I confide in my diagnosis to more people than I have so far?”
