**Standardized Patient Training Summary**

**A. Kaplan (LTBI)**

**Opening Statement:** “I was told I tested positive for tuberculosis (TB). Can you explain what this means for me?”

**First Position:** Sitting in chair

**Affect/Physical Symptoms:** Concerned affect but in no acute distress. No symptoms.

**Current Condition:**

The patient is 25-35 year old A. Kaplan. The patient was born in Los Gatos, CA but spent the majority of childhood overseas in China because the parents were missionaries. The patient did travel back to the U.S. numerous times over the years because there is still family in California. The patient was homeschooled while overseas and attended university in Shanghai but decided to return to the U.S. for a PhD. Although the family encouraged choosing a school closer to home, the patient was accepted to the local university’s PhD program in biochemistry and moved at the end of last summer. While obtaining the degree, the patient was hired to work in one of the biochemistry labs. As part of the hiring process, the patient was tested for TB and 4 weeks ago was told that the IGRA was positive. The patient was advised to get a chest x-ray, which was done two weeks ago and was within normal limits with no evidence of active pulmonary disease. The patient made an appointment at the infectious diseases clinic to get more information but in the interim has returned to work. The patient feels well and has no symptoms such as weight loss, night sweats, cough, or swollen lymph nodes. The patient has had no known TB exposures; however, the patient has heard about people in some of the towns visited and worked in while in China that had TB in the past but was never knowingly around someone sick with TB.

The patient is not against taking preventive therapy, but wants to be better informed before making a decision. The patient has never had a TB test before and is not sure if anyone in the family has either. The TB test that was given was a blood test although the patient has researched that the skin test is a more common TB test.

**Past Medical History:**

* Tonsillectomy as a child
* Had BCG (Bacille Calmette-Guerin) vaccination as a two-year-old child
* Had an HIV test two years ago at a doctor’s recommendation on a visit back to the U.S.; results were negative

**Allergies:** None

**Social History:**

* Occupation: Graduate student at a local university, pursuing a PhD in Biochemistry.
* Marital status: Not married but started seeing a new partner two months ago. Last relationship ended when the patient decided to move to Nashville for school.
* Sexual Status: Not currently sexually active.
* Sexual History: Heterosexual with 4 prior partners. No history of sexually transmitted infections.
* Children: None
* Diet: Mostly vegetarian but does eat fish.
* Caffeine: Has 1-2 sodas per day.
* Smoking: No.
* Alcohol use: Only on special occasions (i.e. 2-4 times per year).
* Illegal drug use: None.
* Exercise: Runs three times a week.

**Family History:**

* Mother: 55; has hypertension; currently lives in China and works as an ESL teacher for SEND International, an interdenominational Christian mission organization.
* Father: 58; has hyperlipidemia; currently lives in China and works as a youth minister for SEND International, an interdenominational Christian mission organization.
* Brother: three years younger; had the BCG vaccination as a child; otherwise healthy.
* Maternal and Paternal Grandmothers: both 78, living in Vallejo and San Jose respectively. Both are healthy with the exception of arthritis.
* Maternal Grandfather: died from a heart attack when the patient was a child.
* Paternal Grandfather: 84; living in San Jose; has asthma and uses an inhaler.

**Patient prompts (must ask all questions unless given in the fellow’s explanation):**

1. “Am I contagious? How do I know if I have the contagious form of TB?”
2. “What does the blood test mean? I heard some people get a bubble put under the skin after their test. Does this mean I have TB in my blood?”
3. “Well, I had the TB vaccine as a child. Is that what is making my test positive? I did some reading on the internet.”
4. “So do I need to take this preventive medication? What happens if I don’t take it?”
5. “What’s my risk of getting sick if I don’t take the preventive therapy?”
6. “What side effects should I be aware of if I take the preventive medication?”
