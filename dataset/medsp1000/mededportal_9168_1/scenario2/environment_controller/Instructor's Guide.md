<u>**INSTRUCTOR'S GUIDE**</u>

<u>Procedure Order</u>

"Communication with a Colleague" and "Pediasure Please!" are professionalism cases.  They can be used as stations within the context of a larger OSCE (clinical or multidisciplinary) or by themselves in the context of a lecture or small group exercise.

1. The SR is filmed in advance having an unprofessional encounter with an ambulance worker (Communication with Colleague) or in the presence of a medical student (Pediasure Please!).  The encounters are detailed in the "Standardized resident instructions" submitted.
2. On the day of the OSCE, DVD players are made available outside of the stations together with a set of instructions.  Inside the station the SR is dressed as a doctor.  A faculty observer is present as well.
3. The resident first reads a short set of instructions and then views the video described above.  This task is completed in 2 minutes.
4. The encounter between the SR and resident lasts 10 minutes.  2 minutes prior to the end, a sound (usually a knock on the door) indicates that it is time to wrap up the discussion.
5. Each encounter is followed by 5 minutes of feedback.  Initially the resident is asked to evaluate his/her own performance and then both SR and faculty provide feedback (see feedback protocol with teaching points).
6. After the resident leaves, both SR and faculty fill out their own evaluation.

<u>Practical Implementation</u>

The SRs must be trained in advance for content, emotional tone and timing of the case.  They do not require a great deal of medical knowledge but they must be familiar with the resident's educational and work environment (e.g., site specific terms and abbreviations, practice routines).   The training focuses on making the SRs seem angry, harried or prejudiced.  Costuming requires a doctor's lab coat and a stethoscope, to make the SRs appear real.  The SR should also have a clipboard or fake chart to pretend writing notes when the resident enters the room to confront him with his unprofessional behavior.  This prop illustrates how busy the SR is, making it more challenging for the resident to address the unprofessional behavior.

A portable DVD player (or laptop computer with DVD player capability) is necessary for both stations.  It should be set up right outside of the station and placed next to the station instructions.
