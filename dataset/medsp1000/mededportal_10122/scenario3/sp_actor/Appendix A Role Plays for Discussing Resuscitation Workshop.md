**Appendix A**

**Role Plays for Teaching Residents to Discuss Resuscitation with Hospitalized Patients**

**Role Play for “Teaching Residents to Discuss Resuscitation”**

**Patient/Family Script:**  The patient is a 74 year-old man who presents to the hospital with symptoms of worsening dyspnea and acute confusion.

The patient has severe underlying COPD and has required continuous oxygen therapy at 2L via nasal cannula.

He has been admitted two other times in the past four months for AECOPD.

He is sleepy but arousable, although when aroused he is confused.

He is admitted to the MICU in an improved but still critical condition.

The patient is a retired auto mechanic.  He was divorced many years ago and never remarried.  He has two adult children, a daughter Sara, a homemaker, who he has lived with for the past several years and a son John, who lives out of state and has his own business.  The patient is mostly home bound.  He requires some assistance with dressing and bathing due to his dyspnea.  He enjoys having his grandchildren around and he still appreciates his daughter’s cooking.  His passion is cars and he frequently reads magazines about automobiles.

After each of his last hospitalizations he was released home with his daughter with his clinical condition worse each time.  He was not mechanically ventilated on either occasion.

**Role Play – Variation 3**

The patient’s daughter Sara is aware that her father’s COPD is a serious medical problem but sees that he has made it back home after each of his last hospitalizations and is focused on that fact.  The patient’s son lives out-of-state and was not aware of how serious the patient’s medical problems have been.

In this variation, after the patient’s daughter states “Dad wouldn’t want to be kept alive on machines.  He made that pretty clear,” it becomes clear that the son is frustrated and feels that the patient was readmitted because of poor care/premature discharge during his last admission.  He may state, “He’s back here again because you let him go to soon last time,” or “You must have given him the wrong antibiotics last time.”
