<u>**SP Feedback Case Guidelines**</u>

**General comments on providing feedback:**

The trainee may take the conversation in a number of ways in this scenario.  However, the main points are as follows:

The trainee is expected to utilize the main components of motivational interviewing in attempting to

get you to accept referral to the Quitline.  General concepts of this are included in the checklist below (what went well with the interview) – but in brief, the trainee should recognize that you have both motivation to quit and resistance to quitting.  The trainee should attempt to elicit your motivation and guide you to accept referral.

<u>**SP Steps for Successful Feedback Session**</u>:

1. Invite participant back into the room.
2. Introduce yourself.
3. Offer a chair for participant to sit down; you should sit as well.
4. Inform participant that all feedback will be from patient’s perspective. “When I say ‘I’ it will be from the patient.”
5. Always start with; “How do you think that went?”
6. Begin feedback with something positive about participant’s performance.
7. Include at least one area for improvement, if relavant.
8. To end feedback session:
    1. Ask participant if they have any questions about what was discussed.
    2. Tell the participant that the feedback session is over.
    3. Wish them well.

<u>**Criteria for Effective Feedback**</u>

1. Be descriptive.
2. Be specific instead of general.
3. Base your feedback on observable behavior; what you saw or heard: **Eye Contact, Positioning in Room, Tone of Voice, Word Choice, Body Language.**
4. Use of an “I” statement.
5. Direct feedback toward a behavior the participant can do something about and how that behavior made you feel.

<u>**Tips to Assist in Providing Feedback**</u>

1. If you are stuck or have a student who isn’t being verbal, here are some suggestions:
    1. “What did you feel was the best part, or what did you think went well?”
    2. “Is there anything that you would do differently next time you have a patient encounter like this one?”
2. Areas for concentration when giving feedback:
    1. Interview (introduction, rapport, closing)
    2. Physical exam (comfort)
    3. Counseling (interest in patient)
