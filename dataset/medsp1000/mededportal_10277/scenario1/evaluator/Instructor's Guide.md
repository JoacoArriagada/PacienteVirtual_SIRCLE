**Instructor’s Guide**

Sick Newborn: A case of critical congenital heart disease

Learning Objectives

Primary Objectives

1. Demonstrate an appropriate initial approach to a critically ill newborn including stabilization of ABCs.
2. Recognize absence of femoral pulses and hypoxic neonate as congenital heart defect.
3. Identify cardiomegaly on CXR.
4. Recognize need for prostaglandin.
5. Identify locations for emergency access in a neonate and utilize one of these routes promptly when an IV cannot be established promptly

**Secondary Learning Objectives**

1. Perform advanced airway management when patient becomes apneic from prostaglandin initiation
2. Disposition the patient to a location that includes pediatric cardiology, cardiothoracic surgery and intensive care resources.

Background

This educational resource provides case information and materials for a simulation case suitable for physicians in training in emergency medicine and pediatrics. This case is currently utilized at our institutions for Emergency Medicine residents and Pediatric Emergency Medicine fellows completing required cases in Emergency Medicine Simulation. It has also been utilized for required simulation time completed by Pediatric residents on rotation through our Emergency Department.

This high-fidelity patient simulation case involves a neonate presenting to an Emergency Department in extremis due to a previously undetected critical congenital heart lesion, specifically Aortic Coarctation. Evaluation in the emergency department reveals a tachycardic, tachypneic, hypothermic and poorly perfused neonate who requires aggressive stabilization including oxygen, fluids and warming. The patient will also require prostaglandin therapy and airway management. The diagnosis of Critical Aortic Coarctation should prompt learners to involve cardiology and/or cardiothoracic surgery and admit the patient to the intensive care unit. Debriefing materials are provided to illustrate key concepts in recognition of critical congenital heart lesions as important considerations in the differential of a critically ill newborn and to stimulate discussion of the important treatment steps in resuscitation and stabilization of these children.

Implementation

**Creation and Testing of Case:**  This simulation scenario has been used at two institutions for the past two years.  It is typically used with Emergency Medicine residents, Pediatrics residents and Pediatric Emergency Medicine fellow physicians with an Emergency Medicine faculty member.  This case has been used for small groups of 2-4 learners in each session.  Testing of this scenario has been accomplished via repetition with various learners.   All participants include medical students, emergency medicine and general pediatrics residents in years 1-3 of post-graduate education or pediatric emergency medicine fellows in years 4-6 of post-graduate education.

**Anticipated Use of Case:**  Typical timing for this case involves approximately 20 minutes to run the patient simulation, and 20 minutes for debriefing.  One program has typically used this case in conjunction with 1-2 additional pediatric cases in a 1.5-2-hour simulation session for experiential learning. The other program has used it as a single case during a pediatric workshop which includes other case based simulations, independent procedural simulations and case based discussions in a round robin format.

Limitations and Areas for Future Improvement

1. **Resource-intensive experience:**  As with all simulation cases, implementation of this case requires significant resources:  a high-fidelity patient simulator, appropriate environment, medical equipment, and personnel to provide the educational experience for the learners.  We have found this case to require one faculty or staff member capable of running the simulator and one faculty member with the required clinical expertise to facilitate the case and conduct the debriefing and educational session.

Additionally, there are some physical exam findings that can be challenging to implement even with high fidelity simulation. Moulage for cyanosis and mottling can be helpful to help set the scene for this critically ill appearing newborn. The type of simulator utilized will impact the ability to demonstrate murmurs, precordial heaves and absent femoral pulses for learners. Facilitators should provide physical exam findings which are difficult to simulate but would aid in the diagnosis during a real patient encounter.

1. **Validation of scenario:**  While this educational case has been used for simulation sessions as part of the didactic component of two residency programs and a fellowship program it is not validated as a formal assessment tool for evaluation of clinical competency.
2. **Different learner populations:**  We have used this case primarily for resident physician education in Pediatrics and Emergency Medicine as well as for fellows in Pediatric Emergency Medicine. Medical students have participated at times in supporting provider roles but not as team leaders given degree of difficulty of this case.
