**Additional Materials #1**

**Dialogue between Classroom Instructor and the Patient**

**Setting:** Patient interrupts the lecturer’s first slide by entering the room and asking for help: “Can you help me? I need to know what medications I am taking and if they are causing me to have all these problems. I feel achy all over and my muscles hurt.” The patient has moulage applied to arms to simulate bruising, but does not mention it overtly.

**Instructor: Tell me what medications you are taking.**

**Patient:** I’m not really sure about their names. I think I take about 8 or 9 pills in the morning around breakfast, 1 pill with dinner, and 5 or 6 at night. Some of the pills that I take, my daughter-in-law has been splitting in half for me. It’s cheaper that way. I don’t know the names of my medications from memory, but I do have a list that I can show you. My daughter-in-law knows all about my medicines. She puts them in my pillbox every week, but I accidentally dropped it on the floor today and pills went everywhere. My daughter-in-law is traveling through Europe right now, so I need help figuring all this out. Why don’t you call the doctor’s office and see if they can help? *(Patient digs through purse/bag to find a business card that contains the phone number for a “doctor’s office” and hands a business card to each team of students.)*

**Instructor: Tell me about where you obtain your medications.**

**Patient:** We usually go to City Pharmacy around the corner *(Patient hands each team of students a business card with a number for the pharmacy*). They are so nice there. They can probably help you figure out what I am supposed to be taking…. But, sometimes, my daughter-in-law gets some of my medicines at another pharmacy. I am not sure, but I think the other pharmacy has cheaper generics. Plus, when I get new prescriptions filled there, they give me a $10 gift card. I don’t know the name of that pharmacy.

**Instructor: Tell me about any over-the-counter medication that you take**

**Patient:** What do you mean over-the-counter? Like that a pharmacist hands me, over his counter? Most of my medications come from behind the pharmacist’s counter.

**Instructor:** ***Explains to the patient that “over-the-counter” refers to medicines that can be bought anywhere without needing a prescription).***

**Patient:** I really don’t know for sure because my daughter-in-law usually picks my medicine up and organizes my pills. But, I do know that I don’t take any of those herbal things. I asked my doctor about that one time, after seeing something on Good Morning America, you know, and my doctor told me that I should just stay away from all that stuff.

**Instructor: Some people find it difficult to take all their medications as prescribed. Tell me about any difficulties you have with skipping doses of your medicines or being able to afford them.**

**Patient:** I take everything just as my daughter-in-law lays it out for me. I rarely miss a pill.

**Patient:** I used to be on all expensive drugs. I would run out of my pills in October when I couldn’t afford them anymore and hit that…that…what do they call it…. the doughnut hole? This year my doctor switched me over to as many generics as possible.

**Instructor: What medical conditions do you take medications for?**

**Patient:** Let’s see now. I’ve got heart problems. My doctor was concerned about my sugar; it was up a couple of times. And my thyroid – has some problems, too, I think. My bones are brittle and I see an eye doctor.

**Instructor:** **Tell me anything else that might help us figure out which medications you are taking.**

**Patient:** (*Digs through purse [or other bag] and proudly states*): I keep this medication list. It’s probably not as up-to-date as it should be, but I **did** remember to add the blood thinner when my dose was changed recently (*patient states this proudly*). (The medication list is dated July 19, 2011.) *Patient dumps out the contents of purse/bag and hands each of the medication vials, drug samples, and vitamin bottles to the instructor (see props for details).*
