**APPENDIX A**

**Transitions in Care: Case Vignettes**

**Part I: Outpatient→ Emergency Department**

Jerry is a 19 y/o young man with a developmental disorder, whose primary language is Spanish, though he routinely declines an interpreter. He has a history of depression and anxiety, lives with his mother and father (who are his legal guardians), and attends a therapeutic high school. He is prescribed fluoxetine and presents to the Outpatient Clinic for a routine psychopharm visit with his trainee psychiatrist, Dr. A.

Jerry’s history is notable for a paradoxical reaction to benadryl leading to agitation, and intense GI distress with sertraline. During the visit with Dr. A, he mentions that he has been particularly sad this past week, since a beloved staff member from his therapeutic school retired. Dr. A asks if Jerry has had any suicidal thoughts, and he becomes tearful and quiet, and says “I’m so hopeless, what’s the point? I’d be better off dead.” Dr. A asks if he has a plan and he says “I know where my mom keeps her Costco-size bottle of Tylenol.” Dr. A recommends that Jerry go to the Emergency Department for an evaluation by the Psychiatric Emergency Service (PES) team.

**Guiding questions**

*1. What are the logistical issues of transferring a patient from the outpatient setting to the emergency department?*

*2. What information should be communicated to the receiving team when transferring a patient from the outpatient setting to the emergency department?*

**Part II: Emergency Department→ Medical Floor**

Jerry is brought into the ED. His vital signs are within normal limits. The ED attending sends off some basic labwork, and informs the Psychiatric Emergency Services psychiatry resident, Dr. B, that the patient is ready to be evaluated.

Dr. B begins an exhaustive evaluation, including obtaining collateral from the therapeutic high school and from Jerry’s mother, who is present at bedside. The ED attending informs Dr. B that the labwork is back and the patient is “medically clear.” Soon after, Jerry begins to complain of nausea, and starts to vomit, and looks unwell. Jerry’s father arrives in the ED and informs Dr. B that he discovered an empty bottle of acetaminophen in the bathroom at home.

**Guiding questions**

*1. How can Dr. B work together with the ED attending to ensure appropriate disposition for Jerry, in particular if there is a disagreement between the ED and psychiatry?*

*2. How can Dr. B be helpful in facilitating a smooth inpatient medical admission for Jerry?*

*3. Whom should Dr. B call to provide handoff information, and what information should Dr. B convey?*

**Part III: Medical Floor → Inpatient**

Jerry is admitted to the medical floor, where he is treated for an acetaminophen overdose. He is kept on 1:1 observation and followed by the CL team daily, led by the CL psychiatry trainee, Dr. C. Jerry is treated with N-acetylcysteine, and LFTS and coags are monitored.

Jerry’s hospital course is complicated by two episodes of possible delirium early in his admission during which Jerry became agitated. In the first episode Jerry became agitated and required 2mg of haloperidol IM, with good effect; in the second episode, nursing was able to get Jerry’s mother on the phone, who was able to reorient and calm Jerry down, without the need for chemical restraint. Jerry’s labs stabilize and he is medically cleared. However, Jerry continues to report sadness and regret that he did not succeed in ending his life. Dr. C and his supervisors determine that Jerry still requires inpatient psychiatric admission.

**Guiding questions**

*1. Should Jerry be admitted to an adolescent unit or adult inpatient unit? What if there are no beds?*

*2. What is the Dr. C’s role in passing information on to the inpatient psychiatry team? What information should be communicated and how?*

*3. Which faculty supervisor(s) should an on-call psychiatry trainee consult for any questions about this case?*

*4. Jerry and his mother are agreeable to admission. When admitted to the unit, who can consent for treatment?*

**Part IV: Inpatient → Outpatient**

Jerry is admitted to the inpatient unit, where he attends groups and engages with the staff and milieu. He is cared for by the inpatient psychiatry trainee, Dr. D.

Jerry’s dose of fluoxetine is increased and he is able to process with the IP team the loss of the staff member from school. After 1 week, the inpatient team determines Jerry is ready for discharge. He denies SI and is eager to return home and to school. However, the director of his therapeutic school, who was aware of the suicide attempt and inpatient admission, is hesitant about Jerry returning to school, wondering if he may still need a higher level of care. Jerry and his parents are eager for him to come home and resume his routine.

**Guiding questions**

*1. What are the different options for aftercare for Jerry once he is discharged?*

*2. When and how should the inpatient psychiatry trainee, Dr. D, communicate with Jerry’s outpatient psychiatrist, Dr. A? What should be discussed?*

*3. How can Dr. D, the inpatient psychiatrist, and Dr. A, the OP psychiatrist, facilitate and support Jerry returning to school and home?*
