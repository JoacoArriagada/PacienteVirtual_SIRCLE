**Case Information:**

*Scenario Given to Participants:*

A previously healthy 52 year-old man arrives in the ED after a construction accident (a steel girder fell on his left thigh). He has left thigh pain and arrives in the ED 20 minutes after the accident.

*HPI:*

* He saw steel beam falling and jumped out of the way and the beam hit him in the thigh
* No chest pain, neck pain, back pain, abdominal pain
* He was not hit in the head and did not have LOC
* His thigh pain is constant, severe, and throbbing in nature
* His leg was initially pinned but the other workers were able to remove the beam after 2 minutes

*Past Medical History:*

* Past medical history: None
* Past surgical history: Appendectomy
* Meds: None
* All: Aspirin (causes GI upset)
* Social History: Smokes 2 PPD, drinks a six pack of beer on weekends, denies any drugs. Has worked as a construction worker for 27 years.
* Family History: Hypertension, Stroke, MI in his father at the age of 47 and in his brother at the age of 49

*Scenario Conditions Initially:*

* Alert and oriented in obvious pain

VS: HR 130, RR 18, BP 88/64, O2 Sat 98% on room air.

* A, B, C’s all intact
* HEENT: No facial injury or tenderness, PERRL, EOMI
* NECK: No neck pain, FROM
* CHEST: No chest pain to palpation, Lungs CTA B
* C/V: RRR S1S2, no murmurs rubs or gallops. 2+ pulses throughout
* ABDOMEN: Soft NT/ND, no rebound or guarding
* BACK: Midline spine without tenderness or step off
* MUSCULOSKELETAL: Large amount of swelling and ecchymosis to the left thigh. He is neurovascularly intact distally. No other injuries to any other extremities

*Patient physiology:*

* Hypotension, tachycardia, and anemia secondary to femur fracture and bleeding in the thigh

**Instructor Notes:**

2. Potential complication paths:

* 1. Not controlling pain = patient thrashes about worsening injuries (closed to open fracture)
  2. Does not recognize shock and treat appropriately with IVF = worsening shock
  3. Does not control injured extremity and treat appropriately = swelling worsens and patient becomes more hypotensive
  4. Once labs are reviewed, patient is not given blood appropriately = shock worsens and patient again becomes hypotensive
  5. Inadequate or no IVF are given and rhabdomyolysis is not recognized = briefly address in debriefing as to how rhabdomyolysis should be treated
