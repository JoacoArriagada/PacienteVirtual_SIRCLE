**HPI**: The patient is a 35-year-old female who presents to the clinic with two weeks of shoulder pain and deficits in shoulder movement. The pain is localized to her right side (in the arm and especially the shoulder) and radiates along her back, especially with movements of the shoulder or arm. She has no trouble rotating her arm or bringing it back to her side, but it is difficult for her raise her right arm and reach for items. This is due both to the pain as well as a weakness that causes her to feel that she does not have the strength to lift her arm or shoulder. She underwent a mastectomy on the right side for a breast adenocarcinoma with axillary lymph node dissection fifteen days ago and reports no complications during the surgical procedure. Initially she thought that her symptoms may be attributed to trauma of the surgery, but they have not abated despite her otherwise uncomplicated recovery. The surgical site is healing well, and she has no fever, swelling, or signs of infection around it. She has not tried anything to alleviate her symptoms for fear that it might interfere with her recovery from surgery but worries that her condition might be a result of some complication during surgery that was not noted by the surgeons at the time.
