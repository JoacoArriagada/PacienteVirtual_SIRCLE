VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

1. Scenario background given to participants ID/Chief complaint: 54 y.o. male/weakness

The patient presents to the ED with his wife who drove him in.

2. Past medical history $:$ DM, kidney stones, asthma as a child, appendectomy; no thyroid disease (only if asked)   
3. Meds and allergies $:$ Glyburide, ASA for prevention   
4. Family/social history: Lives with his wife and two dogs; works in banking; occasional alcohol, occasional cigar, no cocaine or other drug use (only provide information about cocaine if asked for)

A. Scenario conditions initially

1. History patient gives

Has been feeling weak the last 2 days. He has not had syncope.   
. Mild shortness of breath. No chest pain.   
. No abdominal pain. He has had a recent viral infection: cough, runny nose, body aches, vomiting and diarrhea, poor appetite, low grade fever (provide this information if any infectious symptoms are asked for)

2. Patients initial exam

General: awake, alert but tired-appearing  VS: T 99.4 HR ${ \sim } 1 5 0$ BP 100/66 RR 22 O2 sat 99%RA HEENT: NCAT, PERRL, mucous membranes somewhat dry (only give if asked for)

 Neck: Supple, no masses or thyromegaly, no jugular venous distension Heart: Tachycardic, irregularly irregular Lungs: Clear to auscultation bilaterally Abdomen: Soft, mild epigastric tenderness, no rebound or guarding Extremities: No edema or tenderness, no calf girth asymmetry Neurologic: Awake, alert, moving all extremities, no gross asymmetry   
Skin: dry, cool, slightly decreased turgor   
 Pulsus paradoxus (only if participants request it): $1 0 \mathrm { m m }$ Hg

3. Patients physiology

The patient has atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response. His underlying cause is dehydration from vomiting, diarrhea and decreased PO intake due to his viral infection.

B. Scenario branch points

 The patient presents with weakness. He is in atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response.   
His initial BP is stable.   
 If he is treated with nodal agents:

o HR decreases by 2 every 1 minute for 3 minutes and then increases by 3 every minutes to a maximum of 160-163 o SBP/DBP will decrease by 6/3 every minute until it reaches an SBP in the low 80s.

 If he is given IVFs, for the first liter, his HR decreases by 4 and his BP remains the same.   
 If he is given 1L IVF and a nodal agent, his vitals change as in the line above.   
 After the second liter is initiated, his HR decreases by 10 every minute and his SBP/DBP progressively increases (if lowered previously from baseline due to delay in IVFs or administration of nodal agents). If he is ordered for a third liter or maintenance fluids, his HR becomes 96 and BP 126/70. If this happens towards the end of the case, he should convert to sinus rhythm.   
 He should be admitted to a telemetry bed or observation unit.

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer o Have monitors beep loud enough to draw attention to the patient’s changing vital signs

B. Tips to direct actors

o Encourage the leader to request recycling of vital signs with a specified frequency, i.e. every 1-2 minutes – the nurse should ask how the physician would like the cuff parameters to be set.   
o If the leader is not recognizing dehydration as the cause and administering IVFs 10 minutes into the scenario, the wife should mention that the patient’s urine has been very dark and that she thinks he hasn’t been drinking enough fluids given his vomiting.   
o If the participants have not discussed nodal agents, the nurse should ask about administering $\beta$ -blockers or calcium channel blockers.
