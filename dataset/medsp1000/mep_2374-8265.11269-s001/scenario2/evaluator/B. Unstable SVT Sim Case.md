**Ideal Scenario Flow**

*One learner enters the room to assess the patient. After recognizing that patient is unresponsive and hypotensive the learner checks for a pulse and recognizes narrow complex tachycardia. The learner will name the rhythm unstable SVT and call for a crash cart and defibrillator. The rest of the team will arrive, and the original learner will deliver SBAR and be distinguished as the leader. The team immediately begins putting pads and limb leads on the patient. Other teammates will define their roles. The leader will then instruct the team to prepare for synchronized cardioversion. Patient’s oxygen increased to 100% by nonrebreather or bagging with BVM. The leader will instruct the person operating the defibrillator to put it in manual mode, synchronize it, and charge it to 50-100J. Leader will then instruct the team to appropriately clear from the patient and announce to the room that shock will be delivered. Shock is then delivered. The rhythm is immediately analyzed, and the leader will state that the patient is still in SVT. Leader will instruct a person to re-cycle blood pressure and the defibrillator operator will be instructed to synchronize it and charge it to appropriate energy. Once again, the leader will instruct all team member to “clear”, and the charge will be delivered. The new rhythm is analyzed and named sinus tachycardia by the team leader. The team will then delegate consulting and consider disposition.*

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. *Not evaluating patient for responsiveness and pulse: The first learner to enter the room must assess the hypotensive patient for responsiveness and pulse. Failure to do so will require a pause of the case to allow for coaching of the learner. The case will then start from the beginning.*
2. *Failure to define roles and use closed loop communication: Clearly defined roles and closed loop communication will be assessed by instructors. Failure by learners to display these behaviors will lead to a pause in the simulation for micro skill improvement.*
3. *Inappropriate use of adenosine: Learners should understand that Adenosine may only be used in management of stable SVT and is not appropriate for treating unstable SVT*.
4. *Failure to use defibrillator in manual mode: Learners that have not used a defibrillator may require instruction in this area.*
5. *Not placing leads on patient properly: Learners should receive instruction in this area if instructors feel the necessity.*
