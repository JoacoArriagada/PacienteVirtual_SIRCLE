**Instructor Guide**

**Title of module:**

Geriatric Nephrology OSCE: Managing drugs with aging patients and kidneys

**Conceptual Background**:

At the Medical College of Wisconsin (MCW), geriatricians, nephrologists and medical educators worked together to create and implement a geriatric nephrology OSCE for nephrology fellows. Key curriculum content gaps (pathophysiology of the aging kidney, medication and renal dosing and renal toxicity in elderly patients) were identified through a needs assessment including: 1) ACGME nephrology requirements, 2) Nephrology fellows' in-service exam scores, 3) survey of fellows and faculty to identify areas of perceived weakness, 4) literature review and 5) brief MCQ of basic science linked to clinical case questions.
 Curriculum content was delivered using the Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) educational / assessment method. The curriculum session highlighted kidney injury and pharmacology topics: drug toxicity + underlying science through an OSCE session with post session debriefing provided by faculty. Fellows were evaluated by faculty and standardized patients using a checklist, and fellows evaluated the session at its completion.

This MedEdPORTAL submission contains all materials for the geriatric nephrology OSCE session.

**Purpose of OSCE station:**

By the conclusion of this session, learners will be able to better provide care to geriatric patients in the ACGME Competency Domains including:

1. *Medical Knowledge:* applying knowledge of pharmacology and renal physiology to determine a differential diagnosis of acute kidney injury.
2. *Patient Care*: communicating that medical assessment and management plan clearly to the patient and family member.

**Educational Objectives:**

1. Demonstrate competence in the prevention, evaluation and management of geriatric aspects of nephrology, including disorders of the aging kidney and urinary tract.
2. Apply knowledge of physiology and pathology of the aging kidney + drug dosing and renal toxicity in the elderly patient.
3. When called as a consultant, to quickly:

a. Identify signs of adverse drug reactions (drug overdose/toxicity) in an elderly patient and,

b. Communicate a management plan to the patient and family.

**Context:**

This module was created for the nephrology fellowship core curriculum. It is appropriate for fellows at all levels of training. At our session, it was used for PGY4 residents. It is also applicable for other learners, including residents (e.g. family medicine, medicine, surgery). The curriculum was found to be too complex, particularly in the compilation of the differential diagnosis, to be effective in education of medical students.

1. The OSCE Case Development Template should be used in preparing for the session to give the instructor information regarding session goals, objectives, case structure and practical details of the OSCE.
2. SP Training Notes are given to the standardized patient and caregiver at least one week ahead of the session to allow for best preparation and understanding of their roles.
3. OSCE Agenda & Timeline is used to add structure to the session. This may be modified to fit your number of learners, standardized patients and space available.

3. What is your differential diagnosis for the patient’s acute kidney injury?

1. Faculty OSCE Checklist is to be used by the faculty observer and the standardized patient to rate the fellow. The checklist may be used by the faculty member while observing the OSCE encounter, with the questions near the end (relating to communication skills) completed by the SPs at the end of the encounter. The time after the Debrief Session, when the faculty member, SP and fellow are together in the exam room is a good opportunity to provide the fellow with feedback about the session and to get his or her thoughts of areas of strengths and areas for improvement.
2. Group Discussion & Debrief time has no specific curriculum or questions piece but provides time for interaction and questions/answers between the fellows and faculty. The time frame for this portion may be relatively brief depending on the level and degree of discussion.
3. Curriculum Evaluation Form is used to assess learner perception of the session and learner self –assessment of knowledge and skills gained at the end of the session.

**Practical Implementation Advice:**

The session is designed to be approximately 1.5 hours in length. All needed materials are attached. Teaching with two faculty facilitators is ideal although the session content could be given by one faculty member alone. Having an extra person (or two) to assist with coordination of learners and standardized patients, as well as time-keeping, is a key to a smooth flow of the educational session. As the faculty also are used to observe and score the fellows' performance on the OSCE using the Faculty OSCE checklist, as well as scoring them on the debrief session with the Faculty Debriefing Checklist at the conclusion of the OSCE, the faculty to learner ration is best at 1:1 during the OSCE session itself and the Faculty Debrief and Feedback portions. However, during the Group Discussion and Debrief portion, only one faculty member would be needed to lead and facilitate that section.

The OSCE session time is set at 20 minutes for the actual learner and standardized patient encounter time. This span was chosen to more closely simulate the actual clinical time that may be given to a practicing provider. This time could be increased if you wish to be certain fellows have ample time available to talk with the standardized patient.

**Successful Use of Resource (Outcomes) & Resource Limitations:**

**Outcomes:**

This curriculum was successfully implemented in the nephrology fellowship core curriculum but could also be implemented with other levels and types of learners (residents, internal medicine, family medicine learners). We have not piloted the curriculum with those learners as of yet. Our curricular evaluation strove to demonstrate that an OSCE is an effective, well received instructional method to teach and assess fellows' knowledge as related to drug toxicity in patients with renal disease. This data was collected from the learners' pre-curriculum survey (as a needs assessment) and post-session, in the form of a survey as well as learner checklists for both the OSCE session and the debriefing session with the faculty.

The OSCE curriculum session (N=6) showed strong learner evaluations (1= poor, 7=excellent) including "Session objectives were clearly stated & accomplished" = 6.8, "Feedback provided in debriefing will improve my ability to care for geriatric patients" = 5.8, and "Overall effectiveness of session in improving my ability to care for geriatric patients"= 6.5.

Fellow performance on the faculty and SP completed OSCE rater checklists showed the fellows' range of completion/inclusion of OSCE elements to range between 69-80% complete. The program director identified a discrepancy between fellows' (PGY4s) knowledge (good), and some communication abilities in summarizing the information and providing closure to the session. The ability of the OSCE to provide direct observation of the fellows' performance was highly valued by the program director and has led to further adjustments in teaching and assessment over time.

**Resource Limitations:**

Advance preparation of standardized patients is important to an accurate portrayal of the case. Providing standardized patient/caregivers with the SP Training Notes at least one week in advance and allowing 20 minutes prior to the session start time for questions, explanations and brief demonstration of any key points will increase realism of your case portrayal and the value of the SP feedback. Additional resource limitations may include space and standardized patients. The case, when piloted by medical students, was found to require a higher level of medical knowledge than known at that level of training. This complexity made it difficult for students to process the information and communicate it clearly.
