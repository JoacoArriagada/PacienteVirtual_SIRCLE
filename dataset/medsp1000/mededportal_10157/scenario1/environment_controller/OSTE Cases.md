Logistics

Each group of 4 will rotate through 3 different “stations”, each of which will last 20 minutes. 2 of your scenarios will be at the bedside, with a patient present, and the 3rd will involve a feedback session with a single student.

There are 4 roles (Teacher, Co-Pilot, Verbal Communication Observer, Non-verbal Communication Observer). Please decide among yourselves who will take which role. You should not repeat roles. Since there are only 3 stations, not all 4 of you will have a chance to be the “Teacher”. If you are an “Observer”, feel free to use your sheet as a checklist.

First 7 minutes= enact scenario

Second 7 minutes= discussion, then **teacher selects area to work on**

Next 5 minutes= re-enact scenario

Last 1 minute= brief discussion

Always start the Discussion by asking the teacher how it went. And make sure and ask the student their opinion!!

Station 1: Standardized Learner

You are an M3. You have just completed a brief history and physical on this new ED patient. You now present the patient to the attending. Read the description below, and translate it into an oral presentation. You may have to invent some answers to your attending’s questions, if they don’t appear below. NOTE THIS IS NOT A TEST OF YOUR ACTUAL CLINICAL SKILL; your job is to act as an average M3. (You may use this sheet as you present)

**HPI:** The patient presents with shortness of breath. It started this morning. The patient feels like they just can’t catch their breath. The patient has had a cold for the past several days, consisting of a runny nose, sinus congestion, sore throat, etc. The patient is coughing, and has missed the past few days of work. The patient is having trouble breathing with climbing stairs or even walking quickly. The patient is having some associated chest tightness as well, located mid-sternum. The patient had to sit up in a chair last night. The patient has had a little leg swelling, mostly at night. The patient denies fever, chills, night sweats. The patient has a history of asthma and has been using an inhaler every 4 hours with partial relief. The patient doesn’t see a doctor very regularly.

**PMH:** HTN, hyperlipidemia, asthma.

**MEDS:** HCTZ 25 mg daily, lisinopril 20 mg daily, simvastatin 40 mg daily

**SH:** smokes ½ ppd x 20 years.

**PE:** T 100.5, HR 105, RR 28, BP 110/60. The patient is in mild respiratory distress. HEENT- no conjunctival pallor, oropharynx clear, MMM; Neck- you think the JVP is 6 cm above the sternal angle, but you are not sure, no LAD; Lungs- mild diffuse expiratory wheezing, bibasilar rales; CV- tachycardic, regular rhythm, there might be a systolic murmur but you’re not sure, no S3/S4; ABD- soft, NTND, no masses; EXT- trace bilateral LE edema.

**LABS:** WBC 12,000 with normal differential; CBC and BMP otherwise normal; CXR seems clear by your reading

**ASSESSMENT-** You believe this is probably an asthma exacerbation brought on by a URI. You would recommend starting a steroid inhaler and following up with the PCP in 3-4 days. You don’t think admission is needed.
