Logistics

Each group of 4 will rotate through 3 different “stations”, each of which will last 20 minutes. 2 of your scenarios will be at the bedside, with a patient present, and the 3rd will involve a feedback session with a single student.

There are 4 roles (Teacher, Co-Pilot, Verbal Communication Observer, Non-verbal Communication Observer). Please decide among yourselves who will take which role. You should not repeat roles. Since there are only 3 stations, not all 4 of you will have a chance to be the “Teacher”. If you are an “Observer”, feel free to use your sheet as a checklist.

First 7 minutes= enact scenario

Second 7 minutes= discussion, then **teacher selects area to work on**

Next 5 minutes= re-enact scenario

Last 1 minute= brief discussion

Always start the Discussion by asking the teacher how it went. And make sure and ask the student their opinion!!

Station 3: Standardized Learner

You are an M3. You have just completed a brief history and physical on this new ED patient. You now present the patient to the attending. Read the description below, and translate it into an oral presentation. You may have to invent some answers to your attending’s questions, if they don’t appear below. NOTE THIS IS NOT A TEST OF YOUR ACTUAL CLINICAL SKILL; your job is to act as an average M3. (You may use this sheet as you present)

**HPI:** The patient presents with abdominal pain. The pain started about a week ago, and has gotten progressively worse. The pain seems to be located diffusely across the abdomen. It seems to begin in the morning, and gets worse later in the day. At times it gets quite severe. Curling up seems to make it better. Resting quietly helps too. Moving around makes it worse. The patient has had some nausea, but no vomiting. The patient has lost appetite, and has lost about 4-5#. No hematochezia or melena. The patient has felt warm but has not checked their temperature.

**PMH:** Depression, chronic sinusitis

**MEDS:** Zoloft

**SH:** No smoking; Drinks about 2-3 ounces of alcohol 3-4 days/week

**PE:** T 99.8, HR 95, RR 16, BP 145/90. The patient is in mild distress due to pain. HEENT- no conjunctival pallor, slightly dry mucus membranes; Neck- no LAD, no thyroid but you never can feel one; Lungs- CTA bilaterally; CV- RRR, nl s1s2, no murmurs, no S3/S4; ABD- tender diffusely but not rigid, +guarding, no masses, you tried to percuss the liver and thought it might be 8 cm, you don’t think you felt a spleen, no distension; EXT- no edema.

**LABS:** WBC 11,000, hemoglobin 14.3, CBC otherwise normal; BMP is normal

**ASSESSMENT-** You believe this might be an ulcer. It also could be cholecystitis, but you doubt it. You would recommend checking an H Pylori antibody, and starting the patient on Prilosec, with outpatient follow up with their PCP.

Station 3: Standardized Patient

This scenario mostly involves an interaction between the teacher and the student. There may or may not be any questions asked of you. The teacher may or may not wish to demonstrate physical exam technique with you.

The student will present your situation as follows:

The patient presents with abdominal pain. The pain started about a week ago, and has gotten progressively worse. The pain seems to be located all over the abdomen. It seems to begin in the morning, and gets worse later in the day. At times it gets quite severe. Curling up seems to make it better. Resting quietly helps too. Moving around makes it worse. The patient has had some nausea, but no vomiting. The patient has lost appetite, and has lost about 4-5#. No bloody stools or black/tarry stools. The patient has felt warm but has not checked their temperature.
