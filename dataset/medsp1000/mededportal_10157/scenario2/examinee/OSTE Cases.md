Station 2:

Feedback

Setting: ED, away from the bedside

Learner= M3 student, in 6th month of M3 year

For the Attending:

\* You have just spent a month working with an M3 in the ED. You are now meeting to give the student your feedback for the month. Read the information below **before** delivering your feedback. Feel free to use this sheet as a reference as you talk.

You have noticed several things while observing this student:

**Oral Presentations: Below average.** His/her oral presentations were usually over-thorough. The student usually listed the entire review of systems, including all the negatives. The exam was also over-detailed, containing all results, whether or not they were pertinent to the chief complaint. The student often completed the presentation after presenting the Labs, without giving an assessment or plan unless prompted by you. The student did make progress over the month, however.

**Knowledge Base: About average for level of training**. You had some patients with cardiac conditions, and the student seemed to lack knowledge about standard-of-care treatments for these conditions. The student also didn’t know the most appropriate symptomatic treatments for URIs. On the other hand, in emergency situations, the student did great. S/he was very familiar with all the algorithms, and was always calm and collected.

**Physical exam skills: About on par with level of training**, though you didn’t get to watch a lot of this. You saw a patient together with pneumonia who clearly had rales, which the student missed. You also had a patient with low back pain, and the student did a sensory exam of all the appropriate dermatomes.

**Professionalism: A few concerns.** The student seemed overconfident, and at times insensitive. Your impression was that of a cocky, self-assured student who knew “what to do” but was often unsure why they were doing it. Once, there was an elderly demented patient with a large abscess; his wife felt unable to care for him at home, but the student insisted that she could be taught how to pack it and rather callously recommended discharge. The I&D was done expertly, but the student seemed to disregard the social aspects.
