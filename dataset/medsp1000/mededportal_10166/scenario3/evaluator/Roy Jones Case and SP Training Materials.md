**SCENARIO**

<u>**Case: M-2 Roy Jones/Behavior Change**</u>

Visit 2

***Why the patient is seeing the student doctor***: You are visiting the doctor's office for a follow up for your previous heart attack that you had three months ago.

***Brief Summary of Case*** (Limit to one paragraph): Three months ago you experienced chest pain and went to the ER. The doctors told you that you had had a heart attack. The doctors inserted a tube through a vein in your thigh, found blockage and inflated balloons inside your arteries. You stayed in the hospital for three days during which time further test were completed and the doctors were pleased with your recovery. Over the past 3 months, you have had no more chest pain and generally felt good. You were told to stop smoking but you continue to smoke 1-2 packs per day. You were given a diet sheet at discharge but have not really followed it. You have had no formal nutrition counseling by a physician, registered dietitian nutritionist or other provider.

Visit 2 you will be in the Precontemplation stage. You don't want to quit smoking since smoking is one of the few joys you have left in life since your heart attack. You've been told you can't eat anything "good southern foods", can't drink and have to take all those medicines which cost so much money. You have been told to "lose some weight".

## 3.1. Age: 55

## 3.2. Gender: Male

## 3.4. Affect (Mannerisms, Behavior):

**1st interview** - You are going to demonstrate difficulties, by not being as spontaneous, you are not sure if you believe that the doctors knows what is right for you, and you don't really listen to the student physician. Sit back in your chair and cross your arms. Minimal eye contact. Look at your watch periodically. You are angry about all the changes you have had to make in your life and the cost of the medication is causing serious financial problems. Feel free to show some anger and irritation. You love smoking and it is the only "joy" left in your life since your heart attack. You are a tobacco farmer and feel it is important for you to smoke. If the student doctor acknowledges your feelings and listens to your concerns, become less resistant and listen a little more. You do know that smoking may be harmful to your heart. Be willing to make a list of things that are good about smoking and things that are not so good. If they talk about ways to stop smoking such as medications, get angry and tell them you can't afford any more medicine. Be willing to come back for a follow up visit.

Have the same affect about any suggestions for changing your diet. You don't really believe it will make any difference, you like the way you eat, and "eating healthy" is expensive.

If the student doctor "lectures" you, become more resistant and upset. Tell him/her it is your business whether you smoke or not and how you eat and he/she should mind their own business.

Do agree to come back for a follow up visit.
