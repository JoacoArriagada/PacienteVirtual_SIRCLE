**CASE ONE:**

Dawn is a 49-year-old woman with a past medical history of diabetes mellitus type 2 who presents today to establish care with you as her new PCP. You learn that she has been generally healthy in the past without major illnesses. Her family history is remarkable for diabetes and hypertension in both of her parents, but no history of cancers or autoimmune disorders. She works as a manager at a local department store. She reports drinking alcohol occasionally, mostly in social situations, and smoking approximately 1 pack per day for the past 25 years. She denies illicit drug use.

**CASE ONE continued:**

Dawn reveals that she is currently exclusively sexually active with her female partner of 25 years, Louise. They have two young adult children together who were conceived using a sperm bank.

**CASE ONE continued:**

Screening for intimate partner violence is negative. A review of systems reveals that she recently has been having trouble with insomnia because of worrying about financial problems. She requests a referral to a therapist. She states that despite her worries, her mood is generally good, and she continues to enjoy her hobbies and her time with her family. She denies any suicidal or homicidal ideation. She scores a 2 on the GAD-2, so you do not pursue other management options for her anxiety currently. The remainder of the history and physical exam is normal. You refer her for psychological counseling and a yearly eye exam.

A few months later, Dawn returns. Upon reviewing her EHR, you notice that she attended only one session with the counselor. She states that when the therapist asked her if she had any children and she said two, he went on to ask about her children’s father. Dawn avoided the questions, completed the interview, and did not return.
