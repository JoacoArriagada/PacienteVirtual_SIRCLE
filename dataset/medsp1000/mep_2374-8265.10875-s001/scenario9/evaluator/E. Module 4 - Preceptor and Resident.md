**CASE TWO:**

**Sarah is a 38year old woman who presents to your clinic for follow up of hypertension; her partner, Janice has accompanied her to the visit. They would like to discuss options for biologic parenthood.**

1. **What are some options for same sex couples desiring biological parenthood i.e. children who share DNA with the parents.**
2. Artificial insemination: This involves using donor sperm to fertilize an egg in vivo. This is an option for female same sex couples, if one woman is providing the egg, and carrying the pregnancy. Gay couples may also employ this technique with a gestational carrier. Some choose to use both partners’ sperm so that the fertilizing partner is unknown.
3. In vitro fertilization: Could be an option to involve both partners in a female same sex couple, this may involve taking an egg from one partner, fertilizing it and implanting the embryo in the other woman.
4. In vitro fertilization using an egg donor, as well as a gestational carrier is an option for gay couples with use of one or both partners’ sperm.

**CASE TWO continued:**

**As you finish your discussion about options for conception, Sarah mentions that she worries that if she ever ended up unconscious in the hospital, Janice would not be allowed to act as a decision maker since they are not married. This worry stems from an experience that friends of theirs had. They ask if there is anything they could do to prevent this from happening to them.**

1. **As a physician, is there any advice you can give them?** **Who does health care surrogate decision making default to in Pennsylvania? (\*\*this question would need to be updated to reflect local laws)**

Advise them to meet with a lawyer to institute advance directives in the case of incapacitation, as well as health care surrogate/representative.

In the absence of a designated healthcare agent under a valid advance healthcare directive, the following persons can act as health care representatives in descending order of priority

1. A person chosen by the individual by informing the attending physician, while the individual was of sound mind
2. The individual’s spouse (unless a divorce action is pending)
3. The individual’s adult child
4. The individual’s parent
5. The individual’s adult brother or sister
6. An adult who has knowledge of the individual’s preferences and values

As you can see, in Pennsylvania, non-married partners do not automatically default as healthcare surrogate decision makers, but instead to family members, with the adult child as first priority.
