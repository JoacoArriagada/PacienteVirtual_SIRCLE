**CASE TWO**

**Joan is a 42-year-old woman with hypertension, obesity and tobacco use who was previously seen by a physician who is no longer in your practice. She has not seen a doctor in the last three years since her prior PCP moved and would like a “checkup”. As you take her social history, she identifies as bisexual and reports that she has been exclusively sexually active with women for the past 2 years, and recently began a new sexual relationship. Since that time, she developed vaginal discharge and is wondering if there is a problem.**

She should have pap testing with HPV co-test. A discussion about mammography for breast cancer screening is recommended.

In the Women’s Health Initiative study, Valanis et al (2000) showed that 14% of lifetime lesbians and 17.6% of the bisexuals reported ever having had any cancer, compared with 11.9% of heterosexual women11.

**Cervical Cancer:** The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends Pap testing every 3 years for all women ages 21 to 65 years who have a cervix, **regardless of sexual history**, or every 5 years for all women aged 30-65 with HPV co-testing.

A study performed prior to the 2013 USPSTF recommendations showed that under the previous screening guidelines, 75-84% of US women aged >18 reported having a pap test within the last 3 years, while the percentage among lesbians was 44-57%14.

A national US survey of self-identified lesbians suggests that the barriers to routine cervical screening include

* perceived susceptibility to cervical cancer (from women and physicians)
* perceived lack of benefits to screening and seriousness of cervical cancer.

These findings suggest that PCPs have a role to discuss sexual orientation, and to educate patients on susceptibility to cervical cancer and benefits of screening.

**Breast Cancer Screening**:

* Studies on relative incidence of breast cancer in lesbian and bisexual women compared to others are mixed
* However, a 2013 study utilizing the National Health Interview Survey showed **an increased mortality rate** from breast cancer in lesbian and bisexual (LB) women (RR 3.2, 95% CI 1.01-10.21)12.

The Institute of Medicine suggests a higher risk for breast cancer in this population because of higher prevalence of some breast cancer risk factors, such as nulliparity, alcohol use, smoking, and obesity13.

Other postulated reasons for this disparity include decreased screening rates, lower income, lack of insurance coverage, as well as a lack of positive relationships with providers11.

Physicians should be sensitive to the need for shared decision making required for breast cancer screening and the first step to accomplish this is by relationship-building.

**Endometrial and Ovarian Cancer Screening:** Risk factors which are increased in LB women are nulliparity, obesity, and decreased use of oral contraceptives. However, despite these risk factors, **routine screening for endometrial and ovarian cancer is not recommended.**

The recommendations for screening of bacterial STIs in WSW **do not differ** from other women; however, in a study of US women aged 15-44, self-reported viral STD rates were significantly higher among bisexual women (15.0% to 17.2%) than among lesbians (2.3% to 6.7%) 15.

In another cross-sectional survey, the following incidence was noted

* 31.4 % bacterial vaginosis
* 18.4% candida yeast infections
* 1-2% Genital warts > genital herpes > trichomoniasis
* <1% Chlamydia, pelvic inflammatory disease and gonorrhea, and were diagnosed only in women who had sexual histories with men16.

**CASE TWO continued:**

**You diagnose her with bacterial vaginosis and recommend treatment with metronidazole.**

A study of monogamous couples demonstrated that female sex partners share identical genital Lactobacillus strains, suggesting that it is transmitted between partners. However, **routine screening of asymptomatic partners is not recommended**. There is also no recommendation by the CDC for empiric treatment for partners of women diagnosed with BV.

Safe sexual practices for women include condoms with sex toys or sex with men, or dental dams for oral sex. Sex toys should be washed between uses.
