**CASE THREE:**

You are assigned a new patient who has established care with your clinic. Jordan is a 30-year-old patient beginning graduate school looking for a medication refill. On your standard admission paperwork, you notice that the patient has not selected "M" or "F" on the form. Past medical history is notable for asthma and current medications include albuterol, spironolactone, and monthly estradiol valerate injections. The patient is roomed, and review of vitals includes height of 5'7" and weight 174 lbs., BP 132/78, and HR 74. You enter to find Jordan is Caucasian with medium length hair, and baggy clothing.

**CASE THREE continued:**

Jordan reveals that she identifies as a woman. She reports feeling "trapped in the wrong body" since she was in elementary school. While born a male, she reports that she began transitioning to a female when she was in college. She struggled with depression during this period, but for the past 5 years has been meeting with a therapist and describes her mood as good. She is beginning graduate school in psychology. She lives in an apartment with a supportive partner. She denies tobacco or alcohol use.

**CASE THREE continued:**

Jordan is in a relationship with a male who is aware of her transition. Screening for intimate partner violence is negative. The remainder of the history and physical exam are performed. The patient has not undergone gender affirming surgeries. She is happy with her breast size which has increased since taking estrogen. After age-appropriate vaccinations, the annual exam is complete. She asks, "Oh by the way, can you refill my estrogen and spironolactone? I have never been off of them and would hate to start seeing negative effects.
