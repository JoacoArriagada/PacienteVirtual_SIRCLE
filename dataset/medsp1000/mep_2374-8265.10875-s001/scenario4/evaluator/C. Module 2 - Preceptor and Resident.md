**Optimal Primary Care for LGBT Patients**

**Module 2: Cultural competencies; performing a sensitive history and physical exam**

**Learning Objectives:**

1. Elicit relevant information about sexual history, sexual practices, and gender history in preferred patient terminology.
2. Implement basic gender-neutral practices in ambulatory clinics.
3. Recognize that implicit bias about sexuality, gender, and sex anatomy may adversely affect communication.
4. Engage in effective self-reflection processes to diminish the effects of implicit bias.
5. Conduct a sensitive physical exam using gender-affirming terminology.

**CASE TWO:**

Jamie is a 32-year-old woman with an unremarkable past medical history who presents to your clinic for a routine pap smear. She is accompanied by her boyfriend, Trent who remains in the room during the procedure. You leave the room to give Jamie some privacy to get dressed. When you reenter the room to discuss follow up, she says “Doctor, my boyfriend and I were talking, and we were wondering if he should get a pap smear also?”

1. **How would you respond to Jamie’s question? What initial reaction/thoughts did you have to her question?**

*Residents can discuss initial reactions and subsequent responses*

Key Messages

* You could appropriately respond by looking nonjudgmentally at Trent and affirm the appropriateness of the question. Some words to use are

“You’re wondering what medical care you need to stay healthy. What makes you think a Pap smear is important for you?”

**Preceptor**: “In this case Trent is a transgender man who was born with female anatomy, but his gender identity is male. He refers to himself as a man, may take on stereotypically male mannerisms and style of dress, and may also medically or surgically alter his body to affirm his male gender identity. Overall, it is important to have the tools to affirm your LGBT patients*.”*

1. **What is the concept of gender affirmation?**

**Gender affirmation** is the **interpersonal, interactive** process where a person receives social recognition, and support for their chosen gender identity and expression. As physicians, we have a role in gender affirmation either by prescribing hormonal therapy to patients, or by simply supporting our patients. Gender affirming surgery is the term used for surgical gender transformation
