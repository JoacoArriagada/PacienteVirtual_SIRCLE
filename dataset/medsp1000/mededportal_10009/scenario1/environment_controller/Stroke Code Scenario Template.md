Stroke Code Simulation

# **Case No1** *(what learners will see if they are given a schedule ahead of time )*

# **Case Title / Topic: TPA Stroke Case**

## Environment

1. Venue Set Up
2. Type of Room / Area: ED room / critical care bay
3. Beds / stretchers: usual ED stretcher
4. Other large equipment: Cardiac/BP monitor, crash cart

Electronic/computer screen

B. Supplies

1. IVs & Fluids Needed: IV -18-gauge which will be taped on standardized patient, not inserted. This will be “placed” prior to the neurology resident arriving.
   1. Foley
   2. EKG leads for cardiac monitoring
2. Drugs needed (empty vials with labels on, nurse confederate will act as if administering when prompted but no drug actually given to the actors)
   1. Hydralazine
   2. Labetalol
   3. Nicardipine
   4. TPA
   5. Atropine
   6. Dopamine / Dobutamine
   7. D50
3. Other Medical Equipment Needed:
   1. EKG lead tracing
   2. Lab result print out for basic chemistry, glucose, Hgb. Most commonly basic labs would be sent out by ED resident before calling stroke code, so they should be back upon the resident asking about them. Only glucose is necessary before TPA administration, but in this case INR would be critical as well since the patient is on warfarin. INR will not return during the case, but if participants ask to do a chart review, they find that the INR was 1.4 earlier that morning from a clinic visit.

C Props

1. EKGs: Regular sinus rhythm, also BP results on patient monitor
2. CT scans: CT head, CTA head and neck (shows thrombus in Left MCA) but no cerebral hypodensity involving greater than 1/3 territory of MCA territory
3. Lab results in a printable / electronic format to show participants
   1. Chemistry is normal, aside from glucose 45
   2. Hgb normal
   3. INR 1.4
4. Hospital chart information or other materials (*basic EMS run sheet – does not have a fingerstick blood glucose listed or performed)* to show the participants
5. Other Props - Pocket card/book with tpa inclusion/exclusion criteria.

## Actors

1. Roles
2. ED Nurse
3. Patient
4. Family member
5. Neurology faculty
6. Action Role
   * + 1. ED Nurse – nurse’s competence will be inversely proportional to the learner’s competence
       2. Patient – aphasic but cooperative
       3. Family Member – as per instructions below in play of case guidelines. Overall cooperative and provides necessary information when asked.
       4. Neurology faculty (over phone only) – only is used if residents require additional assistance in the case and cannot be directed well enough with nurse confederate prompts.
7. Participants of simulation
8. Neurology residents in groups of 3 (one resident running stroke code and other 2 acting as team members), arrive once stroke code is called
9. Additional nurses and medical students can be learner team members when desired.
10. **Case Information**
11. **Case Narrative:** Scenario background given to participants (scene introduction should be given freely but additional details only revealed if asked for)
12. Scene introduction for Neurology resident: You are rounding at your hospital as G2 resident with your attending and your pager goes off indicating a stroke code. You are about to enter the critical care bay in the ED.
13. Case Synopsis (to be provided to moderator and standardized patient, family member and nurse):

Mr Frazer (MRN 1234567) is a 68 year old male who was last seen normal at X pm (time should be set to fall within 2 hours of stroke code simulation) by his son. Son went out for 10 minutes, when he came back, he saw Mr. Frazer lying on the floor with unintelligible speech and unable to move his right side. Son immediately calls 911 and patient is brought to the ED. Patient came within 2 hours of time of symptoms onset.

ED resident first assesses the patient and then stroke code should be activated, at which time neurology resident joins the simulation.

Neurology resident with his/her team(total of 3) enters the room, examines the patient, and performs the required assessment, formulates the plan based on exam, labs, imaging and talking to family members.

The confederate nurse should provide lab results when asked for by any of the participating residents.

This encounter is closed after 20 minutes.

1. Chief complaint: Cannot talk/speech is garbled, is not moving right side.
2. Past medical history: Hypertension, Paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, Chronic renal failure, Congestive cardiac failure, Diabetes. No contraindications to tPA.
3. Medications: Warfarin, Digoxin, Diltiazem, Atenolol, Lipitor, Insulin (lantus and aspart)
4. Allergies: NKA
5. Family history: no history of stroke or vascular risk factors in past
6. Scenario conditions initially
7. History patient (or family, parent, etc) gives: Since patient cannot talk family member will provide history, details mentioned in above case synopsis.
8. Patients initial exam:
   1. VS: BP 190/85 HR 88 RR 15 SpO2 98% T 37.4
   2. General:
      1. HEENT: normal, PERRL
      2. Neck: supple, FROM
      3. CV: No m/r/g
      4. Pulm: CTAB
      5. Abd: normal
      6. Extremities: no trauma, no c/c/e
      7. Skin: no rash
   3. Neuro: Weakness of right face and right arm more than leg. Garbled unintelligible speech, unable to read aloud and write if asked to.
9. Scenario branch points:
10. If no initial self-identification by the resident lead, he or she is to be ignored by patient and family member. Family member will continue to address and talk to the nurse, and be dismissive of questions asked by the resident. If no self-identification by the resident lead 2-3 minutes into the case, the family member will ask the resident “who are you?”. Once the resident introduces themselves to the family member, they will be cooperative and conversant.
11. If no time of onset clarified within 5 minutes of neurology resident arriving to case, nurse prompts and asks the patient / family member what time the symptoms started.
12. If glucose checked, nurse will report that fingerstick glucose is 45.
13. If glucose not checked, allow the case to proceed. If not self corrected, patient would receive tpa and provider will learn that patient was hypoglycemic only after BMP comes back (Glucose 45)
14. If D50 is given or not given, neuro exam does not change.
15. BP continues to rise over first few minutes of the case regardless of learner actions. BP is cycled at 2 minutes into the case and reads 206/95.
16. If BP not treated, BP continues to rise to 250/130 over first 8 minutes of the case.
17. If one dose of =<20mg labetalol given, no change in BP
18. If total of 30-50 mg labetalol given, BP goes to 165/72
19. If total of 51-80mg labetalol give, BP drops to 124/62
20. If hydralazine, nicardipine, or other antihypertensives are given, the appropriate starting dose does produce desired effects of BP < 185, and a second dose or increase in dose is required to meet this endpoint. If excessive doses of any antihypertensives are given, BP will precipitously drop as in labetalol dosing instructions above.
21. If participants discuss tPA administration with the patient’s family member, the family member is easily agreeable to this plan.
22. If participants do not discuss tPA with the family member within 10 minutes of neurology resident arriving to the case, then
23. Family member asks “isn’t there anything you can do to help my dad?”
24. If no response from resident regarding tPA, family member states “can you give him some sort of medication or something to help him?”
25. If resident still does not discuss tPA, neurology faculty calls in and asks resident about the patient including their thoughts on the management plan, and gives some guidance / suggestions if needed. Neurology faculty will also call in if participants are having difficulty with management of other aspects of the case after 15 minutes has elapsed in the case.
26. If INR is sent, this will not return during the case.
27. If participants already asked nurse to send INR prior to asking, s/he will state “the result isn’t back yet, I sent the tubes though.”
28. If the participants did not ask the nurse to send INR prior to asking, s/he will state “oh, did you want me to send that? I can do it now.”
29. If participants ask to look in the patient’s chart to see his last INR, the nurse can look in the chart and report that the INR was 1.4 this morning. If participants do not ask to do chart review, the case will continue.
30. If INR is not ordered or asked about, there is red fluid in foley following tPA administration (nurse trained to prompt)
