# **Case Title / Topic: TPA Stroke Case**

   * + 1. Patient – aphasic but cooperative

**Case Information**

**Case Synopsis:** Mr Frazer (MRN 1234567) is a 68 year old male who was last seen normal at X pm (time should be set to fall within 2 hours of stroke code simulation) by his son. Son went out for 10 minutes, when he came back, he saw Mr. Frazer lying on the floor with unintelligible speech and unable to move his right side. Son immediately calls 911 and patient is brought to the ED. Patient came within 2 hours of time of symptoms onset.

ED resident first assesses the patient and then stroke code should be activated at which time neurology resident joins the simulation.

Neurology resident with his/her team(total of 3) enters the room, examines the patient, and performs the required assessment, formulates the plan based on exam, labs, imaging and talking to family members.

1. Chief complaint: Cannot talk/speech is garbled, is not moving right side.
2. Past medical history: Hypertension, Paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, Chronic renal failure, Congestive cardiac failure, Diabetes. No contraindications to tPA.
3. Medications: Warfarin, Digoxin, Diltiazem, Atenolol, Lipitor, Insulin (lantus and aspart)
4. Allergies: NKA
5. Family history: no history of stroke or vascular risk factors in past
6. History patient (or family, parent, etc) gives: Since patient cannot talk family member will provide history, details mentioned in above case synopsis.
7. Patient deficits to act out: Weakness of right face and right arm more than leg. Garbled unintelligible speech, unable to read aloud and write if asked to.
8. Scenario branch points:
9. If no initial self-identification by the resident lead, he or she is to be ignored by patient and family member. Family member will continue to address and talk to the nurse, and be dismissive of questions asked by the resident. If no self-identification by the resident lead 2-3 minutes into the case, the family member will ask the resident “who are you?”. Once the resident introduces themselves to the family member, they will be cooperative and conversant.
10. If participants discuss tPA administration with the patient’s family member, the family member is easily agreeable to this plan.
11. If participants do not discuss tPA with the family member within 10 minutes of neurology resident arriving to the case, then
12. Family member asks “isn’t there anything you can do to help my dad?”
13. If no response from resident regarding tPA, family member states “can you give him some sort of medication or something to help him?”
