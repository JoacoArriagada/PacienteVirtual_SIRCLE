**Case Instructor Guide: A Pediatric Death from Non-Accidental Trauma**

**Title:**  A Pediatric Death from Non-Accidental Trauma

(*Alternatively known as Kobayashi Maru)*

**Resource Files:** Please review the scenario prior to running the case. Vital signs with regard to scenario branches are included in the provided table and in some cases may be preprogrammed or modified as needed.

* 1. Flow Diagram - Helpful for case perspective, and SIM mannequin setup.
  2. CXR images should be shown when prompted via distributed hard copy or projection to participants.
  3. EKG images should be shown when prompted via distributed hard copy or projection to participants.
  4. Blood work should be shown when prompted via hard copy or projection
  5. If CT head is requested, it should be simply stated the scan is normal with no acute findings.
  6. Labs not included herein should be stated to be within normal limits.
  7. PowerPoint resource presentation with hyperlinks to resource items.

**Deployment:** This case was run several times as part of our simulation education training during our resident conferences. Teams are selected beforehand, and know that they will be performing in a simulation on the assigned day. No prior information about the case was divulged to team members or the audience before the case begins.

Prior to implementing the case, the faculty, academic chief residents, and technician rehearsed the case several times. In these rehearsal sessions, we played out a variety of pathways driven by potential participant choices. Each team member had a printout of the Kobayashi Maru case flow diagram (Page 5). The day of the case, we recruited two medical students to play the mother and grandmother. They were coached in terms of their grief reactions.

The teams were comprised of four trainees (PGY-3, PGY-2, PGY-1, MS-4, with the team being lead by the upper level resident. One faculty member had the sole responsibility of observing the training scenario. One faculty and one academic chief resident played roles in the case (Nurse, EMS), and also served as backup to our technician and stimulus presenter. Our second academic chief ran the PowerPoint slides that projected the case stimuli and displayed the decorticate posturing during the case. Our technician controlled the Laerdal SimMan(R).

We recommend at minimum one faculty member have the sole role of observer with no technical responsibilities to capture the nuances of the team's medical and ethical choices. The debrief session should be led by the faculty with knowledge of the case construct and without responsibilities during the case enactment.

**Limitations:**

The case is complex. It requires rehearsal and preparation beforehand, and is not easily implemented on a whim. At minimum it requires 3 confederates, a technician, someone to project the stimuli, and a faculty observer.

There are case nuances - trainees may focus on the historical aspects of the case to try to sleuth out the cause of the injuries. It is important to know that the case can be moved forward through a decline in patient vitals. We have our technician put the vitals on a timeline of decline to ensure the case moves forward appropriately.
