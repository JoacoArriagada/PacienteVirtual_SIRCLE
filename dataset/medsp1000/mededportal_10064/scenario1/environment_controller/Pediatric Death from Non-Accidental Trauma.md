1. Case Narrative:
   1. The patient is Eric, an 8 year old boy who was found unresponsive in bed by grandmother who lives across the street. The patient was left in the care of his step-father while his mother was working late all week. The grandmother came over to check on her grandchild, and found him in bed obtunded and called the emergency medical services (EMS). The patient's step-father was nowhere to be found, and his car was gone. Upon EMS arrival, the patient was found to have a GCS of 3. The patient was intubated at the scene and brought to the ED. Upon arrival, abuse should be suspected when the patient is undressed: the patient presents with multiple bruises and abrasions evident upon exposure. [While no-one in the SIM knows exactly what happened. The back story is that the step-father threw the patient down the cellar stairs after he got mad at him for breaking his dinner plate. The step-father then placed the patient in bed and fled upon realizing the extent of the patient's injuries and noting the patient was unresponsive.] Regardless of correct management plan, correct or not, the patient will end up dying. There are various paths of decline:
      1. If management is correct - the patient will recover from his hemorrhagic shock, his epidural hematoma will expand with the improved blood pressure, and the patient will herniate and die.
      2. If management is incorrect - without addressing the patient's hemorrhagic shock, the patient will have PEA arrest from hypovolemia and eventually die.
      3. If there is delayed recognition of hemorrhagic shock - upon treatment, the patient's blood pressure will improve, his epidural hematoma will expand, and the patient will herniate and die.
   2. Patient Background (per Grandmother):
      1. ROS - unable to obtain
      2. Past medical history - None
      3. Surgical history - None
      4. Medications - None
      5. Allergies - None
      6. Social History - Divorced parents, Mother recently re-married. Father lives upstate.
      7. Family History - Older sibling (different father) who is in college.
   3. Initial Presentation: The patient is wheeled in by EMS on a stretcher, with an endotracheal tube in place. EMS was called by the Grandmother when she could not arouse patient. EMS intubated the patient out of concern for the airway given the patient's GCS of 3. The patient was placed in a cervical collar and transported to the hospital on a backboard. The patient arrives GCS 3-T after etomidate, succinylcholine, and vecuronium. Fingerstick per EMS is 97mg/dL. En route, EMS noted tachycardia and an initial blood pressure (BP) of 85/40, obtained a 16guage IV in the LEFT antecubital area, and administered a normal saline bolus. The patient's grandmother has called the patient's mother who is now on her way to the hospital. The patient's mother will arrive about 5-10 minutes into the simulation and will be extremely emotional and concerned about her son on arrival. The SIM team will have to decide how to engage the mother.
   4. After the paramedics exit, careful inspection and disrobing of the patient will reveal multiple bruises over the body, a left parietal hematoma, a large left chest contusion, and abrasions over the LEFT pelvic area. Radiology will be immediately available, if there are questions as to the findings. An EKG (EKG 1) will show sinus tachycardia.
   5. PEA Branch - Failure to identify and treat the LEFT hemothorax, the pelvic fracture, or both will result in the patient losing pulses and going into PEA secondary to hypovolemia / hemorrhage. The patient will remain in PEA arrest until the causes of the hypovolemic shock are identified and treated. If the causes of PEA are identified and addressed in a timely fashion (<10min from simulation start), the patient will enter the Decorticate Branch of the simulation. Otherwise the patient will remain in PEA, the rhythm will change to asystole and the patient will die (circa 20min from simulation start).
   6. Decorticate Branch - Identification and treatment of the traumatic injuries will result in an improvement in blood pressure. Unfortunately, this will also increase cerebral perfusion, and worsen Eric's epidural hematoma. About 1-2 minutes after addressing the patient's hemothorax and pelvic fracture, the patient develops decorticate posturing. A Cushing reflex (hypertension and bradycardia, no abnormal respirations - needed because the patient is intubated & paralyzed) will be present on vital signs. If an EKG is performed (EKG2), cerebral T waves will be seen.
   7. Final stage - Regardless of the path taken, the patient's perfusion status will decline, he will manifest an idioventricular rhythm, which further declines to asystole. At this time Mom will become distraught, and the simulation team will need to address the emotional distress of the mother and grandmother. With advanced learners, the instructor may choose to have the family insist upon further resuscitative efforts. The SIM team should report the abuse to child protective services and/or law enforcement.
   8. Vital Signs - Vital signs will continuously trend between stages. Specific stages are outlined in the following table:

1. Instructors Notes
   1. Medical Case Flow Details
      1. The patient will present with tenuous vital signs which will worsen with inaction.
      2. If there is inaction or a delay in identifying any of the traumatic causes of hemorrhage / hypotension, the BP will decline, and the patient will develop pulseless electrical activity (PEA). In this particular case, if the trauma is identified / considered, hypovolemia should be a leading potential cause of PEA.
      3. If the catastrophic injuries are identified and treated appropriately, a brief (1-2min) period of stable VS will occur. This stage can also be reached even if the patient enters the PEA pathway as long as the PEA resuscitation is appropriate. This brief period will end with the patient having an episode of decorticate posturing (one of the actors should be designated to demonstrate this posture as needed).
      4. Upon decorticate posturing, the patient will enter a stage where the Cushing reflex is present: hypertension and bradycardia will be evident. Abnormal respirations will not be present as the patient is intubated and paralyzed. An EKG (EKG2 - if obtained - shows deep cerebral T waves.)
      5. Over approximately 5 minutes, the bradycardic rhythm will trend towards an idioventricular rhythm. No interventions will alter this course. If fluids / blood products are administered the presentation of the idioventricular rhythm can be accelerated.
      6. After 2-3 minutes, asystole will be present. No interventions will alter the rhythm. The simulation participants will have to decide when to end resuscitative efforts and pronounce the patient dead.
   2. Social Case Flow Details
      1. The simulation participants will have to make a choice early on as to whether to include the mother and grandmother in the resuscitation room.
      2. Someone on the simulation team should be designated to communicate with the family, and explain the resuscitation process to the grandmother and mother when she arrives.
      3. If the family is not invited into the resuscitation by the time the patient passes away, when the outcome is divulged, Mom will suffer an acute grief reaction and pass out on floor.
      4. A social worker or pastoral/spiritual care provider should be offered during the resuscitation to help the family through the process.
   3. Non-Accidental Trauma flow details
      1. This is a case of non-accidental trauma or child abuse, and the SIM team should recognize this.
      2. The team will need to need to report their suspicions of non-accidental trauma to the local child protective services and / or law enforcement.
   4. In the initial phase, the case should move forward based on the declining trend of the vital signs. The nurse can make statements to cue the participants if clinical findings remain unrecognized. PEA should be considered when the monitor blood pressure cuff is unable to obtain a blood pressure.
