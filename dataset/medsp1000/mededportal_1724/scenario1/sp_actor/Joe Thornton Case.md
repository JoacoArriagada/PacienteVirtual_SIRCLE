# 3. **BELIEFS & PREFERENCES**

It is expected (hoped) that the student will learn various things about what is going on in Mr. Thornton’s life and how his health is affecting his life, as well as how his life is affecting (or has the potential to affect) his health.

In Mr. Thornton’s case, this may include (but is not limited to):

- Various reasons why he is not compliant with recommendations for diet and exercise
- That he is beginning to realize that his sadness over the loss of his wife has impacted his physical health

ANY of these issues may be addressed by the student when discussing treatment.  What is important is that the student indicates that s/he has heard your desires by reflecting AT LEAST ONE OF THEM back to you when discussing/recommending the next step(s) in your care (his/her “action plan” -- ***framed the action plan in such a way as to incorporate my beliefs and preferences***).  For example, rather than just saying, “I want you to take all your medications, eat a low salt diet, and start exercising daily,” it is desirable for him/her to say something like:

***“It sounds like you are feeling overwhelmed by your medical conditions and the other stressors in your life.  Perhaps we should start with some small changes that won’t feel too overwhelming.”***

(This is just an example; do not be waiting for this specific phrase.)  The student does not necessarily need to solve Joe’s problem or get him to agree, only to address it -- to indicate that s/he has heard the patient’s beliefs.
