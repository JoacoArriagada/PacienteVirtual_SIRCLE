| **PATIENT NAME:** John  **PATIENT AGE:** 6 years old  **PATIENT WEIGHT:** 20 kg  **CHIEF COMPLAINT:** Rash, fever, cough | |
|  | |
| **Brief narrative description of case** | John is a 6-year-old male presenting to the emergency department with rash, fever, and cough, found to have hyperleukocytosis and a new diagnosis of leukemia, requiring treatment of possible sepsis, evaluation of blood product transfusion requirements, management of electrolyte abnormalities, and recognition of neurologic complications requiring airway management. |

| **HPI** | John is a 6-year-old male with fevers to 102 deg F for 3 days during the COVID-19 pandemic. He has had cough, congestion, headaches, and fatigue and is brought to the ED by his mother from home with fever, fast breathing, feeling tired with worsening headache and a new onset rash.  On presentation, he is brought in by mother to the ED after mom found him uncontrollably agitated at home and uncommunicative with her. Per mom, she last saw him normal 1.5 hours prior to arrival when she denied his request to leave the house. When she went to check on him 1 hour later, he appeared angry and aggressive and was noted to be "worked up," sweating, and unwilling to talk to her. This is not his first behavioral outburst requiring him to come to the ED, and with father's help they were able to load him into the car and bring him to the hospital.  SAMPLE history if asked:  S: Was sent to an outside lab by primary care physician (PCP) after telehealth visit this morning for COVID-19 testing and basic labs. Advised to go to ED as mom called PCP later that day and reported he was hot, breathing heavy and less active this afternoon. He did not want to come down for breakfast or watch videos/play games/read books. Sleeping on couch and not wanting to play with four-year-old brother or eat lunch now. Increasing complaint of headache that started last night but improves with ibuprofen. Also, mother just noticed about 30 minutes ago red spots on legs, arms, face, and trunk. She also notes that he looks paler and had a nosebleed that resolved earlier today and had one last week, but she thought it was from digital trauma (picking). She also notes that he has had gum bleeding when he brushes his teeth too hard.  A: Amoxicillin--rash  M: ibuprofen every six hours for past 3 days for pain, two doses of ondansetron yesterday prescribed virtually by PCP  PMH: febrile seizure at 2 years of age, vaccines up to date, developmental normal in 1st grade doing well with e-learning, played soccer regularly pre-pandemic, no sick contacts- has not been in school for three weeks because of the pandemic  L: has not had anything to eat all day, vomited after drank Gatorade  E: hot, breathing heavy and less active this afternoon | | |
