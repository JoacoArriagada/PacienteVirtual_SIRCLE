**Ideal Scenario Flow**

***Provide a detailed narrative description of the way this case should flow if participants perform in the ideal fashion.***

Participants are oriented to the video conferencing platform. They are provided a brief scenario introduction and allotted two minutes to organize their team and assign roles. The telesimulation opens with a video of the patient, who is crying/agitated, tachypneic, and covered in petechiae/purpura. The survey doctor immediately asks for a primary survey, states that they are placing the patient on monitors and asks for intravenous access with rapid fluid resuscitation. Initial diagnostic testing includes laboratory studies and chest x-ray. Teams recognize the possibility of new onset leukemia with hyperleukocytosis and initiate treatment with antibiotics, blood products, and continued IV fluids. The team recognizes electrolyte derangements and danger of hyperkalemia with tumor lysis syndrome along with peaked T waves on ECG. The team continuously reassesses the patient’s vital signs, airway, breathing and circulation. The team recognizes that the patient is displaying stroke-like symptoms likely secondary to leukostasis and discusses the need for leukoreduction, stat head CT, and the need for intubation. Teams may request oncology or other specialty consultation, consultants will not

be immediately available. Team continues acute stabilization until facilitator arrives as embedded participant Pediatric Intensive Care Unit physician and asks team leader for clinical summary statement.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Failure to recognize new onset leukemia and tumor lysis syndrome. Most participants quickly identified the possibility of new onset leukemia and tumor lysis syndrome and initiated treatment. If participants do not recognize this, we suggest that an embedded participant nurse suggest “Could this be oncologic?” or “What about tumor lysis syndrome?”
2. Failure to begin treatment with hydration and antibiotics. If participants do not initiate aggressive hydration and antibiotic therapy, facilitators may have embedded participants suggest administration of fluids and antibiotics with a statement such as “I think these patients need a lot of fluid and I think they always need antibiotics.”
3. Failure to recognize and support the patient’s airway and breathing. Most learners reassessed airway, breathing and circulation frequently throughout the scenario and in response to interventions. If participants are not performing repeat assessments, facilitator or embedded participant may call out a vital sign to prompt participants.
4. Failure to recognize electrolyte derangements. Most learners addressed this expediently, but if not, an embedded participant such as the parent may state, “Are those labs normal? How will we fix them?” Additionally, the embedded participant nurse may state “Should we worry about hyperkalemia? What about an ECG?” and/or “I think we should treat the high uric acid.”
5. Failure to recognize hyperleukocytosis. Many learners recognized concern for stroke right away and initiated appropriate management. If participants do not verbalize this concern, a facilitator may verbally prompt participants.
