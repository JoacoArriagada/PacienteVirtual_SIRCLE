Simulation of Respiratory Distress for the 3rd Year Medical Student

Learning Objectives:

1. Identify respiratory distress and its etiology in children.

2. Determine appropriate initial assessment of patients with respiratory distress.

3. Identify the vital signs necessary for assessment of respiratory distress and the variations in children.

4. Demonstrate preliminary management of patients presenting with respiratory distress.

5. Identify necessary resources to use with a patient in respiratory distress including different oxygen delivery methods, cardiopulmonary monitors, and IV access.

6. Perform the key skill of providing bag mask ventilation to a child in respiratory distress.

7. Use key elements involved in teamwork when working in an emergent situation, including assigning roles and designating team leaders.

Station 2

Simulation Portion

1. Introduction: The students will be expected to participate as if the simulation is in real time. Review what your simulation mannequin is capable of (breathing, heart sounds, pulses, vascular access points etc.). Allow the students to have access to the same equipment they would have in the patient care areas and give them time to familiarize themselves with the equipment. Discuss with the students the concept of team roles during a code situation, choosing a code leader and assigning specific roles to each participant. Choose a student to be the initial responder. During the case, the person running the simulation should not interact with the students except to answer prompts (eg state laboratory results) and the students should be reminded of this prior to starting the case.

6. Simulator set up: 6 month old with stridor with vital signs of HR 170, RR 20, BP not obtainable, O2 sat 88%

7. Case Scenario: You’re taking care of a 6 month old patient who was admitted with a cough and poor PO intake. Mom comes to find you to tell you that she is looking worse and has now started making a strange noise every time she breathes in that she has never heard before.

8. The case prompt should be enough to get the students started. Only give the included information that is asked for by the students.

1. History: The patient is a 6 month old who developed a cough yesterday and has been congested. She was admitted to the hospital because she was having trouble breathing and had a high fever to 102 but now she is making a new sound every time she breathes in, both when she is active and when she is resting. She doesn’t have any medical problems, she was born full term and she has gotten all of her vaccines. Her sister is at home and sick as well.

2. Physical Exam: Vital signs are HR 170, RR 20, BP not obtainable, O2 sat 88%. On exam she is well appearing and has audible inspiratory stridor. Her head and neck exam is otherwise normal. She has abdominal breathing and subcostal and supraclavicular retractions. On lung exam she has rhonchi bilaterally without rales or wheezing. The remainder of her exam is normal for age.

3. Critical Points: Students should ask for a more detailed history and a more detailed exam which can be provided from the above information and from examining the high fidelity simulator if available.
   1. Based on the above history and physical the appropriate action would be to administer oxygen via nasal cannula or facemask, give nebulized racemic epinephrine and give steroids
      1. If oxygen is given via nasal cannula, the child’s oxygen saturation should only improve slightly but the stridor remains, even at rest
      2. If nebulized racemic epinephrine is given, the child improves significantly with improved oxygen saturation, decreased respiratory rate and stridor that is only audible during times of distress.
      3. If decadron is given, improvement will not be seen for approximately 2 hours and will not affect the initial management of this case.
      4. If steroids and racemic epinephrine are given together, the child will stabilize with normal vital signs and an exam only significant for persistent rhonchi bilaterally.
      5. If albuterol is given with oxygen, this will likely improve the oxygen saturation and decrease the respiratory rate, but this is a temporary intervention and will only help while the nebulizer is running. When the treatment finishes the patient’s symptoms will all return.
      6. If IM or IV epinephrine is given (for the management of anaphylaxis) this will lead to a temporary improvement, with a decreased respiratory rate, increased oxygen saturation and improved stridor. The heart rate will increase and this would not be the appropriate care for this clinical situation.
      7. Inappropriate actions at this point would be a chest xray or lateral neck xray as they would both delay acute care and initial management. If the patient were not to respond to racemic epinephrine and steroids, they would be appropriate steps, looking for a foreign body or epiglottitis.
   2. Ideal management is nebulized racemic epinephrine and decadon. If these steps are not followed, oxygen saturation will decrease, the respiratory rate will increase and the child will eventually go into respiratory failure and require BVM.

4. End points for this case:
   1. Instructor can set a time limit, we recommend 15 minutes allowing time for a debrief after the case.
   2. Alternatively, instructor can stop the case if the students perfectly manage and stabilize the child.
   3. If the students management does not meet the critical points do not let the child expire but rather remind students that a child can be supported by bag mask ventilation for a long time while awaiting further support from the PICU team and stop the case after the students have demonstrated proper bag mask ventilation which will increase oxygen saturation.

9. Discussion/Debriefing: Ask the team members what happened in the case, what they felt went well and what could have gone better. Important comments should include concepts of teamwork, communication and leadership as well as the medical management. The instructor should not intervene until the group is finished. At this point the instructor may comment on what went well and what could have gone better and should review the ideal management of the case to allow for future learning. The discussion of case management should include the initial differential diagnosis based on the history. This should include croup, foreign body obstruction, epiglottis, retropharyngeal or peritonsilar abscess and anaphylaxis. Discuss that croup is the most likely in a patient with cough and stridor at this age. Foreign body would be unlikely at this age, though possible, especially with an older sibling, but often there are asymmetric lung findings. Epiglottis and abscess are both less likely given that she is fully immunized and an allergic reaction often would present with wheezing as opposed to stridor. They should discuss the important physical exam findings, in this case lack of cyanosis, stridor, rhonchi, abnormal vitals, increased work of breathing, lack of wheezing and symmetric lung exam. Discuss physical exam findings that would change their differential diagnosis and management. The medical management discussion should include optimal initial therapy and treatment including medications, laboratory tests, and imaging studies as well as evaluation of the decision of which tests should and should not be ordered.

Instructor Notes:

1. Groups generally flow well through the cases- even if they provide the wrong therapies, allow them to continue and adjust as necessary

2. If the group is failing to progress or becoming chaotic, give a reminder for what the next step should be.
