Simulation of Respiratory Distress for the 3rd Year Medical Student

Learning Objectives:

1. Identify respiratory distress and its etiology in children.

2. Determine appropriate initial assessment of patients with respiratory distress.

3. Identify the vital signs necessary for assessment of respiratory distress and the variations in children.

4. Demonstrate preliminary management of patients presenting with respiratory distress.

5. Identify necessary resources to use with a patient in respiratory distress including different oxygen delivery methods, cardiopulmonary monitors, and IV access.

6. Perform the key skill of providing bag mask ventilation to a child in respiratory distress.

7. Use key elements involved in teamwork when working in an emergent situation, including assigning roles and designating team leaders.

Station 2

Simulation Portion

1. Introduction: The students will be expected to participate as if the simulation is in real time. Review what your simulation mannequin is capable of (breathing, heart sounds, pulses, vascular access points etc.). Allow the students to have access to the same equipment they would have in the patient care areas and give them time to familiarize themselves with the equipment. Discuss with the students the concept of team roles during a code situation, choosing a code leader and assigning specific roles to each participant. Choose a student to be the initial responder. During the case, the person running the simulation should not interact with the students except to answer prompts (eg state laboratory results) and the students should be reminded of this prior to starting the case.

2. Simulator set up: 21 month old with vital signs of HR 120 RR 40 O2 sat 94% with diffuse wheezing.

3. Case Scenario: You are seeing a patient who was just admitted to the floors. She came in through the ER and your intern got sign out but is now busy with another patient.

4. The case prompt should be enough to get the students started. Only give the included information that is asked for by the students.

1. History: This is a 21 month old girl with no past medical history who has been having a runny nose and cough for the past day. This morning she began wheezing and her father thought she was having trouble breathing. She had a fever at home to 101 and she was not eating or drinking as much as usual. She has never been sick before, she was born full term, has all of her vaccines and has no allergies. In the ER, she got some “medicine” but dad doesn’t know what it was.

2. Physical Exam: Vital Signs are HR 120 RR 40 O2 94% on room air. She is generally well appearing, acyanotic. She has a normal head and neck exam. She has subcostal retraction and abdominal breathing. She has diffuse wheezing bilaterally on her lung exam. The remainder of her exam is normal for age.

3. Critical Points: Students should ask for a more detailed history and a more detailed exam which can be provided from the above information and from examining the high fidelity simulator if available.
   1. Based on the above history and physical the appropriate action would be to administer oxygen and nebulized albuterol or saline.
      1. If oxygen is given via nasal cannula, oxygen saturation should rise to 96%.
      2. If oxygen is given via face mask, oxygen saturation should rise to 98%
      3. If nebulized saline or albuterol are given, the oxygen saturation should rise to 98% and the respiratory rate should trend toward normal, with an improved physical exam.
   2. Inappropriate actions at this time include checking labs, imaging, leaving the child alone, advanced airway support (intubation or bag mask ventilation) or giving other medications (ie antibiotics).
   3. Students have to provide a reasonable management plan at this point such as continuing oxygen administration or q2 hour nebulized saline.
   4. Instructor should state, 20 minutes have now passed and the child again has an increased respiratory rate and decreased oxygen saturation. At this point students should initiate further evaluation.
      1. If they provide another saline or albuterol neb the child will only display minor improvement.
      2. Appropriate steps at this time include increasing respiratory support to 100% non-rebreather mask and getting a chest x-ray looking for signs of pneumonia or foreign body.
      3. Although laboratory findings are unlikely to change management at this point, it would not be wrong if students request basic lab studies. Viral studies would show that the child has RSV bronchiolitis.
   5. If these critical actions are not taken the child’s respiratory and heart rates should increase while the oxygen saturation decreases. This decline should continue with impending respiratory failure. At any point the clinical status can improve with oxygen, albuterol or saline. If they continue to not give appropriate support then the child will progress to respiratory failure requiring bag mask ventilation.

4. End points for this case:
   1. Instructor can set a time limit, we recommend 15 minutes allowing time for a debrief after the case.
   2. Alternatively, instructor can stop the case if the students perfectly manage and stabilize the child.
   3. If the students management does not meet the critical points do not let the child expire but rather remind students that a child can be supported by bag mask ventilation for a long time while awaiting further support from the PICU team and stop the case after the students have demonstrated proper bag mask ventilation which will increase oxygen saturation.

5. Discussion/Debriefing: Ask the team members what happened in the case, what they felt went well and what could have gone better. Important comments should include concepts of teamwork, communication and leadership as well as the medical management. The instructor should not intervene until the group is finished. At this point the instructor may comment on what went well and what could have gone better and should review the ideal management of the case to allow for future learning. The discussion of case management should include the initial differential diagnosis based on the history. This should include reactive airway disease, bronchiolitis, and foreign body obstruction. The students should discuss the important physical exam findings in this case including the lack of cyanosis, the presence of bilateral wheezing, lack of rales, and only mildly abnormal vital signs. Discuss physical exam findings that would change their differential diagnosis and management. The medical management discussion should include optimal initial therapy and treatment including medications, laboratory tests, and imaging studies as well as evaluation of the decision of which tests should and should not be ordered.

Instructor Notes:

1. Groups generally flow well through the cases- even if they provide the wrong therapies, allow them to continue and adjust as necessary

2. If the group is failing to progress or becoming chaotic, give a reminder for what the next step should be.
