# Severe Preeclampsia-Eclampsia Simulation Case Debrief Notes Organized by learning objective

# Recognizing that there is a spectrum of preeclampsia1,2

o Preeclampsia does not present with a uniform set of symptoms. o Determining a patient’s placement on the spectrum is not a function of a single point in time:

 Mild preeclampsia

 Presence of hypertension $( \mathrm { B P } \geq 1 4 0 / 9 0 \ \mathrm { m m } \ \mathrm { H g } )$ on 2 occasions, at least 6 hours apart (treatment in the ED should not be delayed for a 6 hour interval if BP is significantly agreed and the clinical scenario mandates treatment)

o If the patient has preexisting essential hypertension, the SBP must have increased by $3 0 \mathrm { m m H g }$ or DBP must have increased by 15 mm Hg

Mild proteinuria  Severe preeclampsia

 Presence of one of the following in the setting of preeclampsia BP parameters:

o SBP of $1 6 0 \mathrm { m m H g }$ or higher or DBP of $1 1 0 \mathrm { m m H g }$ or higher on 2 occasions at least 6 hours apart o Proteinuria of more than 5 grams in a 24-hour collection or $3 +$ or greater on 2 random checks collected at least 4 hours apart

o Pulmonary edema or hypoxia   
o Oliguria $\angle 4 0 0 \mathrm { m L }$ in 24 hours)   
o Persistent, severe headaches   
o Epigastric pain or elevated liver function tests   
o Thrombocytopenia   
o Fetal complications: oligohydramnios, impaired fetal growth or placental abruption

 Eclampsia

Seizures that cannot be attributed to any other cause in a preeclamptic patient

o Presenting symptoms may include any of the following:

Headache Blurry vision or scotomas   
Confusion/altered mental status   
. Dyspnea Edema, especially facial Epigastric or RUQ pain Weakness or fatigue   
Clonus   
Bleeding at IV insertion sites

 Recognizing the need for blood pressure management in patients with severe preeclampsia and knowing the appropriate agents1,2

o The goal of hypertensive treatment for severe preeclampsia is to lower SBP to $<$ $1 6 0 \mathrm { m m H g }$ and diastolic to $< 1 1 0 \ \mathrm { m m H g }$ prevent progression to severe complications.3   
o BP management goals of patients with SBP 140-159 and DBP 90-109 is debated due to some evidence suggesting harm from lowering BP, such as small for gestational age infants or low birth-weight.3   
o The following medications may be used:

Hydralazine\*  Labetalol\* Nifedipine\* Nitroglycerine Nitroprusside (only if other medications have failed) \*first-line agents

Recognizing that seizure prophylaxis with magnesium is indicated for patients with severe preeclampsia1,2

o Patients who meet criteria for severe preeclampsia are at high-risk for seizures therefore prophylactic treatment with magnesium should be administered.4

 Recommended dose: 4-6 grams IV over 15 minutes followed by an infusion of 1-2 grams/hour

o Studies have not demonstrated a benefit in patients with mild preeclampsia.

 Consulting obstetrics for potential delivery of patients with severe preeclampsia1,2 o Delivery in such patients is considered after 34 weeks’ gestation. o Criteria for delivery:

 Non-reassuring fetal heart activity   
 Uncontrollable blood pressure   
 Oliguria Markedly elevated creatinine   
 Pulmonary edema   
Hypoxia   
 Development of HELLP (hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets)   
RUQ tenderness   
 Pregnancy complications: ruptured membranes, oligohydramnios, intrauterine growth restriction (fetal weight less than $5 \%$ )

#  Recognizing that care of pregnant patients includes evaluation of fetal status1,2

o When caring for a pregnant woman, a provider is taking care of two patients. o Fetal evaluation should be conducted – fetal heart rate should be determined and obstetrics consulted for fetal monitoring.

Considering a postictal phase during assessment of a pregnant patient with acute decline in mental status even in the absence of a witnessed seizure1,2

o Pregnant patients in the second half of pregnancy who present to the ED should be evaluated for signs of preeclampsia/eclampsia. o In the correct setting, postictal alteration should be on the differential diagnosis.

 Knowing the next-line agent for seizures in the eclamptic patient if seizures are refractory to magnesium1,2

o After initiation of treatment with magnesium for eclamptic seizures, in the setting of an additional seizure, a magnesium bolus of ${ 2 \bf \delta g }$ may be given and the infusion rate may be increased up to ${ \bf \nabla } 2 { \bf \delta g } /$ hour.   
o Lorazepam and phenytoin may be used for magnesium-refractory seizures.
