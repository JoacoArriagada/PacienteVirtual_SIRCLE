**Severe Preeclampsia-Eclampsia Simulation Case:
Instructor’s Guide**

Summary of case: This is the case of a 41 y.o. female at 34 weeks’ gestation who is brought to the ED by her husband for acute altered mental status. A careful history and physical reveal that the patient has severe preeclampsia. Based on this, one consideration for her altered mental status is her being postictal, which would make her eclamptic. She will have a witnessed seizure in the ED (the time from the start of the scenario to seizure onset and the extent of treatment required to break the seizure are determined by the participants’ recognition of the underlying process and treatment considerations). The participants should evaluate the fetus and consult obstetrics for consideration of delivery.

Resource files

* Severe preeclampsia-eclampsia case:
* This file should be read through first.
* It includes the following components:
* The practical components such as features to provide the manikin, if possible, and roles for other participants
* The underlying physiology
* The way that the scenario unfolds and demonstrates the eventual goals
* The evolution of the patient’s symptoms
* The patient’s course if participants do not make certain management decisions
* The instructor should be familiar with the case and all of the programming parameters ahead of time.

Implementation:

* Materials needed:
* A simulator
* Cardiac monitor and code cart
* IV equipment, medications (to create realism)
* Personnel needed:
  + Programmer:
    - Someone who knows how to program the simulator and run the case
  + Faculty/instructor:
    - Should be familiar with the case and debriefing materials
    - Is responsible for helping the case move along
  + Role players, if available:
    - Nurse:
      * Will place the IV and administer the medications ordered by the resident/student leader.
      * May prompt participants to provide appropriate treatment.
    - Patient’s husband:
      * If other residents/students are available to play this role, they will need to be briefed ahead of time.
      * The instructor can play this role.
* If other residents/students are not available, participants can give verbal orders for medications and the instructor can provide acknowledgement. Simulated administration is not critical to the learning. The instructor can play the role of the husband.
* Preparation needs:
  + The instructor should read the case and understand how it evolves.
  + The instructor should ensure that the case has been appropriately programmed.

Prior implementation:

* Since residents were unable to obtain much history from the patient and not all physical examination findings can be accurately portrayed by the simulator (such as edema), the patient’s husband was given a larger role so that he can help create the background scenario more accurately.

Limitations:

* This resource requires a simulator and a programmer along with a faculty instructor. To increase realism, another student or resident should play the patient’s husband. This setup requires multiple people to be available simultaneously.
* Since the simulator cannot depict the patient’s lethargy, the participant has to rely on verbal cues and inflection by the instructor and possibly prompts by the patient’s husband.
* Since not all simulations can demonstrate a seizure, the participant may have to rely on verbal clues instead of being able to visualize them.

Improvements:

* Having a standardized patient (SP) would allow the physical findings to be directly visualized and would enhance the opportunity for assessment of participants’ physical examination skills (such as the patient’s mental status) and interpretive ability. It would still require a simulator for vital signs to be displayed. Since an IV cannot be placed in an SP and medication cannot be administered, either a manikin arm or IV pad can be provided or if someone is available to play the nurse, he/she can apply the tourniquet to the SP and simulate placing the IV without actually doing so.
