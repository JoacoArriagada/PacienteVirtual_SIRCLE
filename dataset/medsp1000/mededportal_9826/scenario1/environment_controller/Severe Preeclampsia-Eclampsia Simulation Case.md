# Simulation Interest Group Scenario Template

I. Title : Altered mental status in a third trimester pregnant patient

IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up  ED
B. Manikin Set Up Simulator, preferably one that can depict pregnancy and a seizure
– Medications: magnesium, antihypertensives (Labetalol or Hydralazine), benzodiazepines
– Physical findings: gravid abdomen, tonic-clonic seizure, lower extremity edema, periorbital edema, hyperreflexia

C. Props

IV placement equipment Medication syringes . Nasal cannula . Nonrebreather mask  Doppler for fetal evaluation

D. Distractors – the blood pressure cuff should be set for every 2-3 minutes and the noise of this might serve as a distractor (but also a reminder)

# V. Actors

# Roles

Patient’s husband

– Provides history since the patient is postictal and cannot provide it herself
– May be played by the instructor or another student/resident who is briefed ahead of time

Nurse

– Places IV and administers medications – May be played by another student or resident

Obstetrician

– Consulted given the patient’s diagnosis; makes decision about delivery
– May be played by the instructor

VI. Case Narrative

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants

1. Chief complaint: altered mental status in a gravid 41 year old female, 34 weeks gestation

The patient was brought to the ED by ambulance,
accompanied by her husband
Medic report: “We were called by the patient’s husband for altered mental status. He went out grocery shopping and when he returned an hour later, she was altered. No history of trauma. We placed her on oxygen and the monitor. She has remained minimally responsive during the ride over but has been maintaining her airway.”

2. Past medical history $:$ HTN, migraines

Past OB history: s/p vaginal delivery 12 years ago, her husband was not with her at that time so he does know details of the pregnancy

3. Meds/allergies $:$ Hydrochlorothiazide/NKDA

4. Family/social history $:$ From what her husband knows, the patient’s father has a history of CAD, diabetes, and “some kidney issue”.

Former tobacco, $\%$ ppd, quit 9 years ago; social alcohol but none while pregnant, no illicit drugs, does not work

# B. Scenario conditions initially:

1. History patient gives : She is postictal and tired but does answer “yes” or “no” when asked questions.

Denies trauma Global headache –rates it as 6 out of 10 No vaginal bleeding or abdominal pain She is feeling the baby move
 No chest pain or shortness of breath
Mild nausea
No visual changes
. No focal weakness
. No ingestions
If asked specifically if she had a seizure, she says she is not sure

2. History the husband gives:

The patient has been very tired recently but he thinks that is because she is pregnant.
. She has been having some contractions from time to time.
. He went to the store this morning with his stepdaughter and when he returned, his wife was lying on the bed and looked “dazed.”

She seemed tired but otherwise fine when he left to go shopping. When he asked her what was going on, she said that she was tired so she decided to lie down and then she might have fallen asleep. She was slower than usual in her responses.

3. Patient’s initial exam

 General: nontoxic, somnolent but arousable, no distress, eyes closed
 VS: T 99.6, HR 104, BP 162/93, RR 18, 02 100% NRB HEENT: NCAT, PERRL $\cdot 4 \to 2 \mathrm { m m } \mathrm { b } / \mathrm { I } _ { . }$ ), EOMI, mild periorbital swelling bilaterally
 Neck: supple, normal inspection
 CV: slightly tachycardic, regular rhythm
 Respiratory: CTA b/l
 Abdomen: gravid, soft superiorly, mild RUQ tenderness, no rebound or guarding
 Extremities: $1 { - } 2 +$ pitting edema b/l
 Neurological: somnolent, arousable, moans answers (sounds fatigued), moves all extremities symmetrically, hyperreflexia with clonus

4. Patients physiology:

 The patient has eclampsia however her seizure at home wasn’t witnessed and can’t be corroborated. Participants should recognize severe preeclampsia based on physical findings.
 She is hypertensive and needs blood pressure lowering
 She meets criteria for seizure prophylaxis with magnesium.
 Though she didn’t have a witnessed seizure, in the setting of acutely altered mental status and severe preeclampsia, a seizure should be assumed.
 The patient has a seizure despite getting magnesium.

C. Scenario branch points

The patient presents altered which is her postictal phase. This might not be recognized or considered immediately. However based on the fact that she is so hypertensive (and will remain so on repeated blood pressure checks), she meets criteria for magnesium administration.

o Every 2 minutes after arrival, her systolic BP/diastolic BP should increase by 5/5.
o Once an antihypertensive agent is administered, her systolic BP/ diastolic BP should decrease by 2/2 every minute.

She will seize despite receiving anti-hypertensives and/or magnesium.

o If magnesium is not administered, she should seize within 4 minutes of the first BP check.
o If magnesium is administered, she should seize 6 minutes into the scenario.

 When the patient has a seizure, treatment must be administered. Despite receiving magnesium either earlier or in the setting of the seizure, she will continue seizing until additional treatment is given.

o When the patient has a seizure, her blood sugar should be checked.
o If the patient’s blood pressure was not addressed, more than one round of seizure treatment may be required before the seizure stops. If the blood pressure was addressed, one round may be sufficient.
 Obstetrics should be called for fetal evaluation and decision about immediate delivery. She will need to be admitted to the ICU.
 An anesthesia consult may be called as there is a high likelihood of delivery.

II. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer:

 Setting the BP cuff to recycle every 2-3 minutes will emphasize the importance of blood pressure treatment.

B. Tips to direct actors:

 If treatment for the patient’s BP is not administered, the nurse may gently ask about treatment. Having the husband ask if his wife and baby are at risk may prompt more aggressive treatment as well as consideration of a seizure if it has not already occurred in the case.
If treatment for the patient’s hypertension is not administered, after she seizes, the nurse should ask about treating her blood pressure.
