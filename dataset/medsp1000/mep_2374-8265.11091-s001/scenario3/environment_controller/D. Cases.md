**Appendix D. Health Literacy Cases (Facilitator Version)**

**3. Case of Mrs. Zimdahl**

**Scenario:** Mrs. Zimdahl called the clinic to make an appointment because her baby was having continuous diarrhea. The person working in the phone bank making the appointment told her to give the baby oral electrolyte solution. Three weeks later Mrs. Zimdahl took the baby to the emergency room because the baby would not stop crying and was still sick. Mrs. Zimdahl had stopped giving the baby milk and had been feeding the baby only electrolyte solution. She was starving her baby.

**Directions:** Role play this scenario where the provider explains to Mrs. Zimdahl how to treat her baby for diarrhea using the “How to treat your infant for diarrhea” instructions below.

**How to treat your infant for diarrhea**

Infants can get dehydrated quickly if they have a high output of diarrhea. Their small body size results in relatively small fluid reserves and a high metabolic rate that makes it easy to lose the water and electrolytes the body needs to function. If fluids are not replaced, the outcome can be critical within a matter of days.

1. Give your baby an oral rehydration solution (ORS) such as Pedialyte, Ceralyte, or Gastrolyte.
2. Since your baby is over 22 lbs (10 kg), give her 4 to 8 ounces (120 to 240 mL) of ORS for each diarrheal or stool or vomiting episode.

**During acute diarrhea**

1. If you are breastfeeding, continue to do so.
2. Do not give your child soda, juices, or sports drinks such as Gatorade to treat the diarrhea.

**After the acute phase of diarrhea**

1. Resume age-appropriate normal food diet. If your child has begun eating solid foods, consider resuming with the BRAT diet: bananas, rice, applesauce, and toast.
2. If your child shows signs of moderate to severe dehydration, seek medical attention.
3. To prevent the spread of the virus that is causing the diarrhea, wash your hands well and often.
