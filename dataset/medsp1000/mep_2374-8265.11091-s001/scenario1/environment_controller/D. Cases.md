**Appendix D. Health Literacy Cases (Facilitator Version)**

**1. Case of Mrs. Ali**

**Scenario:** Mrs. Ali was given an asthma inhaler by her doctor. He told her it was for her chest and would help her breathe better. When Mrs. Ali did not get better, she went to the emergency room and the doctor said to her, “Show me how you use your inhaler.” They discovered that she had been spraying her chest with the inhaler.

**Directions:** Role play this scenario where the provider gives Mrs. Ali instructions on how to use her inhaler using the “How to use an inhaler” instructions below.

**How to use an inhaler - no spacer**

Using a metered-dose inhaler (MDI) seems simple. But many people do not use them the right way. If you use your MDI the wrong way, less medicine gets to your lungs and most remains in the back of your mouth.

1. Remove the cap and shake the inhaler vigorously.
2. If you have not used the inhaler in a while, you may need to prime it.
3. Hold the inhaler 1 to 2 inches (2.5 to 5 centimeters) in front of your mouth.
4. Start breathing in slowly through your mouth, then press down once on the inhaler.
5. Continue breathing in slowly and deeply.
6. If you can, hold your breath as you slowly count to 10, attaining deep penetration into your lungs.
7. If you are using inhaled, quick-relief medicine (beta-agonists), delay taking your next puff by 1 minute. No wait time is necessary for other medicines.
8. After using your inhaler, flush out your mouth with water to reduce side effects from your medicine.
