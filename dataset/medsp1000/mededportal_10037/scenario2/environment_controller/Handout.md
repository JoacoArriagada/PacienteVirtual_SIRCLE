**Treating Older Adults with Hypertension: When Are the Guidelines Not Applicable?**

**Resource Files 1: Classroom handouts for students**

1. **Group Application Exercise (GAE) II-Patient 2: “Muse is more important than Metoprolol!”**

Mr. G is an 82 y/o retired officer who was recently admitted to hospital due to mental status change secondary to his pneumonia. After he received antibiotics, his pneumonia was resolved and went to home. He has a long list of co-existing conditions including hypertension, heart failure with left ventricular ejection fraction of 30%***,*** coronary artery disease (CAD), atrial-fibrillation (A-Fib), hypercholesterolemia (HLD), depression, benign prostate hypertrophy (BPH), osteoarthritis (OA), and erectile dysfunction (ED). He had 2 hospitalizations for heart failure exacerbations. He takes following multiple medications including Furosemide, Digoxin, Metoprolol, Spironolactone, Ramipril, ASA, Isosorbide mononnitrate, Atorvastatin, Warfarin, Citalopram, Tamsulosin, Muse, Acetaminophen, Senna, and Omeprazole. Since he left the hospital, his activities of daily living (ADL) remains independent except that he feels tired and needs a cane to help with walking and balance. His 72 y/o wife is doing most of the house work. His wife is wondering why he is taking so many pills.

You see this patient in your clinic. He states clearly that he is tired of taking so many medications especially blood pressure pills. He wants to have a good quality of life and maintains independent physical function. He thought that Metoprolol really has slowed down everything. He says “Muse is more important than Metoprolol!”He asks you to discontinue the antihypertensive drugs so that he could feel good.

On physical examination, he is not in acute distress. His blood pressure is 142/70 mg Hg (sitting) and 126/64 mmHg (standing), Pulse of 58 beats per minute, Respiration rate of 16 per minute, SO2 98% on room air. The remainder of the physical examination is benign except for a few crackles at the right lung base, a localized 2/6 systolic murmur in aortic area, and mild bilateral lower extremity edema. He fails the “timed up and go test”. He passes the Mini-Cog test. He has a score of 4/15 on the geriatrics depression scale. All laboratory tests are in the normal range except for a Bun/Cr of 35/1.6 and BNP of 290.
