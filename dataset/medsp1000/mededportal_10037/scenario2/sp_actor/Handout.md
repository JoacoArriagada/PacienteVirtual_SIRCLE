**Treating Older Adults with Hypertension: When Are the Guidelines Not Applicable?**

**Resource Files 1: Classroom handouts for students**

1. **Group Application Exercise (GAE) II-Patient 2: “Muse is more important than Metoprolol!”**

Mr. G is an 82 y/o retired officer who was recently admitted to hospital due to mental status change secondary to his pneumonia. After he received antibiotics, his pneumonia was resolved and went to home. He has a long list of co-existing conditions including hypertension, heart failure, coronary artery disease (CAD), atrial-fibrillation (A-Fib), hypercholesterolemia (HLD), depression, benign prostate hypertrophy (BPH), osteoarthritis (OA), and erectile dysfunction (ED). He had 2 hospitalizations for heart failure exacerbations. He takes following multiple medications including Furosemide, Digoxin, Metoprolol, Spironolactone, Ramipril, ASA, Isosorbide mononnitrate, Atorvastatin, Warfarin, Citalopram, Tamsulosin, Muse, Acetaminophen, Senna, and Omeprazole. Since he left the hospital, his activities of daily living (ADL) remains independent except that he feels tired and needs a cane to help with walking and balance. His 72 y/o wife is doing most of the house work. His wife is wondering why he is taking so many pills.

You see this patient in your clinic. He states clearly that he is tired of taking so many medications especially blood pressure pills. He wants to have a good quality of life and maintains independent physical function. He thought that Metoprolol really has slowed down everything. He says “Muse is more important than Metoprolol!”He asks you to discontinue the antihypertensive drugs so that he could feel good.
