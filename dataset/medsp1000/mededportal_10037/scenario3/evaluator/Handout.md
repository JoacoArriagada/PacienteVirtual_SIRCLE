1. **Group Application Exercise (GAE) III: patient 3: “Doctor, my mother likes to pass away comfortably”.**

Ms. N is a 95 year old woman with advanced dementia who was admitted to the nursing home about two months ago because of her progressive dementia with occasional behavioral disturbance. This patient had a recent hospitalization due to aspiration pneumonia. Her son (who has Power of Attorney) states clearly that his goal is to let his mother pass away comfortably in the nursing home. Ms. N had expressed that as her choice if she became demented. He tells you that his mother has taken too many pills for far too long. In the nursing home, this patient is wheelchair-bound. She is not in any distress. Her nurse and certified nurse’s aide say to you that Ms. N always sits in the activity room all day long. She is incontinent and wears adult briefs. She is not eating much, but is taking 10 pills for her multiple chronic conditions including hypertension (HTN), coronary artery disease (CAD), hyperlipidemia (HLD), diabetes mellitus (DM), osteoarthritis (OA), gastro-esophageal reflex disease (GERD), constipation, and stroke.

On physical examination, she looks frail and malnourished, but comfortable. Her blood pressure is 120/60 (sitting), no blood pressure is measured standing because she is unable to stand up at all. Pulse rate is 70 beats per minute. Respiration rate is 18 per minute. Her weight is 85 lbs (she has lost 5 pounds since coming to the nursing home two months ago). Physical examination of the heart, lung, and abdomen is normal. Mini-cog is not done because of her advanced dementia. The question is whether you can discontinue any medications including her antihypertensive medications.
