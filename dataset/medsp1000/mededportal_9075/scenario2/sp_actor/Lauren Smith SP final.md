# Training Materials Lauren Smith

# Case Summary

Ms. Lauren Smith is a $2 5 \mathrm { y } / 0$ female who has come to the urgent care center because of nausea that she has been experiencing for the past week. She is an elementary school teacher, and recently married. She and her husband recently moved here from Michigan for him to attend the law school here. She loves her husband, and they have a nice rented apartment, but she feels lonely as she has no close friends or family in the local area. Although her husband has told her that he will support her decision, she is torn about this pregnancy. She feels that she has no local support and that her husband will be very busy with law school. Mostly though, she simply doesn’t know if she is ready to have a child. There has been no emotional or physical abuse in the relationship.

# Your role as the standardized patient:

1. To appropriately and accurately respond to questions about Lauren Smith’s symptoms and medical history while conveying the emotions Lauren might feel when told she is pregnant.

# Presentation/Emotional Tone

Lauren is a pleasant woman. However, it is clear that she is in a state of transition. She feels lonely because she has no local support here and her husband has been pre-occupied with law school. Although she enjoys working with children as a schoolteacher, she is unsure if she is mentally and emotionally prepared to have her own child. She will answer the doctor’s questions, but her state of internal conflict is very apparent.

# Dress

You are an introverted woman and do not wear too much make-up or jewelry. You are sitting in a gown on the side of the exam table. You have on only simple earrings and a watch.

# Reason for Clinic Visit

When the student asks what brings you into clinic today, you respond: “I’ve been very nauseated for the past week. I even had to call in sick yesterday and today.”

If the student asks you if you have any idea what might be wrong, you can say: “I think it might be that I started my pill after being off of it for a couple of weeks…When I first started the pill, I was nauseated too.”

# Changes in Demeanor During the Encounter

At the beginning of the encounter, you think you might have a stomach virus because you have been feeling generally tired also. The portion of the interview focusing on your symptoms seems logical - you assume the doctor is also thinking you have the stomach flu and is trying to figure out how bad it is, and/or if it could be something else. During this line of questioning, you're just feeling relieved to finally have gotten medical care and hopeful that you'll leave with something to help you feel better. (You hadn't had the time to get recommendations from co-workers for good local physicians on the insurance plan offered by your new employer. None of them were at the University where your husband is studying. You are assured to find that this urgent care center runs well and professionally.) However, when the doctor tells you that you are pregnant, you are shocked and truly feel torn about your situation. You wonder how you will do this essentially by yourself. You feel that the last few months have already been difficult, as you are adjusting to a new city, your husband is usually busy and you have close friends available only by phone. Finally, since he is only a student, and has no income, you are the sole provider.

History of present illness: Initially the nausea was mild, but it has been getting worse the past few days and you vomited this morning.

# Concerns about pregnancy:

If the students asks you if you think you might be pregnant, BEFORE finding out that you are sexually active OR on Ortho-cyclen Low, you answer straightforwardly, “No I don’t.” If the student asks you if you think you might be pregnant, AFTER finding out that you are sexually active OR on Ortho-cyclen Low, you hesitate, wondering if there is actually a chance that you might be pregnant. Finally, you decide that you don’t think you are, and answer, “No… I don’t think so. I can’t be pregnant….”  When the student reveals the results of your pregnancy test, your response should be of anxiety and disbelief. You can repeat that you are on birth control pills and that you and Jake used condoms most of the time when you were off the pill. Your mind then becomes flooded with other thoughts of what this might mean for your life. You become silent with quiet tears, looking down or away, until the student breaks the silence. When the student asks how you feel, slowly describe your financial situation and your lack of local support. Finally, a bit about how you simply feel that the time is not right for you to have a child, that you just don’t feel ready.

# Dealing with Open-ended questions and guidelines for disclosure

#  How do you feel about being pregnant?

“This is so…..confusing. I didn’t want it to happen this way…..”

You always envisioned starting a family with Jake, but not until he was at least near graduation from law school and about to become more available to fully participate as a father. You never envisioned yourself alone in a new city with a baby, and a husband gone most of the day. You had assumed you would become pregnant only when you planned it, and that being told of your pregnancy would be a truly joyous moment in your life. Your life has not been particularly joyful, and now you feel disappointed that one of the joys you had counted on - learning of your first pregnancy - has occurred in circumstances that preclude full happiness.

#  How do you feel about continuing this pregnancy?

You are horribly torn. Continuation would be very difficult, financially and emotionally.

# How do you feel about abortion?

You never gave it much thought, assuming this would never happen to you. While you have never articulated a position, somehow you felt it was for "extreme situations" - rape, incest, threat to the mother's life, poverty......those sorts of things. None of your friends or family have ever told you they had an abortion, and you've never even missed a period and started to think through the “what ifs" for yourself, so it has been a somewhat abstract concept for you.

# How do you feel about adoption?

You can't imagine doing it yourself. You've had some students who were obviously or likely adopted. Most have seemed fine to you, but one or two seemed disturbed, causing you to suspect that adoption might pose longer-term problems for children. You also couldn’t imagine carrying a pregnancy for nine months and then giving it up, especially when you don’t have any REAL reason why you couldn’t keep the baby – such as poverty, drug addict, rape, etc.

#  Do you want to be a mother? Now? In the future?

You definitely want to be a mother in the future to feel complete as a woman and a human being. You do love children and have always pictured having a family.

You just didn’t plan to start your family this soon.

# How do you feel about your financial situation?

You love your husband and see the value of his career to himself and to your marriage - so you have been willing to take on the role as the primary breadwinner. However, the responsibility is a new feeling for you, and sometimes leaves you insecure. You know that eventually you will both be financially stable and hopefully without worries. You had planned on being the main financial support for the two of you, though, while he is finishing school. You are not what you would consider “poor” right now, but you live on a tight budget and don’t feel as financially comfortable as you would like before you bring a child into your lives.

#  How do you feel about your social situation?

You are very sad at the separation from your closest two girl friends. You are also sad about being apart from your family - while you are not emotionally expressive with your mom, sister or father - you have always felt their love and support. However, asking them to move to your new city or visit for an extended stay feels impossible, given their jobs and financial situations. You could never ask for that sort of sacrifice from them. You were raised to be independent.

#  How is the nausea affecting you?

You've had trouble concentrating, especially in the mornings. You have to sit

much more, and therefore move around the classroom much less. Holding your students' attention and keeping them focused has become much more difficult.

Prompts are used to standardize the scenario and give all students an opportunity to address the same questions/issues:

Prompt 1 – at hearing of pregnancy, “it’s not a good time, I didn’t want this to happen now…..my husband is not going to like this” and then to tearful silence and looking away

Prompt 2 – “this is so confusing…..”

Prompt 3- “what should I do?”

Prompt 4 - “what would you do?” or “What would you do if it were your wife?”

Prompt 5- (if, by 5 minutes into the interaction, the student has not revealed the results of the pregnancy test you may inquire): “what’s going on with my nausea? Did you get the results from the tests that the other doctor did earlier?”
