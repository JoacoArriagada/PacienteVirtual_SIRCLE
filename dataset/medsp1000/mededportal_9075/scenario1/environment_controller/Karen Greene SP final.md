# Training Materials Karen Greene

# Case Summary

Ms. Karen Greene is a $3 8 \mathrm { y } / 0$ white female who has come to the urgent care center because of nausea that she has been experiencing for the past week. Based in New York City, she is a photographer on assignment in this new town. She is in a new relationship of 6 months which is going unexpectedly well. While finding herself increasingly hopeful about the long-term prospects with David, she has become very cautious in relationships with men, and is not yet fully convinced that this one will work out. Having children was never clearly important to her. She had always told herself that she would let herself think about it more only if the right person came along. Now as things with David seem to be going so well, she has started to consider that maybe before she reaches 40 they could have a child.

# Presentation/Emotional Tone

Karen is an extroverted, mature and relaxed person. She is successful, confident and satisfied in her work. She is typically a very healthy woman who doesn’t tolerate illness easily, and is impatient to feel better and get back to the special assignment.

# Concerns about pregnancy:

If the students asks you if you think you might be pregnant, BEFORE finding out that you are sexually active OR on the Nuvaring, you answer straightforwardly, “No I don’t.”   
If the student asks you if you think you might be pregnant, AFTER finding out that you are sexually active OR on Nuvaring, you hesitate, wondering if there is actually a chance that you might be pregnant. Finally, you decide that you don’t think you are, and answer, “No… I don’t think so. I can’t be pregnant….”   
When the student reveals the results of your pregnancy test, your response should be of anxiety and disbelief. You can repeat that you are on the Nuvaring and that you and David used withdrawal most of the time when you were off the Nuvaring, which was really only a few days longer than you were supposed to be. Your mind then becomes flooded with other thoughts – of what this might mean for your life. At first you smile and feel amazed. You then become silent with quiet tears, wondering to yourself if David will make a commitment or just leave if you decide to keep the pregnancy. You also don’t want David to commit to you simply because of the pregnancy. You are a very level-headed woman who thinks things through and you have waited a long time for the “right” man – the idea that you may not be entirely sure about David yet scares you. You look down or away, until the student breaks the silence. When the student asks how you feel, slowly describe your interest in having a child but how this has happened earlier than you would have wanted. You truly feel very torn and confused. Finally, a bit about how you wanted a more solid relationship and the time to figure out how to mold your career around motherhood.

# Dealing with Open-ended questions and guidelines for disclosure

#  How do you feel about being pregnant?

You had assumed you would become pregnant only when and if you planned it. And if planned, it would be a smooth transition for you. If you had planned to become pregnant you could make the proper arrangements for work, your apartment, etc.

#  How do you feel about continuing this pregnancy?

You are horribly torn. Since you and David have barely talked about a long-term relationship, this is hardly the best time to bring up pregnancy. He seems comfortable with your niece and nephew, but he has not directly said anything about wanting his own kids. Maybe he’ll embrace the prospects of commitment and kids; maybe he’ll panic and leave you all together.

#  How do you feel about abortion?

You never anticipated another unintended pregnancy, especially after so many years of successfully using contraception. You picture yourself as being older and wiser, and never imagined being in this situation again. You feel that you have been in such control of your life.

#  How do you feel about adoption?

You can't imagine doing it yourself. In your mind, that is something that only teenagers who find out too late to have an abortion ever resort to. You can’t imagine deciding to continue this pregnancy with David and then giving the child up.

 Do you want to be a mother? Now? In the future?

You have certainly considered it, but only under the “right” circumstances. You have never felt that you should accept an unfulfilling relationship just to have a father for your children. Although you really think things with David are going great, you don’t want to rush this relationship if it is not “meant to be the right one”. If David is not supportive of you keeping the pregnancy and you were to go through with things on your own, you don’t see how you could both maintain your career and be a competent single/solo mother.

# How do you feel about your financial situation?

You are doing well enough financially. But not well enough to stop work altogether for long periods of time. And anyway, you would need the benefits. You had always pictured working part-time if you were to become a mother, but that is the kind of thing that would take some more planning. You also feel that if you decided to keep the baby that you may need a bigger place than your one-bedroom. That brings up another factor with David – would you move in together? Would that be too soon?

 How do you feel about your social situation?

You were raised to be independent and have enjoyed your professional life and at least most of your relationships. You are close to your sister, who lives outside of the city. Your mom is 65 and in New Jersey; your father died 3 years ago from heart disease. You have a good core group of friends from your years in the magazine business and art world.

# How is the nausea affecting you?

You've had trouble concentrating, especially in the mornings. You have to sit much more, and have not been quite as adept with your camera, letting your assistant do more than usual.

# Prompts are used to standardize the scenario and give all students an opportunity to address the same questions/issues:

Prompt 1 – at hearing of pregnancy, “it’s not a good time, I didn’t want this to happen now…..my boyfriend is not going to like this” and then to tearful silence and looking away

Prompt 2 – “this is so confusing…..”

Prompt 3- “what should I do?”

Prompt 4 - “what would you do?” or “What would you do if it were your wife?”

Prompt 5- (if, by 5 minutes into the interaction, the student has not revealed the results of the pregnancy test you may inquire): “what’s going on with my nausea? Did you get the results from the tests that the other doctor did earlier?”
