**A simulated geriatric care team meeting with the Coopers**

**Part 3. Learn about the Coopers: The Family Scenario**

Your team is holding a family meeting to discuss placement options and recommendations for the Coopers. Have a team member read the following case information to the group.

Mrs. Cooper, 87 years old, is nearing being discharged from a skilled nursing facility after her hip fracture resulting from a fall. She is currently using a walker and her Timed Up and Go score is 28 seconds. Mrs. Cooper has early stage Alzheimer’s disease, but up until the fall she could still perform all activities of daily living. Her husband, Mr. Cooper, 90 years old, has been her primary caregiver and has managed many of the instrumental activities of daily living. They have lived in their two-story home for 53 years. Their daughter, Mrs. Allen, 65 years old, lives nearby and helps out with shopping and laundry on the weekends.

Mr. Cooper is in good health for his age. His brother, 92 years old, lives in a nursing home and Mr. Cooper has vowed he “will never go down that road.” The Coopers’ son lives out-of-state and though he is remote from day-to-day activities, he helps out by paying a cleaning service to provide weekly housekeeping for his parents.

Mrs. Cooper has been seeing a neurologist at the University for the past four years. When she was diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment, she and her daughter decided to enroll in a longitudinal, observational study for Alzheimer’s disease. Mrs. Cooper missed her last study appointment while she was hospitalized. Mrs. Allen notified the study coordinator that they were “on hold” until further notice.

The interdisciplinary team will meet with Mr. and Mrs. Cooper and their daughter, Mrs. Allen, to lay out living and long term care options and to prioritize residential placement recommendations for the Coopers following Mrs. Cooper’s discharge from the skilled nursing facility.

***Instructions: Before beginning the meeting, follow instructions for orientation to the scenario. At the bottom of each role page is information for the orientation, including about residential options to be considered.***

**Orientation to the Scenario -** Students with the relevant information at the bottom of their role pages answer the following questions about the Coopers’ case.

**Concerning scenario concepts-** Coordinator reads these questions.

1. What is a skilled nursing facility?

2. What are Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)?

3.What are Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs)?

4. What is the Timed Up and Go test?

5. What is mild cognitive impairment (MCI)?

6. What is Alzheimer’s disease?

7. What is an interdisciplinary team (IDT)?

**Concerning Residential Options-** Coordinator reads options. Recorder returns to white board and makes a table showing each residential option and its average cost. Students with relevant information describe the option and its cost: **1.** Nursing Home; **2.** Continuing Care Retirement Center; **3.** Assisted Living Facility; **4.** Group Home; **5.** Own Home with Assistance

**Part 4. Meet with the Coopers: The Interdisciplinary Team Meeting**

***Instructions: Work as an interdisciplinary team to help the Coopers prioritize their options for the best residential placement for Mrs. Cooper following her discharge from the skilled nursing facility.***

**The Team Meeting –**

**●** Members re-introduce themselves to the Coopers if necessary. (e.g. *“Mr. and Mrs. Cooper, I believe you have met everyone on the team. If there is someone you don’t recognize, please let me know.”)*

**●** Recorder takes notes. Timekeeper allots 40 minutes for the meeting.

**●** Team coordinator restates the purpose of the meeting.

**●** Members share their information and perspectives with the Coopers.

**●** Family listens to team members’ contributions and asks questions.

**●** The family ends up with a prioritized list of residential options.
