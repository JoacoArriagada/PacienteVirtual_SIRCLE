**Instructor Guide**

*“Acknowledging roles and abilities of team members: A simulated geriatric care team meeting with the Coopers”*

**When, how and the order of use for each resource file**

* Instructor Guide – read before implementation.
* Cooper Case and Discussion Materials for Students – should be made available to students in advance of the session. Students should bring this resource with them to the small group session.
* Role Page Handouts – should be printed ahead of time, one set per group. Each student in the group will receive a single page with his/her role in Part 2 of the session as described in the Materials. There are twelve roles, including nine professionals and three family members.
* Facilitator Guide for Cooper Case – should be made available to faculty facilitators in advance of the session. The facilitator guide contains 1) a cover sheet with overview and suggested points for reflection, 2) Cooper Case and Discussion Materials for Students, and 3) an appendix with the role page handouts.

**Purpose and specific educational objectives**

This session is designed to lead first-year students in the health professions to introductory milestones (**recognition and understanding**) on the path to obtaining selected core competencies for interprofessional collaborative practice. Through this exercise students 1) are exposed to the roles/skills and training of different health professionals, and 2) practice working respectfully with individuals of other professions in a team setting.

**Objectives**

* Recognize and understand the importance of the following core competencies for interprofessional practice that are drawn from the four domains identified by the *Interprofessional Education Collaborative*.1
* Respect the unique cultures, values, roles/responsibilities, and expertise of other health professions. *(Values/Ethics Competencies: VE4)*
* Communicate one’s roles and responsibilities clearly to patients, families, and other professionals. *(Roles/Responsibilities Competencies: RR1)*
* Listen actively, and encourage ideas and opinions of other team members. *(Interprofessional Communication Competencies: CC4)*
* Engage other health professionals – appropriate to the specific care situation – in shared patient-centered problem solving. *(Team and Teamwork Competencies: TT3)*
* Recognize that the elderly are likely to have complex health challenges that are best served by a team approach.

**Conceptual background for why and how the resource was created**

The simulated team meeting was created as a component of Convergence, an initiative for interprofessional education at UT Southwestern Medical Center. Convergence brings together first-year students from the medical, health professions and graduate schools for three, small-group sessions to learn about and practice skills such as teamwork and communication. Each year Convergence is focused on a unique science-of-medicine theme. This resource was developed for an interactive session on the theme of Aging. Content experts in clinical research, geriatrics, neurology, physical therapy, education and assessment met to develop a family case study with elements relevant to students in all schools. Objectives were selected from the *Core competencies for interprofessional collaborative practice: Report of an expert panel.1* The targeted milestone for progression toward these competencies is recognition and understanding of their importance.

**Practical implementation advice**

* Length of session – 90 min, with suggested timing for parts in the materials
* Materials needed – Student case materials and facilitator guide should be distributed and read in advance of the session. Facilitators should provide or be provided with one set of role page handouts per group. It would be desirable to have a white board in the meeting room. If not, the recorder can list residential options and costs on a sheet of paper.
* Group composition – the activity was designed for students from multiple programs to participate in the small group. If possible, each group would be facilitated by two faculty members, each from a different program, to model interprofessional collaboration. In cases where it is not possible to have mixed groups of students or faculty, the activity will still serve as an introduction to different health professionals and concepts of teamwork through roleplaying.
* Role assignments – there are nine professions on the role pages. Feedback suggested that roles be given to students studying for that profession, when possible.
* Be sure timekeeper leaves about ten minutes for reflection at the end of the session.
* The course administrator may want to collect and tabulate residential decision selections from the groups after the session. These could be sent to all participants afterwards.
