Pediatric Trauma Resuscitation – Pelvic fracture, Splenic laceration, Hypovolemia

1. Pediatric trauma
2. Target Audience: Emergency Medicine Residents
3. Scenario Goals and Objectives
    1. Goal – The student will lead a resuscitation team in the evaluation and management of a pediatric patient presenting as a multiple trauma victim after a motor vehicle accident (MVA)
    2. Learning objectives
        1. Demonstrate an appropriate primary assessment of a pediatric trauma victim
        2. Complete an appropriately timed secondary survey
        3. Recognize and treat hypovolemia due to internal hemorrhage
        4. Demonstrate correct sequence of steps to diagnose/rule out potential intra-abdominal and pelvic pathology
        5. Obtain appropriate laboratory and radiology studies
        6. Initiate a timely surgical consult
    3. Assessment objectives/Critical actions
        1. During case
            1. The leader must quickly complete a primary survey within 2 minutes of presentation
            2. Two large bore IV or IO lines must be obtained within 3 minutes of patient presentation
            3. AMPLE history must be obtained
            4. Leader must recognize hypotension and initiate fluid resuscitation
            5. The leader must order and interpret portable radiographs of the pelvis, C-spine, and chest x-ray.
            6. The leader must recognize potential need for blood transfusion, order a type and cross, and consider the need for emergency release blood
            7. The leader must direct the team assertively and delegate tasks appropriately
            8. Patient’s clothes should be removed
            9. Surgical consult must be obtained. This should be attained early (soon after the patient arrives) and again after patient has been resuscitated and injuries identified.
        2. During Debriefing
            1. Review quick assessment of ABCs and IV, oxygen and monitor placement
            2. List signs of hypovolemia and treatment
            3. Discuss stabilization of the pelvis

1. Environment
    1. Lab set up – A METI PediaSim will be placed on an ED stretcher. The scenario takes place in a tertiary care trauma pediatric- capable emergency department
    2. Manikin Set up – The PediaSim will be lying on a stretcher with C-Collar in place and fully clothed.
    3. Props - A stethoscope will be needed. A cardiac monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, pulse oximeter, supplemental oxygen by nasal cannula, supplies for starting an IV and/or intraosseous line, and simulated emergency release blood, will be needed.  In addition, medications for resuscitation, the treatment of pain, and for rapid sequence intubation should be readily available along with a full spectrum of emergency airway equipment.
    4. Audiovisual: A plain film X-ray of a pelvic fracture and a CT scan showing a splenic laceration will be needed. Also a normal chest x-ray, normal c-spine, CBC with an H/H that shows a slightly low hemoglobin with normal WBC and platelets, normal coags , normal LFTs,  normal BMP, normal UA.
    5. Distracters – Any distracters can be used including family members.

1. Actors
    1. Roles
        1. One physician leader
        2. One nurse to administer medications
        3. One medical technician to assist
        4. Other participants can help as directed by the leader
        5. One instructor to control the scenario
    2. Who can play them – The leader should be played by a resident.  The instructor should be familiar with the PediaSim. Other roles can be played by residents, students, or other people as appropriate,
    3. Action Role
        1. Physician leader – The leader must coordinate and direct the team, obtain a focused history, perform a pertinent physical exam, order and interpret labs and radiological studies, order treatments and perform/direct advanced procedures.

1. Case Narrative
    1. Scenario given to participants
        1. Chief Complaint – MVA
        2. The physician leader will be given a triage note stating an 8 y/o F arriving by EMS after being a unrestrained backseat passenger involved in a MVA. Vital signs: HR 140 BP 100/65 RR 18, Temp 98
        3. History of present illness (Must be requested): Provided by EMS- The patient was unrestrained sitting in the back seat. Her car was hit while turning left.  The other car was going 35mph.  The patient is crying and says her left upper leg and belly hurt.
        4. Past Medical history (Must be requested): None
        5. Past Surgical history (Must be requested): None
        6. Medication and allergies (Must be requested): None
        7. Social and Family history (Must be requested): Lives at home with her mom, dad, and 2 sisters, no significant family history
    2. Scenario initial conditions:
        1. The leader will be given the triage note above. The patient will be lying on the bed in a c-collar, still clothed, crying. Vitals signs per triage note.
        2. General appearance: Appears in mild distress, Airway intact, Breathing spontaneously, Good pulses bilaterally
        3. HEENT: normocephalic atraumatic, PERRL, no hemotympanum, mouth and throat normal, trachea midline
        4. Respiratory: Good breath sounds bilaterally, no wheezes
        5. Cardiac- tachycardic with no m/r/g
        6. Abdomen: Soft, tender diffusely, but greater over the LUQ with no rebound or peritoneal signs
        7. Pelvis: Pain to palpation over the left ant. pelvis with instability noted.
        8. Extremities: Pain to palpation over left hip, no obvious deformity, will slightly move left leg but says she gets a lot of pain, FROM of all other extremities
        9. Skin: No bruising, rashes or abrasions
        10. Neurological: Intact, symmetric reflexes
        11. GU: Normal rectal tone without blood, no blood in the urethra.
    3. Scenario branch points
        1. Changes in clinical condition: The patient should remain stable until return from the CT scanner if the patient has been stabilized with an initial fluid bolus. After the patient returns or after 8 minutes (if the patient is not sent to CT), the patient’s blood pressure will drop and heart rate will increase until blood is given to the patient. If blood products not given patient’s blood pressure will continue to drop and patient’s rhythm will deteriorate to Asystole.
        2. Responses to therapy: The patient should be given a 20cc/kg fluid bolus and will transiently improve blood pressure and lower heart rate. If a second bolus is given, vials will again transiently improve. However, blood pressure will again decrease and only improve after packed RBCs at 10 ml/kg are given.
        3. Pain medication should be given at appropriate dose for weight. It will decrease the patient’s pelvic pain but not change clinical status.
        4. Oxygen – will not change clinical status
        5. Pelvic compression – A sheet should be wrapped around the pelvis after fracture is identified, but the treatment will not change clinical picture.
        6. If patient’s resuscitation is delayed, the patient will become unresponsive.  The patient should be intubated if this occurs.
        7. A trauma code should be called and surgery should be initially notified that a hypotensive trauma victim has presented. They will not be immediately available. After the splenic laceration and pelvic fracture are identified, surgery should again be notified for further care.

1. Instructor notes
    1. Tips to keep scenario flowing
        1. Prior to scenario patient should be clothed.
        2. HPI should be provided by EMS. Child will only answer pain questions.
        3. Fluids should be given in 20cc/kg, if the correct volume is not ordered, nursing can question the order.
        4. If ABCs and primary survey not done in a timely manner the patient should decompensate quickly.  If the decompensation is recognized and treated, the patient should stabilize so that the work up can be completed.  If the decompensation is not treated within 3 minutes, the patient will deteriorate to PEA. PEA will progress from STach to SBrady.  If IV fluids followed by emergency release blood are not given, then the patient deteriorates to asystole and cannot be resuscitated.  If the PEA is aggressively treated, the patient returns to a hypotensive state with a pulse.  Surgery must be immediately consulted for successful management at this point.
        5. Child should continue to say her abdomen hurts until pain medication is given or she loses consciousness.
    2. Tips to direct actors
        1. The leader should be informed when the child return from the CT scanner.
        2. The physician leader is expected to consult surgery. If he/she does not initiate a consultation at the appropriate time, the actors can prompt leader by asking where the patient will be admitted or how the injuries will be treated.

1. Debriefing plan (Attachment 1) –
        1. The debriefing can occur as a group.  The debriefing should start with a discussion of the main decisions made by the team leader during the resuscitation and his/her thoughts leading to each decision.  Team member comments should be solicited.  Feedback should include review of the initial presentation, findings on exam, and lab/radiology ordered. Binding pelvis and techniques should be covered. Also a discussion of the risks/timing of transporting this patient to the CT scanner should be addressed. Early surgical consultation can also be discussed. Grading and treatment of splenic lacerations can also be discussed.  Any learning objectives not raised during these segments should be reviewed at the end of the debriefing.
        2. Assessment form (Attachment 2) – Completion of critical actions will be recorded on a modification of the standardized direct observation tool (SDOT).2

Attachment 1

Pediatric Case Debriefing Points

1. What went well and what would you do differently if you were going to repeat the exercise? (Direct question to team leader first and then to team members)
2. What was your thought process for the key actions taken or not taken? (Direct question modified to address specific actions to the team leader)
3. Discuss simulation events related to the specific learning objectives (Use a group discussion format led by the instructor):

1. Perform an appropriate primary survey
    1. Airway – with C-spine precautions
    2. Breathing
    3. Circulation
    4. Disability
    5. Exposure
    6. Broslow tape
    7. Pain control

1. Perform a timely and appropriate secondary survey
    1. After the primary survey and interventions a head to toe secondary survey should be performed to identify all injuries.

1. Recognize and treat hypovolemia secondary to internal hemorrhage
    1. Tachycardia is usually the first sign of hypovolemia but vital signs initially can be normal due to cardiovascular reserve
    2. Other signs are mental status changes, delayed capillary refill, skin pallor and hypothermia

1. Demonstrate correct sequence of steps to diagnose/rule out  potential intra-abdominal and pelvic pathology
    1. Perform initial abdominal exam focusing on any bruising, distension, tenderness and peritoneal signs.
    2. Perform initial pelvic exam focusing on stability, pain, rectal tone and blood, and genital exam.
    3. Timely evaluation of a portable pelvic x-ray for fractures.
    4. If patient is stable and abdominal injury is suspected from history or exam, CT is the modality of choice. Initial resuscitation and stabilization should occur before the patient is sent to the CT scanner.  Also the patient should travel on a monitor accompanied by a nurse.

1. Obtain appropriate laboratory and radiology studies
    1. Radiographic evaluation of trauma victims includes C-spine, chest and pelvis.
    2. After these initial studies further radiological studies can be ordered depending on mechanism of injury and physical exam
    3. Laboratory work for trauma victims includes CBC, BMP, UA, and coagulation studies.

1. Initiate a timely for surgical consult
    1. Early surgical consult for decreased GCS or hypovolemia decreases mortality in trauma victims.
