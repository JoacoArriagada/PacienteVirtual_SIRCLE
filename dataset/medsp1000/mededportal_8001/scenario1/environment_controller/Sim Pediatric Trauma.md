1. Pediatric trauma

1. Case Narrative
    1. Scenario given to participants
        1. Chief Complaint – MVA
        2. The physician leader will be given a triage note stating an 8 y/o F arriving by EMS after being a unrestrained backseat passenger involved in a MVA. Vital signs: HR 140 BP 100/65 RR 18, Temp 98
        3. History of present illness (Must be requested): Provided by EMS- The patient was unrestrained sitting in the back seat. Her car was hit while turning left.  The other car was going 35mph.  The patient is crying and says her left upper leg and belly hurt.
        4. Past Medical history (Must be requested): None
        5. Past Surgical history (Must be requested): None
        6. Medication and allergies (Must be requested): None
        7. Social and Family history (Must be requested): Lives at home with her mom, dad, and 2 sisters, no significant family history
    2. Scenario initial conditions:
        1. The leader will be given the triage note above. The patient will be lying on the bed in a c-collar, still clothed, crying. Vitals signs per triage note.
        2. General appearance: Appears in mild distress, Airway intact, Breathing spontaneously, Good pulses bilaterally
        3. HEENT: normocephalic atraumatic, PERRL, no hemotympanum, mouth and throat normal, trachea midline
        4. Respiratory: Good breath sounds bilaterally, no wheezes
        5. Cardiac- tachycardic with no m/r/g
        6. Abdomen: Soft, tender diffusely, but greater over the LUQ with no rebound or peritoneal signs
        7. Pelvis: Pain to palpation over the left ant. pelvis with instability noted.
        8. Extremities: Pain to palpation over left hip, no obvious deformity, will slightly move left leg but says she gets a lot of pain, FROM of all other extremities
        9. Skin: No bruising, rashes or abrasions
        10. Neurological: Intact, symmetric reflexes
        11. GU: Normal rectal tone without blood, no blood in the urethra.
    3. Scenario branch points
        1. Changes in clinical condition: The patient should remain stable until return from the CT scanner if the patient has been stabilized with an initial fluid bolus. After the patient returns or after 8 minutes (if the patient is not sent to CT), the patient’s blood pressure will drop and heart rate will increase until blood is given to the patient. If blood products not given patient’s blood pressure will continue to drop and patient’s rhythm will deteriorate to Asystole.
        2. Responses to therapy: The patient should be given a 20cc/kg fluid bolus and will transiently improve blood pressure and lower heart rate. If a second bolus is given, vials will again transiently improve. However, blood pressure will again decrease and only improve after packed RBCs at 10 ml/kg are given.
        3. Pain medication should be given at appropriate dose for weight. It will decrease the patient’s pelvic pain but not change clinical status.
        4. Oxygen – will not change clinical status
        5. Pelvic compression – A sheet should be wrapped around the pelvis after fracture is identified, but the treatment will not change clinical picture.
        6. If patient’s resuscitation is delayed, the patient will become unresponsive.  The patient should be intubated if this occurs.
        7. A trauma code should be called and surgery should be initially notified that a hypotensive trauma victim has presented. They will not be immediately available. After the splenic laceration and pelvic fracture are identified, surgery should again be notified for further care.

1. Instructor notes
    1. Tips to keep scenario flowing
        1. Prior to scenario patient should be clothed.
        2. HPI should be provided by EMS. Child will only answer pain questions.
        3. Fluids should be given in 20cc/kg, if the correct volume is not ordered, nursing can question the order.
        4. If ABCs and primary survey not done in a timely manner the patient should decompensate quickly.  If the decompensation is recognized and treated, the patient should stabilize so that the work up can be completed.  If the decompensation is not treated within 3 minutes, the patient will deteriorate to PEA. PEA will progress from STach to SBrady.  If IV fluids followed by emergency release blood are not given, then the patient deteriorates to asystole and cannot be resuscitated.  If the PEA is aggressively treated, the patient returns to a hypotensive state with a pulse.  Surgery must be immediately consulted for successful management at this point.
        5. Child should continue to say her abdomen hurts until pain medication is given or she loses consciousness.
    2. Tips to direct actors
        1. The leader should be informed when the child return from the CT scanner.
        2. The physician leader is expected to consult surgery. If he/she does not initiate a consultation at the appropriate time, the actors can prompt leader by asking where the patient will be admitted or how the injuries will be treated.
