**Case Summary/SP Training Notes:** Dennis is a 43 YO man with three children. The family has just moved to the area. This is the first visit to a psychiatrist in town. His last treatment was with a family practitioner six months ago.

**SP demographics: name, gender, age range, and ethnicity:** Dennis, male, 30-50 years old, any ethnicity.

**Setting (i.e. clinic, ER, etc**.): Doctor’s office.

### **History of present complaint:** 43 years old male who presents to clinic complaining of insomnia. He stays up at night writing a book about his life and moving furniture and may fall asleep from 3-6 AM. He states that he has been unable to sleep for several days. He also states that his family told him that he is talking more than usual, louder than usual, and faster than usual. He states that he is very irritable at times. He actually threw the vacuum cleaner at the kids because they didn’t do their chores. He has gone shopping and maxed out all of his credit cards. He has quit his job, and bought a new car. He has had a similar episode six months ago, with similar symptoms for 3 weeks, followed by 2 weeks of low moods, when he “crashed”. At that time the PCP prescribed Prozac but he stopped it after few weeks because he felt better.

In the past, when “crashing”, Dennis stays in bed for days, feels hopeless, like he is not a good father, thinks of suicide. Never attempted suicide or made plans of suicide because “I don’t want to do this to my children; my mother committed suicide when I was 12”. During this time he loses weight because of not eating, doesn’t take a shower every day and can’t pay attention to things. Everything makes him nervous and he is irritable, people “step on his nerves”.

He has never seen a psychiatrist before.

**Past medical history:** Diabetes, diagnosed five years ago.

**Family medical history:** Father died ten years ago of natural causes. Mother suffered from depression and committed suicide when Dennis was a teenager. Mother had diabetes. Brother has bipolar disorder. Children are alive and well.

**Social history:** Dennis found a job as an office manager as soon as he moved to town with his wife, but he quit his job a few days ago. He’s been married for twenty years with three children aged 10, 12, and 15. He recently moved to town so that his wife could take a job as an engineer at a paper plant here. He denies drug use, but states that he has been drinking more wine than usual every night to help with sleep**.**

**Physical examination findings (if indicated):**

**MSE:** Dressed in bright colors with cowboy boots or another distinctive feature. He is fidgety and can’t sit still. His affect is elated and silly and he laughs a lot. Speech is fast and pressured (it is difficult for others to interrupt him).

**Diagnosis:** Bipolar Disorder, current episode manic. Rule out substance induced manic episode.

**Management plan:** The student will likely offer to bring the attending physician in for management suggestions. The student may offer to admit voluntarily to psychiatric hospital for medication management or call wife and ask her to supervise the patient closely and bring him for appointment next day after labs are done and medication is started. The student may state that the attending physician will prescribe something for sleep.

**If identified as a communication case:** The student must remain focused on obtaining vital information including performing a suicide risk assessment in a patient who has fast and pressured speech and has flight or ideas. Eliciting this information in a 15 min interview represents a challenge.

**Patient presentation (affect, appearance, position of patient at opening (i.e. sitting, lying down, holding abdomen etc.):** Silly elated affect. Lots of laughing. Dressed in bright colors with another distinctive feature for Dennis (boots, silly hat, etc.). Fast and pressured speech.

# **Example student write-up for DENNIS SP**

**Chief complaint:** “Trouble sleeping.”

**History of present complaint:** 43 years old male who presents complaining of insomnia. He has been unable to sleep for several days. He states that his family told him that he is talking louder and faster than usual. He is very irritable at times (threw the vacuum cleaner at the kids because they didn’t do their chores). Went shopping and maxed out all of his credit cards. Quit his job and bought a new car. He has had a similar episode six months ago. He has never seen a psychiatrist before. After episodes of elevated mood which last about 3-4 weeks he “crashes” (has depressed mood). When depressed he also becomes nervous, irritable, loses weight and does not take care of himself. He denies having made suicide attempts but has had thoughts of suicide in the past. He does not have weapons in the house and is not suicidal now.

**Past medical history:** Diabetes, diagnosed five years ago, n metformin. P**ast psychiatric history:** His last treatment was with a family practitioner six months ago. Treated with Prozac for Major Depression; stopped medication on his own because he felt better.

**Family history:** Father died of natural causes. Mother suffered from depression and committed suicide when Dennis was a teenager. Mother had diabetes. Brother has bipolar disorder. Children are healthy.

**Social history:** Dennis found a job as an office manager as soon as he moved to town with his wife, but he quit his job a few days ago. He’s been married for twenty years with three children aged 10, 12, and 15. He recently moved to town so that his wife could take a job as an engineer at a paper plant here. He denies drug use, but states that he has been drinking more wine than usual every night (3-4 glasses) to help with sleep**.**

**Mental Status Exam:** Well-groomed Caucasian male dressed in bright colors with distinctive clothing. He is fidgety. His affect is elated and silly and he laughs a lot. Speech is fast and pressured. Thought process with flight of ideas and thought content with grandiosity. Denies suicide, homicide thoughts and hallucinations. His insight and judgment are limited. Memory is preserved but he is distractible. MMSE is hard to perform due to flight of ideas.

**Differential diagnosis:**

Bipolar Disorder, current episode manic: elevated mood, rapid pressured speech, flight of ideas, increased goal directed behavior, increased energy level, decreased need for sleep in past 2 weeks.

Rule out manic episode induced by substance or another medical condition but patient denies using drugs, recently increased alcohol consumption; only medical problem is diabetes.

Work-up: physical exam, complete blood count with differential, comprehensive metabolic profile, thyroid function tests, urine drug screen, and urinalysis to look for possible drugs that the patient did not disclose or medical conditions that may cause his presentation.
