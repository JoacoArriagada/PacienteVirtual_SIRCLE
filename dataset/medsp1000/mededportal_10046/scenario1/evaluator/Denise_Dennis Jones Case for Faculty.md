**Case Summary/SP Training Notes:** Denise is a 43 YO woman with three children. The family has just moved to the area. This is the first visit to a psychiatrist in town. Her last treatment was with a family practitioner six months ago.

**SP demographics: name, gender, age range, and ethnicity:** Denise, female, 30-50 years old, any ethnicity.

### **History of present complaint:** 43 years old female who presents to clinic complaining of insomnia. She stays up at night writing a book about her life and moving furniture and may fall asleep from 3-6 AM. She states that she has been unable to sleep for several days. She also states that her family told her that she is talking more than usual, louder than usual, and faster than usual. She states that she is very irritable at times. She actually threw the vacuum cleaner at the kids because they didn’t do their chores. She has gone shopping and maxed out all of her credit cards. She has quit her job, and bought a new car. She has had a similar episode six months ago, with similar symptoms for 3 weeks, followed by 2 weeks of low moods, when she “crashed”. At that time the PCP prescribed Prozac but she stopped it after few weeks because she felt better.

In the past, when “crashing”, Denise stays in bed for days, feels hopeless, like she is not a good mother, thinks of suicide. Never attempted suicide or made plans of suicide because “I don’t want to do this to my children; my mother committed suicide when I was 12”. During this time she loses weight because of not eating, doesn’t take a shower every day and can’t pay attention to things. Everything makes her nervous and she is irritable, people “step on her nerves”.

She has never seen a psychiatrist before.

**Past medical history:** Diabetes, diagnosed five years ago.

**Family medical history:** Father died ten years ago of natural causes. Mother suffered from depression and committed suicide when Denise was a teenager. Mother had diabetes. Brother has bipolar disorder. Children are alive and well.

**Social history:** Denise found a job as an office manager as soon as she moved to town with her husband, but she quit her job a few days ago. She’s been married for twenty years with three children aged 10, 12, and 15. She recently moved to town so that her husband could take a job as an engineer at a paper plant here. She denies drug use, but states that she has been drinking more wine than usual every night to help with sleep**.**

**Physical examination findings (if indicated):**

**MSE:** Dressed in bright colors with lots of eye make-up. She is fidgety and can’t sit still. Her affect is elated and silly and she laughs a lot. Speech is fast and pressured (it is difficult for others to interrupt her).

**Diagnosis:** Bipolar Disorder, current episode manic. Rule out substance induced manic episode.

**Management plan:** The student will likely offer to bring the attending physician in for management suggestions. The student may offer to admit voluntarily to psychiatric hospital for medication management or call husband and ask him to supervise the patient closely and bring her for appointment next day after labs are done and medication is started. The student may state that the attending physician will prescribe something for sleep.

**If identified as a communication case:** The student must remain focused on obtaining vital information including performing a suicide risk assessment in a patient who has fast and pressured speech and has flight or ideas. Eliciting this information in a 15 min interview represents a challenge.

# **Example student write-up for DENISE SP**

**Chief complaint:** “Trouble sleeping.”

**History of present complaint:** 43 years old female who presents complaining of insomnia. She has been unable to sleep for several days. She states that her family told her that she is talking louder and faster than usual. She is very irritable at times (threw the vacuum cleaner at the kids because they didn’t do their chores). Went shopping and maxed out all of her credit cards. Quit her job and bought a new car. She has had a similar episode six months ago. She has never seen a psychiatrist before. After episodes of elevated mood which last about 3-4 weeks she “crashes” (has depressed mood). When depressed she also becomes nervous, irritable, loses weight and does not take care of herself. She denies having made suicide attempts but has had thoughts of suicide in the past. She does not have weapons in the house and is not suicidal now.

**Past medical history:** Diabetes, diagnosed five years ago, n metformin. P**ast psychiatric history:** Her last treatment was with a family practitioner six months ago. Treated with Prozac for Major Depression; stopped medication on her own because she felt better.

**Family history:** Father died of natural causes. Mother suffered from depression and committed suicide when Denise was a teenager. Mother had diabetes. Brother has bipolar disorder. Children are healthy.

**Social history:** Denise found a job as an office manager as soon as she moved to town with her husband, but she quit her job a few days ago. She’s been married for twenty years with three children aged 10, 12, and 15. She recently moved to town so that her husband could take a job as an engineer at a paper plant here. She denies drug use, but states that she has been drinking more wine than usual every night (3-4 glasses) to help with sleep**.**

**Mental Status Exam:** Well-groomed Caucasian female dressed in bright colors with lots of make-up. She is fidgety. Her affect is elated and silly and she laughs a lot. Speech is fast and pressured. Thought process with flight of ideas and thought content with grandiosity. Denies suicide, homicide thoughts and hallucinations. Her insight and judgment are limited. Memory is preserved but she is distractible. MMSE is hard to perform due to flight of ideas.

**Differential diagnosis:**

Bipolar Disorder, current episode manic: elevated mood, rapid pressured speech, flight of ideas, increased goal directed behavior, increased energy level, decreased need for sleep in past 2 weeks.

Rule out manic episode induced by substance or another medical condition but patient denies using drugs, recently increased alcohol consumption; only medical problem is diabetes.

Work-up: physical exam, complete blood count with differential, comprehensive metabolic profile, thyroid function tests, urine drug screen, pregnancy test, and urinalysis to look for possible drugs that the patient did not disclose or medical conditions that may cause her presentation.
