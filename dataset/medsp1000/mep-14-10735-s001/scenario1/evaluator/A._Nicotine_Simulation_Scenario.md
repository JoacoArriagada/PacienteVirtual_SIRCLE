| **Brief narrative description of case** | The triage nurse informs you that she is bringing back a child with altered mental status. The patient’s mother reports that he became sleepy and minimally responsive and has been this way for about half an hour. She noticed at one point, on the way to the hospital, that he was looking off to the right and had stiff arms. He has also been drooling, and he had one episode of non-bloody and non-projectile emesis. **Ideal Scenario Flow**

The learner enters the room to find the patient seizing. The patient is placed on bedside monitors. The patient is recognized to be having altered mental status consistent with seizures, but patient protecting airway. ABCs performed. IV access obtained, and lab work is sent. First dose of benzodiazepine given with good response. Now that the patient is somewhat stabilized, the learner obtains more information from mom/parent, and uncovers that there could have been a liquid nicotine ingestion. The learner exposes/undresses the patient to remove any nicotine-stained clothing. After this step, and while awaiting lab results, the patient begins to vomit. This is treated by the learner with antiemetics. After the emesis, the patient begins to seize again, at which point a second dose of benzodiazepine is given, with good result (the patient stops seizing and vitals improve). After this, there are desaturations to the mid 80%s, at which point the learner performs airway protection maneuvers (jaw thrust, oxygen mask, bag ventilation, NP/oral airway insertion, and potentially intubation). As the patient is stabilized and there is an explanation for his symptoms, the learner calls the PICU to admit the patient.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Failure to identify history of toxic ingestion as a cause of seizures: Good history taking from mom is important, as it will alert the learner to a cause for the patient’s symptoms.
2. Requesting activated charcoal: In cases where a patient is vomiting, activated charcoal is not indicated, given that there is potential for aspiration.
3. Failure to expose/decontaminate patient: All tox victims should be appropriately decontaminated, especially in the setting of potential nicotine exposure to skin.
