**Application Exercise**

Although she has no family history of MCAD, Dee reports that she has a brother and a maternal uncle who are disabled. Both males have intellectual disabilities, behavioral problems, and they are confined to wheelchairs. Dee states that her family has focused on serving the needs of these young men and has not questioned the cause.

During the genetic counseling session, you informed Dee that there are many causes of the type of disabilities present in her brother and uncle, including some that may be inherited. An accurate diagnosis would be needed in order to know if the condition is heritable. Afterward, Dee shared this information with her mother. One year later, Dee returns and reports that her brother and her uncle have been diagnosed with Lesch-Nyhan syndrome. Dee is seven weeks pregnant. What is the risk that Dee’s child will have Lesch-Nyhan syndrome?
   1. 2/3
   2. 1/2
   3. 1/4
   4. 1/8

*Instructional Notes: The correct answer is 1/8 (½ x ½ x ½)*

*Students must determine the mode of inheritance (X-linked recessive) from the reading assignment on Lesch-Nyhan, and they must correctly determine that Dee’s mother is an obligate carrier. Then, Dee’s risk to be a carrier is ½, the chance she would pass the mutation to a child is ½, and the probability of a male is ½.*

*Typically, team discussions begin with an assessment of X-linked segregation as it applies to the pedigree. Since early learners often find X-linked inheritance more challenging to master, discussion of this question has been especially useful for weaker students. Some teams have requested confirmation that the sex of Dee’s fetus is unknown in order to decide if they need to include the ½ probability for a male. Some students are confused by the existence of two independent conditions within the pedigree, and team discussion is needed to resolve the sources of risk in the family history. In spite of the fact that students have only superficial understanding of the clinical aspects of these two disorders, the family history often generates rich discussion of the ways in which families’ perceptions of disease burdens vary. To solve the problem, some teams have attempted to follow the same approach used for question 1, using the disease incidence given in the reading assignment for a Hardy-Weinberg calculation, but eventually realize that population risk does not come into play.*

1. Reflecting on all aspects of the case, how would you proceed in making recommendations to the parents about genetic evaluation and testing during the current pregnancy?
   1. Focus on the risk of Lesch-Nyhan syndrome based on the risk estimate for the fetus.
   2. Focus on the risk of MCAD based on the parents’ anxiety about the possibility of sudden infant death.
   3. Focus on enabling the parents to make an informed decision about managing the risk to their fetus for Lesch-Nyhan syndrome and MCAD.
   4. Use the estimated risks to reassure the parents that the fetus is unlikely to be affected with either condition. Offer to revisit the issue after the birth of their child.

*Instructional Notes: The intended best answer is option c, Focus on enabling the parents to make an informed decision about managing the risk to their fetus for Lesch-Nyhan syndrome and MCAD. After comparing the numerical risk estimates, team discussions tend to focus on how the couple’s family experiences have influenced their perception of risk. In our experience, every team has chosen the intended best response, recognizing the value of genetic counseling and patient autonomy.*

*The purpose of this question is to allow students to reflect on various aspects of the case:*

* *Compare the numerical risks for the fetus to inherit each condition. Then consider the clinical implications and possible outcomes of the two disorders. Clearly, the genetic risk was much higher for Lesch-Nyhan syndrome. However, the numerical risk needs to be evaluated within the context of the disease burden and the couple’s attitudes and beliefs about prenatal testing and other personal issues. In this case, the couple’s initial concern was greater for risk of sudden death than it was for risk of physical and intellectual disability.*
* *Nondirective genetic counseling is a tenet of medical genetics. Students should reflect on the autonomy of the couple to make their own informed decision that is independent from past family history and from the influences of others, including family members and healthcare providers. The couple has options (and thus decisions to be made) that were not available to their parents. Only option c is consistent with nondirective counseling; the other three options include a value judgment of the risk.*
* *Students may not be familiar with testing options for Lesch-Nyhan syndrome. As stated previously for MCAD, a thorough discussion is beyond the scope of this exercise, but a source of additional information for facilitators is available at GeneReviews [*[*http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1149/*](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1149/)*].*
