**Application Exercise**

A young couple (Dee and Abe) seeks genetic counseling based on concern about their risk to have a child with MCAD deficiency (medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency).

In collecting the family history, you learn the following.

Abe had a brother who died suddenly in infancy. The brother was diagnosed with MCAD after death. There are no other individuals with MCAD in either family. The unexpected death of his apparently healthy brother remains a source of grief and anxiety for the family.

Although she has no family history of MCAD, Dee reports that she has a brother and a maternal uncle who are disabled. Both males have intellectual disabilities, behavioral problems, and they are confined to wheelchairs. Dee states that her family has focused on serving the needs of these young men and has not questioned the cause.

During the genetic counseling session, you informed Dee that there are many causes of the type of disabilities present in her brother and uncle, including some that may be inherited. An accurate diagnosis would be needed in order to know if the condition is heritable. Afterward, Dee shared this information with her mother. One year later, Dee returns and reports that her brother and her uncle have been diagnosed with Lesch-Nyhan syndrome. Dee is seven weeks pregnant.
