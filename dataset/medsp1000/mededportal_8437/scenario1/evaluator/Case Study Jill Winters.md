**INSOMNIA – Jill Winters CONFIDENTIAL**

**Chief Complaint:**

“My hands hurt. I’m working on a big paper, and I can’t type because it hurts.”

**History of Present Illness**:

Ms. Winters is a 35 year old student at the Duke Divinity School. She came to Duke this fall from Connecticut.

For the past month, she’s had increasing discomfort in her hands, right worse than left. The thumb, index, and middle fingers tingle, and sometimes feel like they’re being pricked or stabbed with pins. The pain has awakened her at night. It also is bad when she tries to do a lot of computer work. She has to take a break and shake out her hands before she can go back to the keyboard. Perhaps some vague ache in the forearms, and sometimes in her neck and shoulders attributed to stress. She’s had no recent or prior injuries. No new activities other than she’s started grad school this fall.

When asked about sleep or general status, she also reveals “I’m having a lot of trouble falling asleep. My thoughts seem to be racing and I’m very jittery.” The severity of insomnia has been progressive. After her first few days in Durham, she had difficulty falling asleep; it took about an hour for her to fall asleep when sleep formerly had been achieved within 5 minutes of lying down. At the time, she attributed this to a new environment, school starting, and some “runny nose and congestion.” The nasal congestion lessened after she began taking some medication recommended by a classmate. However, the difficulty falling asleep has progressed, and it now takes her at least 1-2 hours to fall asleep at night, and she wakes frequently through the night. She has no prior history of insomnia.

Allergies: No known drug allergies; she had seasonal allergies as a young teen in New Hampshire and received desensitization injections for 1-2 years.

Medications: Yasmin (Birth Control Pill). Also takes a small red allergy pill recommended by classmate (pseudo-something?? – has to sign for them at pharmacy). She takes 2 of these every 4 hours as needed for nasal congestion. Has tried Tylenol for the hand pain, without much relief.

Alcohol: Glass of white wine once/week when socializing with friends.

Drugs: Denies recreational drug use

Tobacco: Smoked cigarettes as part of a rebellious phase in high school but that lasted for only a few weeks.

**Observations**:

Ms. Winters is an appropriately dressed adult female (mature college student attire; backpack, tote, or casual briefcase) who is friendly and interactive. She does appear intense and nervous and shakes one of her crossed legs continuously during the interview. She also may rub her hands/wrists. If her jitteriness is commented up on by the interviewer, she will acknowledge that her classmates have told her she “always seems in a hurry.”

**Personal and Social History**:

Ms. Winters was born and raised in New Hampshire in a loving and supportive family. She wanted to “strike out on her own” when she chose to attend Penn State as a philosophy major. She subsequently earned a master’s degree in Asian Civilization at Yale, and it was there that she became interested in pursuing a career in ministry. She admires her older brother greatly and he is now a minister. She has held employment as a graduate assistant, research assistant, and college instructor, and she waited tables during the summer right before she came to Duke for divinity school. She has been in a stable, monogamous, heterosexual relationship for three years. Her boyfriend/partner is teaching math at a local prep school and they plan to marry when Ms. Winters finishes divinity school. She is happy with her decision to come to Duke and to pursue ministry, although she will state that she was not prepared the amount of school work she has.

She describes her diet as “lousy” – lots of food “on the go” and plenty of coffee (3-4 “Grande” Starbucks/day) and occasional energy drinks when she doesn’t have time to eat. Although she once practiced tai chi and ran several times a week, she finds she has had no time to do either anymore. Once in a while, she and her boyfriend enjoy cycling out in the country.

Safety measures: Wears seat belt consistently and home has smoke and CO detectors.
