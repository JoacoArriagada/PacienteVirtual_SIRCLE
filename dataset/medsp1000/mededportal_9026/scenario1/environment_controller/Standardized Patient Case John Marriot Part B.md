**Patient Name:** J. Marriot

**Patient Presentation:** Middle-age. Physically healthy-appearing. Loquacious with rapid speech. Difficulty sitting still. Elated affect.

**Necessary equipment, props, makeup, moulage, costume, etc.:** Religious/New Age reading material (can be a journal with flyers), crystal/pendant, all-white or very colorful attire.

Standardized Patient Training Summary

Opening Statement: "My spouse and my brother insisted that I come in. They think I need help but it's just some stress I'm having with work."

**Summary of case:**

This scenario is a psychiatric clinic evaluation of a middle-aged married adult employed as a budget analyst for a military contractor. The spouse recommended that the patient see a psychiatrist because they were concerned with erratic behavior recently. Particularly concerning to them are personality changes, that the patient is now very excitable and talking about a "soulmate" who is not the spouse, and new-found religiosity. The boss has also expressed concern about work performance. The patient agreed to the evaluation, but thinks the family is "concerned over nothing."

Patient Affect and Attitude:

**Physical:** The patient stands, and remains standing, acting very animated and excited for about 20 seconds when the medical student enters the room, and introduces themself using their full name: J. Fitzgerald Marriott ("My mother had a thing for the Kennedys!!"). The patient is extremely excited about the new spiritual group and feels it is their duty and privilege to impart those feelings upon whoever will listen. The patient is also fidgeting a lot, and frequently stands up to pace around. The patient is generally cooperative with the interview, but gets distracted and has a hard time staying sitting still or on topic.

**Verbal:** The patient speaks clearly but very rapidly. The patient is friendly, and the tone is exceedingly upbeat. The patient smiles and even chuckles at times during the interview. The patient goes off on verbal tangents during the interview.

*** At some point in the interview, the patient asks the student if it would be okay to hold the student's hands to release "negative energy" as this is something taught in the religious group. Many students will decline, which the patient accepts after asking, "Are you sure? I think it will be very helpful to you." If the offer is accepted, the patient lightly squeezes both hands for ten seconds while closing their eyes, then releases them with the comment, "There's a lot of energy there."

**Recent History:**

For the last 2 months, the patient has been spending a lot of time with a spiritual group of people and reports being a "New Age Believer". The patient has joined a group called "The Third Mind". The patient does not see the group as a "religion in the traditional sense of the word" but as "my spiritual anchor." The leader of the group is referred to as "The Ella". Her real name is Alice Clark. With this group of people, the patient feels appreciated and loved, and it was at one of these meetings where the patient met a "soulmate." The group is located in a store front in a strip mall near the patient's house. The patient was walking by an open door and made eye contact with someone inside, and "was drawn in, like there was some sort of immediate connection." When asked to provide details about this group, the patient is unable to be too specific about the group or its beliefs. For the last couple of months, the patient has spent all free time reading about spirituality, new age philosophy, and mysticism.

Other Recent Behaviors:

* The patient charged $500 worth of books on these topics on a credit card, which is now past the credit limit. The patient also reports a need to buy more appropriate new clothes that are more fitting for a "New Age Believer". The patient withdrew $700 from a savings account last week to buy clothes that reflect "new spiritual wisdom."
* The patient no longer needs much sleep, and is sleeping 2 to 4 hours each night, waking up energetic and either reading or entering New Age chat rooms on the computer. "It's great. It's amazing how much I can get done."
* The patient has also started weight lifting for the first time in 10 years, and spends 2 hours per day at the gym. "My body is a temple."
* Appetite has been normal.
* The patient feels like they might be the Archangel Michael, but isn't sure. The patient does not believe they have any special powers, and is not hearing voices.
* The patient has been having a lot of insight lately into spirituality and is keeping a journal to jot down all of these thoughts. The patient thinks that this book might be a future "Bible-like" text for others.
* If asked about the relationship with the spouse, the patient notes that the spouse is never interested in the patient's beliefs, and that over the past 4 days the patient has concluded the spouse is not a good match.
* The patient met another adult a few months ago who the patient feels is a "soulmate," and is thinking of leaving the spouse. The patient had sex with this person, a 22-year-old adult, once about 2 months ago. Shortly after that the patient talked with the spouse about opening the marriage to include this person "in every way." The patient has not had sex with either the spouse or this person since then. Sex drive is high. However, all of the patient's energy is going to new activities and acquaintances. The patient is "ecstatic" about the changes in life. The patient describes the interest in this person as "beyond sex".

Other Psychiatric Symptoms:

* The patient does NOT see things or hear things that others do not see or hear.
* Mood is "wonderful." The patient is NOT depressed. The patient is NOT anxious.
* The patient is NOT suicidal: "Don't be silly. Why would I do that? Life is a wonderful journey."
* The patient is NOT homicidal or having thoughts of harming others.
* The patient normally drank about one beer per night, but since joining the spiritual group has stopped drinking any alcohol, believing that it will "harm my spirit". The patient doesn't use any illicit or over the counter drugs. The patient does consume about 2 cups of coffee per day. The patient quit smoking 10 years ago.

**Past Psychiatric History:**

* If asked whether the patient has ever felt like this before, the patient indicates that 5 years ago they got very involved in a Protestant Church for a month. The patient started attending church twice a day and volunteered for activities which conflicted with work. The patient missed work during this time period, and supervisors insisted on a commitment to the job or discipline would follow. The patient forced themself to stop participating in all these activities, and after a week or two, lost interest in this church. The patient almost had an affair during this time period, but refrained. The patient also only slept a few hours per night, but gradually returned to a normal 7 hours. The spouse insisted the patient needed help, but the patient never sought any medical or mental health care.
* The patient has never been psychiatrically hospitalized, taken psychiatric medications or attended any therapy.
* The patient has never attempted suicide.

**Past Medical History:** Physical development was normal and the patient has been in good health. The patient does not have any medical problems. The patient has never had any surgeries or hospitalizations. The patient does not take any medications.

**Family Psychiatric History:** The patient is not sure about a family history of mental illness, although the patient remembers the mother as having lots of energy followed by periods of moodiness and sadness. She never sought help for this as far as the patient knows.

**Social and Developmental History:**

The patient is the older of 2 children who were raised by both parents in Baltimore. The patient describes having a "good" childhood, and was never abused in any way. The patient was raised as a Roman Catholic, and the family attended church once a week. The mother was a sales clerk at Sears Roebuck, and the father was an accountant. The patient describes the mother as "very giving, but I wasn't in tune with that sort of love". The patient describes the father as "extremely harsh". The patient has never been close to the father, even as an adult. The parents are both retired and still live together in Baltimore. The brother, Paul, is 2 years younger, lives nearby, and works as a Dominican priest. The patient and brother are on friendly terms, and he is usually a good source of support. However, at times the patient worries about his motivations, and feels he is jealous of the patient's brains, skills, looks, family and, most recently, spiritual insights.

The patient did well in school and reports having been able to graduate early, but chose not to. The patient had some good friends in high school, and almost got expelled senior year for lighting a firecracker in a pipe in a water fountain. Although suspended for that, the patient never had any troubles with the law. The patient attended college, got a BA in liberal arts, worked for a while at a local community college before becoming a government contractor. The patient met the spouse, a nurse, while in college, and has been married since shortly after college. The patient has 3 children, ages 20, 19, and 15, and gets along alright with each of them. The patient believes the children will appreciate the patient later, through the patient's "writings".

The patient works as a budget analyst and oversees a large government contract for computer services provided to the military. The contract involves about 200 employees located in 3 states that are overseen by 5 or 6 program managers. The program managers are the patient's primary contacts. The patient describes being bored with the job, particularly with the lack of challenge. The patient is angry about being passed over for promotion twice, and feels that the company has not appreciated the patient's skills. When asked about problems at work, the patient describes being "talked to a couple of times" for missing work to attend the religious meetings and work on the book. The patient says that coworkers comment about the patient "having a lot of energy but get nothing done," and "talk too much about this religious group." The retort is that "They just don't understand." The patient has no history of marital problems, legal problems, or previous thoughts of divorce.

**NOTES on BEHAVIOR/RESPONSES, ATTITUDE TOWARD STUDENT:** In general, the patient is familiar and very friendly with the student. If the student brings up a "manic" diagnosis, the patient laughs that off. The patient might ask if there is some sort of conspiracy between the spouse, the brother, and the student, but remains pleasant.
