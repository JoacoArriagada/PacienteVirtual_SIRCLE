**Instructor Guide: Disclosing the Death of a Child**

**Goal**: This activity is meant to teach residents how to communicate a death disclosure to a family member with clarity and compassion.

**Learning Objectives**: At the end of this activity, the learner should be expected to:

1. Describe the steps involved in a death disclosure.
2. Perform a death disclosure to a patient’s family member.
3. Reflect on a death disclosure and identify areas for improvement.

**Implementation**

Files included in the submission

1. DD\_Child\_Door\_Prompt
   1. Should be provided to the learner for review prior to entering the room.
2. DD\_Child\_SP\_Info
   1. Provide to SP as part of training
   2. Details information regarding setting, family dynamics, prior knowledge upon arrival in the emergency department, mood, affect, and demeanor.
3. DD\_Child\_Script
   1. Provide to SP as part of training.
   2. Scripted responses to learner
   3. Presented sequentially in order of steps in death disclosure.
   4. Contains anticipated statements by the learner
   5. Includes verbal and non-verbal cues.
4. MDOT\_DD\_v2
   1. Provide to faculty rater (or SP) in advance of the session.
   2. Presented sequentially in order of steps in death disclosure.
   3. For each critical action, rater should select needs improvement, meets expectations, above expectations, n/a if not observed, and add comments as necessary.
   4. The scores on this form can be verbally discussed with the learner as well.
   5. Can be used for formative or summative assessment.

A one hour didactic session on death disclosure for the interns preceded the SP encounters. Each intern rotated through two different cases, of which this case was one. During the scenario, they were observed by the SP, the faculty rater, and a peer, all of whom delivered feedback. The faculty rater completed the direct observation tool which was created using language which maps to the EM milestones. The tool was reviewed for content validity by palliative care experts.

Debriefing following the session takes place with the faculty, the resident, a peer, and the SP all present. The learner is invited to provide a self-critique first, followed by the peer and SP, and finally the faculty member. Discussion focuses on the emotional toll of this conversation on both the learner and the SP, as well as the resident’s ability to utilize the format presented in the didactic session.
