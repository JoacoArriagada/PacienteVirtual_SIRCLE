**Ideal Scenario Flow**

-Initial – place patient on monitor, oxygen by nasal cannula. Ask for assistance. The learner should request that the nurses attempt to access port, but they will find the port is not working. The learner will then request the nurse attempt IV access. Labs should also be drawn when IV access obtained.

Prompts: if asked for repeat vitals, ask if they would like patient to be placed on the monitor

If asked for bolus, reply that IV access hasn’t been obtained – should they obtain access? If labs are not requested, ask if learner would like for blood to be drawn when access obtained. The learner should request the following: blood glucose, venous blood gas (VBG), blood cultures (two culture sites preferred), metabolic panel, PT/INR/PTT, urinalysis, urine culture, chest xray, c-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin. They may consider a respiratory viral panel. The learner should also order a chest xray at this point.

If learner asks for initial bolus at this point, reply that you will inform learner when access obtained.

-While attempting access, learner should continue taking history. Before physical exam is initiated, inform learner that two nurses have each attempted access x 2. State that they were able to obtain blood for labs, but “the IV blew when we tried to flush it”.

-Learner should move to intraosseous (IO) access at this point. If ultrasound or central line kit requested, reply that these will take several minutes to obtain – is there any way to get access faster? Both manual and drill-inserted devices are acceptable answers for this patient.

Ask the learner for brief explanation of location and technique. Briefly, the learner should palpate the insertion location 1-2 fingers below tibial tuberosity, medial along flat plate. The site should be thoroughly cleaned. Insert needle at 90° angle to skin. Ask the learner where the intraosseous access device(s) can be found at your institution – remind them that they may be the only person who knows this location when a critically ill patient arrives.

-Once intraosseous access is obtained, first bolus should be given as push-pull. If this is not stated initially, note that you have the fluid wide open but it does not seem to be flowing well. Antibiotics may be started at this point as well. If asked, antibiotic recommendations for children on chemotherapy are cefepime, tobramycin, vancomycin. Learners may also consult a “standard text” if asked.

-Following completion of history and physical, state that the first bolus has finished.

On repeat evaluation: HR 152 BP 86/40, RR 45, oxygen saturation 90%RA (or 97% on oxygen, if oxygen place). Flash capillary refill with bounding pulses. Rhonchi right lower lung fields, but otherwise no crackles. Blood glucose and VBG results now available. At this point, the child vomits and appear lethargic after; she is not coughing or gagging. The patient’s mouth should be suctioned at this point – prompt the learner to do so if this is not mentioned. The patient has no response to suctioning.

-At this point, child needs intubation and second bolus. May initiate second bolus while preparing for intubation. Ask the learner to discuss the steps of intubation in this patient, including medications and equipment sizes. Given the patient’s tenuous hemodynamic status due to septic shock, ketamine is the preferred induction agent. If the learner requests another medication, prompt them by asking if they are concerned about the effect on the patient’s blood pressure. For advanced learners, discuss consideration of starting vasopressors prior to intubation or having this medication immediately available.

-The second bolus should be initiated at this point, if not already started. If the learner does not start a second fluid bolus, ask if they would like to give additional fluids. If antibiotics have not been started yet, ask if there are any other medications they would like to give.

-If need for intubation has not been recognized, prompt the learner by noting that the oxygen saturation has dropped to 92%. If the need for intubation is still not recognized, nurse states “Do you think she aspirated? I’m worried that she might have!” immediately before child vomits again.

-Second bolus now finished. On repeat evaluation: HR 148, BP 74/34, RR vent, Sat 100% on vent. On repeat examination, patient now has crackles bilaterally. If asked, ultrasound shows plump IVC (inferior vena cava) with no collapse during respirations.

-Initiate vasopressor support – consider including discussion of what vasopressors are immediately available at your institution.

-On reassessment following initiation of vasopressor support: HR 110, BP 88/54. Oxygen saturation 100% on vent.

-At this point, learner should admit the patient to the PICU. If the learner requests admission but does not specify need for ICU-level care, ask where the patient should go (ie: PICU, stepdown unit, or floor). If admission not requested, ask learner if they would like you to page the floor team for admission. If the admitting team is paged before initiation of vasopressor support, they will not call back until vasopressor support has been initiated.

-Case presentation by the learner. The learner should present the case as if presenting the patient to the PICU attending. If the learner omits any important information or critical steps in the resuscitation, ask for these details or clarification about what was done in the Emergency Department.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Failure to recognize need for IO access: Learners are often reluctant to obtain IO access and may spend too long waiting for a peripheral IV. Discuss importance of early access.
2. Failure to give fluids as push-pull: In cases of severe septic shock, fluids must be given rapidly. Allowing fluids to drip in is too slow, and in pediatric patients, pressure bag may blow small veins. Discuss technique for giving fluids as push-pull.
3. Need for intubation: Learners should recognize decline in patient status and need for intubation. Given patient’s low blood pressure, discuss consideration for starting pressor support prior to intubation or being prepared to start immediately after.
4. Difficulty with presenting the case: Many residents struggle when presenting cases to consultants, especially early in training. Discuss how to present this relatively complex case in a systematic, organized manner.
