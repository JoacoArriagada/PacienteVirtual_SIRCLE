Learning to Beat the Shock Clock:

A Low-fidelity Simulation Board Game for Pediatric and Emergency Medicine Residents

E. Page Bridges MD, Catherine E. Foster MD, Daniel B. Park MD, Kathy L. Lehman-Huskamp MD, Dan W. Mark MD, and, Rachel E. Tuuri MD

***Materials List***

2 x Mini three-part corrugated display boards (22” x 14”)

Hook and loop (Velcro) strips and dots

Copy paper in 2-3 colors

Laminator (can be done at commercial copy/print center)

Construction or backing paper to decorate board (optional)

Card files (separate attachments)

***Board Assembly Instructions***

1. Print Stanley cartoon figure.
2. Print vital sign cards (available in PowerPoint, “Vitals Cards”). Please print these cards as 4 slides/page. Laminate these and cut them into individual cards.
3. Print intervention labels (available in Word, Appendix E: Labels). Print the first two pages (interventions) in one color and the final page (tests to order) in a second color. These should be a different color than that chosen for the vital sign cards. Laminate all of these and cut them into individual labels.
4. Print lab/x-ray results cards (available in PowerPoint, Appendix C: Stanley Labs). Please print these cards as 4 slides/page. Laminate these and cut them into individual cards.
5. On the three-part board, label the top with the name of the “patient” – consider decorating this portion or using colorful backing to add color to the game board!
6. Apply the “hook” side of the hook and loop (Velcro) dots to the back of each of the laminated cards (vitals, interventions, tests, and results).
7. On the left section of the board, apply two columns of the “loop” side of the hook and loop (Velcro) strips or dots. Place the intervention labels in these two columns – these are the interventions available to the players.
8. In the middle section of the board, place two boxes of colored construction paper with a “loop” side of the hook and loop strip or dots. This is where the vital signs will be placed during the game.
9. Above the boxes for vital signs, apply two rows of “loop” strips or dots – interventions and results can be placed here during the simulation session. For storage, results and vital cards can be placed here.
10. On either side of the “patient”, apply columns of the “loop” side of the strips or dots. During the simulation, learners will place interventions and ordered tests here.
11. On the right section of the board, apply two columns of the “loop” side of the hook and loop (Velcro) strips or dots. Place the tests to order labels in these two columns – these are the diagnostic studies available to the players.

***How to Play***

1. Set up the board with all available interventions on the left section of the board and all available diagnostic studies on the right section of the board. Place the initial vitals in the box immediately above the patient.
2. Read the initial (triage) information to the learner and ask them to begin the case.
3. Instruct the learner to move the interventions they would like to perform and diagnostic tests they would like to order to the left of the patient in the center section of the game board.
4. Follow the case (available in Appendix F). Provide updated vitals by moving prior vitals up and moving new vitals immediately above the patient. Place test results to the right of the patient in the middle section of the board. Pause the case at any time to provide prompts or correct errors in the learner’s management of the patient.
5. At the conclusion of the case, ask the learner to “admit” the patient by presenting the patient to the inpatient team.
