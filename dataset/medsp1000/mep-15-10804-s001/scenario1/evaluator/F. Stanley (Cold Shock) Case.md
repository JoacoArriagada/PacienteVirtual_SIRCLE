**Ideal Scenario Flow**

-Initial: place patient on monitor, establish IV access, oxygen by nasal cannula, request additional providers (nurses, techs, attending, etc.) for assistance. Labs should also be drawn when IV access obtained. Learner should verbalize compensated shock.
Prompts: If asked for repeat vitals, ask if they would like patient to be placed on the monitor. If asked for bolus, reply that IV access hasn’t been obtained – should they obtain access? How many? If labs are not requested, ask if learner would like for blood to be drawn when access obtained. Ask which labs (blood glucose, venous blood gas (VBG), blood cultures (two culture sites preferred), metabolic panel, PT/INR/PTT, urinalysis, urine culture, chest xray). If shock not verbalized, ask what condition this patient has.

-First step: give initial fluid bolus (20 cc/kg, total 800cc). Bolus should be started as soon as IV access obtained.

Prompts: If bolus is not given and patient is on monitor, state that the monitor is alarming because patient’s heart rate is now >140 bpm. Bolus should be given push-pull. If this is not stated initially, ask “Is it ok to give the bolus the pump over 60 minutes?”. Antibiotics may be started at this point as well.

-Following completion of history and physical, state that the first bolus has finished.

Repeat evaluation: Vitals: 116/88, HR 122. RR 22. If placed on oxygen, oxygen saturation improved to 99%. Patient’s general appearance is unchanged. Capillary refill improved to 2-3 seconds. On repeat physical examination, patient’s lungs are clear to auscultation and there is no hepatosplenomegaly. If bedside VBG and blood glucose requested, provide these results at this point.

-At this point, second bolus should be started. If not, ask the learner if they would like to start another bolus. If antibiotics not started yet, ask if there are any other medications they would like to give. Provide results of ordered laboratory studies and chest xray. Give the learner several seconds to look at these results, then state (as the nurse): “The patient looks worse to me – could you come take a look at him?”. Vital signs are now BP 88/44, HR 138, RR 24, Sat 97%. Capillary refill worsened to 3-5 seconds and skin cool. On re-examination, lungs still clear.

-Third bolus should be initiated as push-pull. If asked for vasopressors at this point, reply “I can work on getting them from the med room”. If antibiotics still not given, remind learner that patient has a fever and the urinalysis results are concerning. Results of third bolus: Vitals HR 144, BP 84/42. RR now 26 and oxygen saturation decreased to 95%. On physical exam, there are now crackles on pulmonary auscultation. If asked, his inferior vena cava on ultrasound is plump and does not collapse with inspiration. His capillary refill remains 3-5 seconds with cool extremities. His mental status remains normal (unchanged).

-Initiate vasopressor support – consider including discussion of what is immediately available at your institution.

If additional fluid bolus given, administer half of the bolus, then state that patient’s respiratory rate is >30 and oxygen saturation is declining with worsening crackles on lung exam. Learner should recognize pulmonary edema and immediately place additional oxygen (by facemask). If the bolus is stopped immediately, the patient’s oxygen saturation improves with oxygen by facemask alone.

-Reassessment following initiation of vasopressors: HR 108, BP 100/60. RR 22, sat 97%.

-At this point, learner should admit the patient to the PICU. If the learner requests admission but does not specify need for ICU-level care, ask where the patient should go (ie: PICU, stepdown unit, or floor). If admission not requested, ask learner if they would like you to page the floor team for admission. If the admitting team is paged before initiation of vasopressor support, they will not call back until vasopressor support has been initiated.

-Case presentation by the learner. The learner should present the case as if presenting the patient to the PICU attending. If the learner omits any important information or critical steps in the resuscitation, ask for these details or clarification about what was done in the Emergency Department.

**Anticipated Management Mistakes**

1. Failure to obtain complete history: In this case, the patient will not volunteer recent dysuria due to embarrassment. Discuss importance of asking about all infectious symptoms in patients with sepsis.
2. Failure to recognize septic shock: The patient initially improves after fluids, but markedly elevated lactate indicates severe sepsis.
3. Failure to recognize volume overload: After third bolus, patient with evidence of pulmonary edema. Learners should remember to examine the patient after each bolus to assess for this.
4. Difficulty with presenting the case: Many residents struggle when presenting cases to consultants, especially early in training. Discuss how to present this relatively complex case in a systematic, organized manner.
