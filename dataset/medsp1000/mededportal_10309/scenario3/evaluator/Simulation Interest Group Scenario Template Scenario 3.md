**Simulation Interest Group Scenario Template**

1. Unstable Bradycardia
2. Target audience: anesthesiologists, surgeons, registered nurses or registered practical nurses (operating room teams)
3. Learning Objectives:

a) Primary

1. Recognise clinical features and potential causes of compromising bradycardia

2. Describe pharmacological options for treating bradycardia

3. Initiate transcutaneous pacing in a patient with persistent unstable bradycardia

4. Describe site-specific processes for summoning emergency help

5. Describe some key components of clinical teamwork and identify applications in their practice

b) Secondary

1. Review contents of emergency ‘crash’ cart

2. Identify methods of improvement of non- technical skills/behavioral skills

c) Critical actions checklist

1. Adherence to key process checklist

2. Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) score

3. Duration to critical actions (ie: time to begin cardiac compressions after cardiac arrest, time to administer epinephrine in case of cardiac arrest or anaphylaxis)

VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants-a patient chart, consisting of the usual documents as per individual institutional protocol will be available to the participants. 72 yr old man who underwent an inguinal hernia repair 2 hours ago. Stable intraoperative course. Is now in PACU with bradycardia and vital signs unstable. The `patient` will be available for a preoperative interview by perioperative team members, but not very coherent in the PACU during the episode, so won’t be able to get much history.

1. Chief Complaint: Very dizzy and doesn’t feel well.

2. Past Medical History: History of hypertension. He has had an inguinal hernia for years but has had increasing discomfort. He has a good exercise tolerance (METS>4) and review of systems is negative except for arthritis of knees and hips.

3. Medications and Allergies: Losartan 100 mg OD and multi vitamin. Penicillin allergy, (rash).

4. Family/social history: nil of significance

B. Scenario Conditions Initially

1. History patient gives: As above (chief complaint)

2. Patients initial exam: Vital Signs: BP 90/50, HR 48.

3. Patients physiology: 72 year old man with long-standing history of hypertension

C. Scenario Branch Points

1. Changes in patient’s condition: 72 yr old man who underwent an inguinal hernia repair 2 hours ago. Stable intraoperative course. Is now in PACU with bradycardia and vital signs unstable. Patient should just keep repeating “I’m so dizzy, I don’t feel well” If questioned, answer vaguely...I don’t feel well...if administered sedation, will become unconscious.

Will need to brief the attending RN about the patient in advance of scenario start

If RN doesn’t call a code, instructor to tell team there is a code in the PACU

Pt will not stabilize despite interventions and will lose consciousness if condition continues untreated after 3-5 mins or if administered sedation for initiation of transcutaneous pacer.

Allow time for team to get to the point of calling to organize transfer to another facility (if ambulatory facility)

If this doesn’t happen, end scenario at appropriate time.

VII. Instructor’s Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

C. Scenario Programming

1. Optimal Management Path: early recognition of alteration in vital signs; verbalization of problem, 100% oxygen, focused physical examination; hemodynamic support as required; verbalization of differential diagnosis; appropriate leadership and communication skills demonstrated.

2. Potential complications path(s): patient will become unconscious if sedated or if no supportive measures in 3-5 mins.

3. Potential errors path(s): lack of verbalization of problem to surgical team; failure to recognize unstable bradycardia will result in multiple errors and less likelihood of following the CEC
