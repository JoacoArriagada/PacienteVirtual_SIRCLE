C. Action role-the actors would only respond as dictated by the action of the team members managing the scenario. If a call to a specific person/role is made, the actor should respond according to the ‘normal’ response ie) if a second call anesthesiologist is summoned and he/she would normally be at home, then the team should be informed that the person has been called and will likely be ‘x’ amount of time until they are able to arrive. Similarly, if a person is called who would likely be in another area or another site, an appropriate amount of time should be allocated until this person would arrive. People familiar with the environment would be able to make these judgement calls and an overall ‘supervisor’ should oversee the action and responses of the actors. This ‘supervisor’ could also direct the person answering the telephone requests for help as to how to respond.

VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

A. Scenario Background Given to Participants-a patient chart, consisting of the usual documents as per individual institutional protocol will be available to the participants. The `patient` will be available for a preoperative interview by perioperative team members.

1. Chief Complaint:

a) 3 month history of right upper quadrant abdominal pain isolated U/S confirms multiple gallstones. (General surgery)

2. Past Medical History: previously healthy, systems review negative

3. Medications and Allergies: no medications, no allergies

4. Family/social history: nil of significance

B. Scenario Conditions Initially

1. History patient gives: As above (chief complaint)

2. Patients initial exam: Vital Signs are stable. BP 110/60, HR 68

3. Patients physiology: Healthy 35 year old woman

C. Scenario Branch Points

1. Changes in patient’s condition: Patient’s condition stable before the intravenous infusion of cephaloxin. One RN is taken to SDC and is familiarized with the patient

A few minutes after the RN begins, the patient will start to complain about generalized itching and will comment that she notices that her skin is red and that she has a rash. This will then progress to having some difficulty breathing. After a few minutes, the patient will become tachycardic, hypotensive and ultimately unconscious.

The patient will respond to appropriate treatment.

2. Responses to treatment: All changes in VS will improve with the correct treatment interventions. If incorrect or no interventions, VS should return to normal after 5-7 mins.

3. There are several directions the scenario can be taken: If incorrect, slow or no interventions, all VS should deteriorate but not to the point of cardiac arrest. All VS should return to normal at 5-7 mins despite treatment.

VII. Instructor’s Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A. Tips to keep scenario flowing: A manikin operator with sufficient clinical experience is desirable to ensure realistic responses to a wide range of possible actions of the participants. Attention to the time frame of events will ensure that the scenario flows at the appropriate rate. Returning vital signs to normal after 5-7 minutes is appropriate even if proper actions are not taken, and can be discussed in the debrief.

B. Tips to direct actors: Actors called in for help should appear at a point that would be ‘usual’ for the institution. Rather than ‘taking over’ management of the situation, actors should ask “What can I do to help” They should only perform tasks as requested by the team member/leader. Actors should be adequately familiar with the facility to ensure that a participant’s possible unfamiliarity will not pose a barrier to scenario progression.

C. Scenario Programming

2. Potential complications path(s): The patient receives an intravenous infusion of cephaloxin and develops an anaphylactic reaction. Becomes tachycardic, hypotensive and ultimately unconscious..

4. Program debugging: suggested to avoid pre-programmed manikin event for this scenario, but to alter manikin responses to participant’s actions in real time.
