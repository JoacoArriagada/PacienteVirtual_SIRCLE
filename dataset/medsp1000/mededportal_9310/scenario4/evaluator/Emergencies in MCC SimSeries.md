ITB pump malfunction was considered, particularly given patient’s recent spinal surgery. Presentation of symptoms 2 days post-operative is appropriate given that baclofen withdrawal usually presents 12-96 hours after last dose. **SCENARIO 4: Relevant Clinical and Management Information for Debriefing Session**

(For distribution to Participants during Debriefing Session)

TW is a 6 y.o. male with history of TBI due to MVA s/p VPS with severe neurologic impairment, oromotor dysfunction, seizure disorder, tracheostomy-dependence and severe spasticity s/p intrathecal baclofen pump placement, who was admitted for spinal instrumentation for scoliosis. You became involved in his care when he developed fever, severe agitation and increased seizure frequency in the post-operative period.

In this case, the primary etiology of hyperthermia and agitation was related to his intrathecal baclofen pump (ITBP) malfunction. The manufacturer of the ITB pump (Medtronic) estimates that more than 15,000 patients worldwide have an ITB pump in place, and this number is rapidly increasing. Intrathecal baclofen (ITB) is increasingly used for refractory spasticity due to enhanced effectiveness at less than 1% of the systemic dose.

The pathophysiology of baclofen withdrawal is similar to benzodiazepine withdrawal. Chronic baclofen use increases inhibitory tone on the central nervous system and spinal cord, and results in down-regulation of the inhibitory receptors; as a result, withdrawal of the inhibitory agent results in predominance of excitatory effects, leading to a hyperexcitable state.

Baclofen withdrawal may occur after a short period of treatment (days to weeks) and begins within 12-96 hours after the last dose. Early symptoms of withdrawal include itchiness and increased spasticity. Later symptoms of withdrawal include agitation, disorientation, diaphoresis, hallucinations, hyperthermia, tachycardia, hypertension, myoclonus, seizures. Fever up to 43 C (109.4 F), rhabdomyolysis, and disseminated intravascular coagulation have been reported, along with coma and death. Symptoms are difficult to interpret, and may resemble dysautonomia, sepsis, malignant hyperthermia, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, or other conditions associated with hypermetabolic state and rhabdomyolysis.

Once baclofen withdrawal has been recognized, replacement of baclofen at the GABA receptors is essential, and can be achieved with enteral baclofen. But, because baclofen withdrawal represents such an exaggerated excitatory state, enteral baclofen often proves to be insufficient by itself to alleviate symptoms. In addition, in those patients whose enteral intake is restricted, such as pre- or post-surgical state, intravenous medications are preferred. Benzodiazepines are often the next treatment option, as they activate the GABA receptor family as well. Other agents are also used in extreme cases including: cyproheptadine, dantrolene, clonidine, and propranolol.

As this case highlights, intrathecal baclofen withdrawal syndrome should always be considered a possible complication of spinal surgery for patients with ITB pumps. This is important, because there are increasing numbers of patients with neuromuscular scoliosis and ITB pumps who may require spinal surgery.

| For patients receiving intrathecal baclofen therapy and symptoms suggestive of baclofen withdrawal (fever, tachycardia, hypertension or hypotension, seizures, altered mental status, rebound spasticity or myoclonus, pump alarm sounding):   * Administer antipyretics and other cooling techniques for fever * Administer benzodiazepines for seizures and/or spasticity * Rule out medical causes:   Sepsis or infectious  Meningitis  Dysautonomia  Malignant hyperthermia  Neuroleptic malignant syndrome  Other withdrawal syndromes   * Contact the patient’s ITB pump specialist to interrogate the pump and check the reservoir * Abdominal radiographs (AP/lateral) to determine catheter integrity * If catheter integrity is disrupted, eventual surgical exploration and repair is warranted; contact neurosurgeon and treat patient with oral baclofen. * If catheter integrity is intact by plain films and baclofen withdrawal appears the most likely diagnosis, consider spiral CT for further evaluation of catheter integrity. * If the problem is unresolved, contact the manufacturer (Medtronic 1-800-328-0810). |
