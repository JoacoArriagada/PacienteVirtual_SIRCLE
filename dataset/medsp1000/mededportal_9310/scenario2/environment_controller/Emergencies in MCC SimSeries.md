**SCENARIO 2: THE MEDICALLY COMPLEX CHILD WITH ACUTE RESPIRATORY DISTRESS**

**Facilitator Scenario Summary**

**Age:** 2 years

**Weight:** 10 kg

**Environment:** inpatient general pediatrics ward

**Complete Scenario Progression (for facilitator):**

*\*Learner’s will not have all of the information listed below, but the facilitator should reveal the background information as the learners ask for it*

Background Information: 2 year old male with history of traumatic brain injury (TBI) due to a motor vehicle accident (MVA) at one year of age; s/p ventriculoperitoneal shunt (VPS) placement with global developmental delay and oromotor dysfunction, seizure disorder, tracheostomy-dependent with severe spasticity who was admitted for failure to thrive, ultimately requiring surgical gastrostomy tube (GT) placement. On HD#4, he was noted to have acute onset of respiratory distress that did not respond to suctioning, re-positioning or albuterol treatments. His last tracheostomy tube change was 3 weeks ago. He develops significant respiratory compromise with cyanosis and apnea due to an occlusion of his tracheostomy tube.

**Initial Information Provided to Learners:**

**Your sign-out:**

Patient TW

2 y.o. male HD#4 with history of TBI due to MVA s/p VPS with severe neurologic impairment, oromotor dysfunction, seizure disorder, tracheostomy-dependent with severe spasticity who was admitted for failure to thrive, POD#4 s/p GT placement, now working up on home enteral feeding regimen.

**Nurse calls during sign out:**

“He’s really agitated and his heart rate and breathing rate are up. I tried suctioning him and repositioning him, but he’s still the same. It’s been going on for the past several minutes. He is working really hard to breathe, can you please come to evaluate him?”

**After sign out you arrive at the bedside:**

You find TW lying in bed in moderate distress. Mom says he’s been like this for the past few minutes. He appears anxious with perioral cyanosis, nasal flaring and severe retractions.

**Resolution of the Clinical Scenario :**(read out loud to participants after the end of the simulation and prior to the debriefing portion)

***If participants change tracheostomy tube successfully:***

The patient’s respiratory distress resolved and he stabilized quickly. His tube feeds were re-started and he was discharged back to the subacute hospital without event on HD#7.

***If participants DO NOT change tracheostomy tube successfully:*** The patient went apneic. The charge nurse attempted tracheostomy tube change, but had difficulty replacing both the same size tracheostomy and the down-size tracheostomy. He underwent rapid sequence intubation and transfer to the PICU for further management and consultation with pediatric ENT.
