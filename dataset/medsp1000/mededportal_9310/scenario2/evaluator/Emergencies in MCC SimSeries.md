**SCENARIO 2: Relevant Clinical and Management Information for Debriefing Session**

(For distribution to Participants during Debriefing Session)

TW is a 2 y.o. male with history of TBI due to MVA s/p VPS with severe neurologic impairment, oromotor dysfunction, seizure disorder, tracheostomy-dependence and severe spasticity, who was admitted for failure to thrive. You became involved in his care due to acute onset of respiratory distress and perioral cyanosis.

In this case, the primary etiology of his acute respiratory distress was a tracheostomy tube occlusion. The most common cause of respiratory distress in children with tracheostomy tubes is mucous plugging. The obstruction of mucous can occur at the site of the stoma, inside the inner cannula or at the distal end of the outer cannula. The tracheostomy tube bypasses the upper airway that warms, cleans and moistens the air we breathe, leading to the production of more mucus. The tracheostomy tube should be routinely suctioned to remove mucus from the tube and trachea.

Some symptoms of mucous plugging include resistance when trying to suction, difficulty with bag ventilation and signs of respiratory distress. If the tracheostomy tube is obstructed, preoxygenate the patient with 100% oxygen and attempt to suction the tube. Do not be forceful to suction due to increased risk of causing bleeding and infection. If suctioning catheter cannot progress, consider repositioning or reinserting the cannula. Reinsertion can lead to complications including, bleeding, pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, creation of a false tract, or perforation of the tracheal wall. Granulomas and strictures can also form if reinsertion of the cannula is done incorrectly. If inserting the cannula is unsuccessful, change the tracheostomy tube.

The steps of changing a tracheostomy tube generally require two participants. Understanding the order of a proper tracheostomy tube change is vital to a successful change. The key steps include having the correct size and type of tracheostomy tubes (including smaller sizes), checking for defects of the new tracheostomy tube, placing the child under a towel/roll to how for hyperextension of the neck, using a smooth continuous motion with removing and reinserting a tracheostomy tube, and to evaluate for correct placement of the tracheostomy tube.

| --- |
| ***Management of Patient with Respiratory Distress due to Mucous Plugging of a Tracheostomy Tube*** |
| * Stabilize airway, breathing and circulation * Preoxygenate with 100% oxygen * Attempt to suction tracheostomy tube or reposition/reinsert inner cannula * Attempt bag ventilation * Change tracheostomy tube * If suspected, urgent consultation with ENT may be helpful |
