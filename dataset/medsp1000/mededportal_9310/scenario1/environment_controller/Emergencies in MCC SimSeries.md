**SCENARIO 1: THE MEDICALLY COMPLEX CHILD WITH ACUTE ABDOMEN**

**Facilitator Scenario Summary**

**Age:** 2 years

**Weight:** 10 kg

**Environment:** inpatient general pediatrics ward

**Complete Scenario Progression (for facilitator):**

*\*Participants will not have all of the information listed below, but the facilitator should reveal the background information as the learners ask for it*

Background Information: 2 year old male with history of traumatic brain injury (TBI) due to a motor vehicle accident (MVA) at one year of age; s/p ventriculoperitoneal shunt (VPS) placement with global developmental delay and oromotor dysfunction, seizure disorder, tracheostomy-dependent with severe spasticity who was admitted for failure to thrive, ultimately requiring surgical gastrostomy tube (GT) placement. On HD#4, his GT was dislodged at nursing change of shift, was replaced and since that time, he has been extremely fussy. He developed increasing abdominal distension and intolerance of enteral feeds. He proceeds to hypotensive septic shock because his GT replacement from the morning created a false tract and he has peritonitis.

**Initial Information Provided to Participants:**

**Your sign-out:**

Patient TW

2 y.o. male HD#4 with history of TBI due to MVA s/p VPS with severe neurologic impairment, oromotor dysfunction, seizure disorder, tracheostomy-dependent with severe spasticity who was admitted for failure to thrive, POD#4 s/p GT placement, now working up on home enteral feeding regimen.

**Nurse calls during sign out:**

“He’s really agitated and his heart rate and breathing rate are up. I tried suctioning him and repositioning him, but he’s still the same. It’s been going on for the past 30 minutes. I am worried about how he looks, can you please come to evaluate him?”

**After sign out you arrive at the bedside:**

You find TW crying despite attempts at soothing by mom. Mom says he’s been like this for the past 30-45 minutes, which is unusual. He has an episode of emesis as you walk into the room.

**Past Medical History:** Victim of MVA at 1 year of age (unrestrained passenger), resuscitated at the scene, initial hospital course complicated by severe TBI s/p craniotomy and VPS placement; during that hospitalization had tracheostomy placement due to failure to extubate; transferred to subacute care facility for continued management where found to have oromotor dysfunction with persistent nasogastric tube (NG) dependence. **Resolution of the Clinical Scenario:** (read out loud to participants after the end of the simulation and prior to the debriefing portion)

The patient was taken emergently for exploratory laparotomy by the pediatric surgery team after successful stabilization by the primary team of caregivers. In the OR, the surgeon found copious amounts of formula and purulent material throughout the peritoneal cavity and noted the false tract created by the GT. The patient’s abdomen was irrigated, GT revised and broad-spectrum antibiotics were continued upon transfer to the PICU.
