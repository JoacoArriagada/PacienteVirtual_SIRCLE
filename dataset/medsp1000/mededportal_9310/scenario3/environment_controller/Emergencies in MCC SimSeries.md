**SCENARIO 3: THE MEDICALLY COMPLEX CHILD WITH ALTERED MENTAL STATUS**

**Facilitator Scenario Summary**

**Age:** 9 year old

**Weight:** 25 kg

**Environment:** emergency department

**Complete Scenario Progression (for facilitator):**

*\*Participants will not have all of the information listed below, but the facilitator should reveal the background information as the learners ask for it*

**Initial Information Provided to Participants:**

**Sign-out:** Ryan Smith

Ryan is a 9 year old male with a history of traumatic brain injury (TBI) due to motor vehicle accident (MVA) 6 years ago, seizure disorder, gastrostomy tube (GT) dependent for medications and feeds, who presents to the ER with a three day history of nonbloody, nonbilious emesis. He has run low grade temperatures x 2 days. No diarrhea. His younger sister has had a "stomach bug" x 1 week (vomiting, diarrhea, fever). His mother is concerned he is in pain.

**Discussion with ER nurse prior to seeing patient:**

"His mother is concerned he might be in pain because of the vomiting. He doesn't appear

dehydrated. He is a bit sleepy. I just gave him Tylenol for his low grade fever."

**You examine the patient in the ER prior to admitting him:**

Ryan is lying on the stretcher and appears to be uncomfortable. He has just finished vomiting. He feels warm. He is responsive but has difficulty remaining awake during your exam.

**Background Information:** **(provide only when asked)**

**HPI**: 9 year old male presents to the emergency room at a community hospital with three day

history of non-bloody, non-bilious emesis. His mother states the vomiting is worst in the mornings. Low grade temperature present x 2 days. No diarrhea. No recent URI symptoms or infections. No rashes. Has younger sister with a "stomach bug" (vomiting, diarrhea, fever). Patient has had a 1 month history of decreased participation and interest in favorite school activities. Some increased sleepiness x 2 days. His mother is concerned that he may be in pain.

**Past Medical History**: **(provide only when asked)**

• Full term gestation

• TBI s/p MVA (unrestrained passenger) at age 3, resuscitated at the scene

• Initial hospital course complicated by severe TBI requiring craniotomy and VPS placement

• Initially required tracheostomy but has subsequently been decannulated

• GT placed for severe oromotor dysfunction - all feeds and medications are given via G

tube

• VPS revised 4 months ago for obstruction and associated hydrocephalus; upon direct

questioning, his mother states that current symptoms are similar to those 4 months ago

when his shunt was obstructed

• Seizure disorder (post MVA)

• Baclofen pump placed at 7 years of age for spasticity

**Allergies:** NKDA

**Resolution of the Clinical Scenario:** (read out loud to participants after the end of the simulation and prior to the debriefing portion)

The patient was taken emergently to the OR at nearest children’s hospital, where pediatric neurosurgery externalized his VPS and relieved pressure. Cultures from the fluid were positive for *Staphylococcus epidermidis*. He was started on an extended course of antibiotics. Once cleared by Infectious Disease and Neurosurgery, his shunt was re-internalized and he was sent home. He slowly returned back to neurologic baseline.
