**Patient Presentation Examples Using FREE TIPSS Rounding Tool**

<u>**Established Patients**</u>

**Use the acronym: 	FREE TIPSS**

**F=Family and team introductions**—presenter introduces attending, senior resident and on-call intern (if present).  If the team is small enough, all members may be introduced.  With large teams, the family will be overwhelmed with too many introductions.  If a rounding face sheet is available, this is given to the family.

**R=Recap**—presenter gives a one line introductory statement about the patient including the reason the patient was admitted

**E=Events overnight**—any pertinent events overnight or since previous rounds

**E=Exam pertinents:** pertinent exam, including vitals

**(Rx=Medications, if requested)**

**T-Tests:** any new labs or xrays

**I-Impression:** summary statement including assessment of how the patient is doing

**P=Problem-based plan:** state problem and then plan for the day

**S=Staff input/nurSe input:**  Ask if the nurse, RT or other staff have anything to add

**S=Summary for family:**  summarize the plan for the family; ask if there are questions

**Total time=<5 minutes for presentation of simple patient, 5-10 min for complex pts.**

**Example:**

**Family and team introduction:** Intern: “Good morning Mrs. Jones.  This is the general pediatric team.   This is Dr. Anderson, our attending, Dr. Johnson, our senior resident, and the rest of the team.  We are going to discuss how your son is doing, and then we’ll summarize the plan for the day.  Please chime in if you have any comments or questions.”

**Recap**: “Jimmy is a 3-year-old male who was admitted two days ago for vomiting, diarrhea and dehydration”

**Events:** “Jimmy had one loose stool overnight, no emesis, and tolerated oral intake well.”

**Exam:** “Jimmy was afebrile overnight. All other vital signs were stable, including his heart rate, which was within normal limits.  Weight wasn’t back this morning.  Mucus membranes were moist, abdomen was soft and nontender.”

**Tests:** “A repeat BMP this morning was entirely normal.”

**Impression:**  “In summary, Jimmy is a 3-year-old male admitted two days ago with gastroenteritis who is recovering nicely.”

**Problem-based plan:**

“Problem #1: Gastroenteritis.  He has had no further episodes of emesis since yesterday.  Diarrhea is still present, but improving.  Today, I think we should saline lock his IV, and encourage him to feed by mouth. If he can take adequate amounts of fluid by noon, then I think we can discharge him to home.

**“** Problem #2:  Hypokalemia.  This was likely secondary to vomiting and dehydration, and has now resolved.  We don’t have to check another basic metabolic panel.

“Disposition:  I think we can probably discharge him later today.”

**Staff input:** “Alice, anything from a nursing perspective?”

**Summary for family:**  “Jimmy is doing well.  I think if he can drink this morning and hold it down, then we could send you home in the early afternoon.  How do you feel like things are going? Do you have any questions for us?”

Good bye.
