**Title:** Teaching and Evaluating Oral Presentations on Family-Centered Rounds using the FREE TIPSS tool

**Contents:**

Facilitator’s Guide

FREE TIPSS Rounding card- 2 sides

Scorecards for evaluating presentations on FCR

Patient presentation examples

**Purpose:** Presentations on family-centered rounds (FCR) provide a critical forum for information exchange, decision-making and teaching at the bedside. However, great variation exists in how this is done. This variation can lead to inefficient oral presentations on rounds, and frustration amongst learners, attending physicians and families. The purpose of this module is to provide a tool for clinical teachers responsible for educating learners on inpatient hospital units to give more concise, accurate patient presentations on FCR.

**Learning Objectives for Teachers:**

1. Effectively utilize a tool for teaching patient presentations on FCR
2. Effectively utilize a tool for assessing learner performance on FCR

**Context in which this tool can be used:** This tool is given to all pediatric interns (16 per year) and third year medical students (130 per year) for use during their general inpatient pediatric rotations. This tool is also used individually with medical students and interns who struggle with oral presentations. This tool can be used to address and assess many of the ACGME competencies including: Interpersonal and Communication Skills, Medical Knowledge, and Patient Care.

**Lessons learned in the process of developing this module:** While this module focuses on providing a standardized tool for oral presentations on rounds, it is important to allow some flexibility so that it may be adapted to the different needs of different rounding teams. For example, some attending physicians wanted to hear a medication list on their patients. In response, we added a section for medications in parentheses. Obviously, different institutions, services and attending physicians may have slightly different preferences for what information is reported, and we give full permission to users to adjust the card to suit these preferences. As such, this tool provides a structure for presentations, but should be seen as flexible to the nuances of different situations. This tool also presumes that learners have appropriately pre-rounded on their patients, during which time they can assess how the family feels things are going and whether or not they have questions to be addressed during rounds. In terms of the scorecards, the basis for the point cutoffs for medical students, interns and residents relates to the relative expertise each learner possesses at each stage of training. For example, a third year medical student would not be expected to present as detailed or thorough an assessment or plan as an intern or resident, and therefore, would not likely get full points in those categories. Additionally, early in training, most trainees have difficulty determining the correct amount of information to report, and therefore, would be expected to lose points in those categories due to over or under-reporting.

**Instructor qualification and responsibilities:** Any supervising physician

**Facilitator’s Guide:**

1. Have the learners review the FREE TIPSS rounding card and patient presentation example prior to rounds on the first day of the rotation
2. Review the FREE TIPSS rounding card with learners on the first day of the rotation
3. Answer questions learners may have, and clarify specifics to the attending physician’s own style
4. Use scorecards to assess learner performance on FCR, and provide feedback (optional)

**Other resources:** none

**Time: 20 minutes**, including learner preparation prior to the first day of the rotation, as well as review of the tool on the first day of the rotation
