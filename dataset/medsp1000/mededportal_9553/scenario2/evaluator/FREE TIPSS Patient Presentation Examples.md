**Patient Presentation Examples Using FREE TIPSS Rounding Tool**

<u>**New Patients**</u>

**Same acronym:  FREEE TIPSS, except with an extra E for extra info, like pmhx, fam hx, surgical hx, home meds that wasn’t mentioned in the Recap.**

**F=Family and team introduction**

**R=Recap**—mention presenting complaint, pertinent HPI details including events leading to admission and pertinent PMHx

**E=Events—** events since admit

**E=Extra information-** Other pmhx, fam hx or surgical hx not previously mentioned

**E=Exam**

**T=Tests**-no need to mention again if stated in the Recap

**I=Impression**

**P=Problem-based Plan**

**S=Staff  input**

**S=Summary for parents**

**Total Time=10-15 minutes**

**Example:**

**Family and Team Introductions:**  “Hello, Mrs. Anderson, we are the general pediatrics team.  This is Dr. Jones, our attending, Dr. Johnson, our senior resident, and the rest of our team.  We are going to discuss Martha’s case and then we’ll summarize for you at the end.  If you have any questions, or would like to add anything, please chime in.

**Recap:** “Martha is a 3-month-old female who was admitted last night from the ER with presumed pyelonephritis.  She was previously well until 2 days ago when she developed fevers up to 102 at home, and complained of back pain.  She was taken to her pediatrician yesterday, was diagnosed with a viral infection, and sent home.  Unfortunately, her fevers continued, and her appetite dropped off.  Then last night she had 2 episodes of nonbloody, nonbilious emesis, and she was taken to the ED.  In the ED, a UA showed many WBCs, positive leukocyte esterase and positive nitrites.  Urine and blood culture are pending.  A CBC showed a WBC of 16K with a left shift.  BMP was normal.  CRP and ESR were not done.  She was given a dose of ceftriaxone and admitted to the floor

**Events:**  “Overnight, she slept well and had no further episodes of emesis.

**Extra information:**  “She has no history of pyelonephritis, has never been hospitalized or had surgery.  Family history is also negative.

**Exam:**  “She was febrile overnight, with a Tmax of 39, otherwise HR, BP and other vital signs were within normal limits.  On exam, she was alert, nontoxic, but in some distress.  She did have some abdominal and flank tenderness.  Otherwise, a normal exam.

**Tests:**  “No additional tests were done other than those already mentioned.  Blood and Urine cultures are no growth this morning.

**Impression:**  “In summary, this is a 3-month-old female with pyelonephritis who is clinically improving.

**Problem-based plan:**

“Problem #1: Pyelonephritis.  She is on ceftriaxone, which should cover the most likely organism, e. coli.  We will continue this, and await results from the blood and urine cultures.  We will order a renal ultrasound today, but hold off on VCUG.

“Problem #2:  Vomiting.  She did not appear dehydrated on exam this morning.  We will continue IV fluids at maintenance until she is able to take adequate amounts of fluid orally, then saline lock her IV.

“Problem #3: Disposition.  If she is able to eat and drink today, and has improvement in her fever curve, we could discharge her as early as tonight, or more likely, tomorrow, when her urine culture results may be back.

**Staff/nurse involvement:**  Janet (the nurse), is there anything from a nursing perspective that you’d like to add?

**Summary for parents:**  Mrs. Anderson, Martha has an infection in her kidneys… How do you feel Martha is doing?  Do you have any questions?

Goodbye.
