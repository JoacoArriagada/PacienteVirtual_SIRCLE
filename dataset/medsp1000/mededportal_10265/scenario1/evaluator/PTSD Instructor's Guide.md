The Development of a PTSD Standardized Patient Encounter

**Implementation of the PTSD SP Encounter**

A simulated hospital suite was modified to mimic an emergency room. The clinical group of 8 students and 1 instructor arrived at the center 30 minutes prior to the start of the simulation. The instructor served as the facilitator and content expert. Students were oriented to the simulation suite, received a student guide and signed consent to be video recorded. Then the instructor assigned student roles. Two students for each simulation were either the primary nurse or the secondary nurse and the other 6 were observers. The encounter was digitally recorded for review in debrief. Each SP simulation was allowed to run a maximum of 20 minutes with an announcement of "5 minutes are remaining" to cue the students to close the encounter. Faculty or students could end the scenario before the full 20 minutes if the learning objectives were successfully achieved. At the end of the scenario students and instructor proceeded to the debrief room while the SP had a maximum of 15 minutes to come out of character, review the guidelines for debrief, and collect his relevant thoughts for verbal feedback. Meanwhile, the instructor began to facilitate the debrief session with all 8 students based on the instructor's debrief guide. When ready the SP joined the nursing students and faculty in debrief and engaged in guided reflection on the encounter by giving positive and meaningful feedback to the students. At the end of the debrief session nursing students completed a web-based evaluation of the SP encounter. Results were overwhelmingly positive with the majority of students (n=40) agreeing or strongly agreeing the SP encounter was an effective learning experience.

**The student will be able to:**

**Methods used for training**

The SPs participated in two 4-hour rehearsals covering the PTSD case with a read-through, clarification of details, introduction to the simulation space and technology, role play, review and discussion of an example video, how to give verbal feedback in a faculty led group debrief setting, and faculty feedback on SP character development.

The SP for the PTSD case is not required to evaluate performance; however, the SP was trained to address the learning objectives in feedback to the learners. The SP reviewed the learning objectives for both the course and scenario. A set of trigger questions based on the learning objectives (Table 3), were developed to help the SPs identify relevant feedback comments. The SP also rehearsed techniques for transitioning out of the psychiatric character and into their professional SP persona, prior to entering the debrief room. The SP was trained to follow the instructor's lead for when to speak, to lead with positive comments, followed by constructive comments, and end with a positive comment.

Table 3 Debrief Guidelines for PTSD Case

**Guidelines for Debrief**

**PTSD Case**

**"The feedback I give you is from the perspective of the character I just played"**

Standardized patient:

* How did the learners make you feel? Did they diffuse your impending attack? If not, what specific actions or mannerisms made you uncomfortable?
* Did the learners address your emotions sensitively?
* Did you feel judged or stigmatized by having your behavior and psychology assessed? If so, what specific words or actions made you feel this way?
* Did you feel safe during the encounter? If not, what specific actions made you feel at risk of harm? Were there specific things the learners did that made you feel safer?
* Did you feel the learners understood your perspective and preferences? Did you feel your views were respected and incorporated into the plan of care?
* Did you feel cared for and nurtured by the learners? What specific actions led to this feeling?
* Did you feel you could trust the learners? Why?
