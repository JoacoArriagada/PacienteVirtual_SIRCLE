TRAINING MATERIALS

CASE SUMMARY

You are a 30-year-old war veteran who spent 6 years in the Army. You have been married for seven years and have a three-year-old son who was born during one of your deployments. You served two tours in Afghanistan before being discharged in 2011. Toward the end of your tour you began having severe insomnia with vivid nightmares. You would wake up sweating, fearful, and would do sit-ups with other exercises as a coping strategy. This would relieve some of your anxieties. However on your return home these symptoms worsened, and you began to experience flashbacks of the war during the daytime. The exacerbations of these symptoms affected your marriage. Due to encouragement and support from your wife you sought treatment. You were subsequently hospitalized for seven days and diagnosed with PTSD. Your discharge medications included Paxil for depression, Xanax for anxiety, and Ambien for insomnia. You were also given a referral for outpatient counseling, but you didn't follow through with outpatient counseling. Your wife brought you to the emergency department today due to recurrence of increased insomnia and frequent flashbacks.

Presenting complaint: You are in the ER today due to having daily flashbacks of the war with visions of active combat and difficulty sleeping.

Your challenge, as the standardized patient, is threefold:

1. To appropriately and accurately reveal the facts of the patient's case;
2. To observe the examinee's behavior while you are performing this case; and
3. To give appropriate and constructive verbal feedback to the student during the debrief that will immediately follow the encounter.

PRESENTATION/EMOTIONAL TONE

When the student enters the room, you should be sitting up on a gurney wearing your regular clothes. You are looking straight ahead with a blank stare and sweating.

In general you are anxious. You continually look around, checking the environment out of fear as if you are anticipating something bad happening to you. You have difficulty focusing on the present. In nursing terms you are hypervigilant. This means you are always tense and display an increased awareness of your surroundings (looking around; will only sit in positions where all exits are visible). You are constantly "on guard" to identify and respond to any perceived threat in your surrounding environment, so the sound of a siren may trigger behaviors of seeking shelter in response to flashbacks of a bomb threat. You answer all questions incorporating these behaviors.

OPENING LINE

In response to the typical opening question from the student, "What brings you in here today?" you respond (as close to verbatim as possible):

"I can't take this anymore; I need help before I take matters into my own hands."

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS/CONDITION

Two days ago you began having worsening insomnia, vivid nightmares, and flashbacks during the day. Last night you woke up sweating and fearful and screaming. You agreed to let your wife drive you to the ER today.

<u>Your chief concern / patient perspective of illness</u>

If the student asks, "What concerns you most about this?" (or something of that nature), you reply that "I can't take this anymore; I need help before I off myself."

If the student asks, "What effect does this have on your daily life?" (or something of that nature), you reply that: "I feel like I'm unable to function in society."

- *Medical history: Hearing loss from combat explosions*
- *Past psychiatric history : You were hospitalized for PTSD for one week, 6 months ago*

MEDICATIONS

**Prescriptions:** Paxil 30mg daily for depression, Xanax 2mg three times daily as needed for anxiety, and Ambien 10mg nightly for insomnia.

No family history of psychiatric problems or other health-related problems.

You are in a seven-year marriage with a three-year-old son. You live in a single family home. You are receiving veterans' benefits for financial support.

LIFESTYLE/HABITS

<u>*Occupation*</u>: You had no career prior to joining the military straight from community college.

<u>*Alcohol:*</u> Increased alcohol intake from a couple of beers on weekends to two six packs.

<u>*Tobacco:*</u> Two packs daily for past 6 months, increased from 1 pack daily.

<u>*Diet*</u>: This week went from three full meals daily to skipping breakfast.

<u>*Caffeine:*</u> Two Red Bulls daily and two cups of coffee daily.

<u>*Exercise*</u>: You go jogging 5 out of seven days in the wee hours when you wake up with nightmares.

<u>*Activities/hobbies*</u>: Unmotivated.

<u>*Sexual History:*</u> Decreased libido (from 2 times weekly to once monthly)

<u>*Sleep*</u>: 3-4 hours every night. Toward the end of your tour you began having severe insomnia with vivid nightmares.

<u>*Spirituality / Religion*</u>: None

EMOTIONAL PROFILE (state of mind)

You are depressed and have occasional suicidal thoughts.

You are anxious, hypervigilant, and tense with poor focus. During the interview you will hear an emergency vehicle siren. You immediately respond with hypervigilant behaviors; stand up; facial expression is filled with fear, looking for shelter with hands over ears. Another video demonstrating the effects of PTSD could be substituted successfully.

After the student reassures the patient, he will agree to continue the assessment interview.

GENERAL RULE OF THUMB: If the examinee asks a question that has not been addressed in these training materials, your answer should be "no," meaning that you have not had that symptom or problem. The examinee might ask you personal or social history questions that have not been addressed in these materials. In this case you answer appropriately for the character you are playing.

DEALING WITH OPEN-ENDED QUESTIONS FROM THE EXAMINEE

If the examinee asks an open-ended question about any other concerns you have, such as, "Besides your insomnia and flashbacks are there other things that you are concerned about?" you should say:

"I'm frustrated and scared that this will never get better, and I feel a sense of doom."
