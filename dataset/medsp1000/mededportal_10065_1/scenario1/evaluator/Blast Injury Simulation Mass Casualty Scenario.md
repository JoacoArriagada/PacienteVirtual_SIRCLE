Multiple Casualty Scenario from Bomb/Blast Injury

# **Title: A Bomb Went off at the Bus Station**

# **II. Target Audience**: EM residents

## Learning Objectives or Assessment Objectives

1. Primary
2. Manage and prioritize care for multiple casualties from a bomb / blast incident concomitantly
3. Organize team and individual roles in order to care for all casualties effectively
4. Prioritize which patients need to go to the operating room expediently
5. Be aware of unique safety issues following bombing/terrorist events
6. Secondary
7. Manage patients in hemorrhagic shock; volume resuscitation with crystalloid and Packed RBCs (PRBC)
8. Manage amputations - demonstrate correct technique when applying tourniquet
9. Manage exsanguinating junctional wounds – demonstrate correct technique when packing a junctional wound to control hemorrhage
10. Manage pneumothoraces
11. Manage severe traumatic brain injury patients requiring intubation
12. Manage pregnant patients with severe blunt abdominal trauma
13. Manage children with blunt trauma
14. Manage patients with penetrating neck wounds and airway compromise
15. Effective and expedient use of ultrasound (US) in trauma
16. Scenario Summary

The scenario begins with participants caring for a severe asthmatic patient when a Mass Casualty Incident (MCI) occurs nearby and patients start arriving at the hospital. There are a total of 11 trauma patients from this MCI who arrive over approximately a 15 minute time period. Since only 4 learners are present at the start of the scenario, when multiple casualties begin arriving quickly they need to call for help, transfer the asthma patient to the PICU for ongoing care, and activate the hospital disaster plan to obtain additional hospital resources and assistance. All casualties have significant traumatic injuries involving major airway, breathing, and circulation issues including massive hemorrhage. Three of the patients need to be prioritized for operative intervention, and the remainder of the patients need to be stabilized, treated, and dispositioned from the ED. All patients except for the 13 year old minor will require hospital admission for ongoing management of traumatic injuries. The scenario ends when all patients are dispositioned appropriately, it is ensured the minor is not allowed to leave prior to locating his/her parents, and the unattended backpack in the room is addressed.

1. Critical actions checklist

Overall for entire scenario management

1. Identify a team leader to coordinate all resuscitation efforts and consultants
2. Team leader delegate at least one physician to be in charge of each patient
3. Team leader communicate with each physician leader caring for individual patients to ascertain urgency of operative intervention / diagnostic needs
4. Prioritize Sim 4, Sim 6, Sim 7 as first 3 patients for operative intervention with the others that are more stable to follow
5. Activate the hospital disaster plan (and/or other appropriate actions) to gain more resources for patient care
6. Identify unclaimed backpack in the room as potential bomb

Sim 1 (severe asthma exacerbation): None (Distractor to start the case, only objective is to admit the child to make room in the ED for the multiple casualties that are arriving and assure that child’s respiratory distress is managed appropriately despite the ensuing chaos)

Sim 2 (amputation, junctional wound, tension pneumothorax (PTX), hemorrhagic shock)

1. Place effective tourniquet
2. Perform effective junctional wound packing
3. Perform needle decompression for tension PTX
4. Place chest tube
5. Volume resuscitate with crystalloid AND PRBCs
6. Pain management
7. Stabilize patient in ED with above interventions and does **not** prioritize this patient for immediate operative intervention

Sim 3 (amputation, penetrating open sucking chest wound)

1. Place effective tourniquet
2. Cover open sucking chest wound (with 3 sided dressing)
3. Place chest tube
4. Pain management
5. Does **not** prioritize this patient for immediate operative intervention

Sim 4 (amputation, penetrating abdominal wound)

1. Place effective tourniquet
2. Perform FAST US exam
3. Volume resuscitate with crystalloid AND PRBCs
4. Does not remove the large piece of stabilized shrapnel
5. Pain management
6. Prioritize this patient for operative intervention

Sim 5 (open head injury)

1. Intubate patient with GCS 3
2. Place intraosseus (IO) for difficult IV access
3. Order hypertonic saline or mannitol
4. Obtain head CT
5. Consult Neurosurgery

Sim 6 (26 wks pregnant, penetrating abdominal injury, head injury, eye injury)

1. Perform FAST US exam
2. Volume resuscitate with crystalloid AND PRBCs
3. Assess fetal heart rate with US
4. Determine need for stat C-section
5. Discuss with consultants head CT vs. ventriculostomy placement in OR (no right answer, but should be able to justify their decision-making as time in CT may risk fetal demise and if CNS mass lesion missed may risk herniation)
6. Consult ophthalmology for penetrating eye injury
7. Pain control
8. Prioritize this patient for operative intervention with OB and surgery

Sim 7 (pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade, GCS 6)

1. Needle decompression for tension PTX
2. Place chest tube
3. Perform pericardiocentesis
4. Perform intubation for GCS 6
5. Prioritize this patient for operative intervention

LV 1 (13 y/o, open forearm fracture with arterial bleeding)

1. Control arterial bleeding with manual pressure on artery / packing or tourniquet placement
2. Assign someone to look after the patient, as she is a minor

LV 2 (penetrating abdominal injury)

1. Perform FAST US exam
2. Volume resuscitate with crystalloid AND PRBCs
3. Pain management
4. Stabilize the patient; does **not** prioritize patient for immediate operative intervention

LV 3 (penetrating neck injury)

1. Perform cricothyrotomy (on task trainer)

LV 4 (radial artery injury, penetrating flank injury with + FAST)

1. Control arterial bleeding with manual pressure on artery / packing or tourniquet placement
2. Perform FAST US exam
3. Volume resuscitate with crystalloid AND PRBCs
4. Pain management
5. Stabilize the patient; does **not** prioritize patient for immediate operative intervention

LV 5 (partial amputation leg)

1. Place effective tourniquet
2. Pain management
3. Volume resuscitate with crystalloid AND PRBCs

## Actors

*Number of people required for each role in parentheses.*

* Sim Director (1)
  + Played by physician directing entire scenario
  + Located in Sim Booth with other Sim Operators. Able to communicate face to face with Sim Operator’s to field questions and direct traffic
  + Radio communication with the Staging Manager and EMS (Channel 1)
  + Responsible for overall flow of scenario and any changes to the pace/order of victim and student entry
* Sim Operators (4+)
  + Played by anyone familiar with running mannequins
  + Need one Sim Operator to control each Sim mannequin and control VS / monitors
  + Should be familiar with their patient(s) / victim(s) as well as the critical actions which will determine the trajectory of the case
  + One Sim Operator in Radio Communication with each RN on the floor (Channels 2-5)
* Content Experts (2+)
  + Played by faculty physicians
  + Primary role was advising Sim Operators on physiologic responses to treatment AND observing actions in the case to assist with the debriefing
* RN (4)
  + Preferably played by ED nurse, alternatively a well trained actor can play this role
  + Located on the floor, taking direction from the providers managing the case
  + Radio communication with a Sim Operator via ear-piece to get updates or information during the scenario
  + Help providers locate equipment and carry out desired tasks
  + Portrayed competence of nurse is inversely proportional to learner’s demonstrated competence (i.e. if learners are doing well, nurse is less helpful, if learners are struggling, nurse is more helpful)
* EMS (1-2)
  + Preferably played by EMS personnel, alternatively a well trained actor can play this role
  + Bringing in new patients under direction of Staging Manager and Stage Director
  + Using standard equipment (e.g. backboards, c-collars, etc.)
  + Radio communication on same channel as Staging Manager and Stage Director
* Parent (1)
  + Can be played by any actor
  + Accompanies Sim youth with asthma
  + Radio communication with Sim Operator assigned to the Sim youth or associated RN
* Staging Manager (1)
  + Must be played by someone very familiar with all logistics of the case and with good organizational skills
  + Located just outside the simulation area
  + Preparing the next patients to be ready for entry at time of request by Stage Director and letting in next group(s) of students and the staff surgeon (in radio communications with Stage Director)
  + Organizing and managing EMS, LV and Sim/HF patients
* Staff Surgeon (1)
  + Should be played by a senior level resident, attending, surgeon, or experienced Sim Actor
  + Radio Communications with Sim Director/EMS/Staging Manager
  + Collaborator in decision making regarding OR cases
  + May be more or less helpful depending on flow of scenario and competence of learners. Portrayed competence of Sim Actor is inversely proportional to learner’s demonstrated competence (i.e. if learners are doing well, surgeon is less helpful, if learners are struggling, surgeon is more helpful)
* Live Victims (5)
  + Can be played by any well trained actor, residents, other students
  + Roles as described in grid
* Additional Sim Actor (0-1)
  + Can be played by just about anyone – Stage Manager can also do this but best if the Sim Actor has no other role and isn’t recognized as an attending physician or other known healthcare provider
  + Places a backpack or bag in the room - clearly visible (but not in center of room or anywhere overtly obvious) and then quickly leaves
  + Will likely be missed and will add an excellent discussion point regarding secondary attacks during the debrief
  + If participants recognize the backpack/bag as potential secondary device / ask about it one of the RNs will claim it to prevent this from becoming a big distraction during the scenario

## Pilot Testing and Revisions

1. Numbers of participants: 10 participants.
2. Performance expectations : see above under critical actions
3. Anticipated management mistakes
4. Not electing one person to be in charge of the entire team
5. Not prioritizing appropriate patients for the OR
6. Attempting to do all typical ED cares instead of switching to disaster priorities (hemorrhage control, airway, evaluation of respiratory and hemodynamic compromise)
7. Not collaborating with the surgeon when making OR decisions
8. Poor technique of tourniquet application
9. Poor technique of junctional hemorrhage control
10. Not recognizing unattended backpack
11. Not assigning someone to LV1, resulting in the minor “wandering off”
12. Evaluation form for participants – The critical actions checklist can be used as an evaluation form. Our priority in this scenario is emphasizing the overall scene management over the individual patient management.
