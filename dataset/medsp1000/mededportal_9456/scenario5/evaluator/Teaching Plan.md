# Scripted Role Play 4

Student Role playing the Adolescent

You are John Carlson, an 18-year-old male adolescent presenting for your annual physical. You have dropped out of Colorado HS and are now working at McDonalds. You failed as a sophomore and did not go to summer school. You would like to become an auto mechanic. You live with your mother and younger brother and sister. If asked, you may share that you have never met your father and this bothers you a lot. You don’t smoke cigarettes and have tried them. You do smoke Marijuana and if asked specifics do about 3 bowls a day. You have got into a fight and are on probation for possession of marijuana. You are attracted to boys and girls and have had about 15 sexual partners. Your last sexual activity was yesterday morning. You do not have a girlfriend currently. You have tested positive for Chlamydia in the past and took some medicines for it. You do not use condoms since you don’t like how they feel. You have had both receptive and penetrative anal sex. When you had gone to visit your uncle during the summer of your $7 ^ { \mathrm { t h } }$ grade, your uncle touched you in your genital area 3 consecutive nights and forced you to have oral sex with him. You have not shared this information with anyone. Sometimes you do not like how you feel and you continue to get into fights at home and at work. You have not had suicidal or homicidal thoughts. You have irregular eating habits and eat your meals either at the McDonalds or at other fast food places.

13. Facilitator describes the situation to the student playing the adolescent role to clarify the focus of the scenario. Facilitator allows this student to clarify the role.

14. Facilitator assists the student to remember their goal for this particular scene. It is helpful to ask the student to repeat the goal and verbalize understanding. (e.g., I want to take a comprehensive sexual history for this teen. Here the facilitator will ask the student to identify what they think makes a comprehensive sexual history and ask students to list them, e.g., sexual attraction, number of partners, type of sex (oral, vaginal and anal), consensual sex, sexual abuse, history of sexually transmitted infections, date and time of last sexual activity). The facilitator should illustrate the clinical significance of each of these questions. E.g., $\ " \mathrm { I }$ will get a complete substance abuse history in this adolescent who has depression.”

15. Facilitator also reminds both the student playing the physician and the student playing the adolescent that they can call a timeout during the scenario to ask for help or clarification. At this time also ask the student playing the physician how much time they think they would need to achieve their goal.

16. Facilitator selects specific students from the group to observe how the student is achieving their goal. Facilitator also asks students to verbalize the criteria they will use to make the assessment that the goal is being met. This should be done for each scenario/role play so that as many students as possible are actively engaged during the session. Each student in the group should have a specific task for observation during the role play, ideally related to their own learning goals. These students should be ready to provide feedback after the role play.

17. Once roles are clarified, the facilitator asks the students playing the physician and the adolescent to start and sits next to the student playing the physician.

18. Facilitator keeps notes/observes students’ interactions with the student playing the adolescent.

19. After completion of the encounter, either when the student completes the tasks or runs into a tough spot or time is complete, the facilitator asks the student who played the physician for his/her selfreflection of the interaction. Did they think they achieved their goal? What went well? Where did they find the most struggle and why? What do they think they could have done better?

20. Facilitator asks the student who played the adolescent how this encounter went. What goals did the student achieve and what could they have done better?

21. Facilitator then asks other students how this encounter went. What goals did the student achieve and what could they have done better?

22. Facilitator then gives feedback to the student incorporating feedback from peers if those goals were achieved. If the facilitator feels student feedback was not accurate the facilitator explains his or her own perspective (E.g., “[This student’s] goal was to take a comprehensive sexual history, but there were missing elements like $\mathsf { x } ,$ y, z so it was not truly comprehensive.”)

23. Facilitator asks the larger group of students whether observing and providing feedback helped any of them make progress on their goals, and if so, how. (Index cards with learning goals are collected at the end of the session and reviewed by the teacher afterwards, as described in the Instructor’s Guide.)
