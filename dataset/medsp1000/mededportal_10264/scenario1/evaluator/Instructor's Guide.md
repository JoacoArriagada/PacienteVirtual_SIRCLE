**Instructor Guide for Button Battery Misadventures**

**Resource Files and Use of Resource Files**

The case submission contains six elements to help run the simulation.

1. Case Narrative - includes all pertinent case information, interpretations of the case information, and anticipated case branch points.
2. Supplemental Case Material - a PowerPoint that contains pertinent case lab information and x-ray to be used during the case simulation.
3. Simulation Evaluation Form – contains recommended care actions.
4. Schematic – shows path to pass/fail
5. Didactic Case Debrief -a PowerPoint that contains teaching points for the debriefing session
6. Participant Evaluation Form – a form used to gather feedback on the case

**Purpose and Education Objectives**

Acute button battery ingestion is an uncommon but life threatening ingestion. The purpose of this simulation is to present a button battery lodged in the esophagus case that allows residents and fellows to test their knowledge base and critically evaluate the management of this type of ingestion.

Primary learning objectives include

1. Recognize potential causes of a fussy toddler
2. Recognize symptoms of potential esophageal or tracheal foreign body
3. Order X-ray to evaluate for foreign body
4. Identify esophageal (vs. tracheal) foreign body on x-ray

Secondary learning objectives include

1. Identify the difference between coin and button battery on x-ray
2. Discuss mechanisms of injury of button battery ingestion
3. Recognize need for emergent endoscopic removal of impacted esophageal button batteries

**Conceptual Background**

Acute button battery ingestion is an infrequent but life threatening ingestion that requires an adequate knowledge base and critical thinking to manage. A simulation case allows real-time, hands on training for residents and fellows to practice managing this type of ingestion.

**Practical Implementation**

The simulation programming is not difficult, but the instructor should be familiar with their simulator software. Approximately 20 minutes is required for this simulation, 10 minutes to do the case, and approximately 10 minutes for debriefing and discussion. The debriefing session should be conducted in a nonjudgmental manner, while focusing on identifying knowledge gaps and critically evaluating the case management.

**Deployment and Limitations**

This case was developed to review and teach PGY1-PGY3 Emergency Medicine residents and Pediatric Emergency Medicine fellows critical aspects to the evaluation and management of button battery ingestion. Even though this is an uncommon ingestion, it is important for residents and fellows to recognize a button battery based on radiographic characteristic and know the importance of immediate removal. A group simulation case scenario was chosen to promote clinical decision making and to critically evaluate those management decisions. The simplicity of the case lends itself best to a low fidelity experience with individuals or a small group of residents. Expected discussion topics include a basic overview and evaluation of a foreign body ingestion, particularly a button battery, and management guidelines. The combination of participation in the simulation scenario, interactive feedback session that focused on debriefing questions, and review of the brief PowerPoint lecture addressed all of the listed objectives for the learners. The case was definitively effective in teaching about button battery ingestions.

This is a real case with patient x-rays – during implementation, no changes were made to the case based on feedback. This case was run several times with both senior and junior emergency medicine residents, as well as pediatric fellows. The case runs smoothly and is effective at relaying the teaching points (recognition on x-ray) and management overview. Some junior residents didn’t recognize the object as a button battery, or were unaware of the importance of immediate removal. Senior resident usually recognized the location of the battery as well as understood the importance of immediate removal. The case has good teaching value for medical students or mid-levels as well.
