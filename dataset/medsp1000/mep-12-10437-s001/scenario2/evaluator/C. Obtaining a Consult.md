# Obtaining a Consult

Pediatric Boot Camp

# Objectives

• List the specific components of information needed to be given to a consultant in order to obtain expert input regarding a specific problem

• Practice eliciting recommendations for diagnostic evaluation or therapeutic management of a specific problem

• Practice establishing appropriate follow-up for a patient

• Demonstrate self-reflection and ability to participate in giving and receiving peer feedback about calling consultants

# Purpose- What do we want?

• Elicit recommendations for diagnostic evaluation or therapeutic management of a specific problem Secure a diagnostic or therapeutic procedure for a patient • Provide appropriate follow-up for a patient

# Components of a Consult Request

Your name, service/department and role   
• Name, age and gender of the patient Relevant history and exam findings Pertinent results- labs, radiographic studies, etc. Your impression   
• A clearly framed question Urgency of the consultation Clarification on how consultation will be communicated   
Be courteous ☺

# Example

• “I’m calling from the ED. I have a kiddo who is refusing to walk today. I’m worried he has septic arthritis of the knee. Can you come see him?”

What information is missing from this consult request?

# Example

“This is John Doe. I’m an intern in the ED and am calling you for a consult. There is a previously healthy 4 year old boy here, Peter Jetson, with high fever and refusal to walk since this morning. His right knee has been very red and swollen. There is no other joint involvement. There is a scab over the knee from a fall he had a week ago but no other recent trauma. His white count is 21 with a left shift, his CRP and ESR are both elevated at 5.3 and 113. We are getting x-rays of the knee, but I am worried that he might have septic arthritis. We would like you to see him and help us decide if he needs an US or if you should tap the knee without any other tests. He is stable, but we would like to start antibiotics soon if it is, in fact, a septic joint. We can discuss your recommendations in the ED after you have seen the patient.”

# Example

“Hi. I’m calling from the pulmonary service. I have a 16 year old girl with CF who seems depressed to the team. My attending wanted me to consult you to see if she needs an antidepressant.”

As a consultant, what else would you like to know?

# Example

“Hi, this is Jane Doe calling from the pulmonary team. I have a consult for you. Sally Sue is a 16 year old girl with CF who was admitted yesterday for weight loss after she stopped taking her pancreatic enzymes. Her affect is very flat, and we are concerned that she has depression or adjustment disorder that is affecting her adherence to her medication. She denies any suicidal ideation. We would like you to come evaluate her and help come up with a plan, if you agree. It is non-emergent, and we are fine if you can’t see her until later this afternoon. We expect her to be here for a few days. Can you give me a page when you have completed the consultation?”
