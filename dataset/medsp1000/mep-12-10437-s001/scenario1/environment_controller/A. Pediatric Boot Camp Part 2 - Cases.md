# Case 1- Abdominal mass

Chief Complaint: Sam Smith is an 18 month old boy who has been sent to the emergency department by his pediatrician for an abdominal mass.

History of Present Illness: The abdominal mass was discovered during his routine well child check today. After the mass was identified on his exam, his parent mentioned that they thought his belly had looked a little bigger over the past month but had just thought it was normal weight gain. In retrospect they also have noticed a slight decrease in his appetite in the past two weeks and he has seemed more tired. He has not had any recent fevers, emesis or diarrhea. He has had a normal amount of wet diapers, and there has been no blood in the urine. He has been growing normally. His grandmother mentioned he looked pale earlier this week. He has been reaching his developmental milestones on time. He walks and has 4-5 words.

Past Medical History: Born at 39 weeks by normal spontaneous vaginal delivery. No medical issues

Immunizations: Up to date

Family History: 6 year old brother with asthma, maternal grandfather died from lung cancer

Social History: He lives with his parents and 6 year old brother. He has never traveled outside the United States. There are no pets at home.

Medications: Multivitamin

Allergies: None

Review of Systems: Negative other than as reported in HPI

# Physical Exam:

Vitals: HR 132 BP 92/49 RR 20 O2 sat $100 \%$ on RA Temp 36.9 Wt 12.5kg

Gen: Alert, no acute distress, somewhat paler in complexion than his parents

HEENT: Normal

Pulm: Clear to auscultation, comfortable respirations, no crackles/rhonchi/wheeze

CV: Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs, $^ { 2 + }$ pulses

Abdomen: Normal bowel sounds, soft, non-tender, a firm mass in the RUQ approximately 5 cm in diameter. No hepatosplenomegaly appreciated.

Back: Normal, no CVA tenderness

GU: Normal, testes down bilaterally

Extremities: Normal

Neuro: Exam limited by age. PERRL, face symmetric, moving all extremities well. Able to sit and stand. Normal reflexes

Skin: Pallor, no bruising or petechiae

Labs (from primary doctor in clinic today):
CBC: WBC 12.1 $45 \%$ PMNs, $52 \%$ lymphs, $2 \%$ monos, $1 \%$ eos), Hbg 10.4, platelets 498
Chemistry: Na 140, K 4.0, Cl 115, HCO3 25, BUN 8, Cr 0.4, Glu 112
Urinalysis: normal

US abdomen: 6x8cm solid mass arising in or adjacent to the superior pole of the right kidney with extension to the inferior aspect of the liver. The inferior vena cava and left kidney appear normal.

Consult: Based on your initial evaluation and work-up you are suspicious for a malignant tumor and would like to consult hematology/oncology about what further hematologic and radiographic evaluation is needed. You are planning on sending a liver function panel and repeating the CBC and electrolyte panel. You know that urine should be sent for homovanillic acid and vanillylmandelic acid to evaluate for neuroblastoma but are not sure if this should be sent from the emergency department. You would like to know if there are any additional diagnostic labs that should be sent now. You would also like recommendations about further imaging. You feel the child needs a CT of the abdomen to further evaluate the mass but wonder if they would recommend more extensive imaging to evaluate for other tumors and metastases.
