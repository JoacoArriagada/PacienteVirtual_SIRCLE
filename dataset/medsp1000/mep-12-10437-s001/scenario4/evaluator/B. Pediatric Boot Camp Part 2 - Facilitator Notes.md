# Consultant

You are busy in clinic this morning and will come see the patient in 3-4 hours. In the meantime you would recommend sending ASO titer, anti-DNAase titer, C3, C4, ANA, and anti-DS DNA anti-body. You suggest limiting fluid intake to a 1 L daily max and giving an IV dose of furosemide.

# Case 4- Edema

# Discussing Difficult News

Physician

The kidney fellow called you to tell you they had discussed the case with their attending and that they would like to admit to the renal service and get a kidney biopsy in the morning. The complement levels were normal and ASO titer, anti-DNAase and anti-DS DNA were all negative. The ANA was mildly positive and should be repeated in several weeks. Your team is concerned for nephritis due to Henoch Schonlein purpura and needs to quantify the proportion of glomeruli involved before initiating steroid therapy. The fellow will come by later this evening to get consent for the procedure, but the family would like an update now about the plan. Her overall prognosis is dependent on the results of the biopsy and her response to therapy. Sara is very afraid of needles and was very distressed by having her IV placed.

Parent

You don’t want Sara to have to have any more needle sticks because she is so afraid. You hope that they can just take a picture of her kidneys without having to do anything invasive but want to know what is going on with Sara.

# Debriefing Guide for Module: Obtaining a Consult

Questions to consider when facilitating feedback to the student asking for a consult:

1. Did they identify themselves by name, team and role?
2. Did they provide the name, age and gender of the patient?
3. Did they provide relevant history and exam findings to the consultant?
4. Did they discuss pertinent results such as labs and radiographic studies?
5. What was the impression stated by the participant calling the consult?
6. What was the specific question to the consultant?
7. Was the urgency of the consultation conveyed?
8. How will the consultant’s impression and recommendations be communicated to the team?
9. Was the communication between the participants courteous and professional?

# Tips

Role playing can feel difficult for learners at first. Establish that this course is a safe place to practice their communication skills before having to use them in real situations.  Consider sharing your personal experiences with requesting/being ask for consultation. Establish that communication is a skill which must be learned and continually practiced throughout a career.  Consider having students reflect on consults that they have seen or participated in and ask them to share things they thought went well and things that could be done differently in the future.

# Debriefing Guide for Module: Discussing Difficult News

Questions to consider when facilitating feedback to the student discussing difficult news:

1. Did they setting up the interview in some way? (e.g. find a private space, arrange for seats, silence pager/phone, ect.)
2. How did they assess the parent’s perceptions of what was happening before discussing the news?
3. Did they establish that they had difficult news to discuss and/or obtaining the parent’s invitation to discuss the news at this time?
4. Did they share knowledge using non-technical words and at a pace that was reasonable for the patient and their family?
5. How did they express empathy and identify emotions expressed by the recipient of the news?
6. Did they conclude with a strategy for next steps and a summary of what was discussed?

# Tips

Role playing can feel difficult for learners at first. Establish that this course is a safe place to practice their communication skills before having to use them in real situations. Consider sharing your personal experiences with difficult conversations with patients and families. Establish that communication is a skill which must be learned and continually practiced throughout a career.
 Consider having students reflect on difficult conversations that they have seen or participated in and ask them to share things they thought went well and things that could be done differently in the future. Ask participants (both the person delivering the difficult news and the participant role-playing the parent) how it felt as the first step of the debrief. Many students find it useful to acknowledge the emotions associated with this exercise before being able to analyze the event.
 During the debrief, if participants identify a step or phrase that felt difficult to them ask the group to problem solve, and allow the student to rephrase that particular piece of the conversation during the debrief.
