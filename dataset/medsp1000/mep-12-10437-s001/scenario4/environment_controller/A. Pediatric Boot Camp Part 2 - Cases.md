# Case 4 - Edema

Chief Complaint: Sara Lin is a 10 year old girl admitted to the general medicine team for edema and decreased urine output over the past 24 hours.

History of Present Illness: Sara’s mother felt that her face was becoming a little swollen in the morning two days ago. Since then Sara has complained of swelling in her feet and legs as well. She thinks she has only urinated once in the past 24 hours. She was diagnosed with Henoch-Schonlein Purpura 4 weeks ago in the emergency department after she developed pain and swelling in her feet and ankles followed by palpable purpura over the legs and pain and swelling in both knees. A urinalysis sent from the emergency department on day two of the illness was normal. She did not have any abdominal pain and was able to ambulate after taking ibuprofen which she has been taking 3-4 times a week since the diagnosis. Her arthritis has resolved in all joints except some mild residual swelling and pain in the right knee. Her rash resolved one week ago. She has been seen by her pediatrician weekly since diagnosis for UAs which have all been negative for protein with 10-20 RBCs. She had a fever one week before the initial onset of her symptoms but none since. She does not have any abdominal pain, headaches, chest pain or dysuria.

In the past 12 hours since her admission you have noticed poor urine output despite IV hydration. Her blood pressure has also been trending up.

Past Medical History: Tonsillectomy at age 3 for obstructive sleep apnea, now resolved. Mild intermittent asthma

Immunizations: Up to date
