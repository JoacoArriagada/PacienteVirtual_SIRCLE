History of Present Illness: The pain initially started 5 days ago. One the first day it was diffuse and did not radiate. He was seen in the emergency department 3 days ago. He has had frequent episodes of non-bloody, non-bilious emesis for 2 days and 2 loose nonbloody stools today. He had a headache this morning but has not had a cough or sore throat. He denies dysuria, constipation or back pain. Although he was initially able to eat normally in the first day of his illness he has only had a few crackers and very little to drink in the past 2 days. He says the pain is a little better today than yesterday, but his mother is worried since he hasn’t been able to drink well. # Components of a Consult Request

Your name, service/department and role
• Name, age and gender of the patient Relevant history and exam findings Pertinent results- labs, radiographic studies, etc. Your impression
• A clearly framed question Urgency of the consultation Clarification on how consultation will be communicated
Be courteous ☺

“This is John Doe. I’m an intern in the ED and am calling you for a consult. There is a previously healthy 4 year old boy here, Peter Jetson, with high fever and refusal to walk since this morning. His right knee has been very red and swollen. There is no other joint involvement. There is a scab over the knee from a fall he had a week ago but no other recent trauma. His white count is 21 with a left shift, his CRP and ESR are both elevated at 5.3 and 113. We are getting x-rays of the knee, but I am worried that he might have septic arthritis. We would like you to see him and help us decide if he needs an US or if you should tap the knee without any other tests. He is stable, but we would like to start antibiotics soon if it is, in fact, a septic joint. We can discuss your recommendations in the ED after you have seen the patient.”

# What is difficult news?

• Malignancy
Life-long chronic illness Degenerative disease Devastating neurologic outcome
• Death
• Concern for abuse Painful or invasive diagnostic or therapeutic intervention
Inability to find an organic cause for chronic pain
Medical error

# Goals During the Discussion of Difficult News

• Gather information
• Transmit medical information
• Provide support Elicit collaboration and support in developing further diagnostic and/or treatment plans

# SPIKES Protocol

• Setting up the interview
• Assessing the patient’s perceptions
• Obtaining the patient’s invitation Giving knowledge and information to patient Addressing emotions with empathy Strategy and summary

# Setting Up the Interview

• Mentally prepare yourself
• Provide privacy Determine who should be present
. • Sit down
Connect with the patient via eye contact and body language
• Minimize interruptions

# Assessing the Patient’s Perceptions

• Gauge understanding based on concerns that have been raised

Use open ended questions to determine what the patient understands

• Why tests were done? • What results they may know about? • What are their biggest concerns?

# Obtaining the Patient’s Invitation

• Establish that you are prepared to discuss medical information with the patient Assess whether the patient is prepared to proceed • Warn the patient you have difficult news

# Giving Knowledge and Information to Patient

• Use nontechnical words
Avoid excessive bluntness Give information in small pieces and assess comprehension in between
Avoid insinuating that there “is nothing left to do”

# Addressing Emotions with Empathy

• Look for emotion
• Identify the emotion
• Identify the reason for the emotion
• Validate the emotion

# Strategy and Summary

• Establish that there will be more opportunities to review
• Elicit patient’s goals
• Discuss options or planned course
• Identify resources for patient
• Encourage patient to gather questions for next meeting
