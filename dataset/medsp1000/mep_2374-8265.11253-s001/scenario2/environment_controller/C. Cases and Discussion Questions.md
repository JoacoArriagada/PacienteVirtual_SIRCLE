**Case B: Disability and the Patient Experience**

Mrs. Jones, a 30-year-old female patient, has spina bifida (SB), a congenital malformation of the spine, which can result in lower extremity paralysis and autonomic dysfunction. She is a wheelchair user and requires bladder management. Mrs. Jones missed her menstrual period and thinks that she may be pregnant. She presents to her OB/GYN physician for evaluation. A pregnancy test is performed, which is positive. The physician enters the exam room, sits down, and says, "I think you should think about abortion." Mrs. Jones is caught off guard, then becomes angry. She explains that this is a desired pregnancy. "Oh, I didn't know, the physician said. I just worry that you might not be able to take care of the baby and that the baby could be taken away." Sobbing, Mrs. Jones leaves the clinic. She finds another provider who was much more supportive. However, even when she was in the hospital having her child, Mrs. Jones couldn't shake the fear that somebody was going to take away the child because they felt that she couldn't take care of him." (Adapted from Streur et al., Disability and Health Journal, 2019)
