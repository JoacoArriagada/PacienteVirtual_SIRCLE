Differences in Sex Development (DSD):
Team-Based Learning Module for Pediatric Residents

**Team Application Activity**

**Case 2**: You are examining a 15-year old girl who presents to the office with concern for amenorrhea. She reports starting breast development at age 10 years. Patient has no chronic medical problems but she has been suffering from depression so taking an SSRI. She is not sexually active. Mom thinks that her depression is partly because she feels 'less than her peers'. Family history reveals one of the aunts had infertility.

She weighs 56 kg and is 165 cm tall. Her vitals show HR 72/min, BP 100/60 mm Hg. Her general exam shows Tanner 4 breast development but sparse pubic hair. There is no clitoral enlargement or a palpable gonad. She is not comfortable with a more detailed genitourinary exam. While you are awaiting the results of laboratory testing, ultrasound of pelvis returns showing absence of uterus or other Mullerian structures.

Objective information available in the source for scene advancement:

- Most likely diagnosis in the source: Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome.
- Laboratory pattern most likely in the source: FSH 2.5 IU/ml, Testosterone 300 ng/dl, prolactin 15 ng/ml, chromosome 46, XY.
- Source note: the patient's presentation is framed as androgen deficiency in a 46 XY child.
- Source note: the patient is able to make AMH and has not had gonads removed.

Follow-up disclosure setup from the source:

- You advise the parents to return for a follow up visit and have the child attend as well.
- The laboratory test confirms diagnosis of a difference in sex development.
- Parents request that the child not be informed about the results because it will be traumatic for the child.

Facilitator notes retained for runtime control:

- Discuss age appropriate sharing of information about the diagnosis.
- Discuss consequences of withholding information.
- Discuss controversy around gonadectomy and tumor risk in AIS.
