# Case 5: Dead Space

Scenario

Intubated patient with improperly assembled ventilator tubing (interposition of an extended section between the Y-piece and endotracheal tube). This creates a large section of deadspace.

# Learning objectives

 State the relationship between minute ventilation and $\mathsf { P a C O } _ { 2 }$ .   
. Describe dead space ventilation and its implications.

# Case Narrative

A. Background: Students are informed by the instructor: A post-surgical patient is being weaned from the ventilator. He becomes agitated and the nurse requests an order for sedation.   
B. Initial Conditions: Rapid, deep respiratory pattern, hypertension and tachycardia.   
C. Scenario Progression (no sedation administered): Continually elevating CO2, both inspired and expired.

# Instructors Notes

Student evaluates patient BEFORE ordering sedation. Hyperventilation is ruled out after ABG analysis or assessment of the capnogram (CO2 is high, not low). Auscultation and additional studies are not helpful, though usually requested before someone recognizes an elevated baseline to the CO2 tracing on the capnogram. Occasionally this requires that a student breathe into the sampling tubing to compare a normal waveform with that of the \`\`patient.'' Removal of the additional tubing causes immediate improvement and normalization of the respiratory pattern and capnogram.

# Debriefing Plan

Discussion is with the whole group of students immediately after the scenario. The extension is explained as bidirectional gas flow with no exchange (the definition of deadspace) and allows a brief description of the circle system and valves. Hypercapnia is described as caused by inspiring too much (e.g., a problem with the ventilator valves allowing exhaled CO2 into the inspiratory limb, or inadvertent use of a CO2 canister instead of oxygen or air), making too much (e.g., malignant hyperthermia or another hypermetabolic disorder), or exhaling too little (e.g., hypoventilation or a problem with the endotracheal tube). Anatomic, physiologic and apparatus deadspace are described. Emphasize that physicians should always look at patients and available data before sedating an agitated patient.
