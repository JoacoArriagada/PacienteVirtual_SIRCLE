# Respiratory Physiology Clinical Correlation

Environment: Basic simulation lab with full-scale human patient simulator (HPS in our case).

# Case 4: Pneumothorax

Scenario

Healthy patient with a spontaneous pneumothorax that progresses to a tension pneumothorax in “phase $2 "$ .

# Case Narrative

A. Background: Students are informed by the instructor: An Emergency Department patient is complaining of shortness of breath and right-sided chest pain.   
B. Initial Conditions: Rapid and shallow respiratory pattern, only one side of chest is moving, hypertension and tachycardia, eyes are open.   
C. Scenario Progression (phase 2): Spontaneous expansion of the interpleural air into a tension pneumothorax with consequent severe hypotension and tachycardia. Rapid improvement after needle decompression.

# Instructors Notes

Auscultation reveals unilateral breath sounds. Pulse oximetry records saturation around $90 \%$. Nasal cannula provides little improvement. There is gradual worsening of the saturation with progressive hemodynamic compromise (hypotension and tachycardia). ABG analysis confirms the large P(A-a)O2 but normal PaCO2. Chest radiograph reveals a large right-sided pneumothorax. Intercostal needle decompression relieves the pneumothorax with an impressive hissing sound and immediate improvement in the patient's condition.
