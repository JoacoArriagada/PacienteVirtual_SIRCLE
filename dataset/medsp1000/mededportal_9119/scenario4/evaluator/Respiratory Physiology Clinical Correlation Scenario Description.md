# Case 4: Pneumothorax

Scenario

Healthy patient with a spontaneous pneumothorax that progresses to a tension pneumothorax in “phase $2 "$ .

# Learning objectives

Explain why supplemental oxygen is ineffective with a large shunt. . Describe ventilation/perfusion mismatch and its implications.

# Case Narrative

A. Background: Students are informed by the instructor: An Emergency Department patient is complaining of shortness of breath and right-sided chest pain.   
B. Initial Conditions: Rapid and shallow respiratory pattern, only one side of chest is moving, hypertension and tachycardia, eyes are open.   
C. Scenario Progression (phase 2): Spontaneous expansion of the interpleural air into a tension pneumothorax with consequent severe hypotension and tachycardia. Rapid improvement after needle decompression.

# Instructors Notes

Auscultation reveals unilateral breath sounds. Pulse oximetry records saturation around $90 \%$ and the students apply nasal cannula. The patient shows little improvement. The students then usually elect to apply a non-rebreathing mask to increase the inspired oxygen concentration, but there is a gradual worsening of the saturation with progressive hemodynamic compromise (hypotension and tachycardia). ABG analysis again confirms the large P(A-a)O2 but normal PaCO2. Usually someone suggests a chest radiograph, which reveals a large right-sided pneumothorax. A student is instructed in placement of an intercostal needle, relieving the pneumothorax with an impressive hissing sound and immediate improvement in the patient's condition.

# Debriefing Plan

Discussion is with the whole group of students immediately after the scenario. Explain the effects of tension pneumothorax on venous return, as well as the need to follow the needle decompression with chest tube placement. Discuss V/Q mismatch. The slide provided in the attachments seems to work well, describing shunt and deadspace as two extremes of V/Q. Describe a spontaneous pneumothorax (or traumatic), and refer back to Case 3 regarding the ineffectiveness of supplemental oxygen with either diffusion block or shunt. Explain how positive pressure ventilation could actually worsen a pneumothorax or convert it to tension.
