# Case 3: Pulmonary Edema

Scenario

Congestive heart failure in a patient who failed to take his digoxin for several days.

# Learning objectives

 Explain why supplemental oxygen is ineffective with severe intrapulmonary shunting.   
 Draw and label the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve.   
 Describe pulmonary compliance.   
 State the implications of alveolar-arterial oxygen tension difference, $P ( A - a ) 0 _ { 2 }$ .

# Case Narrative

A. Background: Students are informed by the instructor: An Emergency Department patient is complaining of shortness of breath.   
B. Initial Conditions: Rapid and shallow respiratory pattern, rales, mild hypotension with tachycardia, $+ / -$ S3, eyes are open.   
C. Scenario Progression: Improvement in saturation after intubation.

# Instructors Notes

Auscultation reveals rales and an S3 gallop. Pulse oximetry records saturation around $90 \%$ and the students apply nasal cannula; however the patient shows little improvement. The students then usually apply a non-rebreathing mask to increase the inspired oxygen concentration, which again provides only moderate improvement in saturation. The students are asked to estimate the PaO2. ABG analysis confirms the large P(A-a)O2 and decreased PaCO2. The patient is intubated to allow the students to feel the difference between a poorly compliant lung, and the improved compliance after administration of a diuretic (and a time warp), which results in outflow of \`\`urine'' to a Foley catheter bag. Usually someone suggests a chest radiograph, which reveals dramatic pulmonary edema and the students immediately ask about a history of heart failure.

# Debriefing Plan

Discussion is with the whole group of students immediately after the scenario. Display and explain the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve, explaining why an ABG is not necessary to determine PaO2 when the $\mathsf { S p O 2 }$ is $90 \%$ or lower, as it can be extrapolated from the curve itself (though would be useful for the PaCO2 in many circumstances). Explain alveolar-arterial difference, including “normal.” Describe compliance (change in volume over change in pressure). Explain that he stopped taking his digoxin causing heart failure with pulmonary edema, that supplemental oxygen is not always effective with a large A-a gradient, and why positive pressure ventilation may help. If a unit on cardiovascular physiology precedes this course, this scenario encourages review and emphasizes the interrelated systems.
