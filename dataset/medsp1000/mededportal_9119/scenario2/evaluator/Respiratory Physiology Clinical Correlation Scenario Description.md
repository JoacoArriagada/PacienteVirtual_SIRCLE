# Case 2: Hypoventilation

# Learning objectives

Explain why supplemental oxygen is effective, even with hypoventilation.   
Describe why pulse oximetry fails to indicate significant hypoventilation when supplemental oxygen is administered.   
List arterial blood gas values in hypoventilation.   
State and explain the alveolar air equation.

# Case Narrative

A. Background: Students are informed by the instructor: One day after a knee operation this otherwise healthy patient has become confused and sleepy.   
B. Initial Conditions: Slow and deep respiratory pattern, eyes are closed, pupils pinpoint.   
C. Scenario Progression: None.

# Instructors Notes

The patient is barely arousable to verbal stimulation, but he also \`\`passes'' his ABC's. Often the students wish to intubate immediately. If mask ventilation or laryngoscopy is attempted, they are told the patient fights against the effort. Supplemental oxygen is applied with improvement in oxygenation over several minutes. The students usually have to be encouraged to draw an ABG at this point, revealing the elevated PaCO2. With prompting, someone suggests narcotic overdose, and naloxone is administered with improvement.

# Debriefing Plan

Discussion is with the whole group of students immediately after the scenario. Display and explain the alveolar air equation emphasizing how supplemental oxygen prevents desaturation as detected with a pulse oximeter even with severe hypoventilation. The relatively short half-life of naloxone is discussed and the need for continued monitoring demonstrated.
