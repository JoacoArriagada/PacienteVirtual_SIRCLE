# Case 1: Respiratory Muscle Paralysis

Scenario

Progressive paralysis of respiratory muscles due to inadvertent administration of vecuronium instead of vancomycin.

# Learning objectives

 Distinguish between hyperventilation and tachypnea.   
 Institute interventions in the acutely hypoxic patient including: o Application of supplemental oxygen o Mask ventilation o Intubation (understand the necessity and the basic steps) o Mechanical ventilation

State basic ventilator settings (minute ventilation $\mathbf { \Psi } = \mathsf { V } _ { \intercal } \times \mathsf { R R } )$ ).

# Case Narrative

A. Background: Students are informed by the instructor: A patient on the medical service has become uncommunicative over the last few minutes; he is drooling and is having difficulty opening his eyes. He begins to look “blue.” You are cross-covering on the patient and know little about him.   
B. Initial Conditions: Rapid and shallow breathing, eyes are closed, tachycardia and hypertension.   
C. Scenario Progression: Total paralysis occurs over several minutes with a goal of clear desaturation within 2-3 minutes.

# Instructors Notes

Though the patient only moans and grunts to verbal stimulation and shaking, he \`\`passes'' his basic assessment of Airway, Breathing and Circulation (the ABC's). Usually the students inaccurately describe him as hyperventilating rather than tachypneic. They proceed to obtain vital signs, including pulse oximetry. Over several minutes, and usually while the students are distracted by placing a blood pressure cuff or discussing whether to draw an arterial sample for analysis, the oxyhemoglobin saturation (SpO2) begins to fall. The timing of this can be adjusted based on the group and their level of distraction.

While attempting to place a nasal cannula, eventually someone realizes the patient is no longer breathing and mask ventilation is instituted. Discuss why intubation is preferred over long-term mask ventilation and allow a student to intubate the patient. A larynx model and laryngoscope are passed around for others to understand the procedure. Mechanical ventilation is instituted and arterial blood gases are monitored to titrate minute ventilation.

# Debriefing Plan

Discussion is with the whole group of students immediately after the scenario. Discuss the learning objectives above, describe capnography to confirm intubation. In addition to the teaching points noted, this scenario offers an excellent reminder that unresponsive patients may be able to hear and understand though unable to react. Students are reminded always to talk to the patient in a reassuring manner even if uncertain whether the patient is aware of what is going on.
