IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – The scenario is to take place in an Emergency Department setting. Students will complete the 4 cases in groups of 6 students each. One student group will be in the lab for each case with the remaining students observing from the classroom. Each group of students will have an opportunity to provide hands-on care during one case and to observe the other 3 cases. An audiovisual feed from the lab will be set up to allow

the groups not involved in the hands-on evaluation to observe from the adjacent classroom (Figure). Additional feeds into the classroom will provide the monitor readings and waveforms from the patient monitor and the radiographic images ordered during each case. One faculty preceptor will be in the lab and one will be in the classroom for each case.

B. Manikin Set Up – A Laerdal $^ \mathrm { \textregistered }$ SimMan will be used to run the scenarios. Case 2: The mannequin will be moulaged with carbonaceous material around the mouth and nose and in the pharynx. The breath sounds will be set to produce wheezes bilaterally.

C. Props – A cardiac monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, a pulse oximeter, and a mask with a nebulizer chamber will be available, as well as supplemental oxygen by nasal cannula and a non-rebreather mask. A full complement of vasoactive agents, ACLS medications, and medicines necessary for sedation, rapid sequence intubation, and analgesia will be available. In addition, a fully stocked code cart with defibrillator will be available for use, along with a selection of supplies for direct laryngoscopy, intubation, and rescue airways.

D. Audiovisual Case 2: A chest radiograph will be available for viewing which shows either normal findings or hyperinflation.

E. Distractors – Since this exercise is being provided to second year medical students, no distractors will be added.

V. Actors

A. Roles and who may play them – The team leader will initiate the history and may request team members to perform various portions of the examination. Team members can suggest additional questions for the team leader to ask. They will also perform assessment tasks as requested by the team leader or the faculty facilitator. The faculty role is to serve only as a facilitator to ensure the students complete the tasks necessary to derive maximum benefit from the simulation. In some instances the facilitator may need to model the technique for completing a focused history and physical exam on an acutely ill patient.

B. Action Role

1) Treating physician – to be played by a student volunteer from the group in the lab for each case. The role involves coordinating the team, obtaining a history, and performing a physical exam.

2) Other health care professionals – to be played by the remaining students. The roles involve collaborating with and assisting the treating physician in any requested fashion. This may include minor procedures (IV access), placing the patient on monitoring equipment, performing CPR, and giving medications.

3) Students in the classroom viewing the simulation – This group’s role includes:

1. Discussing the data obtained in the lab and listing additional information desired.
2. Discussing the differential diagnosis as the scenario evolves.
3. Discussing their thoughts on the key questions asked during scenario pauses.

# Case 2 - Smoke Inhalation Paul Green

Presenting Complaint: 35 YO man who is carried into the ED by police after he was rescued from a house fire. The patient was initially unconscious with no signs of trauma. He regained consciousness en route to the ED and denied any trauma, but is confused.

Vital Signs: P 120, BP114/70, T 99, R 36, Room Air pulse ox $8 8 \%$ on room air
No medications
No allergies

Operator Note: The patient will initially have audible wheezing and is confused, but will be able to speak in order to answer questions. He will deny any history of injury with the episode and report that he fell asleep on a couch while something was cooking on the stove. He awoke to smoke and flames and then can’t remember what happened until he woke up in the back of the police car. As the students approach the patient, he is confused, coughing and asks what is going on.

# Facilitator Notes:

START SCENARIO: If the students do not detect the patient’s hypoxia as part of their initial assessment, have each student assess one of the vital signs and the information on the monitor and describe their findings.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Ask the students in the lab: What it the most important treatment priority at this time?

RESUME SCENARIO: Initiate high-flow supplementary oxygen. The patient’s O2 saturation will improve to $9 3 \%$ , but the wheezing will persist. You have approximately 5 minutes for the students to obtain a history from the mannequin, to examine the mannequin, and to request any ancillary studies. If the student is not obtaining a good focused history, then stop him/her after approximately 2 minutes and ask him/her to summarize. Next direct the students to listen to the breath sounds (point out the marked areas where the sounds will be best heard). Ask the students performing the exam to describe their findings so that the students in the classroom will have all the same information as those in the lab. If the students don’t appreciate the wheezing, then have them reexamine the mannequin and point out the wheezing if necessary.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Discuss the pathophysiologic process causing this patient’s hypoxia and wheezing. Question 2: What pharmacotherapy would be most appropriate at this time?

RESUME SCENARIO: Initiate bronchodilator treatment. (The nebulizer will be attached to the mask and will be prefilled with fluid. Instruct the students to attach it to the oxygen source and turn it up to 10 liters/minute). The patient’s wheezing improves and pulse ox increases to $1 0 0 \%$ during the treatment, but he is still confused. If the students don’t ask for a CXR, then suggest one. Review the image with them (Official reading will state: Normal Chest) and discuss the changes they might expect to find and the associated pathophysiology. Direct the students to reassess their patient. Upon reassessing the patient, he is still confused despite the improved oxygenation.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Question 3: What ancillary study should be ordered to evaluate this patient’s persistent altered mental status?

RESUME SCENARIO: Patient’s condition stabilizes, but remains confused. His carboxyhemoglobin comes back at $5 5 \%$ .

PAUSE SCENARIO: Question 4: What additional treatment modality should be considered in the management of this patient?
