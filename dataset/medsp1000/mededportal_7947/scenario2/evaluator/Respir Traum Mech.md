All Cases:
1) Obtains a focused history from the patient, family, EMS or referring health care providers.
2) Initiates monitoring as appropriate for each scenario including pulse oximetry, blood pressure, and cardiac monitoring.
3) Completes a focused physical exam.
4) Assesses patient’s oxygenation status and places patient on supplemental oxygen in response to low oxygen saturation.
5) Actively participates as a team member and is respectful of other team members and their input.
6) Lists findings indicative of respiratory failure (increased/decreased respiratory rate, worsening hypoxia, mottling, bradycardia, thready pulses, altered mental status).

Case 2 Specific:

1) Orders nebulization treatment with a beta agonist in response to wheezing and poor oxygenation.

2) Describes physical findings consistent with smoke inhalation (e.g. soot in the nose and pharynx, hoarseness, stridor, wheezing, carbonaceous sputum).

3) Orders a chest radiograph to clarify the nature of the pulmonary pathology.

4) Describes the pertinent history and physical findings which support measuring a carboxyhemglobin level.

5) Discusses the controversy regarding hyperbaric oxygen therapy in the treatment of carbon monoxide poisoning.

# Case 2:

1. Chief Complaint: Rescued from a burning building

2. The participants will be provided with a triage note stating: 35 YO man who is brought into the ED by the police after he was rescued from a house fire. The patient was initially unconscious with no signs of trauma. He regained consciousness en route to the ED and denied any trauma, but is still confused.

3. Vital signs (on the triage note): Blood pressure 114/70, Heart rate 120, Temperature 99 F, Respiration 36/min, Oxygen saturation $8 8 \%$ on room air.

4. Medications, past medical history, and allergies: (Must be requested) Patient doesn’t have any chronic medical problems; he has not been taking any medicines and has no allergies.

5. Pertinent physical findings: No evidence of burns, but the patient has soot over most of his body. He also had carbonaceous material in his mouth and nose with stridorous breathing and bilateral wheezes.

# Case 2 - Smoke Inhalation Paul Green

Presenting Complaint: 35 YO man who is carried into the ED by police after he was rescued from a house fire. The patient was initially unconscious with no signs of trauma. He regained consciousness en route to the ED and denied any trauma, but is confused.

Vital Signs: P 120, BP114/70, T 99, R 36, Room Air pulse ox $8 8 \%$ on room air
No medications
No allergies

Operator Note: The patient will initially have audible wheezing and is confused, but will be able to speak in order to answer questions. He will deny any history of injury with the episode and report that he fell asleep on a couch while something was cooking on the stove. He awoke to smoke and flames and then can’t remember what happened until he woke up in the back of the police car. As the students approach the patient, he is confused, coughing and asks what is going on.

# Facilitator Notes:

START SCENARIO: If the students do not detect the patient’s hypoxia as part of their initial assessment, have each student assess one of the vital signs and the information on the monitor and describe their findings.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Ask the students in the lab: What it the most important treatment priority at this time? (Answer - Initiate oxygen therapy) Guide a brief student discussion to help them arrive at the answer if not offered initially.

RESUME SCENARIO: Initiate high-flow supplementary oxygen. The patient’s O2 saturation will improve to $9 3 \%$ , but the wheezing will persist. You have approximately 5 minutes for the students to obtain a history from the mannequin, to examine the mannequin, and to request any ancillary studies. If the student is not obtaining a good focused history, then stop him/her after approximately 2 minutes and ask him/her to summarize. Next direct the students to listen to the breath sounds (point out the marked areas where the sounds will be best heard). Ask the students performing the exam to describe their findings so that the students in the classroom will have all the same information as those in the lab. If the students don’t appreciate the wheezing, then have them reexamine the mannequin and point out the wheezing if necessary.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Discuss the pathophysiologic process causing this patient’s hypoxia and wheezing. Question 2: What pharmacotherapy would be most appropriate at this time? (Answer - Inhaled bronchodilator - albuterol) Guide a brief student discussion to help them arrive at the answer if not offered initially.

RESUME SCENARIO: Initiate bronchodilator treatment. (The nebulizer will be attached to the mask and will be prefilled with fluid. Instruct the students to attach it to the oxygen source and turn it up to 10 liters/minute). The patient’s wheezing improves and pulse ox increases to $1 0 0 \%$ during the treatment, but he is still confused. If the students don’t ask for a CXR, then suggest one. Review the image with them (Official reading will state: Normal Chest) and discuss the changes they might expect to find and the associated pathophysiology. Direct the students to reassess their patient. Upon reassessing the patient, he is still confused despite the improved oxygenation.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Question 3: What ancillary study should be ordered to evaluate this patient’s persistent altered mental status? (Answer - Carboxyhemoglobin level) Guide a brief student discussion to help them arrive at the answer if not offered initially.

RESUME SCENARIO: Patient’s condition stabilizes, but remains confused. His carboxyhemoglobin comes back at $5 5 \%$ .

PAUSE SCENARIO: Question 4: What additional treatment modality should be considered in the management of this patient? (Answer - Hyperbaric Oxygen therapy) Guide a brief student discussion to help them arrive at the answer if not offered initially.
