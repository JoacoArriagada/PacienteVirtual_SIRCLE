IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – The scenario is to take place in an Emergency Department setting. Students will complete the 4 cases in groups of 6 students each. One student group will be in the lab for each case with the remaining students observing from the classroom. Each group of students will have an opportunity to provide hands-on care during one case and to observe the other 3 cases. An audiovisual feed from the lab will be set up to allow

the groups not involved in the hands-on evaluation to observe from the adjacent classroom (Figure). Additional feeds into the classroom will provide the monitor readings and waveforms from the patient monitor and the radiographic images ordered during each case. One faculty preceptor will be in the lab and one will be in the classroom for each case.

B. Manikin Set Up – A Laerdal $^ \mathrm { \textregistered }$ SimMan will be used to run the scenarios. Case 3: The mannequin will be set to produce rales from the right lung with normal sounds on the left. A prerecorded cough will be looped every few minutes.

C. Props – A cardiac monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, a pulse oximeter, and a mask with a nebulizer chamber will be available, as well as supplemental oxygen by nasal cannula and a non-rebreather mask. A full complement of vasoactive agents, ACLS medications, and medicines necessary for sedation, rapid sequence intubation, and analgesia will be available. In addition, a fully stocked code cart with defibrillator will be available for use, along with a selection of supplies for direct laryngoscopy, intubation, and rescue airways.

D. Audiovisual Case 3: A chest radiograph will be available for viewing which shows infiltrates in the right middle and lower lobes.

E. Distractors – Since this exercise is being provided to second year medical students, no distractors will be added.

V. Actors

A. Roles and who may play them – The team leader will initiate the history and may request team members to perform various portions of the examination. Team members can suggest additional questions for the team leader to ask. They will also perform assessment tasks as requested by the team leader or the faculty facilitator. The faculty role is to serve only as a facilitator to ensure the students complete the tasks necessary to derive maximum benefit from the simulation. In some instances the facilitator may need to model the technique for completing a focused history and physical exam on an acutely ill patient. The nursing home nurse in Case 3 will be played by the simulator operator who is contacted using a speaker telephone (overhead speakers in the simulation lab).

B. Action Role

1) Treating physician – to be played by a student volunteer from the group in the lab for each case. The role involves coordinating the team, obtaining a history, and performing a physical exam.

2) Other health care professionals – to be played by the remaining students. The roles involve collaborating with and assisting the treating physician in any requested fashion. This may include minor procedures (IV access), placing the patient on monitoring equipment, performing CPR, and giving medications.

3) Students in the classroom viewing the simulation – This group’s role includes:

1. Discussing the data obtained in the lab and listing additional information desired.
2. Discussing the differential diagnosis as the scenario evolves.
3. Discussing their thoughts on the key questions asked during scenario pauses.

# Facilitator Notes:

START SCENARIO: The students will have approximately 5 minutes to complete their history and exam of the mannequin. If they only try to get information from the patient and don’t ask for any additional sources of information, then ask them who else might have the information they need. When they ask to contact the nursing home, the operator will come on the loud speaker (speaker telephone) playing the role of one of the nursing home nurses. If the student is not obtaining a good focused history, then stop him/her after approximately 2 minutes, ask him/her to summarize and you complete a brief focused history over the next 1-2 minutes. Next direct the students to listen to the breath sounds (point out the marked areas where the sound will be best heard). Ask the students performing the exam to describe their findings so that the students in the classroom will have all the same information as those in the lab. If they do not detect the rales on the right side, then have them reexamine the mannequin and point out the difference if necessary.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Ask the students in the lab: What is the most likely cause of the patient’s condition?

RESUME SCENARIO: Prompt students to order a CXR if they haven’t done so. Discuss how the film findings correlate with the pathophysiology of aspiration pneumonia and the findings on physical exam. (The views show shadowing in the right lung, radiating from the right hilum and consistent with a right lower/middle lobe infiltrate. This correlates with rales heard on auscultating the right lung with normal breath sounds on the left).

PAUSE SCENARIO: Question 2: What is the most appropriate pharmacologic treatment for this patient?

RESUME SCENARIO: The patient has a coughing paroxysm and then starts to deteriorate with increasing pulse, decreasing BP, worsening tachypnea, and becomes completely unresponsive. Have each student assess one of the vital signs and the information on the monitor during this episode.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Ask each student to describe what he/she observed and then have them answer Question 3: What is your next step if the patient continues this course of deterioration? Also discuss the issue of advanced directives if not previously brought up during the case.

RESUME SCENARIO: The patient worsens. He loses consciousness, and stops breathing. Have two students perform 2-person bag-mask ventilation while you prepare to intubate. Demonstrate the airway anatomy with the fiberoptic scope while placing an endotracheal tube. Instruct one student on proper bagging technique (rate on 12/minute and tidal volume of about $\%$ an adult bag calibrated by chest rise). After completing intubation, instruct another student on how to connect the bag to a high flow oxygen source, talk another student through the steps involved in securing the tube with the commercial tube holder, and direct any additional students to observe the monitor for changes while these actions are occurring. Once all the tasks are completed, each can briefly share what changes he/she saw.

Next direct the students to answer Question 4: What is the most like cause of this patient’s hypoxia?
