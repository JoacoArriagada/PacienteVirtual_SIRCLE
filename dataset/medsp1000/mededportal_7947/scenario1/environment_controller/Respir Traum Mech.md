IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – The scenario is to take place in an Emergency Department setting. Students will complete the 4 cases in groups of 6 students each. One student group will be in the lab for each case with the remaining students observing from the classroom. Each group of students will have an opportunity to provide hands-on care during one case and to observe the other 3 cases. An audiovisual feed from the lab will be set up to allow

the groups not involved in the hands-on evaluation to observe from the adjacent classroom (Figure). Additional feeds into the classroom will provide the monitor readings and waveforms from the patient monitor and the radiographic images ordered during each case. One faculty preceptor will be in the lab and one will be in the classroom for each case.

B. Manikin Set Up – A Laerdal $^ \mathrm { \textregistered }$ SimMan will be used to run the scenarios. Case 1: The left side breath sounds will be decreased to zero and the left pneumothorax function will be activated.

C. Props – A cardiac monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, a pulse oximeter, and a mask with a nebulizer chamber will be available, as well as supplemental oxygen by nasal cannula and a non-rebreather mask. A full complement of vasoactive agents, ACLS medications, and medicines necessary for sedation, rapid sequence intubation, and analgesia will be available. In addition, a fully stocked code cart with defibrillator will be available for use, along with a selection of supplies for direct laryngoscopy, intubation, and rescue airways.

D. Audiovisual Case 1: A chest radiograph will be available for viewing which shows a large simple left sided pneumothorax.

E. Distractors – Since this exercise is being provided to second year medical students, no distractors will be added.

V. Actors

A. Roles and who may play them – The team leader will initiate the history and may request team members to perform various portions of the examination. Team members can suggest additional questions for the team leader to ask. They will also perform assessment tasks as requested by the team leader or the faculty facilitator. The faculty role is to serve only as a facilitator to ensure the students complete the tasks necessary to derive maximum benefit from the simulation. In some instances the facilitator may need to model the technique for completing a focused history and physical exam on an acutely ill patient.

B. Action Role

1) Treating physician – to be played by a student volunteer from the group in the lab for each case. The role involves coordinating the team, obtaining a history, and performing a physical exam.

2) Other health care professionals – to be played by the remaining students. The roles involve collaborating with and assisting the treating physician in any requested fashion. This may include minor procedures (IV access), placing the patient on monitoring equipment, performing CPR, and giving medications.

3) Students in the classroom viewing the simulation – This group’s role includes:

1. Discussing the data obtained in the lab and listing additional information desired.
2. Discussing the differential diagnosis as the scenario evolves.
3. Discussing their thoughts on the key questions asked during scenario pauses.

# Facilitator Notes:

START SCENARIO: You have approximately 5 minutes for the students to obtain a history from the mannequin, to examine the mannequin, and to request any ancillary studies. If the student is not obtaining a good focused history, then stop him/her after approximately 2 minutes and ask him/her to summarize. You can then complete a brief focused history over the next 1-2 minutes. Next direct the students to listen to the breath sounds (point out the marked areas where the sound will be best heard) and describe what they find. Ask the students performing the exam to describe their findings so that the students in the classroom will have all the same information as those in the lab. If the students do not detect the difference in breath sounds, then have them reexamine the mannequin. Direct them to listen in the axillary regions and not just the parasternal regions. Point out the decreased breath sounds on the left if necessary.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Discuss the students’ differential diagnosis at this point. Pose the question to the students in the lab: What is the most appropriate diagnostic test at this time based on your differential diagnosis?

RESUME SCENARIO: Order the chest X-ray. The students can obtain additional information while the radiograph is brought up on the viewer.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Question 2: What do you see on the Chest radiograph?

RESUME SCENARIO: After the students answer the question, review the radiograph findings with them. The patient will subsequently start to deteriorate over several

minutes. Have each student assess one of the vital signs and the information on the monitor during this episode. He will deteriorate until his pulse is up to 147, BP down to 65/26 and his pulse oximetry drops to $8 8 \%$ . You do not need to wait until the vital signs deteriorate to this point, but you can ask them what changes are occurring as soon as significant changes are apparent.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Question 3: What is the pathophysiologic mechanism causing the patient’s deterioration?

Question 4: What is the most appropriate treatment at this point?

RESUME SCENARIO: If they are not familiar with needle decompression, then briefly describe the procedure. Perform the needle decompression while explaining the steps to the students. Assign the students to various tasks such as monitoring the pulse (not the monitor tracing, but the actual pulse), monitoring the respiratory rate, monitoring the pulse oximetry and monitoring the blood pressure. Once the mannequin recovers, each can briefly share what he/she saw.
