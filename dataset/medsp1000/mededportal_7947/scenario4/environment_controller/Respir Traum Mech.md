IV. Environment

A. Lab Set Up – The scenario is to take place in an Emergency Department setting. Students will complete the 4 cases in groups of 6 students each. One student group will be in the lab for each case with the remaining students observing from the classroom. Each group of students will have an opportunity to provide hands-on care during one case and to observe the other 3 cases. An audiovisual feed from the lab will be set up to allow

the groups not involved in the hands-on evaluation to observe from the adjacent classroom (Figure). Additional feeds into the classroom will provide the monitor readings and waveforms from the patient monitor and the radiographic images ordered during each case. One faculty preceptor will be in the lab and one will be in the classroom for each case.

B. Manikin Set Up – A Laerdal $^ \mathrm { \textregistered }$ SimMan will be used to run the scenarios. Case 4: The breath sounds will be set to produce wheezing. In addition, externally audible prerecorded stridorous breathing will be looped until the patient becomes apneic. The tongue and pharynx will be swollen to make orotracheal intubation impossible.

C. Props – A cardiac monitor with leads, blood pressure cuff, a pulse oximeter, and a mask with a nebulizer chamber will be available, as well as supplemental oxygen by nasal cannula and a non-rebreather mask. A full complement of vasoactive agents, ACLS medications, and medicines necessary for sedation, rapid sequence intubation, and analgesia will be available. In addition, a fully stocked code cart with defibrillator will be available for use, along with a selection of supplies for direct laryngoscopy, intubation, and rescue airways.

D. Audiovisual Case 4: A normal chest radiograph will be available for viewing.

E. Distractors – Since this exercise is being provided to second year medical students, no distractors will be added.

V. Actors

A. Roles and who may play them – The team leader will initiate the history and may request team members to perform various portions of the examination. Team members can suggest additional questions for the team leader to ask. They will also perform assessment tasks as requested by the team leader or the faculty facilitator. The faculty role is to serve only as a facilitator to ensure the students complete the tasks necessary to derive maximum benefit from the simulation. In some instances the facilitator may need to model the technique for completing a focused history and physical exam on an acutely ill patient. The friend in Case 4 will be played by the simulator operator who is contacted using a speaker telephone (overhead speakers in the simulation lab).

B. Action Role

1) Treating physician – to be played by a student volunteer from the group in the lab for each case. The role involves coordinating the team, obtaining a history, and performing a physical exam.

2) Other health care professionals – to be played by the remaining students. The roles involve collaborating with and assisting the treating physician in any requested fashion. This may include minor procedures (IV access), placing the patient on monitoring equipment, performing CPR, and giving medications.

3) Students in the classroom viewing the simulation – This group’s role includes:

1. Discussing the data obtained in the lab and listing additional information desired.
2. Discussing the differential diagnosis as the scenario evolves.
3. Discussing their thoughts on the key questions asked during scenario pauses.

Presenting complaint: 45 YO man presents to triage with a chief complaint of difficulty breathing which started about 30 minutes prior to arrival and has been getting progressively worse.

Vital Signs: P 114, BP 136/84, R 42, T97.6, Pulse ox is $9 6 \%$ on 2 L/min nasal oxygen

If asked, he has a history of a previous episode like this about 10 year earlier due to an allergic reaction to nuts. # Facilitator Notes:

START SCENARIO: You have approximately 5 minutes for the students to obtain a history from the mannequin, to examine the mannequin, and to request any ancillary studies. If the student is not obtaining a good focused history, then stop him/her after approximately 2 minutes, ask him/her to summarize and you complete a brief focused history over the next 1-2 minutes. Next direct the students to listen to the breath sounds (he/she will hear wheezes with transmitted stridorous breath sounds in all lung fields). Point out the marked areas where the sounds are best heard. Ask the students performing the exam to describe their findings so that the students in the classroom will have all the same information as those in the lab.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Ask the students in the lab: What is the most likely etiology of the patient’s difficulty breathing?

RESUME SCENARIO: Patient’s stridor worsens, and pulse increases. The patient can no longer speak and the pulse oximetry reading starts to decline slightly. Instruct the students to reevaluate the airway and to describe their findings so that the students in the classroom will have the same information as those in the lab. Point out the swollen tongue if it is not detected.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Ask the students to describe the pathophysiologic process occurring in this patient and what pharmacologic agents could be used to treat this process. Question 2: What is the most important pharmacologic agent needed to treat this patient?

RESUME SCENARIO: Demonstrate the administration of subcutaneous epinephrine after assigning each of the students in the room to observe various parameters. (One – fingers on pulse, second – monitor BP, third – monitor respiratory rate, fourth – monitor patient’s mental status and ability to talk. Fifth (if present) - monitor pulse ox.

PAUSE SCENARIO: Ask each student to describe what he/she observed and then ask the students to answer Question 3: What pharmacotherapeutic agent is best used for longer term treatment of this patient’s condition?

RESUME SCENARIO: The patient worsens despite the treatment. The patient loses consciousness, and stops breathing. Have two students perform 2-person bag-mask ventilation while you prepare to intubate. Demonstrate the lack of visibility of the airway with the fiberoptic scope and note that the patient isn’t being sustained with bag-mask ventilation. Demonstrate a surgical cricothyrotomy with the students serving as assistants. After completing the procedure, instruct a student on how to attach the bag to a high flow oxygen source and how to ventilate the patient, instruct another student how to secure the tube with tracheostomy tube tape. Direct any additional students to observe the monitored parameters for changes while these actions are occurring. Once all the tasks are complete, each student can briefly share what changes he/she saw.
