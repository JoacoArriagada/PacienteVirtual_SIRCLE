1. Case 2: Ventricular fibrillation in DNR/DNI Patient

6. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up: Emergency department trauma bay
   2. Manikin Set Up: The manikin is on a stretcher, fully clothed with CPR in progress by EMT transport (curtain of trauma bay is closed until 2 min after case begins).
   3. Props and Drugs: IV start kits, IVs, fluid, endotracheal intubation kit, defibrillator, monitor, ACLS drugs and intubation drugs (sedation and paralytics)

7. Actors
   1. Roles: Physician, Nurses (2-4), EMTs, Family members
   2. Who May Play Them: Medical staff are played by themselves. Trained actors play the family members.
   3. Action Role: The medical staff are involved in the medical care of the patient. The family members arrive after their mother has been intubated. They are distraught.

8. Case Narrative
   1. Scenario background: Physicians, nurses and EMTs are standing in an empty trauma bay and have no indication as to the incoming case. A phone rings and is picked up by a staff member. The phone call is from EMS with a report of a 75 year-old female picked up for respiratory distress. ETA is 2 minutes.
      1. The EMTs arrive with a patient on the stretcher with CPR in progress, having lost a pulse just prior to arrival.
   2. Initial Scenario conditions
      1. The patient is unresponsive with no pulse
      2. EMTs give report after handoff that the patient was in respiratory distress upon arrival and she had an oxygen saturation of 84% on room air. Oxygen and 40 mg furosemide were given before she lost her pulse in the ambulance.
      3. Nothing is known about the patient’s past medical history and she was alone in the house.
   3. Scenario Play and Branch Points
      1. Initial scenario focuses on resuscitation of the patient.
      2. After the second defibrillation, the patient has a ROSC (return of spontaneous circulation). The monitor shows sinus tachycardia. At this point the patient is unresponsive and has no respiratory effort.
      3. After intubation, the patient’s family members enter the room. They are very upset because their mother has been made DNR/DNI on her last admission to the hospital, and they contend that this information should have been available to the providers.
      4. If the caregivers and the team follow the steps for Conflict Resolution, then the family has understanding, becomes less upset and agrees to continue the resuscitation. The family could also agree to terminate resuscitation based on the wishes of the mother.
      5. If the caregivers, instead, become defensive about why the patient was intubated, the family members become more upset and accuse the care team of ignoring their mother’s wishes.
      6. Ultimately, the patient has another episode of ventricular fibrillation and the family is asked whether care should continue. Regardless of how the previous interaction has proceeded, the family makes a second decision to either defibrillate or to allow the patient to die.

9. Instructor’s Notes
   1. Tips to keep the scenario moving
      1. While there are no specific stimuli besides the cardiac monitor and vital signs, EKG and labs might be ordered. If this is the case, the patient should go into a second round of ventricular fibrillation prior to these being available.
      2. The family enters just after the patient has been intubated.
      3. If the team is successful in empathy, clinical explanation and inclusion of the family in the care of their parent, the actors will relax and express understanding.
      4. If the care team becomes defensive and fails to recognize and acknowledge the family members’ concerns, the family members become more irate and are unable to come to a decision on whether to continue the resuscitation or not.
