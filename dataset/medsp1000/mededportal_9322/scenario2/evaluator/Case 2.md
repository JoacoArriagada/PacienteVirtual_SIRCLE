1. Case 2: Ventricular fibrillation in DNR/DNI Patient
2. Target Audience: attending physicians, residents, midlevel providers, nurses, medical technicians.
3. Goals and Learning Objectives
   1. Goal- The team of emergency staff (Physician, nurses, techs and other support staff) will lead a resuscitation of a patient in ventricular fibrillation. Unbeknownst to them, the patient is DNR/DNI. After resuscitation, the patient’s family enters unannounced and is upset that their family member has been resuscitated. The goals of the care team are to 1) resuscitate the patient and 2) to manage the frustration of the family members.
   2. Primary Learning Objectives
      1. Teamwork skills emphasizing Crew Resource Management (CRM)
      2. Family and patient interactions- Conflict Resolution
      3. ACLS management of ventricular fibrillation
         1. CPR
         2. Defibrillation
         3. Epinephrine
         4. Intubation of trachea
   3. Assessment Objectives
      1. Utilization of CRM principles
   4. Establish the team leader
   5. Clarify roles
   6. Assign tasks by name
   7. Call-outs and check backs when orders are ordered and performed
   8. Situational Awareness by all members of staff- recognition of progression of care.
   9. Decision Making- Leaders guide care (defibrillation, intubation) but other members of team are involved (starting IVs without specific orders).
   10. Transition of leadership from the physician to another team member as the physician turns focus to the family
   11. Recognition of the patient’s return to an unstable state while the physician is talking to the family, and action by the team members to reinitiate ACLS or, at a minimum, inform the physician of the changed status.
4. Family Interactions- Conflict Resolution
   1. Create a calm environment
   2. Acknowledge and validate other’s feelings – the natural reaction is to defend the course of care (“This is what we do when we do not know.”), which does nothing to validate the family’s feelings. Instead we look for a reaction such as, “If this is not what your mother wanted, we can certainly understand why you are so upset.”
   3. Focus on the problem-not the person
   4. Present options for proceeding
   5. Gain agreement on one of the option’s presented
5. ACLS Management of Ventricular Fibrillation
   1. Physician in team must:
      1. Recognize ventricular fibrillation on monitor
      2. Order CPR including ventilations via bag-valve-mask
      3. Order defibrillation at appropriate voltage
      4. Order epinephrine
      5. Order amioderone, lidocaine, or procainamaide
      6. Repeat defibrillation
      7. Recognize unresponsive patient after ROSC (return of spontaneous circulation) and proceed with intubation
   2. Nurses in team must:
      1. Establish IV access.
      2. Put patient on monitor
      3. Call out vital signs and monitor readings
      4. Administer medications
      5. Administer defibrillation
   3. EMTs and other support staff must:
      1. Begin CPR at appropriate rate and technique
      2. Proceed with ventilation with appropriate technique
6. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up: Emergency department trauma bay
   2. Manikin Set Up: The manikin is on a stretcher, fully clothed with CPR in progress by EMT transport (curtain of trauma bay is closed until 2 min after case begins).
   3. Props and Drugs: IV start kits, IVs, fluid, endotracheal intubation kit, defibrillator, monitor, ACLS drugs and intubation drugs (sedation and paralytics)
7. Actors
   1. Roles: Physician, Nurses (2-4), EMTs, Family members
   2. Who May Play Them: Medical staff are played by themselves. Trained actors play the family members.
   3. Action Role: The medical staff are involved in the medical care of the patient. The family members arrive after their mother has been intubated. They are distraught.
8. Case Narrative
   1. Scenario background: Physicians, nurses and EMTs are standing in an empty trauma bay and have no indication as to the incoming case. A phone rings and is picked up by a staff member. The phone call is from EMS with a report of a 75 year-old female picked up for respiratory distress. ETA is 2 minutes.
      1. At this point 30 seconds is given to the team to assign roles and prepare monitors, IVs and assume their positions for the resuscitation
      2. The EMTs arrive with a patient on the stretcher with CPR in progress, having lost a pulse just prior to arrival.
   2. Initial Scenario conditions
      1. The patient is unresponsive with no pulse
      2. EMTs give report after handoff that the patient was in respiratory distress upon arrival and she had an oxygen saturation of 84% on room air. Oxygen and 40 mg furosemide were given before she lost her pulse in the ambulance.
      3. Nothing is known about the patient’s past medical history and she was alone in the house.
   3. Scenario Play and Branch Points
      1. Initial scenario focuses on resuscitation of the patient, with an emphasis on team work and the above mentioned CRM principles.
      2. CPR performed by techs or nurse
      3. Placing the patient on a monitor
      4. Establishing IV accesses
      5. Ventilation with BVM
      6. Recognition of ventricular tachycardia by physician.
      7. Defibrillation
      8. ACLS medications: epinephrine, amioderone etc.
      9. Repeat defibrillation
   4. During this initial part of the case, the physician will have to define him/herself as the team leader, assign roles and rely on team members to provide information. The nurses and techs will respond to these orders, using check backs to confirm orders and call outs after orders are completed. Additionally, openness of communication is emphasized with all members of the team free to speak up with new important information.
   5. After the second defibrillation, the patient has a ROSC (return of spontaneous circulation). The monitor shows sinus tachycardia. At this point the patient is unresponsive and has no respiratory effort and should be promptly intubated using rapid sequence intubation (RSI).
   6. After intubation, the patient’s family members enter the room. They are very upset because their mother has been made DNR/DNI on her last admission to the hospital, and they contend that this information should have been available to the providers.
   7. There are many directions the scenario can go from here. If the caregivers and the team follow the steps for Conflict Resolution, then the family has understanding, becomes less upset and agrees to continue the resuscitation. The family could also agree to terminate resuscitation based on the wishes of the mother. The decision that the family makes can not be reached unless the conflict has been resolved.
   8. If the caregivers, instead, become defensive about why the patient was intubated, the family members become more upset and accuse the care team of ignoring their mother’s wishes.
   9. Particular attention is paid to whether the team deliberately transitions leadership from the physician to another member of the team as the physician moves to take over the communication with the family.
   10. Ultimately, the patient has another episode of ventricular fibrillation and the family is asked whether care should continue. Regardless of how the previous interaction has proceeded, the family makes a second decision to either defibrillate or to allow the patient to die. At this point the physician is the team leader and must direct the care of the patient based on the wishes of the family.
9. Instructor’s Notes
   1. Tips to keep the scenario moving
      1. The initial part of the scenario is testing teamwork skills and CRM. The medical care at this point is straightforward ACLS. If egregious errors are made in the ACLS care, other team members will be responsible for suggestion of the appropriate care.
      2. Emphasis is placed on role assignment and utilization of all members of the team. If this is not done efficiently, delays in things like starting the IV or ventilating the patient will become apparent.
      3. While there are no specific stimuli besides the cardiac monitor and vital signs, EKG and labs might be ordered. If this is the case, the patient should go into a second round of ventricular fibrillation prior to these being available.
      4. The second part of the scenario involves actors portraying upset family members. The family enters just after the patient has been intubated. The actors should be trained to recognize empathy, clinical explanation and inclusion of them in the care of their parent. If the team is successful in this, then the actors will relax and express understanding of the care team’s actions in the face of the patient’s unknown code status. If the care team becomes defensive and fails to recognize and acknowledge the family members’ concerns, the family members become more irate and are unable to come to a decision on whether to continue the resuscitation or not. The goal of the scenario should be the use of SPE and conflict resolution in dealing with a difficult decision with upset family members. The ultimate decision that the family members make can be flexible in regards to the teaching institution’s policy on DNR orders and medical power of decision.
10. Debriefing Plan-
    1. Debriefing begins with a discussion of why the concepts of CRM and the patient experience are important in modern healthcare. Note that this background discussion can take place before the simulated case.
    2. CRM Principles
       1. CRM provides a team structure that can block, trap and mitigate errors.
    3. Group discussion using video of the scenario.
       1. Debriefing is done as a group with review of the video of the clinical scenario. The focus of the debriefing should be on the use of **CRM principles, Conflict Resolution** and family interactionsusing the **Structured Patient Encounter.**
    4. CRM principles- The instructors and participants should be focused on the following actions:
       1. Establish the team leader
       2. Clarify roles
       3. Assign tasks by name
       4. Call-outs and check backs when orders are ordered and performed
       5. Situational Awareness by all members of staff- recognition of changing patient interaction.
       6. Decision Making- Leaders guide care (defibrillation, intubation) but other members of team are involved (starting IVs without specific orders).
       7. Flexibility of decision making- ability of all members of the team to change clinical path during resuscitation after family arrives
    5. Conflict resolution: This case presents the team with an emotionally-charged conflict. We teach a five step approach to Conflict Resolution:
       1. Create a calm environment
       2. Acknowledge and validate other’s feelings
       3. Focus on the problem-not the person
       4. Present options for proceeding
       5. Gain agreement to proceed with the selected option
    6. Debriefing is performed with handouts detailing the above information on CRM, Conflict Resolution and the Structured Patient Encounter
