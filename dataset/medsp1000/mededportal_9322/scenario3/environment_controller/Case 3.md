1. Case 3: Rapid atrial fibrillation with anxious and educated family member

4. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up: Emergency department room
   2. Manikin Set Up: The manikin will be brought in on a stretcher, in a gown.
   3. Props and Drugs: IV start kits, IVs, Fluid, endotracheal intubation kit, defibrillator, monitor, ACLS drugs and intubation drugs (sedation and paralytics). Initial EKG showing normal sinus rhythm, repeat EKG showing rapid atrial fibrillation

5. Actors
   1. Roles: Physician, Nurses (2-4), Family member.
   2. Who May Play Them: Medical staff are played by themselves. A trained actor plays the family member.
   3. Action Role: The medical staff are involved in the medical care of the patient, the family member is at the bedside and is involved in the care of his/her mother.

6. Case Narrative
   1. Scenario background: The patient and her family member are in an emergency department room. The patient is sitting up on the stretcher and the family member is standing at the bedside.
   2. Initial scenario conditions
      1. HPI: The patient reluctantly acknowledges that she passed out twice that day while seated, reporting a prodrome of lightheadedness. There was no trauma. The son who witnessed these episodes reports that he took his mother’s pulse at that time and found it to be rapid, irregular and thready. No trauma or chest pain is reported.
      2. PMH: HTN
      3. Allergies: Penicillin
      4. Meds: Hydrochlorothiazide
   3. Scenario play and branch points
      1. During the initial history and physical the son is anxious and is requesting to speak to a cardiologist. He mentions that he is concerned about atrial fibrillation. The son is an ICU nurse at another hospital; this is unbeknownst to the team unless the son is specifically asked about his background.
      2. The EKG returns first showing normal sinus without ischemia. If this is not explained to the son, he becomes more frustrated and overbearing.
      3. Despite this explanation, the son again requests to see a cardiologist.
      4. Amidst the initial evaluation, the cardiac monitor shows a rhythm change to rapid atrial fibrillation. The patient becomes lightheaded. The blood pressure is now 75/40.
      5. In our setting, a midlevel participates in the initial sequence and then, upon this change in clinical status, transfers care to a physician when the patient decompensates. During this change in condition, the case facilitator changes the initial team’s focus to a manikin in the adjacent trauma bay, saying, “This is now your patient,” as the actor leaves the area.
      6. The son can either become more vocally concerned or reassured depending on how the staff interacts with him during this transition.
      7. If the decision to proceed with cardioversion is made the son agrees with the plan.
      8. If the decision is to intubate, the son protests and recommends cardioversion first. If intubation is still performed without RSI, the patient is very uncomfortable with the endotracheal tube. The RSI causes an additional drop in blood pressure to 65/38 until cardioversion is performed.
      9. If sedation is not performed the son gets upset and the patient is upset.
      10. Cardioversion at 50-100 J, synchronized to the cardiac rate restores a sinus rhythm on the monitor, the patient’s blood pressure improves to normal and symptoms improve.
      11. Closure of the case: informing patient and family of the plan and cardiology consultation.

7. Instructor’s Notes
   1. The actor playing the family member should respond to the staff reassurances and comfort in managing the patient. If the staff communicates well and displays the SPE principles, he is reassured. If the son feels defensiveness or senses a fight for control, he becomes more insistent.
   2. During episode of rapid atrial fibrillation, the team will be required to recognize and respond quickly to the changing scenario.
   3. Different management techniques may be employed.
   4. If medical management is tried, the patient will deteriorate further until electrical cardioversion is performed.
   5. Other pharmacological cardioversion medications are unsuccessful and the patient’s family member becomes upset and requests electrical cardioversion.
