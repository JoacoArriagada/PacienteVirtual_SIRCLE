1. Case 3: Rapid atrial fibrillation with anxious and educated family member
2. Target Audience: attending physicians, residents, midlevel providers nurses and medical technicians
3. Goals and Learning Objectives
   1. Goal- The team of emergency department staff (physicians, nurses, techs and other support staff) will evaluate a patient with two episodes of syncope prior to arrival. The patient’s son is present and is an ICU nurse at another hospital; this is unbeknownst to the team unless the son is specifically asked about his background. The son requests that his mother be seen immediately by a cardiologist. During the provider’s evaluation, the patient goes into rapid atrial fibrillation and becomes lightheaded and hypotensive. Unless the son is managed by a team member, he becomes highly distracting to the provider leading the medical management.
   2. Primary Learning Objectives
      1. Improvement of the patient experience, using the principles in the Structured Patient Encounter (SPE)
      2. Teamwork skills demonstrating Crew Resource Management (CRM)
      3. Transition of care using SBAR (Situation/Background/Assessment/Recommendation)
      4. ACLS management of rapid atrial fibrillation
         1. Recognition of atrial fibrillation with unstable vital signs
         2. Cardioversion
         3. Sedation prior to cardioversion
   3. Assessment Objectives
      1. Interaction with an anxious and educated family member. The team is assessed on their use of the SPE skill set outlined below in the Debriefing section.
      2. Teamwork Skills using CRM
      3. Management of unstable, rapid atrial fibrillation
         1. Recognition of atrial fibrillation
         2. IV fluids, cardiac monitoring, oxygen
         3. Cardioversion at correct voltage and synchronized
         4. Cardiology consultation
4. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up: Emergency department room
   2. Manikin Set Up: The manikin will be brought in on a stretcher, in a gown.
   3. Props and Drugs: IV start kits, IVs, Fluid, endotracheal intubation kit, defibrillator, monitor, ACLS drugs and intubation drugs (sedation and paralytics). Initial EKG showing normal sinus rhythm, repeat EKG showing rapid atrial fibrillation
5. Actors
   1. Roles: Physician, Nurses (2-4), Family member.
   2. Who May Play Them: Medical staff are played by themselves. A trained actor plays the family member.
   3. Action Role: The medical staff are involved in the medical care of the patient, the family member is at the bedside and is involved in the care of his/her mother.
6. Case Narrative
   1. Scenario background: The patient and her family member are in an emergency department room. The patient is sitting up on the stretcher and the family member is standing at the bedside.
   2. Initial scenario conditions
      1. HPI: The patient reluctantly acknowledges that she passed out twice that day while seated, reporting a prodrome of lightheadedness. There was no trauma. The son who witnessed these episodes reports that he took his mother’s pulse at that time and found it to be rapid, irregular and thready. No trauma or chest pain is reported.
      2. PMH: HTN
      3. Allergies: Penicillin
      4. Meds: Hydrochlorothiazide
   3. Scenario play and branch points
      1. During the initial history and physical the son is anxious and is requesting to speak to a cardiologist. He mentions that he is concerned about atrial fibrillation. The son should continue with medically appropriate questions and suggestions until his medical background is ascertained.
         1. Staff members should be focused on not only the initial history, but acknowledging the son’s concerns, explaining the process, and assuring him that cardiology will be involved but the ED staff must initiate her care.
         2. Staff should also focus on non-verbal communication skills.
      2. Initial work up should include IV, cardiac monitor, labs sent off, and an EKG.
         1. The EKG returns first showing normal sinus without ischemia, and this should be explained to the son. If this is not explained, the son becomes more frustrated and overbearing.
         2. Despite this explanation, the son again requests to see a cardiologist.
      3. Amidst the initial evaluation, the cardiac monitor shows a rhythm change to rapid atrial fibrillation. The patient becomes lightheaded. The blood pressure is now 75/40.
      4. The medical team should recognize this change of rhythm and quickly proceed with IV fluids, oxygen, and preparation for cardioversion.
      5. In our setting, a midlevel participates in the initial sequence and then, upon this change in clinical status, transfers care to a physician when the patient decompensates. During this change in condition, the case facilitator changes the initial team’s focus to a manikin in the adjacent trauma bay, saying, “This is now your patient,” as the actor leaves the area. At this point, the initial team is evaluated on the use of SBAR to provide a succinct, efficient and safe transition to the physician and critical care team ready to assume care.
      6. The son can either become more vocally concerned or reassured depending on how the staff interacts with him during this transition.
         1. If the decision to proceed with cardioversion is made the son agrees with the plan.
         2. If the decision is to intubate, the son protests and recommends cardioversion first. If intubation is still performed, the care team must use RSI or else the patient is very uncomfortable with the endotracheal tube. The RSI causes an additional drop in blood pressure to 65/38 until cardioversion is performed.
      7. Cardioversion
         1. Ensure adequate sedation with drug that does not cause hypotension.
         2. If sedation is not performed the son gets upset and the patient is upset.
      8. Cardioversion at 50-100 J, synchronized to the cardiac rate restores a sinus rhythm on the monitor, the patient’s blood pressure improves to normal and symptoms improve.
      9. Closure of the case
         1. Informing patient and family of the plan
         2. Aspirin 325 mg
         3. Cardiology consultation
7. Instructor’s Notes
   1. Tips to keep the scenario moving
      1. The actor playing the family member should respond to the staff reassurances and comfort in managing the patient. If the staff communicates well and displays the SPE principles, he is reassured. If the son feels defensiveness or senses a fight for control, he becomes more insistent.
      2. During episode of rapid atrial fibrillation, the team will be required to recognize and respond quickly to the changing scenario. Focus on team communication is the crux of this part of the case. Different management techniques may be employed.
      3. If medical management is tried, the patient will deteriorate further until electrical cardioversion is performed. Likewise other pharmacological cardioversion medications are unsuccessful and the patient’s family member becomes upset and requests electrical cardioversion.
8. Debriefing Plan-
   1. Debriefing is begun with a discussion of why the concepts of CRM and the patient experience are important in modern healthcare.
   2. CRM Principles
      1. CRM provides a team structure that can block, trap and mitigate errors.
   3. The Patient Experience
      1. Excellence in healthcare is no longer defined merely by the quality of clinical care offered, but also by the superiority of service provided to those who seek care.
   4. Group discussion using video of the scenario.
      1. Debriefing is done as a group with review of the video of the clinical scenario. The focus of the debriefing should be on the use of **CRM principles**, **Conflict Resolution** and family interactionsusing the **Structured Patient Encounter.**
   5. CRM principles- The instructors and participants should be focused on the following actions:
      1. Establish the team leader
      2. Clarify roles
      3. Assign tasks by name
      4. Call-outs and check backs when orders are ordered and performed
      5. Situational Awareness by all members of staff- recognition of changing patient interaction.
      6. Decision Making- Leaders guide care but other members of team are involved.
      7. Flexibility of decision making
      8. Structured hand-offs using SBAR
   6. Conflict resolution: This case presents the team with intra-personal potential conflicts.
   7. The Structured Patient Encounter (SPE)- Should be carried out as a team and can be performed by any one of the staff.
