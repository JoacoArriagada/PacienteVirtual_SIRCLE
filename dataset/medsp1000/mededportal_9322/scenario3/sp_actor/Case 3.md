1. Case 3: Rapid atrial fibrillation with anxious and educated family member

5. Actors
   1. Roles: Family member
   2. Who May Play Them: A trained actor plays the family member.
   3. Action Role: The family member is at the bedside and is involved in the care of his/her mother.

6. Case Narrative
   1. Scenario background: The patient and her family member are in an emergency department room. The patient is sitting up on the stretcher and the family member is standing at the bedside.
   2. Initial scenario conditions
      1. HPI: The patient reluctantly acknowledges that she passed out twice that day while seated, reporting a prodrome of lightheadedness. There was no trauma. The son who witnessed these episodes reports that he took his mother’s pulse at that time and found it to be rapid, irregular and thready. No trauma or chest pain is reported.
      2. PMH: HTN
      3. Allergies: Penicillin
      4. Meds: Hydrochlorothiazide
   3. Scenario play and branch points
      1. During the initial history and physical the son is anxious and is requesting to speak to a cardiologist. He mentions that he is concerned about atrial fibrillation. The son is an ICU nurse at another hospital; this is unbeknownst to the team unless the son is specifically asked about his background. The son should continue with medically appropriate questions and suggestions until his medical background is ascertained.
      2. The EKG returns first showing normal sinus without ischemia, and this should be explained to the son. If this is not explained, the son becomes more frustrated and overbearing.
      3. Despite this explanation, the son again requests to see a cardiologist.
      4. The son can either become more vocally concerned or reassured depending on how the staff interacts with him during this transition.
      5. If the decision to proceed with cardioversion is made the son agrees with the plan.
      6. If the decision is to intubate, the son protests and recommends cardioversion first.
      7. If sedation is not performed the son gets upset and the patient is upset.

7. Instructor’s Notes
   1. The actor playing the family member should respond to the staff reassurances and comfort in managing the patient.
   2. If the staff communicates well and displays the SPE principles, he is reassured.
   3. If the son feels defensiveness or senses a fight for control, he becomes more insistent.
