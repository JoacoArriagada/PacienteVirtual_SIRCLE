1. Case 1: Low risk chest pain with an overbearing family member
2. Target Audience: attending physicians, residents, mid-level providers, nurses, medical technicians.
3. Goals and Learning Objectives
   1. Goal- This is a very simple case from a medical standpoint. Essentially it is a case of a middle age man with low risk chest pain. However, the patient’s wife, while clearly caring deeply for the patient, is overbearing and answers all the questions for the patient. She is also quite frustrated at the perceived delays in the process of care. Multiple members of the team encounter the patient as he and his wife move through the emergency department from triage to the treatment room. The goal of the team member is to provide care in the face of the challenging family member.
   2. Primary Learning Objectives
      1. Use the Structured Patient Encounter (SPE) in interactions with the patient and his family.
      2. Management of low risk chest pain.
   3. Assessment Objectives
      1. Utilization of the SPE
         1. Intentional introduction
            1. Smile and introduction, calling the patient by name.
            2. Personal identification and role on the team
            3. Make a specific introduction the patient’s guests, asking their name and relation to the patient.
         2. Expression of Empathy (recognize cues)
         3. Present the plan
            1. Make recommendations, not mandates
            2. Outline the proposal and why
            3. Summarize the plan
            4. Gain agreement with the plan
            5. Ask questions that that seek the patient’s approval

1. Environment
   1. Lab Set Up: Emergency department triage area and emergency department treatment room
   2. Actor set up: An actor portraying the patient and family member arrive at the emergency department (ED) triage area. The patient is initially fully clothed.
   3. Props and Drugs: IV start kits, EKG machine, cardiac monitor, EKG showing sinus without ischemia, normal lab values including troponin.

2. Actors
   1. Roles: Physician, Nurses, medical technician, patient, patient’s wife
   2. Who May Play Them: Medical staff are played by themselves. Trained actors play the patient and his wife.
   3. Action Role: The medical staff are involved in the care of the patient and addressing the concerns of the wife. The wife is overbearing and answers all the questions for her husband.

3. Case Narrative
   1. Scenario Background: The patient and his wife are sitting in an emergency department triage area. The first person to enter is an emergency department technician who gets a brief history, asks the patient to get into a gown and performs an EKG. A nurse then enters and performs an assessment, starts an IV. The physician is next to enter the room and performs a history and physical exam.
      1. Throughout these encounters the patient’s wife answers first for the patient with every question asked. Feeling insecure and worried for her spouse, she is intrusive and expresses skepticism of the medical care. The patient is more passive and never answers for himself unless the caregiver specifically directs the wife to permit the patient to answer.
   2. Initial Scenario Conditions: note all this information is from the wife who does almost all the talking to the providers.
      1. HPI: 50 year old male with chest pain that started earlier in the day, is gone now. It was left sided, sharp without radiation. His wife drove him to the hospital. There was slight nausea during the episode which lasted 30 minutes. Review of symptoms is otherwise negative.
      2. PMH: Hypertension, well controlled
      3. Meds: None
      4. Allergies: None
      5. Social: Purported non-smoker: patient admits, to wife’s amazement and dismay, that he occasionally has a cigarette. Drinks socially on the weekends
      6. Physical Exam: Normal
   3. Scenario Play and Branch Points: The medicine in this case is straight forward, but the staff are faced with the challenges of dealing with a challenging family member. The staff members evaluate the patient serially starting with a tech, then a nurse and finally a physician. At each step, the staff has opportunity to use the SPE in dealing with the patient.
      1. The initial interaction is with a medical technician in the triage area. The goals of this interaction are to engage the patient and family with an intentional introduction, to show empathy for the wait and to clearly state what is the next step in the plan (the nurse will be in to do an evaluation). The tech should have the patient put on a gown, and should take an EKG. Lastly, the tech should show concern for the patient’s comfort before leaving the room.
      2. The nurse enters the room and must introduce himself/herself and obtain a more detailed history, following the same steps of the SPE.
      3. The physician then enters while the nurse is still in the room. The nurse should summarize what she has learned in an SBAR to the physician. The doctor then evaluates the patient, obtains an HPI and PE, interprets the EKG, and reviews labs that have been sent at triage. All labs are normal. The physician then proposes the plan and treatment. Critical medical actions at this point are to administer aspirin, and discuss the need for further evaluation as either an inpatient or outpatient with his PMD or a cardiologist. The wife will question the plan proposed. The physician will have to answer the concerns and negotiate the final decision with the patient and wife.

4. Instructor’s Notes
   1. Tips to keep the scenario moving
      1. This case is a series of patient and staff interactions. The actor playing the wife of the patient should always answer first and hinder the provider from getting answers directly from the patient, unless specifically directed to allow the patient to answer. Even if directed, after 1-2 more questions, the wife begins again to answer for the patient again, and continues to do so until and unless her fears and concern are addressed.
      2. The actors playing the wife and patient can play off of the staff members and their use of the principles of the SPE.
         1. For instance, if staff does not introduce themselves to the wife, she becomes more obtrusive and difficult.
         2. If the staff acknowledges the wife, and shows empathy, eventually the patient himself will add additional pieces of information of the history.
      3. When presenting the plan and gaining agreement staff should acknowledge the patient and his wife. Here again, if the principles of the SPE are utilized, the wife and the patient are more amenable to the treatment plan.

5. Debriefing Plan-
   1. Debriefing is begun with a discussion of why the patient experience is important in modern healthcare. Note that this background discussion can be done before the simulated case.
   2. The Patient Experience
      1. Excellence in healthcare is no longer defined merely by the quality of clinical care offered, but also by the superiority of service provided to those who seek care. The importance of patient satisfaction has grown over the past decade1. Hospital administrators have increased their focus on service markedly since Medicare’s requirement that hospitals publish their patient satisfaction data from Hospital Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems (HCAHPS) surveys.
      2. Low satisfaction scores are significantly associated with malpractice activity2. Physicians, nurses and other staff members can control the contribution of patient dissatisfaction to risk3. More than technical competency and perceived waiting times, the quality of the patient’s personal interactions with the care team most strongly predicts the patient’s satisfaction with the ED experience4.
   3. Group discussion using video of the scenario.
      1. Debriefing is done as a group with review of the video of the clinical scenario. The focus of the debriefing should be on the use of the **Structured Patient Encounter** to manage the patient and family interactions**.**
      2. Structured hand-offs using **SBAR** (when nurse updates physician at patient bedside)
         1. Situation- start with the punch line
         2. Background- backfill the story
         3. Assessment- your assessment of the problem
         4. Recommendation-Recommend the next step
   4. The Structured Patient Encounter (SPE)- Should be carried out as a team and can be performed by any one of the staff. We have standardized the patient interaction. This standardization improves the implementation, and is a concrete teaching principle.
      1. Introduction
         1. Introduce yourself by name
         2. Identify yourself and your role on the team
         3. Acknowledge family and friends- introduce yourself to everyone in the room
      2. Empathy during evaluation
      3. Present the plan
         1. Make clear recommendations
         2. Outline the plan and why
         3. Summarize
      4. Gain agreement with the plan
      5. Close the encounter
         1. Ask if there is anything that was not addressed
         2. Ask if there is anything that would make the patient feel more comfortable
