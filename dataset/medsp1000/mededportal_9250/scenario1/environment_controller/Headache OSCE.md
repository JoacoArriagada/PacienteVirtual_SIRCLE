Headache OSCE: A 22-year old woman presents to the emergency department with a severe headache

Headache OSCE Instructor Packet

1. You will assess the examinee’s ability to take a focused, but complete headache history from the patient.   
2. You will read aloud the physical examination findings, then ask the examinee to generate an initial differential diagnosis.   
3. You will read aloud the results of basic labs and ask the examinee what diagnostic test he or she would order first.   
4. Regardless of whether brain imaging was ordered, you will pull the head CT from the sheet protector, hand it to the examinee, and ask for an interpretation.   
5. Regardless of whether the CT was interpreted correctly, you will read aloud the correct interpretation.   
6. You will then ask the examinee if any further diagnostic test is necessary.   
7. Regardless of whether a lumbar puncture is ordered, you will remove the results from the sheet protector, hand it to the examinee, and ask for an interpretation.   
8. You will ask the examinee to correctly diagnose and defend this patient’s headache, by listing the pertinent positives.   

Read this, verbatim: “You have 7 minutes to take a complete, but focused history on this patient with headaches. You may take notes. You will be given a warning when 2 minutes remain.”

![](images/5471a0c9344ca0036868ed89ed3997cba69278ee5a3d7bdd44ab741185acc48a.jpg)

Set your stopwatch for 7 minutes. Give the examinee a warning when 2 minutes remain, and continue to the next section when the 7 minutes has elapsed. If the examinee stops before eliciting all of the information on the checklist, inform him or her that there is time remaining. The examinee must continue with the history until all of the required information has been elicited, or until 7 minutes has elapsed. Don’t give the examinee any hints about what questions to ask.

Read this, verbatim: “You examine the patient. There may be slight nuchal rigidity, but you are not certain." She is in extreme pain and very sensitive to the light. Therefore, you cannot visualize her fundi or reliably test her visual fields. Otherwise, the complete neurological examination is normal and non-focal. You have 90 seconds to think about the differential diagnosis. You will then state the 4 most likely diagnoses, from most likely to least likely. Don’t list the pertinent positives and negatives at this time.” Don’t give any hints.

Set your timer for 2 minutes. After 90 seconds, the examinee must start listing the diagnoses. This section must be completed within 2 minutes.

Read this, verbatim: “Basic labs were already ordered, including CBC, platelets, chem 7, PT, PTT, and INR. All were normal. Urine pregnancy test was also negative. What is the first test you would order on this patient?” The examinee must respond immediately. If the examinee hesitates, prompt him or her for a response.

Regardless of what the examinee requested, pull the head CT (examinee version) from the sheet protector and hand it to the examinee. Read this. $" \mathsf { A }$ head CT was performed on this patient.”

Read this, verbatim: “Please interpret this image. You may think aloud, and then state your final interpretation, and whether the study is normal or abnormal.”

![](images/58f76b95c4ea5e5bf4c77464f961b3cfd6b951c31961e7431ff734de6903e54c.jpg)

Set your stopwatch for 1 minute. This section must be completed within that time.

![](images/3dba65b97a65408655416acfcce723e42542fbbb93f064e243eac68f0ff07a40.jpg)

Regardless of the accuracy of the examinee’s interpretation, read this, verbatim: “The head CT was read by the neuro-radiologist as normal.”

Read this, verbatim: “Is there any other diagnostic test you would like to order at this time, or is the evaluation complete?”

If more brain imaging is requested (such as an angiogram, MRI, MRA, or CTA) say that all of the radiology technologists have gone home for the day. We could call them, but it could take 2 hours for them to return. This would not count as an incorrect answer. However, ask the student “Is there any other diagnostic test you would like to order at this time, or is the evaluation complete?”

Whether the examinee ordered a lumbar puncture or not, read this, verbatim: “A lumbar puncture was performed on this patient. You may think out loud, and then state your final interpretation.” Hand the lumbar puncture results to the examinee. No hints.

![](images/2c33cdde057b69d8f4622675ffdbc5e04f786eda1f62c08ad8b0f0ac8849a1bc.jpg)

Set your stopwatch for one minute, as this segment must be completed within that time period. If the examinee hesitates, prompt him or her.

Read this to the examinee, verbatim: “You may take a moment to think about your final diagnosis, including the pertinent positives. As soon as you are ready, please state the final diagnosis, and list the pertinent positives. Do not list any pertinent negatives.”

Set your stopwatch for 3 minutes. Encourage the examinee to keep listing pertinent positives, but end the session after 3 minutes. You can give hints regarding the pertinent positives. For example, “Are there any other clinical

features that support the diagnosis?” Or, “Are there any diagnostic evaluations that support the diagnosis?”

The OSCE is complete.
