# Alex Corason - Abnormal Heart Sounds – Instructor’s Guide

Resource: Alex Corason - Abnormal Heart Sounds

How to use the resource file: The resource file contains information necessary for training standardized patients, presented in a table format. It describes the case, the way used/validated, contains SP training notes and additional details.

Purpose of the resource: To enrich the sources of validated case scenarios available to standardized patient trainers and medical school faculty for objective structured clinical examinations.

# Educational Objectives:

Apply the principles of effective communication to facilitate relationship building, data gathering and patient education
 Use appropriate questioning and physical examination
 Complete a history taking with an SP and complete a heart exam on a manikin
 Determine the type of heart disease the patient is suffering
 Deliver the bad news about patient’s heart condition in a supportive manner, and answer patient’s questions
. Write a SOAP note to communicate in a written form the exam findings, differential diagnosis and plan

Conceptual background: This case was created for use with standardized patients, for teaching and/or evaluation purposes. The case is intended for teaching/examination of medical students, years 1 through 4 (depending on medical school’s type of curriculum), as well as residents and faculty development.

Practical implementation advice: This is a hybrid case, which means that heart sounds simulator will be necessary to implement the case (e.g. Harvey, Cardionics or Ventriculoscope). All original materials/patient exam room needs are described in the case, users are free to change the original case to better suit their needs, as long as due credit is applied.

Use of section “Standardizing the Unknown”: This section is intended for use when training multiple SPs for portraying the same case. Its purpose is two-prong:

(1) It is very hard to predict and standardize the answers to each and every question learners might decide to ask of the SP. Having more details to the portrait makes the character more complete, and the SPs can easier associate with and think as the patient portrayed.
(2) The details referred to in this section might be quite different for different states, cities and schools. These questions are intended for the group of SPs to decide on. This gives them the chance to partake in the creation of the portrayal, bring local detail to the role, and take ownership of the role. The SP trainers should feel free to add/delete questions in this section to make it most applicable to their local specific circumstances.

In my experience with adding this section to the SP case, it takes less than 10 minutes of allotted training time for the SP group to decide on the details, and on numerous occasions has meaningfully contributed to the case presentation. Usually, the last question related to “What else would you like to know about this person?” brings up any parts of the role that might have not been clear or well described during training.

The notion of training standardized patients in the context of a role, rather than for specific interrogative props is gaining momentum across the nation and is a considered a possibility for SP training for the USMLE Step 2 CS examination. Karen Lewis, Board of Directors Member for the Association for Standardized Patient Educators (ASPE), ASPE Publications Committee member and Director of the SP Program at George Washington University, wrote in the September 2011 issue of “ASPE News”: “In my experience, simulated activities where the SP can perform within the confines of a behavior profile almost always accomplish better learning for our students, clearer educator understanding of what they have learned, and more informed ideas about the next steps.”

Recommended length of teaching session: 1 hour 15 minutes: The students have 10 minutes with the patient, next go out to examine a manikin with pre-set heart [and lung] sounds (10 minutes) and return to the room to deliver the news to the patient, present a closing summary and discuss future plans (5 minutes). When the patient encounter is complete, the students have 10 minutes for patient note, and a 30-min debriefing with faculty-observer (for teaching purposes only). Additional 10 minutes are factored in for transitioning between task locations.

Case has been successfully implemented with students at Paul Foster SOM.
