**Instructor’s Notes: DVT, likely missed pulmonary embolism**

**Explanation of resources**

Instructors are encouraged to review the goals and objectives (see below), case narrative with teaching points, and the resident performance evaluation prior to the start of the case. When ready to begin the case, the triage note is provided.

Once the case is initiated, the instructor only needs to follow the provided script closely to provide history, physical exam, and guidance through the case, as the vital signs, pulse oximetry, and physical findings have no substantial changes throughout the case.

The test results for the case get provided only as the learner orders the information or tests. An EKG, CXR, individual laboratory results, and imaging studies (chest x-ray, ultrasound reports, and VQ scans) are provided during the case only if ordered as the case progresses. The medical record from the previous visit is also available if the learner voices the desire to review the records or makes such an order.

At the conclusion of the case, the instructor may wish to evaluate the learner’s performance with the provided resident performance evaluation. The case narrative file also contains the debriefing materials for the case. In general, the learner self-assesses first, and suggested questions to guide the debriefing and teaching points are provided. The major teaching points for the case are also provided, and may be the basis for further discussion.

**Learning objectives**

1. Target audience: 4th year medical students; PGY 1 to PGY 2 Emergency Medicine residents
2. Learning objectives
3. Primary
   1. Obtain history and physical exam for a patient with leg pain persisting since a previous medical evaluation
   2. Utilize previous medical records in medical decision-making process in a return visit for a persisting complaint.
   3. Determine the need for repeat imaging to evaluate for deep vein thrombosis
   4. Determine the management strategies, rationale, and risk:benefit for anticoagulation in patients with deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism
   5. Initiate treatment for deep vein thrombosis and likely pulmonary embolism
4. Secondary
   1. Determine risk:benefit of testing choices (ie, D dimer, ultrasound and CT scanning)
   2. Communicate course of action to patient
   3. Discuss with other providers testing choices and effect on management (use of VQ scan when ultrasound already demonstrates DVT)
5. Critical actions checklist
   1. Obtain necessary history.
      1. Risk factors for arterial disease, DVT, compartment syndrome
      2. Medications and past medical history
      3. Previous evaluation and interventions for the problem
      4. Allergies
   2. Obtain a focused physical exam
      1. Vital signs
      2. Auscultate lungs, heart
      3. Presence of pulses, swelling, and tenderness in the extremities
   3. Review previous emergency department and chest pain unit evaluation
   4. Pain management
   5. Obtain repeat duplex Doppler ultrasound of the left lower extremity
   6. Communicate results to patient and course of action.
   7. Initiate anticoagulation, given suspicion of missed pulmonary embolism with a new finding of a calf deep vein thrombosis
   8. Disposition that addresses patient preferences, follow-up needs (in this case, will need admission)
   9. Discuss with admitting physician who requests additional testing

Background

This simulation is the re-creation of an emergency department case for a patient with a return visit to the emergency department. The complaint of leg pain may have seemed somewhat unrelated to the initial visit, which was for chest pain that the patient assumed was from anxiety. The patient had a work up for the chest pain which included a cardiac evaluation and stress test. In the course of that evaluation, she had developed leg pain, prompting an investigation for a deep vein thrombosis, which was negative.

She returned with continued leg pain. A repeat ultrasound demonstrated a peroneal vein deep vein thrombosis. Though a treatment option for asymptomatic, isolated deep vein thrombosis may include observation and repeat ultrasound imaging, this patient had a marked suspicion of having a missed pulmonary embolism, prompting anticoagulation. The case was developed to discuss both the management of deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism as well as when previous evaluation is sufficient in eliminating significant pathology.

**Tips to keep scenario flowing in the lab**

1. The scenario requires an H&P and an assessment of previous medical records to determine what diagnoses have been reliably eliminated, and a risk:benefit analysis of either repeat or additional testing. As this case relies on clinical reasoning, and no realistic expectation of acute deterioration, no alternate course needs to be developed.
2. The case is static with regard to vital signs and physical findings, and focused more on communications with both patient and other providers.
3. If the learner evaluates the patient and feels no other testing is needed, the only other prompt is the patient saying, “So why do I have this pain?” If still no further evaluation is done, the patient should only say, “So do I get to go home now?”
4. If the learner evaluates the patient and orders a group of tests, a nursing staff member may ask, “Wasn’t she just here, and had all that stuff already done?”
5. The case is concluded if discharged with no other prompts or staff interventions.
