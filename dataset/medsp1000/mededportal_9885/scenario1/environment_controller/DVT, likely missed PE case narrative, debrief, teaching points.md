Chief Complaint "Leg pain"

# History of Present Illness

A 54 year old woman presents to the emergency department with left leg pain. The pain was first noted $^ { 2 \% }$ weeks ago when she had come to the emergency department with chest pain and shortness of breath that she thought was from stress. She had come in then because the episodes of chest pain occurred multiple times. She was initially evaluated in the emergency department, and then moved to the chest pain center for repeat cardiac enzymes and a stress test. While in the chest pain center, she had noted some left leg pain. She states that they did a stress test and an ultrasound of the left leg which didn’t show anything, and was discharged with the advice to follow up with her Rheumatologist.

She is able to recall having both a stress test and an ultrasound at the time of that visit $^ { 2 \% }$ weeks ago, and had been advised to follow up with her Rheumatologist. Review of records from that visit show:

1. She was ruled out for myocardial infarction with serial cardiac enzymes. 2. The stress test was delayed in order to have a lower extremity ultrasound, which was negative for the presence of a deep vein thrombosis (DVT). 3. She had a two day nuclear stress test that was negative for cardiac ischemia.

The pain in the left leg is located from the foot to the back of the calf, and is described as burning. She had not had any injury or strain, and no increase in her usual activities. She had been treated with pain medicines, and did follow-up with her rheumatologist as recommended, who “injected something into my left knee.” The pain seemed better for a few days, but had intensified again. The pain is increased by weight bearing, and now she has to limp to walk. There is no numbness or tingling to the leg. She has no other symptoms, including no return of the chest pain that she had previously experienced, no shortness of breath, no abdominal pain, no vomiting, no fever, no redness, and no muscle weakness.
