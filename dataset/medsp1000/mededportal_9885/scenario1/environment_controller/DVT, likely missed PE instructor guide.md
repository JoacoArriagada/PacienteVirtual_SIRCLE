Once the case is initiated, the instructor only needs to follow the provided script closely to provide history, physical exam, and guidance through the case, as the vital signs, pulse oximetry, and physical findings have no substantial changes throughout the case.

The test results for the case get provided only as the learner orders the information or tests. An EKG, CXR, individual laboratory results, and imaging studies (chest x-ray, ultrasound reports, and VQ scans) are provided during the case only if ordered as the case progresses. Background

This simulation is the re-creation of an emergency department case for a patient with a return visit to the emergency department. The complaint of leg pain may have seemed somewhat unrelated to the initial visit, which was for chest pain that the patient assumed was from anxiety. The patient had a work up for the chest pain which included a cardiac evaluation and stress test. In the course of that evaluation, she had developed leg pain, prompting an investigation for a deep vein thrombosis, which was negative.

She returned with continued leg pain. A repeat ultrasound demonstrated a peroneal vein deep vein thrombosis. Though a treatment option for asymptomatic, isolated deep vein thrombosis may include observation and repeat ultrasound imaging, this patient had a marked suspicion of having a missed pulmonary embolism, prompting anticoagulation.

**Tips to keep scenario flowing in the lab**

1. The scenario requires an H&P and an assessment of previous medical records to determine what diagnoses have been reliably eliminated, and a risk:benefit analysis of either repeat or additional testing. As this case relies on clinical reasoning, and no realistic expectation of acute deterioration, no alternate course needs to be developed.
2. The case is static with regard to vital signs and physical findings, and focused more on communications with both patient and other providers.
3. If the learner evaluates the patient and feels no other testing is needed, the only other prompt is the patient saying, “So why do I have this pain?” If still no further evaluation is done, the patient should only say, “So do I get to go home now?”
4. If the learner evaluates the patient and orders a group of tests, a nursing staff member may ask, “Wasn’t she just here, and had all that stuff already done?”
5. The case is concluded if discharged with no other prompts or staff interventions.
6. The file for initiating the case is provided. There should be no other needed programming.
