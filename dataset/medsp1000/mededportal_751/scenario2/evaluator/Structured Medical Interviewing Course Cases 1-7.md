<table><tr><td><p><strong>Patient Name</strong></p></td><td><p>Michelle Holly</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Gender/Race/Age (age range)</strong></p></td><td><p>Female, 27 years old</p><p>* <strong>Attention to genetic prevalence of Down’s/MD in specific racial groups – Specify for SPs used.</strong></p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Presenting Situation</strong></p></td><td><p>Ms. Holly, age 27, presents to the clinic today for her first prenatal visit, her husband is unable to join her due to a work conflict.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Opening Statement</strong></p></td><td><p>“I want to make sure that I have nothing about this pregnancy to be worried about”</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Elaboration of Complaint - Concerns</strong></p></td><td><ul><li>You want to believe that you have nothing to worry about.</li></ul></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Clinical Content*</strong></p></td><td><p>Present condition: </p><ul><li>Your last period was 8 weeks ago.  </li><li>You are in good overall physical health.</li></ul><p>Pertinent Past Medical History:</p><ul><li>This is your first pregnancy.</li><li>You had been on taking oral contraceptive pills for the last few years but switched to condoms before your wedding due to some concerns about weight gain.  Your periods have been normal and regular up until this pregnancy.</li><li>You do not smoke.  You do drink alcohol – 6 beers on the weekend but have made a conscious effort to not consume any alcohol since you found out you were pregnant. .  You do not use any other drugs and take no medications.  You do not exercise on a regular basis.</li><li>You regularly drink 3 – 4 caffeinated beverages (Soda) a day. </li></ul><p>Family Medical History:</p><ul><li>Your parents are both alive and well, living in Texas.</li><li>You are an only child</li></ul><p>Genetic History:</p><ul><li>Your mother had one brother who died in adolescence- you don’t know much about him. (He might have had muscular dystrophy but no one ever talks about it.)</li><li>Your 1<sup>st</sup> cousin (your mother’s sister’s child) has muscular dystrophy. He is now 12 years old and in confined to a wheelchair. He has been in the hospital several times in the past year and almost died from pneumonia. You know that your aunt and uncle agonized for months about whether he should be on a ventilator next time or be allowed to die. </li><li><em>You don’t think this is a problem for you because it’s a cousin – besides MD is a disease that only boys get and your husband is FINE</em>. <em>– </em>Your husband has a cousin with Downs’ Syndrome (his mother’s side). However when you learn during the interview that your child might be affected you become upset, because your cousin has suffered a lot. </li></ul></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Physical Exam</strong></p></td><td><p>No physical exam will be performed.  </p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Psychosocial Profile</strong></p></td><td><p>You have only been working as administrative assistant for a local insurance agency for 14 months.  You are concerned that you do not have enough Sick Leave / Vacation Time built up to enjoy a paid maternity absence.  You may have to take 2 weeks unpaid.  Your husband works long hours in the hotel industry. You and your husband live is a small apartment in West Allis.  Both of you were looking forward to building enough savings to make a down-payment on a home in Brookfield, closer to your husband’s work.  This baby will put a wrench in all of your well-laid future plans.  </p><p>Your husband has expressed his concerns about the baby being placed in a daycare facility.  He really wants you to be a stay-at-home mom.  You believe that unless he makes manager soon, this will not be a option for your family financially.  Besides, you think you would go stir-crazy at home alone with a newborn.   </p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Scenario Development</strong></p></td><td><p>You should be sitting comfortably in a chair when the student(s) starts the scenario.  In general, let the student(s) set the pace and scope of the interview.  You are cooperative and forthcoming to all questions.  You came here today really needing to hear “everything is just fine” so you become anxious and/or defensive about hearing that you do have some genetic risk. </p><p>If the scenario/ communication should come to a stall you can use one of the following questions to  “jump start” the conversation:</p><ul><li>Am I supposed to have morning sickness by now?</li><li>You don’t really need to know about all my cousins and everything do you?  <strong>OR </strong> My baby couldn’t inherit something from my cousin, so why are you asking me all this?</li><li>Does Downs’ syndrome run in families?  <strong>OR </strong> I don’t have to worry about Downs’ since I’m under 35 right?</li></ul></td></tr></table>
