<table><tr><td><p><strong>Patient Name</strong></p></td><td><p>Rhonda Richards</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Presenting Situation</strong></p></td><td><p>Mrs. Richards, presents to the clinic today for her annual exam.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Opening Statement</strong></p></td><td><p>“I am just here for my check- up”</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Elaboration of Complaint - Concerns</strong></p></td><td><ul><li>This is a new doctor for you – your insurance changed. A few weeks ago you attended a health fair at a church in your neighborhood. Someone there measured your blood pressure, told you it was high and recommended you see your doctor. Someone else had a little machine that measured cholesterol in a drop of blood from your finger- they said that was high too. </li><li>You feel generally well. Sometimes you have pounding headaches – these have been more frequently lately. You’ve always thought “hypertension” meant being very tense and you don’t think you’re tense at all. Your mother died 2 years ago after having a stroke at age 69.  She had struggled for years with her diabetes (eye disease). Your father died of a heart attack at age 66 (about 10 yrs ago).</li><li>You have two younger sisters with no health problems.</li></ul></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Physical Exam</strong></p></td><td><p>No physical exam will be performed.  </p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Psychosocial Profile</strong></p></td><td><p>You are a proud and private individual.  You work full time as a clerical assistant for a local M &amp; I bank for over 15 years.  Your husband Dick is a machinist for Harley Davidson.  You and your husband have worked long and hard hours to ensure that you would be able to send your two children to college.  You have a 15 year old daughter, and a 14 year old son who attend Riverside High School.</p><p>Dick’s mother lives in town and is in failing health.
