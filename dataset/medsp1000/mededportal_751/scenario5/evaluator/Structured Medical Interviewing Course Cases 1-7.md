## **Kelly Morr – Possible Pregnancy**

<table><tr><td><p><strong>Patient Name</strong></p></td><td><p>Kelly Morr</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Gender/Race/Age (age range)</strong></p></td><td><p>Female, 22</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Presenting Situation</strong></p></td><td><p>Ms. Morr, age 22, presents to the clinic today to determine why her period is three weeks late.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Opening Statement</strong></p></td><td><p>“I am worried because my period is late”</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Elaboration of Complaint - Concerns</strong></p></td><td><ul><li>You DO NOT WANT TO BE PREGNANT!</li><li>The nurse just told you your pregnancy test is positive.</li><li>If you are pregnant, you want an abortion – but you’re really conflicted about it.</li></ul></td></tr><tr><td><h2>Clinical Content*</h2></td><td><p>Present condition: </p><ul><li>Your last period was 7 weeks ago.</li><li>You have been too scared to take a home pregnancy test</li><li>You are in good overall physical health.</li></ul><p>Pertinent Past Medical History:</p><ul><li>You had been on “The Pill” since last September.  You have tried to stay faithful to taking the pill everyday but have had some “slip-ups” </li><li>You do not smoke cigarettes.  You do not drink alcohol.</li><li>You exercise on a regular basis, aerobics classes at college gym 2-3 times per week and almost daily running when weather permits.</li><li>You regularly drink 3 – 4 caffeinated beverages (Soda) a day. </li></ul><p>Family Medical History:</p><ul><li>Your parents are both alive and well, living in Evanston, Illinois.</li><li>You are an only child.</li></ul></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Physical Exam</strong></p></td><td><p>No physical exam will be performed.  </p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Psychosocial Profile</strong></p></td><td><p>You are a sophomore at Mount Mary College.  You are a straight-A student with ambitions of graduating with a 4-yr Nursing Degree.  You live in an apartment (which your parents pay for) off campus.  You are active in a Jesuit / Catholic civil service organization and your freshman year was relatively uneventful.  You met your boyfriend, Scott,  at local park – you two shared the same “running route”. He is a liberal arts major at UW – Milwaukee.  Scott and you have been an item” since last spring – (‘I guess opposites attract’).  </p><p>You often drive home to see your parents on the weekends, but your relationship with them has been strained lately, as they disapprove of Scott.  They say they disapprove of his manners, you suspect it is his religion and ½ Hispanic ancestry that really “gets their goat”.  Or it could be that Scott is “non-religious” and his mother is Jewish.</p><p>You told Scott yesterday that your period was late.  You guess he must be in shock, he didn’t say much and simply stated that “he would go along with anything you decided” if the test came back positive.  You can’t image him being ready to settle down and help you raise a child and besides, he has no money and loads of college debt.  You love him but he has never been the guy you imagined yourself marrying.  You are sure your parents would insist that you marry him if you are pregnant.  At this point, you feel you simply have no choice but abortion - even in the face of shame and disapproval – if you are indeed pregnant.  Your parents would be furious, and you fear they will “ruin your future” (ie. Cut off your financial support and “force you” to move back home.) Besides, your plans for your own future do not include caring for a baby while going to school.</p><p>The nurse has just told you the pregnancy test is positive, but you don’t want to believe it.</p></td></tr><tr><td><p><strong>Scenario Development</strong></p></td><td><p>You should be sitting, shifting anxiously in a chair when the student(s) starts the scenario.  In general, let the student(s) set the pace and scope of the interview.  You are shy and fairly uncommunicative in answering the student’s questions.  You avoid significant eye contact. You are somewhat embarrassed to be here at all (you feel you should have known better) and are very afraid of the possible outcome (pregnancy).  </p><p>If the scenario/ communication should come to a stall (more than a necessary).  You can use one of the following questions to  “jump start” the conversation:</p><ul><li>Is there something you can give me that will cause me to miscarry?</li><li>What is the latest that I can get an abortion?  What time frame do I have to make my decision?</li><li>Do you do abortions here or will I have to go somewhere else?  How much is this going to cost?</li><li>(to a woman student) What would you do if you were in my situation?</li></ul></td></tr></table>
