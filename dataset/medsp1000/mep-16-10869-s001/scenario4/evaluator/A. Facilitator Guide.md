**Session Learning Objectives**

**By the end of the in-class session, the student will be able to:**

1. Integrate physical exam findings and presenting features to propose a differential diagnosis.
2. Analyze a family history and create a pedigree to identify genetic transmission patterns and familial risks.
3. Formulate a clinical plan involving genetic testing to refine the differential diagnosis.
4. Translate the clinical findings into patient-centered language to communicate genetic diagnoses, testing options, and patient/family support resources.
5. Synthesize the findings as would be presented when seeking a genetics consultation.
6. Initiate the partnership between physicians and genetic counselors.

**Case 2B. A pregnant woman presents at 17 weeks gestation with a family history significant for a maternal uncle who is in a wheelchair (in home country) who passed in his late teens/early 20s and local maternal aunt with cardiomyopathy who is known to have an elevated creatine kinase (CK)****. No one else in the family is known to be in a wheelchair or have known cardiomyopathy, but the patient’s maternal grandmother seems to have had progressive fatigue before dying unexpectedly. Prenatal ultrasound shows likely female fetus, but legs remain crossed during ultrasound. Differential includes Duchenne muscular dystrophy.**

*To help the students analyze the case above, consider the following questions and leading statements. Information in italics is provided for facilitator use only.*

* Where can you find information about the common features of Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD)? Which features in this case are consistent with DMD?
  + *Lead the students to OMIM.org clinical synopses and GeneReviews.org.*
  + *Note pattern of a male in a wheelchair who died young, his sister with cardiomyopathy and elevated CK and their mother with progressive fatigue and unknown cause of death.*
* If this family is affected with DMD, what risks might exist for other members of the family?  What additional information would you wish to gather?
  + *Discuss the X-linked inheritance pattern of DMD as well as the high chance of a sporadic mutations in the DMD gene (possibly in the maternal grandmother in this case).*
  + *Discuss gathering a family history, focused not only on males with severe muscular dystrophy, but women with fatigue/decreased exercise endurance, cardiomyopathy, and unexplained use of a wheelchair in distant relatives.*

● Would you offer genetic testing to the family? Which relatives are you most concerned about?  Why?

* *The pregnant patient is likely coming solely to see a reassuring ultrasound of her fetus. The idea of genetic testing for something she has not even considered as a risk to herself or her fetus is likely to be overwhelming.*
* *If the patient is not a carrier of DMD, we would not be concerned for her fetus.*
* *If she is a carrier, we would be concerned for her heart during pregnancy.*
* *She is still only 17 weeks pregnant and therefore the option of testing her and then the fetus is still feasible if she would like to use this information for the current pregnancy.*

●      What type of genetic testing would you use to determine if there is DMD in the family? Who would you test first? What could be used as a screening test if the pregnant patient is particularly anxious at the time of the visit?

* + *50-65% of DMD is due to deletions and duplications of the DMD gene. Other cases are due to point mutations which can be detected by sequencing. In this case, we are not entirely sure what the diagnosis is. If possible, we would try to test the patient’s aunt first, as she has symptoms and is in the country.*
  + *A creatine kinase (CK) level, if significantly elevated, can help get a quicker sense of risk for the patient if very elevated. An echocardiogram may be indicated if highly suspicious for DMD carrier status, as dilated cardiomyopathy is the most serious finding in carrier women and can worsen in pregnancy*.

●      What is the chance the fetus is affected based on the family history and inheritance pattern?

* *Ask the students to discuss the risk of X-linked conditions if carried by the mother; 25% chance of being an unaffected male, 25% affected male, 25% carrier female, and 25% non-carrier female.*

●      Assign one person to play the role of the physician and one to play to role of the pregnant woman. Role play delivery of the information to the family.

* + *How are these results presented to the patient? Did she have any sense that there might be a genetic condition in the family? Or that it could affect her or her offspring?*
  + *What level of information do you introduce without any testing to confirm your suspicion?*
  + *How does this patient view the health of her relatives and their quality of life?*
  + *How do you help the mother adjust to concerns for her own health risks and sense of guilt if she passed this condition on to her fetus?*

●      Where could you find information about current management and treatment options?

Guide students to references such as

* + *GeneReviews.org*
  + *UpToDate*
  + *Pubmed*

●      What information might be available to help the family adjust to this diagnosis? How do you help the mother adjust to her own fears for her health and possible feelings of guilt?

Guide students to references such as

* *GeneticsHomeReference.org*
* *GeneticAlliance.org*
* *MDA.org*
* *dmd-guide.org*
