**Session Learning Objectives**

**By the end of the in-class session, the student will be able to:**

1. Integrate physical exam findings and presenting features to propose a differential diagnosis.
2. Analyze a family history and create a pedigree to identify genetic transmission patterns and familial risks.
3. Formulate a clinical plan involving genetic testing to refine the differential diagnosis.
4. Translate the clinical findings into patient-centered language to communicate genetic diagnoses, testing options, and patient/family support resources.
5. Synthesize the findings as would be presented when seeking a genetics consultation.
6. Initiate the partnership between physicians and genetic counselors.

**Case 3A. A 20-year-old pregnant woman presents at 23 weeks gestation for genetic counseling due to her personal history of childhood repair of interrupted aorta type B, hearing loss and mild learning issues. The genetic counselor notes that the patient is quite small and has some atypical facial features. The patient attends on her own and appears quite anxious and you note she stopped her anxiety medication for her pregnancy.**

*To help the students analyze the case above, consider the following questions and leading statements. Information in italics is provided for facilitator use only.*

-Based on the presentation described above, what is the likely diagnosis? Which of the findings (physical or social/emotional) are relevant to the differential?

* *Help the students identify key pieces of information from the case.*
* *Use interrupted aorta type B, hearing loss, learning issues, and anxiety to search for possible diagnoses.*

-What resources can you use to identify possible genetic conditions?

* + *OMIM.org, Pubmed Advance search, Google, etc.*
  + *Suggest that students open the clinical synopsis tab on OMIM.org pages to look further at the list of features and eventually to 22q11.21/Velocardiofacial syndrome/DiGeorge.*

-What further steps would you take to confirm your diagnosis?

* + *Discuss the cause of 22q11.21 syndrome- a microdeletion due to unequal crossover at meiosis due to duplicons-the genetic cause can often be quickly found at the bottom of the clinical synopsis page on the OMIM.org.*
  + *Have students look at GeneReviews.org to find the appropriate molecular testing and sensitivities.*
  + *Explain that we can detect most cases via fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH), but that some smaller deletions cannot be picked up. For this reason as well as broader testing, we often start with a microarray unless results are needed quickly.*
  + *What options are there for testing the fetus if desired? Consider concurrent FISH for the patient and fetus if pregnancy decisions might be based on results and/or fetal echo if decisions might be based on complex cardiac malformations.*

-Do you suspect this condition to be sporadic or familial, and what risks might exist for other members of the family?

* *Ask about expected inheritance pattern for 22qdel-87% sporadic, 13% inherited as AD trait.*

-Assign one person to play the role of the physician and one to be the pregnant patient; role play delivery of the information to the patient. Lead students to consider the following:

* *How might the patient react to this news in terms of the new understanding of her own issues and fetus's future?*
* *How might she feel about the chance of mental health issues? Physical issues? For herself? For the fetus?*
* *Do you think she would want to know everything at once or not?*
* *Is she thinking about other members of her family that may be affected? Does she feel alone with this diagnosis?*
* *Might she feel grief, relief, overwhelmed, guilty, or empowered?*

-What information might be available to help the patient and her family adjust to this diagnosis?

*Guide students to references such as:*

*GeneticsHomeReference.org*

*22q.org*

*Genetic Alliance*
