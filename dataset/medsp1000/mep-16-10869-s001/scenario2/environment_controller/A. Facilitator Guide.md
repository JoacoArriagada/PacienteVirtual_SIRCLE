**Case B. Duchenne muscular dystropy**

**Case 1B. A 9-year-old boy, from a family with very high expectations for their children, presents to his PCP with progressive fatigue. He is first referred for a cardiology evaluation and found to have mild dilated cardiomyopathy. He is then sent to neurology where noted to have some muscle weakness with creatine kinase (CK) in the thousands. He is then sent to genetics where concern is raised for muscular dystrophy and initial deletion/duplication testing for Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is negative. After 5 months of medical appointments, complete sequencing of the DMD gene finds a pathogenic mutation. The family is tired from their medical odyssey and is very focused on the cardiac disease as they had high hopes for him as an athlete. The mother is found to be a carrier.**

* + *Note the fatigue, dilated cardiomyopathy and muscle weakness; not all cases present classically, some present with cardiac signs before other signs are obvious.*
* + *DMD has an X-linked inheritance pattern and there is a high chance of a sporadic mutation leading to this condition.*
  + *Gather family history focused not only on males with severe muscular dystrophy, but women with fatigue/decreased exercise endurance, cardiomyopathy, and unexplained use of a wheelchair in distant relatives.*
* *The family may be in shock and not ready to talk in detail about the implications of DMD for other members of the family, especially the mother.*
* *The family could be educated about the importance of testing for immediate female family members in the near future and/or prior to pregnancy.*
* + *50-65% of DMD is due to deletions detectable on microarray. Once found to be due to a deletion, more specific testing can be done, as microarray is often more expensive, but also may be more readily available in some centers. In this case, as the mutation was found on sequencing, family testing should be specifically for the family variant.*
  + *A creatine kinase (CK) level, if significantly elevated, can help get a quicker sense of risk for other relatives.*
  + *If the mother is pregnant, an echocardiogram may be indicated, as dilated cardiomyopathy is the most serious finding in carrier women and can worsen in pregnancy and diagnostic testing would be available for the fetus.*
* *Future pregnancies have a 25% chance of being an unaffected male, 25% affected male, 25% carrier female, and 25% non-carrier female.*
