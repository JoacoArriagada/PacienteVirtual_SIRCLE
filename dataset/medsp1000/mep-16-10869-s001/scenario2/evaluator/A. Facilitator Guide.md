**Session Learning Objectives**

**By the end of the in-class session, the student will be able to:**

1. Integrate physical exam findings and presenting features to propose a differential diagnosis.
2. Analyze a family history and create a pedigree to identify genetic transmission patterns and familial risks.
3. Formulate a clinical plan involving genetic testing to refine the differential diagnosis.
4. Translate the clinical findings into patient-centered language to communicate genetic diagnoses, testing options, and patient/family support resources.
5. Synthesize the findings as would be presented when seeking a genetics consultation.
6. Initiate the partnership between physicians and genetic counselors.

**Case 1B. A 9-year-old boy, from a family with very high expectations for their children, presents to his PCP with progressive fatigue. He is first referred for a cardiology evaluation and found to have mild dilated cardiomyopathy. He is then sent to neurology where noted to have some muscle weakness with creatine kinase (CK) in the thousands. He is then sent to genetics where concern is raised for muscular dystrophy and initial deletion/duplication testing for Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is negative. After 5 months of medical appointments, complete sequencing of the DMD gene finds a pathogenic mutation. The family is tired from their medical odyssey and is very focused on the cardiac disease as they had high hopes for him as an athlete. The mother is found to be a carrier.**

*To help the students analyze the case above, consider the following questions and leading statements. Information in italics is provided for facilitator use only.*

* Where can you find information about the common features of **Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD)**? Which features in this child are consistent with DMD?
  + *Lead the students to OMIM.org clinical synopses and GeneReviews.org.*
  + *Note the fatigue, dilated cardiomyopathy and muscle weakness; not all cases present classically, some present with cardiac signs before other signs are obvious.*
* Do you suspect this condition to be sporadic or familial? What risks might exist for other members of the family?  What additional information would you wish to gather?
  + *Discuss the X-linked inheritance pattern of DMD as well as the high chance of a sporadic mutation leading to this condition.*
  + *Discuss gathering a family history, focused not only on males with severe muscular dystrophy, but women with fatigue/decreased exercise endurance, cardiomyopathy, and unexplained use of a wheelchair in distant relatives.*

● Would you offer genetic testing to the family? Which relatives are you most concerned about?  Why?

* *The family may be in shock and not ready to talk in detail about the implications of DMD for other members of the family, especially the mother.*
* *The family could be educated about the importance of testing for immediate female family members in the near future and/or prior to pregnancy.*

●      What type of genetic testing would you use for other family members?

* + *50-65% of DMD is due to deletions detectable on microarray. Once found to be due to a deletion, more specific testing can be done, as microarray is often more expensive, but also may be more readily available in some centers. In this case, as the mutation was found on sequencing, family testing should be specifically for the family variant.*
  + *A creatine kinase (CK) level, if significantly elevated, can help get a quicker sense of risk for other relatives.*
  + *If the mother is pregnant, an echocardiogram may be indicated, as dilated cardiomyopathy is the most serious finding in carrier women and can worsen in pregnancy* *and diagnostic testing would be available for the fetus.*

●      What about future pregnancies?

* *Ask the students to discuss the risk of X-linked conditions if carried by the mother. Future pregnancies have a 25% chance of being an unaffected male, 25% affected male, 25% carrier female, and 25% non-carrier female.*
  + *What if DMD was not carried by the mother per her testing? What if there is germline mosaicism?*

●      Assign one person to play the role of the physician, the father, the mother and the child. Role play delivery of the information to the family.

* + *How are these results presented to the family? Would you talk to the parents with the child present or not? For the whole discussion or part?*
  + *How much information is provided immediately? Do you need to present everything at once?*
  + *How might the family react to this news in terms of the understanding these unexpected results?*
  + *How do they feel about physical disability with shortened lifespan versus cardiac disease alone? How do you convey the natural history and need for a wheelchair in late childhood in many cases?*
  + *How did the diagnostic odyssey affect them in terms of coming to terms with this condition?*
  + *How might you help explain this unexpected diagnosis to the 9-year-old patient?*

●      Where could you find information about current management and treatment options?

Guide students to references such as

* + *GeneReviews.org*
  + *UpToDate*
  + *Pubmed*

●      What information might be available to help the family adjust to this diagnosis? How do you help the mother adjust to her own fears for her health and possible feelings of guilt?

Guide students to references such as

* *GeneticsHomeReference.org*
* *GeneticAlliance.org*
* *MDA.org*
* *dmd-guide.org*
