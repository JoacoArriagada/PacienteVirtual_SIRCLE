**Session Learning Objectives**

**By the end of the in-class session, the student will be able to:**

1. Integrate physical exam findings and presenting features to propose a differential diagnosis.
2. Analyze a family history and create a pedigree to identify genetic transmission patterns and familial risks.
3. Formulate a clinical plan involving genetic testing to refine the differential diagnosis.
4. Translate the clinical findings into patient-centered language to communicate genetic diagnoses, testing options, and patient/family support resources.
5. Synthesize the findings as would be presented when seeking a genetics consultation.
6. Initiate the partnership between physicians and genetic counselors.

**Case 4B. A very active, 3-year-old boy with mild hypotonia comes for genetics evaluation due to his diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder.  Due to what appears to be isolated autism spectrum disorder (ASD), he undergoes standard testing for genetic causes of ASD including fragile X syndrome and microarray analysis.  He is found to have a deletion of the DMD gene to the surprise of all.**

*To help the students analyze the case above, consider the following questions and leading statements. Information in italics is provided for facilitator use only.*

* Where can you find information about the common features of DMD? Which features in this child are consistent with DMD?
  + *Lead the students to OMIM.org clinical synopses and GeneReviews.org*
  + *Note the hypotonia, autism spectrum disorder, and young age at which more severe muscle signs (calf hypertrophy) may not yet be apparent.*
* Do you suspect this condition to be sporadic or familial? What risks might exist for other members of the family?  What additional information would you wish to gather?
  + *Discuss the X-linked inheritance pattern of DMD as well as the high chance of a sporadic mutation leading to this condition.*
  + *Discuss gathering a family history, focused not only on males with severe muscular dystrophy, but women with fatigue/decreased exercise endurance, cardiomyopathy, and unexplained use of a wheelchair in distant relatives.*

● Would you offer genetic testing to the family? Which relatives are you most concerned about?  Why?

* *The family may be in shock and not ready to talk in detail about the implications of DMD for other members of the family. There is no urgency for testing at this visit, unless the mother is pregnant or planning a pregnancy in the very near future.*
* *The family could be educated about their risk and the importance of testing in the next few months and/or prior to a pregnancy for this family or immediate female family members.*

●      What type of genetic testing would you use for other family members? What could be used as a screening test if the mother is particularly anxious at the time of the visit?

* + *50-65% of DMD is due to deletions/duplications detectable on microarray. Once found to be due to a deletion, more specific testing can be done, as microarray is often more expensive, but also may be more readily available in some centers.*
  + *A creatine kinase (CK) level, if significantly elevated, can help get a quicker sense of risk for the mother. If she is already pregnant, an echocardiogram may be indicated, as dilated cardiomyopathy is the most serious finding in carrier women and can worsen in pregnancy*.

●      What about future pregnancies?

* *Ask the students to discuss the risk of X-linked conditions if carried by the mother. Future pregnancies have a 25% chance of being an unaffected male, 25% affected male, 25% carrier female, and 25% non-carrier female.*
  + *What if not carried by the mother per her testing? What if there is germline mosaicism?*

●      Assign one person to play the role of the physician and one to be the mother. Role play delivery of the information to the family.

* + *How are these results presented to the family? May need to find child life to play with child so parents can process the information uninterrupted, but they may feel they want to be with their child at this moment.*
  + *How much information is provided immediately? Do you need to present everything at once?*
  + *How might the family react to this news in terms of the understanding these unexpected results? How do they feel about physical disability with shortened lifespan versus autism spectrum disorder?*

●      Where could you find information about current management and treatment options?

Guide students to references such as

* + *GeneReviews.org*
  + *UpToDate*
  + *Pubmed*

●      What information might be available to help the family adjust to this diagnosis?

Guide students to references such as

* *GeneticsHomeReference.org*
* *GeneticAlliance.org*
* *MDA.org*
* *dmd-guide.org*
