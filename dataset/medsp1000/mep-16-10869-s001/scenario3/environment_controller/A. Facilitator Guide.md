**Case A. 22q11.21 deletion syndrome**

**Case 2A. An 11-year-old boy was referred to genetics clinic from a local comprehensive cleft palate clinic. He had his cleft palate repaired early in life. He has residual hypernasal speech due to velopharyngeal insufficiency, is petite, and is on an individualized educational plan (IEP) for learning issues. He struggles in school academically and is frequently bullied. He is examined in genetics clinic where his physical exam shows growth parameters at the 5%, long face, tubular nose, squared ears, loss of nasolabial folds, and slender, tapered fingers. His mother shows a photo of his father which shows very similar features.**

* *Use cleft palate, hypernasal speech, velopharyngeal insufficiency, short stature, long face, tubular nose, squared ears, loss of nasolabial folds, and slender, tapered fingers to search for possible diagnoses.*
* + *The cause of 22q11.21 syndrome is a microdeletion due to unequal crossover at meiosis due to duplicons.*
  + *We can detect most cases via fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH), but some smaller deletions cannot be picked up. For this reason, as well as to pursue broader testing, we often start with a microarray.*
* *22qdel-87% sporadic, 13% inherited as AD trait.*
