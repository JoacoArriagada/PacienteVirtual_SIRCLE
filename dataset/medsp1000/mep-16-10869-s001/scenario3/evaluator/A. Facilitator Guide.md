**Session Learning Objectives**

**By the end of the in-class session, the student will be able to:**

1. Integrate physical exam findings and presenting features to propose a differential diagnosis.
2. Analyze a family history and create a pedigree to identify genetic transmission patterns and familial risks.
3. Formulate a clinical plan involving genetic testing to refine the differential diagnosis.
4. Translate the clinical findings into patient-centered language to communicate genetic diagnoses, testing options, and patient/family support resources.
5. Synthesize the findings as would be presented when seeking a genetics consultation.
6. Initiate the partnership between physicians and genetic counselors.

**Case 2A. An 11-year-old boy was referred to genetics clinic from a local comprehensive cleft palate clinic. He had his cleft palate repaired early in life. He has residual hypernasal speech due to velopharyngeal insufficiency, is petite, and is on an individualized educational plan (IEP) for learning issues. He struggles in school academically and is frequently bullied. He is examined in genetics clinic where his physical exam shows growth parameters at the 5%, long face, tubular nose, squared ears, loss of nasolabial folds, and slender, tapered fingers. His mother shows a photo of his father which shows very similar features.**

*To help the students analyze the case above, consider the following questions and leading statements. Information in italics is provided for facilitator use only.*

-Based on the presentation described above, what is the likely diagnosis? Which of the findings (physical or social/emotional) are relevant to the differential?

* *Help the students identify key pieces of information from the case.*
* *Use cleft palate, hypernasal speech, velopharyngeal insufficiency, short stature, long face, tubular nose, squared ears, loss of nasolabial folds, and slender, tapered fingers to search for possible diagnoses.*

-What resources can you use to identify possible genetic conditions?

* + *OMIM.org, Pubmed Advance search, Google, etc.*
  + *Suggest that students open the clinical synopsis tab on OMIM.org pages to look further at the list of features and eventually to 22q11.21/Velocaridofacial syndrome/DiGeorge.*

-What further steps would you take to confirm your diagnosis?

* + *Discuss the cause of 22q11.21 syndrome- a microdeletion due to unequal crossover at meiosis due to duplicons-the genetic cause can often be quickly found at the bottom of the clinical synopsis page on the OMIM site.*
  + *Have students look at GeneReviews.org to find the appropriate molecular testing and sensitivities.*
  + *Explain that we can detect most cases via fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH), but that some smaller deletions cannot be picked up. For this reason, as well as to pursue broader testing, we often start with a microarray.*

-Do you suspect this condition to be sporadic or familial, and what risks might exist for other members of the family?

* *Ask about expected inheritance pattern for 22qdel-87% sporadic, 13% inherited as AD trait.*
* *Ask for evidence of familial nature in this case.*

-Can you sketch a pedigree?

-Assign one person to play the role of the physician and one to be the mother; role play delivery of the information to the family and lead students to consider the following:

* *Would you have the 11 yo child stay in the room or step out? What information do you share with the child?*
* *How might the family react to this news in terms of the new understanding of the child’s issues and the late age of diagnosis?*
* *How might the child feel knowing that there is a reason for his differences in learning and speech?*
* *How might they feel about the chance of physical issues not yet diagnosed? mental health issues?*
* *Do you think the family will want to know everything at once or not?*
* *Might they feel grief, relief, overwhelmed, guilty, empowered?*

-What information might be available to help the family adjust to this diagnosis?

*Lead students to references such as:*

*GeneticsHomeReference.org*
