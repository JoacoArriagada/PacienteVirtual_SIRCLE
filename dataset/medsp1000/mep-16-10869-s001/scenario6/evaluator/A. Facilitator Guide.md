**Session Learning Objectives**

**By the end of the in-class session, the student will be able to:**

1. Integrate physical exam findings and presenting features to propose a differential diagnosis.
2. Analyze a family history and create a pedigree to identify genetic transmission patterns and familial risks.
3. Formulate a clinical plan involving genetic testing to refine the differential diagnosis.
4. Translate the clinical findings into patient-centered language to communicate genetic diagnoses, testing options, and patient/family support resources.
5. Synthesize the findings as would be presented when seeking a genetics consultation.
6. Initiate the partnership between physicians and genetic counselors.

**Case 3B. A 5-year-old girl presents to her PCP with muscle weakness and developmental delay. Her school is concerned about possible malnutrition, as she sits around, has trouble rising from the floor for which she needs to use her arms, has thin limbs and lacks energy.  She is seen by a neurologist who is concerned for possible muscular dystrophy and orders a creatine kinase (CK) level which later comes back as significantly elevated (10x normal). On the same day, she is seen in genetics clinic where she undergoes a microarray that shows approximately 12% regions of homozygosity, including much of the X chromosome. Her mother is now 19. Having 12% regions of homozygosity raises concern for consanguinity. The mother eventually reveals that the father of the child is her maternal uncle who had Duchenne muscular dystrophy and passed away early.**

*To help the students analyze the case above, consider the following questions and leading statements. Information in italics is provided for facilitator use only.*

* Where can you find information about the common features of Duchenne muscular dystrophy **(**DMD)? Which features in this case are consistent with DMD?
  + *Lead the students to OMIM.org clinical synopses and GeneReviews.org.*
  + *Note muscle weakness, developmental delay, elevated creatine kinase (CK) .*
* What risks might exist for other members of the family?  What additional information would you wish to gather?
  + *Construct a pedigree together.*
  + *Discuss the X-linked inheritance pattern of DMD and how a girl could have DMD. In this case, she has inherited an abnormal copy from her mother as well as her maternal uncle and therefore has no normal copies of the DMD gene. She is expected to have features as severe as would be seen in a boy.*

● Would you offer genetic testing to the family? Which relatives are you most concerned about?  Why?

* *The elevated CK in the little girl and her presentation raise significant concern for DMD.*
* *Her mother would be an obligate carrier and merit cardiac evaluation.*
* *The maternal grandmother(mother to the maternal uncle) would also be an obligate carrier, although the mutation could have been new to her or present as germline mosaicism.*
* *Testing for these women could be considered, but may not be necessary.*
* What type of genetic testing would you use to determine if there is DMD in the family?
  + *50-65% of DMD is due to deletions and duplications of the DMD gene. Other cases are due to point mutations which can be detected by sequencing.*
* *She was not found to have deletions/duplications of the DMD gene on microarray, so sequencing would likely be needed to prove that she has two mutations*
  + *A CK level, if significantly elevated, can help get a quicker sense of risk for the child’s mother (although we do assume she is a carrier). An echocardiogram may be indicated if highly suspicious for DMD carrier status and the mother is pregnant or planning a pregnancy, as dilated cardiomyopathy is the most serious finding in carrier women and can worsen with pregnancy*.

●      What is the chance that future pregnancies to the mother would be affected? (The initial partner has passed, so we assume her new partner is unlikely to have DMD.)

* *Ask the students to discuss the risk of X-linked conditions if carried by the mother; 25% chance of being an unaffected male, 25% affected male, 25% carrier female, and 25% non-carrier female.*
* *If the affected child were to reproduce, what would be her offspring’s chance of being affected with DMD*?

●      Assign one person to play the role of the physician and one to play to role of the mother of the affected little girl. Role play delivery of the information to the family.

* + *How are these results presented to the mother? Did she have any sense that there might be a genetic condition in the family? Or that it could affect her or her offspring?*
  + *What level of information do you introduce prior to further testing to confirm your suspicion?*
  + *How does this patient view the health of her relatives and their quality of life?*
  + *How do you help the mother adjust to concerns for her own health risks and sense of guilt that she passed this condition on to her child after an underage, consanguineous relationship with her uncle?*
* What are the ramifications of consanguinity in this family for this woman who is now 19 and had a child 5 years ago?

●      Where could you find information about current management and treatment options?

Guide students to references such as

* + *GeneReviews.org*
  + *UpToDate*
  + *Pubmed*

●      What information might be available to help the family adjust to this diagnosis? How do you help the mother adjust to her own fears for her health and possible feelings of guilt?

Guide students to references such as

* *GeneticsHomeReference.org*
* *GeneticAlliance.org*
