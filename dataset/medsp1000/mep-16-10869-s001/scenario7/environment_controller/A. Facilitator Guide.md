**Case A. 22q11.21 deletion syndrome**

**Case 4A. A 36- year-old pregnant woman presents for ultrasound at 18 weeks gestation. Her fetus is noted to have tetralogy of Fallot, cleft palate, and is small for gestational age. The father of the fetus has schizophrenia and a cardiac ventricular septal defect. He has hypernasal speech and required extra help in school. When the genetic counselor suggests the fetus' findings could be related to the fetus’ father’s condition, he denies that possibility and becomes enraged. The mother is very nervous about having a baby with a heart condition and possible psychiatric issues.**

* *Use tetralogy of Fallot, cleft palate, small for gestational age, schizophrenia, ventricular septal defect, hypernasal speech, and developmental delays to search for possible diagnoses.*
* + *The cause of 22q11.21 syndrome is a microdeletion due to unequal crossover at meiosis due to duplicons.*
  + *We can detect most cases via fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH), but some smaller deletions cannot be picked up. For this reason, as well as broader testing, we often start with a microarray unless results are needed quickly.*
  + *Testing options for the fetus if desired include concurrent FISH if pregnancy decisions might be based on results in the short term and/or fetal echo if decisions might be based on complex cardiac malformations.*
* *22qdel-87% sporadic, 13% inherited as AD trait.*
