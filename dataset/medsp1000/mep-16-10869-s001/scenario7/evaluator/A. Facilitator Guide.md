**Session Learning Objectives**

**By the end of the in-class session, the student will be able to:**

1. Integrate physical exam findings and presenting features to propose a differential diagnosis.
2. Analyze a family history and create a pedigree to identify genetic transmission patterns and familial risks.
3. Formulate a clinical plan involving genetic testing to refine the differential diagnosis.
4. Translate the clinical findings into patient-centered language to communicate genetic diagnoses, testing options, and patient/family support resources.
5. Synthesize the findings as would be presented when seeking a genetics consultation.
6. Initiate the partnership between physicians and genetic counselors.

**Case 4A. A 36- year-old pregnant woman presents for ultrasound at 18 weeks gestation. Her fetus is noted to have tetralogy of Fallot, cleft palate, and is small for gestational age. The father of the fetus has schizophrenia and a cardiac ventricular septal defect. He has hypernasal speech and required extra help in school. When the genetic counselor suggests the fetus' findings could be related to the fetus’ father’s condition, he denies that possibility and becomes enraged. The mother is very nervous about having a baby with a heart condition and possible psychiatric issues.**

*To help the students analyze the case above, consider the following questions and leading statements. Information in italics is provided for facilitator use only.*

-Based on the presentation described above, what is the likely diagnosis? Which of the findings (physical or social/emotional) are relevant to the differential?

* *Help the students identify key pieces of information from the case.*
* *Use**tetralogy of Fallot, cleft palate, small for gestational age, schizophrenia, ventricular septal defect, hypernasal speech developmental delays* *to search for possible diagnoses.*

-What resources can you use to identify possible genetic conditions?

* + *OMIM.org, Pubmed Advance search, Google, etc.*
  + *Suggest that students open the clinical synopsis tab on OMIM.org pages to look further at the list of features and eventually to 22q11.21/Velocardiofacial syndrome/DiGeorge.*

-What further steps would you take to confirm your diagnosis?

* + *Discuss the cause of 22q11.21 syndrome- a microdeletion due to unequal crossover at meiosis due to duplicons-the genetic cause can often be quickly found at the bottom of the clinical synopsis page on the OMIM site.*
  + *Have students look at GeneReviews.org to find the appropriate molecular testing and sensitivities.*
  + *Explain that we can detect most cases via fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH), but that some smaller deletions cannot be picked up. For this reason, as well as broader testing, we often start with a microarray unless results are needed quickly (FISH takes days while array takes weeks).*
  + *What options are there for testing the fetus if desired? Consider concurrent FISH if pregnancy decisions might be based on results in the short term and/or fetal echo if decisions might be based on complex cardiac malformations.*

-Do you suspect this condition to be sporadic or familial, and what risks might exist for other members of the family?

* *Ask about expected inheritance pattern for 22qdel-87% sporadic, 13% inherited as AD trait.*
* *Can you draw a pedigree?*

-Assign one person to play the role of the physician, the pregnant woman, and the father of the baby to role play delivery of the information to the couple. Lead students to consider the following:

* + *How might the woman react to this news in terms of the new understanding of her husband’s issues and fetus's future?*
  + *How might the husband react to the suggestion that he has a condition that was not diagnosed in the past and being suggested now?*
  + *How might the woman feel about the chance of mental health issues for her husband? Physical issues? For the fetus?*
  + *Do you think the couple would want to know everything at once or not?*
  + *Might the woman feel grief, overwhelmed, or empowered?*
  + *Might the husband feel relief, overwhelmed, guilty, angry?*

-What information might be available to help the couple adjust to this diagnosis?
