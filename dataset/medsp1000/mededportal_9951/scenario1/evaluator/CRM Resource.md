**Pediatric Crisis Resource Management for Medical Students**

# Faculty Handout: Simulation Case Scenario- Respiratory/Cardiac Arrest

1. Title: Pediatric Crisis Resource Management (CRM) for Medical Students
2. Target Audience: Medical Students
3. Learning Objectives:
   1. **Primary goals of the medical student CRM curriculum:**
      1. Understand the implications of effective communication during a pediatric emergency
      2. Learn and apply the elements of CRM
      3. Define specific roles of team members during a crisis
      4. Discuss the importance of effective communication
      5. Define the roles of medical students during a pediatric emergency
      6. Adopt strategies for effective communication
   2. **Secondary goals of the medical student CRM curriculum:**
      1. Detailed technical goals:
         1. Obtain proficiency in stabilizing an airway using head-tilt/ chin-lift.
         2. Obtain proficiency in bag-valve mask ventilation
      2. Behavioral goals: (Communication and Teamwork)
         1. Obtain proficiency in using closed-loop communication (speak/repeat) with team leader
      3. Didactic points:
         1. Review the principles of BLS
         2. Understand the benefit of first provider resuscitation skills
         3. Describe the importance of effective communication during an emergency.
         4. Understand the importance of care escalation
   3. Critical Actions Checklist:
      1. Critical Actions:
         1. BLS:
            1. Call for help
            2. Initiate supplemental oxygen within 30 seconds from the start of case with effective airway support
            3. Initiate bag-valve mask ventilation within 30 seconds of respiratory arrest
            4. Effective ventilation with bag-valve mask
            5. Initiate CPR within 10 seconds of pulseless arrest

7. Case Narrative:
   1. Scenario background given to participants: 3 year-old male is admitted to the hospital with cough and respiratory distress.
      1. Chief complaint: Cough and respiratory distress
      2. PMH: none
      3. Meds and allergies: NKA, takes daily vitamin
      4. Family/ social history: dad has asthma, lives with mom, dad and older sister (5) at home. No pets.
   2. Scenario conditions initially
      1. History patient gives: Mom reports 3 days of cough with fever (Tm 101) with 1 day refusal to take PO and declining urine output
      2. Patient’s initial exam
         1. VS: P 160 RR 50 SpO2 93% RA BP 80/50
         2. Patient is conscious and can speak- says he wants to go home, wants his mom
         3. **General:** Tachypneic with mild retractions
         4. **Cyanosis** at 1 minute.
         5. **Baseline status**: No IV/ no monitor, BVM in room.
         6. **Physical Exam:** Coughing with initial transient desaturations to 85%, scattered rales bilaterally with decreased breath sounds on right
         7. Patients physiology: Pneumonia
   3. Scenario branch points:
      1. Changes in patient’s condition and response to treatments:
         1. *Transition point 1* (0-1 minute) VS: P 160 RR 50 SpO2 93% RA BP 80/50
            1. Time 30s: SpO2 85% with cyanosis
            2. Airway (1-2 people)/ Breathing (1-2): airway stabilized within 30 seconds of desaturations or cyanosis, patient started on non-rebreather mask.

Patient may transiently improve to SpO2 95% if supplemental oxygen is given

If not given supplemental O2, patient progressively desaturates to 0% over 1 minute

* + - * 1. Circulation (1): person designated as pulse check
        2. Procedures (1-2)🡪 1-2 people with IV start kit/ flushes/ IO

Fluids started, labs drawn

* + - * 1. Team leader identified (1 person): assigns roles to other team members.
        2. Other:

patient must be hooked up to monitor

someone must call for additional help (call for resident, attending, or fellow)

* + - 1. Transition point 2 (1-2 minutes)
         1. Physical exam: cyanotic
         2. **Respiratory arrest at 1 min 30 sec**

VS (at 90s): P 180 RR 0 SpO2 65% BP 70/20

BVM must be initiated within 30 seconds of respiratory arrest

If BVM not initiated, patient desaturates to SPO2 0%, and progresses to cardiac arrest

**\*\*\*for less experienced learners, the scenario may be stopped at this point\*\*\***

* + - 1. Transition point 3 (2-4 minutes)
         1. VS: P 0 RR 0 (or rate of bagging) SPO2 95% (with bagging) BP 0
         2. Physical Exam: pulseless, apneic
         3. CPR must be initiated right away (within 10 seconds of pulseless arrest)

If CPR performed for >1 minute, patient achieves return of spontaneous circulation.

* + - * 1. Patient recovers and regains consciousness.
      1. Transition point 4 (5-6 minutes)
         1. Vital Signs- BP 100/65, P 120, R 22, SpO2 98%
         2. Transfer to PICU

  1. *Critical actions.* List 5 critical actions that must be completed for the proper management of the case. (see TRACS checklist as well)
* CRM:
  + Team leader assigned
    - Assigns roles
  + Effective closed-loop communication
* BLS:
  + BLS initiated within 30 seconds
  + Airway supported
  + NRB/ BVM used effectively
  + CPR initiated
  + IV access obtained, fluids started, labs drawn
  + Monitor hooked up
  1. Scenario programming
     1. Optimal management path
        1. Time 0: patient placed on monitor with supplemental O2 given
        2. Time 30s: airway stabilization and call for help
           1. If airway not stabilized/ supplemental oxygen not given, O2 saturation drops to zero followed by cardiac arrest.
        3. Time 90s: Respiratory arrest: bag-valve mask ventilation
           1. If not done, O2 saturation drops to zero followed by cardiac arrest
        4. Time 2 min: CPR initiated, epinephrine and crystalloid given
           1. If CPR not initiated, patient remains asystolic and does not recover.
        5. Time 4 min: Patient recovers and gains consciousness
        6. Time 6 min: Patient transferred to PICU

1. Debriefing Plan:
   1. Method of debriefing:
      1. Group
      2. Without video
      3. Knowledge support items: BLS algorithm, may give PALS pulseless arrest algorithm out as well.
      4. Debriefing rules:
         1. Debrief is confidential, without blame
         2. Environment is supportive, non-judgemental
      5. Questions to facilitate the debriefing:
         1. What do you think was going on with this patient?
         2. How do you think your teamwork was as a group?
         3. How could you have improved your communication with each other?
         4. What could you do differently next time?
2. Pilot testing and revisions:
   1. Numbers of participants: 137 third-year medical students participated
   2. Performance expectations, anticipated management mistakes:
      * 1. Patient not placed on monitor
        2. Care not escalated
        3. Supplemental O2 not started
        4. BVM ventilation performed incorrectly
        5. CPR performed incorrectly
        6. No closed loop communication
   3. Evaluation form for participants: Please use your home institution’s evaluation forms.
