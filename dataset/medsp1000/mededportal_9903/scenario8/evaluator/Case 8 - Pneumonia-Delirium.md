**Case: End of Life Care (Pneumonia)**

**Geriatric Content:**

**ACGME Competencies: Patient Care, Medical Knowledge, Communication**

Writer: Laird-Fick, Keilman (2006) - revised Riekse (November 2013)

CG is an 87 year old female with history of osteoarthritis, hypertension and systolic congestive heart failure who presents to the Emergency Department with a three day duration of a productive cough, worsening dyspnea, fever of 101 degrees, and confusion. She lives independently and refused to go see her primary care physician. Her son attempted to contact her earlier today to see how she was feeling but she did not answer the telephone. Her son decided to go check on her and found her to be very confused and called EMS. She has had a general decline over the past year.

Current medications include lisinopril 20 mg PO daily, hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg PO daily, naproxen sodium 440 mg PO twice daily, famotidine 20 mg PO daily, aspirin 81 mg PO daily as well as a multivitamin daily.

She lives independently after her husband died about ten years ago. She has had a general decline in cognition and is now receiving in home services, including meals on wheels. Her son who lives nearby sets up her medications on a weekly basis. She smoked one pack of cigarettes for about ten years but quit several years ago and only drinks an occasional glass of wine. She has not named a DPOA-HC but has mentioned to her son that she did “not want to ever live in a nursing home.”

**In the ED** she is appears moderately ill. She is confused and restless. Her temperature is 101.2° F, blood pressure 132/76 right arm lying, pulse 105, respirations of 30 with an oxygen saturation of 85% on room air. Her mucous membranes are dry. Cardiovascular exam includes S1S2 tachycardia but regular without murmurs. Pulmonary exam is limited as she is unable to follow directions to take deep breaths with the exam, but includes crackles at the right base and expiratory wheezing. Abdomen is soft and non-tender; no edema noted in her lower extremities.

**Labs:** WBC 15.5 with neutrophil predominance, hemoglobin 11.7, hematocrit 35.2, platelets 152,000, sodium 132, potassium 3.7, bicarb 27, BUN 35, creatinine 1.2, glucose 155, calcium 9.7.

**Chest X-ray:** Extensive infiltrate throughout right lower lobe consistent with pneumonia. Moderate sized right pleural effusion.

**Premise:** Assessment of an acutely ill older adult is complex; history must be obtained from multiple sources in order to identify multiple (often competing) reasons for acute decline in cognitive and physical functioning. Furthermore, family members are often distraught by observing their loved one “act out.”

**Ideally the fellow will:** 1) Determine the status of the patient, 2) elicit the patient’s wishes by interviewing the son (DPoA-HC), and 3) explain the delirium and its management with the DPOA-HC.

**Case Objectives:**

**PRIMARY**

1. Explain current status of the patient to the patient’s son and expected prognosis
2. Obtain wishes of the patient (who is unable to speak for herself)

**SECONDARY**

1. Address the son’s questions about his mother’s status and concerns
2. Address measures that can be taken to treat an acute illness
3. Address measures that can be taken to assure comfort care

2. Elderly female SP (patient)
