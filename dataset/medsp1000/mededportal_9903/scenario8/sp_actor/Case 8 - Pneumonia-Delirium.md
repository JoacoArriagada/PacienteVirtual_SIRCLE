| Case Name | Catherine Goldstein |
| Presenting  Situation | Mrs. G is an 87 year old widowed woman admitted to the hospital for pneumonia after her son found her at home ill-appearing and confused. |
| Psychosocial  Profile | Mrs. G has lived independently in her own home since her husband died 10 years ago. Her memory and stamina have declined over the last year, and she now receives in-home services, including Meals-on-Wheels. Her son lives nearby and checks in a few times per week.  Mrs. G’s son called Mrs. G earlier today, but she did not answer the phone, so he decided to check in on her. When he arrived at Mrs. G’s house, he noticed that she was disoriented, confused, and ill-appearing. She was wearing dirty clothing and was generally disheveled. She looked as if she hadn’t had anything to eat or to drink recently, and she had not taken her scheduled morning medications. There was a bottle of Robitussin and Tylenol on the counter. He called EMS, and in the ED Mrs. G was diagnosed with pneumonia. She is going to be admitted to the hospital. |
| Opening Statement | Mrs. G’s son, “My mom just isn’t herself. I checked in on her a couple days ago, and she had a cough - but I didn’t expect her to get this sick.” |
| History of present illness | Mrs. G   * You have always been a stubborn woman, and you always refuse help and/or assistance. * Your son “pushed” Meals-on-Wheels on you. * Your son insists on managing your medications (he fills your pill box weekly) and your finances. * Your memory is failing, but you can still recognize your son and familiar surroundings. * You need assistance with bathing (and probably dressing) – an aide comes 2 times per week. * You no longer drive. * You’ve had a cough for 3 days, with no improvement using Robitussin or Tylenol. * In the hospital you are very lethargic, and when staff disturbs you, you awaken confused and restless.   + You know your name, but are otherwise disoriented. * You have never filled out an Advance Directive, but you’ve mentioned to your son that, “I don’t ever want to live in a nursing home!”   Mrs. G’s Son   * You are a loving and devoted son. * You know that your mother needs more assistance than she’s willing to accept, but until now you’ve not “pushed it”. * You feel guilty for not insisting on staying with your mother when she developed a cough (start of her illness); you are shocked that she deteriorated so quickly. * You are struggling to understand why a “simple pneumonia” has caused your mother to be confused and lethargic. * You want to know your mother’s prognosis so that you can “plan accordingly” and discuss with your 2 sisters who live out of state. * You know that your mother didn’t want to be in a nursing home, but you also know that she cannot stay with you. |
| Past Medical History | Arthritis, high blood pressure, “mild” heart failure, possible signs of dementia? (never diagnosed) |
| Social History | Widowed, 3 children (1 son lives nearby, 2 daughters live out of state), lives in own home with intermittent home care. Remote history of smoking (1 pack per day for 10 years in her 20s-30s), drinks occasional glass of wine at special occasions. |
| Family Medical History | Both parents died of heart attacks in their 80s. |
| Physical Exam | * Mrs. G is lethargic, but arouses to voice – becomes startled, confused, and restless. * Unable to engage in meaningful conversation. * Unable to cooperate with most aspects of physical exam (e.g. requires cueing and assistance to roll over in bed; able to move all 4 extremities). * Increased respiratory effort, and crackles in right lung base posteriorly **[note overlying R-posterior lung base: crackles]**. |
| Scenario  Development | Mrs. G’s son is shocked about his mother’s rapid decline in health, but he admits that overall her health has declined over the preceding year. He pushes the physician to explain how a pneumonia – which is an infection in the lungs – can cause confusion. Mrs. G’s son also needs to know his mother’s prognosis so that he can update his sisters who live out of State. He feels ill-equipped to make medical decisions, yet he wants to honor his mother’s wishes to avoid nursing home placement. |
