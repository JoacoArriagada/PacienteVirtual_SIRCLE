**End of Life Care (stroke)**

**(Geriatric Content: ACGME Competencies: Patient Care, Medical Knowledge, Communication)**

Writer: Laird-Fick and Keilman (2006), revised Riekse (November 2013)

GH is a 75 year old Caucasian female with history of coronary artery disease (previous MI), essential HTN and hyperlipidemia who was found down by a neighbor on the floor at the side of her bed (unknown down time). No obvious head trauma was noted but it was unknown if she hit her head. She last spoke to her daughter the day before admission and “sounded normal” on the telephone, per her daughter. She is now on her third hospital day and still unable to move the entire right side of her body due to flaccid paralysis. She is also unable to speak and remains lethargic. Vitals: BP 185/92 right arm sitting, pulse 86 regular, respiration rate 10, oxygen saturation 97% on room air.

GH has a history of diabetes mellitus and has been on insulin for many years. Glucose levels have been generally controlled with a most recent HgbA1c of 7.0 % per patient’s daughter. No prior history of atrial fibrillation or other arrhythmias.

Medications prior to admission include aspirin 81 mg PO daily, lisinopril 20 mg PO daily, hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg PO daily, simvastatin 40 mg PO daily, insulin glargine (Lantus) 25 units SQ every morning as well as a daily multivitamin and calcium with vitamin D twice daily.

She lived independently; husband who has severe dementia currently lives in a nursing facility. She has one daughter who lives about three hours away. She smoked one pack of cigarettes for twenty five years, but quit about ten years ago. She does not drink alcohol.

CT of head (preliminary report): Large left MCA distribution stroke with edema.

Speech Therapy Evaluation: Patient had significant aspiration of all consistencies with bedside swallow evaluation. Recommend ongoing NPO status, with placement of NG or PEG tube for enteral feedings if desired.

**Premise:** Assessment of a patient at the end of life is multi-faceted.

**Ideally, the fellow will assess:** 1) patient’s clinical status and prognosis, 2) the patient’s preferences for end of life care, as obtained via a proxy, and 3)the daughter’s concerns for her mother’s care.
