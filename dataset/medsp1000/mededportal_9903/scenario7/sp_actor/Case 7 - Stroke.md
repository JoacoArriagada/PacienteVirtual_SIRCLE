| Case Name | Gertrude Hamlin |
| Presenting  Situation | Mrs. H is a 75 year old married woman who is hospitalized for a large stroke that left her unable to speak and paralyzed on the entire right side of her body. |
| Psychosocial  Profile | Mrs. H lives independently in her own home. Her husband has severe dementia and lives in a nearby nursing home; she has one daughter who lives about 3 hours away.  Despite having insulin-dependent diabetes, Mrs. H has been relatively healthy. She spoke with her daughter last week, and her daughter noted that she sounded “fine” on the phone.  Three days ago, Mrs. H’s neighbor came to visit. The neighbor found Mrs. H slumped on the floor by the side of her bed and unresponsive. The neighbor did not know how long Mrs. H was “down,” so she called EMS. In the ED it was determined that Mrs. H had a large stroke affecting the left side of her brain. Since then (3 days), Mrs. H has been hospitalized. She remains lethargic, unable to speak or swallow safely, and unable to move the right side of her body.  The speech therapists have recommended that Mrs. H receive nothing by mouth because she aspirates all food consistencies, putting her at risk for pneumonia. The therapists recommend a feeding tube, either a temporary tube through the nose (which is typically uncomfortable) or a semi-permanent surgical feeding tube into the stomach. |
| Opening Statement | Mrs. H’s daughter, “I can’t stand to see my mom this way! Will she ever recover?” |
| History of present illness | Mrs. H   * You have always been a very independent woman. * You worked outside of the home as an administrative assistant; retired about 10 years ago when your husband started having troubles with his memory. * You cared for your husband in your home until 2 years ago, when his care needs escalated. He now resides in a nursing home, and you visit him several times per week. * You enjoy cooking, although you cook less now that your husband has relocated. * Many years ago you filled out a DPOA-HC designating your spouse as your advocate, followed by your daughter as successor advocate. * After your husband was moved to the nursing home, you had a frank discussion with your daughter about your goals if you were to become incapacitated (e.g. after suffering a massive stroke).   + You do not want to be resuscitated (DNR).   + You want comfort care.   + You did not explicitly discuss feeding tubes, although you’ve seen several NH residents with them during your visits with your husband. You’ve commented that it takes the pleasure out of nourishment (eating was pleasurable for you).   Mrs. H’s Daughter   * You are your mother’s only child. * You live 3 hours away, but you talk to your mother weekly and you visit monthly. * You struggle with the burden of becoming your mother’s DPOA-HC (your father cannot make decisions due to his advanced dementia). * You would like to honor your mother’s wishes (above), but you are uncertain about her prognosis. * You don’t want your mother to die, but you are very upset watching your mother “suffer” in her current condition. * You are struggling with the option of “feeding tubes”. * You are worried about losing your job because of taking time off to be with your mother, which is most important to you. |
| Past Medical History | * “Small” stroke 3 years ago, no residual symptoms * Insulin-dependent diabetes (well controlled, Hgb A1c approximately 7%). * Coronary artery disease (past heart attack) * High blood pressure * High cholesterol * No history of blood clots or atrial fibrillation |
| Social History | Married, lives independently (previously primary caregiver for husband with advanced dementia), retired administrative assistant, ex-smoker 1 pack per day for 25 years (quit more than 10 years ago), does not drink alcohol, independent in all activities of daily living, manages own medications, finances, and still driving. |
| Family Medical History | Mother and father with “heart disease” – daughter cannot recall causes of death. |
| Physical Exam | * Lethargic, unable to move entire right side of body (face, arm, leg) **[note overlying R-arm and R-leg stating: flaccid paralysis]** * Drool protruding from corner of right-side of mouth **[simulate by smearing with petroleum jelly]** * Opens left eye to voice (keeps right eye closed), but cannot follow other commands. |
| Scenario  Development | Mrs. H’s daughter is distraught by her mother’s condition, and feeling unprepared to make a decision about a feeding tube. She really needs to know her mother’s prognosis, and she looks to you (her mother’s PCP) to tell her the “truth.” She wonders if her mother is dying, because she knows her mother’s wishes were to be “comfortable.” She is uncertain if she should tell her father about her mother’s condition, which she fears would upset him. She is also feeling guilty about the possibility of losing her job if she needs to spend more time with her mother. |
