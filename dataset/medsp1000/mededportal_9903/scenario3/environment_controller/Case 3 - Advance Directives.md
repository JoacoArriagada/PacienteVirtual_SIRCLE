**Case: Advance Directives**

**(Geriatric Content: Prevention)**

**ACGME Competencies: Pt. Care, Communication**

Writer: Foley (October 2010)

Mr. J. is a 68 year old man with well controlled type II diabetes mellitus and hyperlipidemia. He presents for an annual examination with a chief complaint of periodic legs cramps that are relieved with pre-bedtime calf stretching. During the visit, he notes that he has recently divorced and is now living alone in his home. He has no children or siblings and he has begun to worry about “who will take care of me if I get ill?” The lawyer who handled his divorce suggested that he find someone to act as his durable power of attorney for health care and he offered to draw-up a durable power of attorney document for him to sign. But, Mr. J. declined the offer as he did not fully understand what a DPOA for health care could and could not do and he also did not want to “pay the lawyer any more money”. Instead, Mr. J. downloaded a “living will” form from a family law website that he filled out and placed in his safe deposit box. Mr. J. is seeking advice about the DPOA for health care and has many questions about its legality, his rights, how doctors will follow his living will directives and a DPOA-HC at the same time, and your preferences as his primary care physician.
