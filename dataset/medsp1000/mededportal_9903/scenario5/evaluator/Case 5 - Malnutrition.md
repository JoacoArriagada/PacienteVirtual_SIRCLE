**Case: Malnutrition with immobility and falls**

**(Geriatric Content: Geriatric Syndromes)**

**ACGME Competencies: Pt. Care; Medical Knowledge; Communication**

Writer: Noel (Sept. 2010)

Mrs. H is a 75 year woman who has been a widow for 10 years. She was admitted to the long term care (LTC) section of the nursing facility about 1 year ago after a routine hospitalization. Up to that point she had lived in her home of 50 years, a large two story home in an established upper income community. Her family had always lived in the community with her parents’ home across the street from the one she and her husband built. Her parents were both faculty members at a large university and her husband was a prominent business man in the community. She had a college degree before many women in her community. She had 2 children, a son and daughter who are both in their 50s now and live across the country (one east coast, the other in a city about 500 miles away). The son has 2 children – one who lived with Mrs. H while she was in college and still lives nearby; the daughter is single with an estranged relationship with her mother. Students have rented out rooms in Mrs. H’s large home and provided services to Mrs. H such as grocery shopping up until the time that Mrs. H entered the LTC. After the birth of her children she had child birth fever with seizures which at the time was thought to be due to her teeth, so many of her teeth were pulled. No replacements (such as partial dentures or bridges) were made. Over the last ten years as a widow, her only visits to her home are the graduate students who live with her and her children once a year when she is admitted to the hospital (usually in July). This last admission a year ago resulted in the decision that she is unable to continue to live alone since all of the students she has had living with her are moving out, and no replacements can easily be found.

**Premise:** The case may appear to be about “falls” or “depression,” but on further evaluation the most relevant issue is malnutrition and alcohol abuse. These problems are exacerbated by the medical team’s prescription of a “low salt” diet. The patient would benefit from a less restrictive diet, increased socialization, and referral back to PT/OT.

**Ideally, the fellow will ascertain:**

At the hospital admission 2 years ago the physician noted elevated blood pressure so the patient was put on a low salt diet. The patient up to then had been eating 2 processed dinners per day as well as drinking 1-2 White Russians (cream, vodka, coffee liqueur) daily. With this new order patient stopped the processed meals and instead increased her daily consumption of White Russians (2-3 drinks per day; about 500 calories each). This is when her falls became frequent. The unsalted food at the nursing facility is unpalatable to the patient so she eats the sweets that her granddaughter brings or just nibbles at the food that seems interesting to her. Mrs. H would prefer not to go to the dining room since she finds the place depressing and uninteresting. She complains bitterly about the food as well as the invasion of her privacy in the nursing facility. The nursing facility will not allow alcohol though her granddaughter will bring that in when she visits. This helps Mrs. H’s appetite so occasionally she eats well.

**Case Objectives:**

**PRIMARY**

1. Fellow will be able to identify the cause of patient’s malnutrition (special diet – low sodium and high-calorie alcohol use)

**SECONDARY**

2. Fellow will be able to devise treatment plan to improve the functional status of the patient (removal of dietary restrictions and work with family to restrict providing alcoholic beverages into the facility).

**Case specific needs:**

1. Elderly female SP who is overweight and edentulous if possible

2. Young woman (granddaughter) in about her 20s
