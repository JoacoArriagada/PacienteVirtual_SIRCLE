| Case Name | Jack Vaneck |
| Presenting  Situation | Mr. V is an 84 year old married man who has been referred for “memory testing” with an interdisciplinary team, including a physician, a medical social worker or nurse. |
| Psychosocial  Profile | Mr. V lives with his wife who is his sole caregiver. He began having memory loss about 4 years ago. Mr. V’s initial symptoms were frequent repeating, and not being able to remember recent events and conversations. Over the past year, Mr. V’s memory has gotten worse. Now, he cannot follow conversations with others, needs constant reminding, and cannot be left alone. He is unable to manage the finances, doesn’t remember to change his clothes or shave. He continues to drive. He has had two recent accidents from his driving, hitting a neighbor’s mailbox and running a stop sign one month ago. Mr. V is adamant that he is “not going to stop” driving.  Mr. V was not told why this appointment was requested by his PCP and his wife did not tell him the reason for the appointment. Mrs. V admits to being under “some” stress in her role as a caregiver. Before entering the exam room, Mrs. V passed a handwritten note to the medical assistant that reads, “Doctor: I don’t know how much longer I can care for my husband. I need to know what is going on with his memory and what to expect”. |
| Opening Statement | Mrs. V, “I’m worried about my husband. I need to know what is going on with his memory”. |
| History of present illness | Mr. V   * You have been a “take charge” type of man in the past, but now cannot manage and depend heavily on your wife for care. * You are independent with most self-care activities (e.g. brushing your teeth, toileting, feeding yourself), but your wife needs to remind you to change your clothes, bathe and to shave. * You are repeating yourself frequently. * You have difficulty remembering things and cannot follow a conversation well. * You are adamant that you will continue to drive. * You try to follow directions but become confused easily. * You are generally pleasant, however, when frustrated you become somewhat irritable. * You recognize your wife. * You do not know why you are at this doctor’s appointment * You know your name. * You have no specific complaints.   Mrs. V   * You are the sole caregiver for your husband. * You display kindness and reassurance to your husband. * You now have to manage the household money because your husband was making mistakes paying the bills. * You must remind your husband to change his clothes, shave, bathe. * You admit to being under some “stress” in your role as primary caregiver. * When questioned further, you admit that you don’t know how much longer you can care for your husband because it is difficult and you are overwhelmed. * You do not want to ask your daughter for help because you do not want to be a burden to her. * You feel your husband cannot be left alone, “I’m afraid he’ll go out of the house and get lost”. * You don’t know exactly what is going on with your husband’s memory and don’t know what to expect. * You are receptive to suggestions made by the interdisciplinary team.   Medical Social Worker (or Nurse)   * You are part of the interdisciplinary team working with Mr. V at this appointment. You are working with the physician in the following plans:   + You are concerned about Mrs. V’s ability to provide care for her husband.   + You explore the possibility of getting help in the home.   + You explore community resources/adult day care options.   + You recommend a family conference with Mr. V and Mrs. V’s children so they may better understand the situation.   + You speak to Mr. V and tell him you are concerned about his safety and you ask if he would consider a Driving Evaluation. “How do you feel about this?” |
| Past Medical History | * Generally healthy * Six months ago, saw PCP –MMSE 22/30 points * Normal blood work * CAT scan of the head normal except “mild brain shrinking” (atrophy) * PCP stated “probable dementia” 6 months ago and prescribed donepezil and memantine |
| Social History | * Married and lives with wife * Three adult children. 2 live out of state and 1 daughter lives 10 miles away. She visits them about 1 time/week * Retired from management position age 65 |
| Family History | Parents of Mr. V died of natural causes |
| Physical Exam | Mr. V is cooperative with the exam  Positive findings with MMSE (see examples of abnormal responses to mental status exam) |
| Scenario  Development | Mr. and Mrs. V are at an appointment with an interdisciplinary team. Mrs. V is her husband’s primary care giver since he has had progressive dementia over the past 4 years. She is reluctant to seek assistance, but is feeling overwhelmed with the day to day responsibilities in caring for her husband, who is requiring increasingly more care. She does not want to be a burden to her family. She is unclear about what is going on with her husband’s memory and does not know what to expect. She is appropriately concerned and open to suggestions. |

**Examples of Abnormal Responses to Mental Status Exam Questions**

* Current President – “Carter”
  + Before him? – “not sure”
* # States in USA – “48”
* Add pocket change (quarter + dime + nickel + penny) = “40”
* Similarities of common objects – start off providing similarities, then progress to describe differences
  + Orange and tennis ball – “round”
  + Pine tree and pencil – “straight” or “wood”
  + Tricycle and airplane – “one flies, but the other doesn’t”
* Days of week in reverse order
  + Hesitate in response, then “Friday, Thursday, Wednesday. . . Friday, Saturday, Sunday, right?”
* Months of year in reverse order
  + Hesitate in response, then “December, November. . . this is silly.”
