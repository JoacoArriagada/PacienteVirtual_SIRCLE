**Case: End-Stage CHF**

**(Geriatric Content: Palliative Care)**

**ACGME Competencies: Patient Care, Medical Knowledge, Systems Based Practice, Communication, Professionalism**

Writer: Foley (October 2010)

Mrs. P. is a 72 year old Jewish (or alternative minority religion) woman with NYHA class IV congestive heart failure who has been a resident of the Acme Nursing Home for the past three years. Her CHF resulted from four previous myocardial infarctions and she has had a four vessel CABG in the past with angioplasty times two of occluded grafts. Her last echocardiogram reported global hypo-kinesis and an ejection fraction of 10%. She requires nasal oxygen at 4 liters per minute to maintain her oxygen saturations above 90%. High doses of diuretics have been prescribed to treat moderately large bilateral pleural effusions and 4+ leg edema. Mrs. P. also has severe narcotic dependent arthritis, for which she takes hydrocodone/APAP, 10/325 mgs every six hours with limited relief. She requires total care due to her severe functional limitations. Her mentation has remained remarkably clear despite the significant physical declines that she has experienced. Mrs. P.’s durable power of attorney for health care is her eldest son. Although she has been able to make her own medical decisions, she has asked that he be consulted about her care as she has become “too tired” to answer doctors and nurses questions. Mrs. P.’s son realizes that she has a limited life expectancy and has requested that she be made “DNR” and not transported to the hospital for any reason. He is requesting that his mother be “kept comfortable” and that unnecessary medications be discontinued. She is currently prescribed: furosemide, potassium, carvedilol, lisinopril, spironolactone, digoxin, simvastatin, aspirin, hydrocodone/APAP, a multivitamin, chondroitin sulfate, calcium with vitamin D, and vitamin E. Mrs. P.’s physical examination reveals a chronically ill appearing woman in moderate respiratory distress; BP of 140/68 with pulse of 88/min, RR= 30/min; JVD to the angle of the jaw at 30 degrees, poor air movement and no breath sounds in the bottom half lung fields posteriorly with dullness to percussion, soft heart sounds with a summation gallop, an 18 cm liver with a smooth edge, and 4+ pitting leg edema extending to the thighs bilaterally. She is diffusely weak and unable to stand on her feet. She report s her pain to be “8 out of 10”.

**Premise:** The patient’s care is now focusing on palliation and she requires relief of her distressing respiratory symptoms and pain. The patient’s son is now ready to let her die and requests that all measures be extended in the nursing home to assure that she has a “good death”.

**Ideally, the fellow will address:** 1) medical (including invasive) and non-medical management of respiratory distress, 2) management of pain with narcotic substitution, conversion, and titration, 3) certification for hospice and involvement of hospice nurse in management, 4) elimination of all medications that are not prescribed for comfort measures, 5) address other terminal care issues with the patient – including spiritual - and assess need to manage occult psychiatric symptoms

**Case Objectives:**

**PRIMARY**

1. Fellow will demonstrate the ability to manage distressing symptoms related to terminal illness (pain and dyspnea).
2. Fellow will appropriately refer patient for hospice services and initiate care coordination.

**SECONDARY**

1. Fellow will demonstrate the ability to manage with narcotics according to evidence based guidelines (e.g. opiate conversion).
2. Fellow will demonstrate cultural sensitivity in managing the terminal care of an elderly Jewish (or other minority religion) woman.
3. Fellow will perform a focused physical exam of a dying patient.

**Case specific needs:** Elderly woman (preferably of a minority religion) SP
