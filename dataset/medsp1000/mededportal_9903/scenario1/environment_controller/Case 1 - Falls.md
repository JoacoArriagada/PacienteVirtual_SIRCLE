**Case: Falls**

**(Geriatric Content: Functional Assessment)**

**ACGME Competencies: Pt. Care, Medical Knowledge, Communication**

Writer: Foley (October 2010)

Mrs. Q is a 91 year old widowed woman who has had three falls in the past 6 months. She has a history of hypertension for which she takes HCTZ and carvedilol, anxiety for which she takes alprazolam, and dizziness for which she takes meclizine. She has no cognitive impairment, history of stroke, or previous fractures. She believes that her falls were due to “dizziness” and stumbling over her feet. She says that she occasionally “stubs” her left toes on uneven surfaces, but does not notice weakness in her legs. Her last fall occurred while she was in the bath tub. None of her falls were serious enough to warrant transport to an emergency room and she did not strike her head while falling. She lives independently in a senior apartment complex (no stairs) and maintains her household well, although she requires some assistance with “heavy cleaning”. She is physically active and walks to a grocery store, ¼ mile from her apartment, two to three times per week for supplies. Her vision is “fair” and she has not had her eyes examined in three years. Her hearing is also “fair” and she has trouble hearing her television. She consumes one or two “highballs” most nights and she does not use tobacco. She has supportive children who visit her daily.
