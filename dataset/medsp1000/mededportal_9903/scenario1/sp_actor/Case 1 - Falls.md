| Case Name | Winifred Quick |
| Presenting  Situation | Mrs. Q is a 91 year old widowed woman who lives independently in a senior apartment complex. She has had three falls in the past six months. |
| Psychosocial  Profile | * You are alert and have no troubles with memory. * You are independent. * You are cooperative and friendly. * You believe that your falls were due to “dizziness” and stumbling over your feet. * You occasionally “stub” your left toes on uneven surfaces * You do not notice weakness in your legs. * Your last fall occurred while you were in the bathtub. Other falls involved catching toes in a small rug in your home, and turning around too quickly. * You have never been injured by your falls. * You are able to maintain your household well, but need some help with “heavy cleaning”. * You are physically active and walk to a grocery store that is ¼ mile from your apartment, two to three times/week. * Your vision is “fair” and you have not had your eyes checked in three years. * Your hearing is also “fair” but you have trouble hearing your television. You sometimes have trouble hearing the doctor. * You drink one to two mixed alcoholic drinks most nights. * You are a non-smoker. * You have supportive children, who visit you daily. * You take a medicine for your blood pressure, one for some anxiety and one or dizziness. |
| Opening Statement | “My daughter thought I should be checked because I’ve had some falls”. |
| History of present illness | You have fallen three times in the past six months. The first fall occurred in the bath tub. You think you turned too fast. You were not hurt and used your grab bars to get up. The second fall involved catching your left toes in a small throw rug. You were not hurt in that fall. The third fall involved catching your hip against the counter after feeling “dizzy”. You had a bruise on your hip, but otherwise not hurt. |
| Past Medical History | * Hypertension/Anxiety/re-occurring dizziness |
| Social History | * You are widowed and have been for years * Your children, two daughters, live in the area and you see one of them every day * You enjoy socializing with the other residents at the senior living apartment complex and keep busy * You are physically active and enjoy walking to the store * Sometimes you feel anxious because you miss your husband and don’t like living in this small apartment |
| Family Medical History | Parents died from natural causes  Children in good health |
| Physical Exam | * You have some trouble reading the print on the eye chart. * When asked to get up, you complain of feeling a little dizzy (lightheaded) * You ask the doctor to repeat what is said because you are having difficulty hearing. |
| Scenario  Development | Mrs. Q has taken three falls in the past six months and she is seeing the doctor on the encouragement from her daughter. Mrs. Q has been very independent, and lives in her own apartment in a senior complex. Mrs. Q prides herself that she can take care of her home and walks to the store several times/week. She has a good relationship with her daughters and is involved in their lives. Mrs. Q explains her falls occur because she has had some “dizziness” and also stumbles over her feet. There hasn’t been any documented injury. You are friendly and cooperative with the doctor, but you have a strong sense of independence and self-reliance. |
