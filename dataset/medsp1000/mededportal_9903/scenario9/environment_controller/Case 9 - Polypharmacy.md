# Case: Polypharmacy (Geriatric Content: Complex Disease Management, Medication Management) ACGME Competencies:

Writer: Sarzynski – revised from Laird-Fick and Keilman (2006)

Mrs. R is a 92 year old widowed woman who presented to the ED with confusion. She was diagnosed with a UTI three days ago and started on an antibiotic. She has mild dementia (VaD) but has been stable at her assisted living facility until the past 4-5 days when she became fatigued, less interactive, and confused. In addition to VaD, Mrs. R has a history of DM-2 on oral medications, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, osteoporosis, urge incontinence, and chronic insomnia. She takes multiple medications: aspirin, glipizide, amlodipine, enalapril, hydrochlorothiazide, alendronate, diphenhydramine (sleep aid), oxybutynin, and recently trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (for recent UTI). Since admission, she has received IV levofloxacin $( 5 0 0 \mathsf { m g } )$ and lorazepam and haloperidol as needed for confusion (total of $2 \mathsf { m } \mathsf { g }$ and $5 \mathsf { m } \mathsf { g }$ , respectively). At baseline, Mrs. R has been stable and living at Maple Grove Assisted Living for the past 2 years. She is widowed and all but one of her children is deceased. Her son (Tom Richardson) lives in the area and is her primary caregiver and assists with many IADLs. Until recently, Mrs. R has been able to perform all of her ADLs independently. Tom has DPOA for healthcare and financial issues. Mrs. R’s other family members live out of state and have little contact with her. Tom is currently feeling overwhelmed by his own personal stressors (failing small business and ongoing divorce proceedings) as well as his mother’s illness. During the examination Mrs. R is noted to be drowsy with thick, garbled speech, although she demonstrates episodes of increased arousal [restlessness, as evidenced by “picking” at the air and bedclothes] and appears to be hallucinating “tell that boy to get out of my room!” At some point a nurse (or nursing student) enters the room to perform an assessment and attempts to give Mrs. R additional prn doses of lorazepam and haloperidol. Tom should try to stop the nurse, stating, “Those medications haven’t helped at all so far! I want to know why my mother is like this, not just have you make her sleep more!”
