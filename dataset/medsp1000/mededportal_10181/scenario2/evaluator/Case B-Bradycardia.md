1. **Title: Acute Myocardial Infarction (MI)**
2. **Target Audience:** **Medical Students**
3. **Learning Objectives:**
   1. Primary:
      * Recognize and treat acute MI.
      * Recognize and treat bradycardia in the setting of an Inferior MI.
      * Use peer education to facilitate long-term learning.
   2. Secondary:
      * For Student-Instructor/Peer Educator:
        1. Present a succinct case in which your peers identify the above conditions and the treatment strategies for each scenario.
        2. Guide peers through evaluation and management of an acutely ill patient.
        3. Provide a wrap-up discussion for your colleagues to highlight salient questions, physical examination findings, imaging studies, current management and therapies.
      * For Learners:
        1. Establish a broad differential diagnosis for chest pain: MI, angina, pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax, pericarditis, pneumonia, aortic dissection, costochondritis, etc.
        2. Perform a directed history and physical examination for chest pain.
        3. Recognize and interpret appropriate tests/studies for a patient with chest pain.
        4. Implement correct treatment for acute MI.
        5. Call the appropriate consultants if needed.
        6. Recognize change in patient’s status and diagnosis with bradycardia and order appropriate testing including repeat ECG.
        7. Establish a broad differential diagnosis for bradycardia (medications, inferior MI, hyperkalemia, increased intracranial pressure, sick sinus syndrome, hypothyroidism).
        8. Assess bradycardic patient for stability; treat appropriately.
   3. **Critical Actions Checklist:**

**1) CHEST PAIN Critical Action Checklist**

* + - Check ABCs.
    - Request ECG, IV, O2, monitor.
    - Ask appropriate questions (directed history-taking):
* History of chest pain.
* Onset, provoking/alleviating factors, quality, radiation, severity, time course.
* Cardiac risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, smoking, illicit drug use, Family history).
* Bleeding risk factors (GIB, anemia).
* Previous cardiac workup.
* Recent ASA/nitro/illicit/Sildenafil use.
  + - Perform a physical examination (ask specific questions re: findings).
    - Order appropriate labs:
    - ECG and Chest X-ray (CXR) – ask student to interpret.
    - CBC, basic metabolic panel, cardiac enzymes, coagulation studies.
    - The scenario is completed before any labs are resulted.
    - Start appropriate treatment:
      1. Recognize **not** to give nitroglycerin (NTG) due to inferior MI
         1. IF student gives NTG, pt becomes hypotensive – if this happens ask how to correct—i.e. IV Fluids
      2. Aspirin
      3. Morphine
      4. +/- Heparin or plavix
    - Consult cardiology to discuss need for cardiac catheterization.

**2) BRADYCARDIA Critical Action Checklist**

* Recheck ABCs.
* Re-order ECG – ask student to interpret.
* Check if stable/unstable:
  + - 1. Stable: Observe, continue MI work-up-our patient is NOT stable.
      2. Unstable: Start ACLS:

1. Medical Therapy will NOT improve bradycardia:
   * + 1. atropine (0.5mg)
       2. dopamine (2-10mcg/kg/min infusion)
       3. epinephrine (2-10mcg/min)
2. Transcutaneous Pacing is required to improve heart rate and symptoms
   * + 1. Increase voltage until “capture”
       2. Call cardiology for tranvenous pacemaker placement (for more definitive care)
3. **Environment:**
   1. Lab Set Up: Trauma Bay, ED, or simulation room.
   2. Manikin Set Up: Supine with defibrillator near patient, pads not on, but available, IV in place, nitroglycerin sublingual, morphine IV, atropine, dopamine, and epinephrine.
   3. Props: ECGs #1 and #2, CXR (APPENDIX), defibrillator, crash cart, rhythm generator initially set for STEMI (ST elevation MI), inferior MI and needs ability to change rhythm to bradycardia.
   4. Distractors: None.
4. **Actors:**
   1. Roles: Instructor – preferably medical student-instructor but can be attending physician, Senior or Junior Residents
   2. Participants: Medical Students
5. **Case Narrative:**
   1. Information from triage, to be provided at start of encounter:
      * Demographics: J.H. is a 67-year-old man.
      * Chief Complaint: Chest pain.
      * Vitals: BP 189/102 HR 96 RR 18 SaO2 94% on RA Temp 98F
   2. Scenario conditions initially (give only what is asked for):
      * Chest pain started when he was taking out the trash, approximately 30 minutes ago.
      * The pain is a pressure sensation to the left anterior chest, “like an elephant is sitting on my chest.”
      * Pain radiates to the left arm and jaw.
      * It has not changed in character or severity since onset.
      * Currently complains of +SOB, mild epigastric abdominal pain, nausea but no vomiting. Not dizzy/lightheaded. No weakness. No dark colored stools or blood in stools.
      * Previously had symptoms like this that improved with rest.
      * No previous stress testing or cardiac catheterization.
      * He has never seen a doctor for this. He was brought into the ED by his wife.
      * No recent aspirin or nitroglycerin use. Last meal was 6 hours ago.
      * Past Medical History: Hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, COPD, gout, and hyperlipidemia.
      * Past Surgical History: Cholecystectomy, appendectomy, left hip replacement.
      * Meds: HCTZ, Metformin, Simvastatin, Fluticasone Propionate (Advair), Albuterol, Allopurinol.
      * Allergies: No known drug allergies.
      * Social History: 90 pack year hx smoking, quit 2 years ago; occasional alcohol; no illicits.
      * Family history: Parents are deceased, father had fatal MI at age 72; mother had CHF and cardiomyopathy.
      * Patient’s Initial Exam (only give what is asked for):
        1. Vitals: BP 189/102 HR 96 RR 18 SaO2 94% on RA Temp 98F
        2. General: Patient appears diaphoretic and uncomfortable but able to speak in full sentences.
        3. Neck: Supple. No JVD.
        4. Cardiac: RRR, no M/R/G. No tenderness to palpation over chest. Equal pulses.
        5. Pulmonary: CTAB, no W/R/R.
        6. Abdomen: S/NTND.
        7. Rectal: Guaiac negative.
        8. Extremities: Minimal lower extremity edema bilaterally.
        9. If students ask for anything else not pertinent, report findings as “normal.”
   3. **Scenario Branch Points:**
6. See “Critical Actions Checklist for Chest Pain” page 2:
   1. If patient is appropriately treated then patient has less chest pain, BP increases and he feels better
   2. If patient is NOT appropriately treated then he continues to decompensate starts complaining of more chest pain, becoming more hypotense and possibly having altered mental status
7. Regardless of treatment, after 5 minutes the patient enters into symptomatic bradycardia:
   1. Change Rhythm generator to bradycardia at 40 BPM
   2. Pt reports: feels lightheaded, more chest pain, altered mental status
   3. Vitals: HR 40, BP 85/65, RR 23, temp 37, SaO2 90% on RA (93% on 2L O2)
   4. Physical Exam: General: patient is awake, but slow to answer questions, Respiratory: lungs clear, Cardiac: sinus rhythm, heart rate 40, Abdomen: soft
   5. Follow “Critical Actions Checklist for Bradycardia” on page 2 regarding diagnosis and treatment
8. **Instructor’s Notes:**
   1. Tips to Keep Scenario Flowing and Direct Actors:
      * Provide the patient’s chief complaint to the learners on arrival to your station. Try to only give information that would be provided from triage.
      * Give history, demographic, and clinical examination findings only as asked, this is provided in case narrative. Use the outline to guide your peers through the appropriate actions.
      * Elicit differential diagnoses prior to providing imaging results.
      * Encourage participation by prompting learners if necessary, but do not direct them (i.e., ask “Is there anything else you would like to add/additional tests you would like to order?”).
      * Remind peers of the importance of re-evaluating patient, otherwise they will not recognize bradycardia as it develops. The main focus of the case is reacting to the change in the patient’s condition.
      * Follow along and make sure the critical actions checklist points have been addressed. If not addressed during simulation, address them during the debriefing.
      * Although only recommended for advanced learners, if patient progresses to death due to missed diagnosis, stress importance of early recognition and treatment of critical patients along with need for frequent re-evaluation of patient, especially if patient’s status has changed
   2. Scenario Programming:
      * Optimal management path:
        1. Acute MI: order ECG, recognize inferior MI and Consult Cardiology for STEMI
        2. Bradycardia: attempt atropine, dopamine, or epinephrine, but when not improved patient requires transcutaneous pacing and a discussion with cardiology regarding need for transvenous pacing
      * Potential Complications and Errors
        1. Heparin without assessment for bleeding risk (coagulation profile, rectal exam) can lead to hemorrhage
        2. Nitroglycerin given without assessment for inferior MI via ECG- patient will become hypotensive. Consider assessment for recent Sildenafil use (NTG contraindicated within 24 hours).
        3. Not recognizing MI patient continues to complain of chest pain and requires acute intervention (i.e. medications, cardiology).
        4. Not recognizing unstable bradycardia with ongoing chest pain.
9. **Debriefing Plan:**
   1. Method of debriefing: Team leader-led, group style, with support items and references.
      * This discussion should be limited to 5 minutes in total and supported through reading of materials (articles, reference books, etc).
      * Highlight salient questions, physical examination findings, laboratory/imaging studies, and current management/therapies.
   2. Actual debriefing materials:
      * Treatment of Bradycardia (list of possible sources provided below under References).
      * Additional materials student-instructor/team leader finds while preparing for the case.
   3. Rules for debriefing: Establish at onset of debrief by group.
      * Establish goals – what could be done better/differently, what was done well?
   4. Questions to facilitate debriefing:
      * What is the management for stable versus unstable bradycardia?
      * What are indications for and adverse effects of Atropine, Epinephrine, and Dopamine?
      * What are indications for transvenous pacing?
