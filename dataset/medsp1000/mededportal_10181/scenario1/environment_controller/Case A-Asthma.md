1. **Environment:**
   1. Lab Set Up: Emergency Department, simulation room, or trauma bay.
   2. Manikin Set-Up: Supine or sitting-up, patient with peripheral IV, nebulizer with albuterol/atrovent, prednisone both iv and oral forms, adenosine IV.
   3. Props: Xray image (provided in back of packet if ordered), initial EKG (sinus tachycardia), SVT EKG, code cart, rhythm generator or monitor set initially for sinus tachycardia, but need ability to change rhythm to SVT.
   4. Distractors: None.
2. **Actors:**
   1. Roles: Instructor – preferably medical student-instructor but can be attending physician, Senior or Junior Residents
   2. Participants: Medical Students
3. **Case Narrative:**
   1. Information from triage, to be provided at start of encounter.
      1. Chief Complaint: Shortness of breath.
      2. Demographics: LR is a 34-year-old female.
      3. Vitals: BP 148/85 HR 102 RR 26 SaO2 84% on RA Temp 97.9
   2. Scenario conditions initially (give only what is asked for):
      1. Breathing problems started 2 days ago initially with runny nose and cough. Now feeling more short of breath. Has been using her albuterol inhaler every hour without relief.
      2. Has not been on steroids for 2 years.
      3. Has never been intubated.
      4. Hospitalized multiple times as a child, but never as an adult and never in the PICU.
      5. Does not know her normal peak flow.
      6. Triggers include cats, change in seasons, dust, etc.
      7. Past Medical: Seasonal allergies, asthma, hypertension; no surgeries.
      8. Meds: Albuterol, Singulair, Hydrochlorothiazide.
      9. Allergies: Shellfish.
      10. Social Hx: 1ppd tobacco abuser, occasional alcohol and marijuana use.
      11. Family Hx: unknown, patient was adopted.
      12. Initial physical exam:
          1. Vitals: BP 148/85 HR 102 RR 26 SaO2 84% on RA Temp 97.9
          2. General: Patient appears to be in moderate respiratory distress. +Accessory muscle use, nasal flaring. Speaking in 3-word sentences.
          3. Cardiac: Tachycardic, no murmurs, rubs or gallops.
          4. Pulmonary: Diffuse end-expiratory wheezing, decreased air entry bilaterally.
          5. Extremities: No calf swelling or tenderness, no swelling.
          6. Skin: Evidence of perioral cyanosis and acral cyanosis.
          7. If students ask for anything else not pertinent, report findings as “normal.”
   3. **Scenario branch points:**
      1. If patient is appropriately treated she improves initially.
      2. If patient is NOT appropriately treated she continues to decompensate with worsening shortness of breath and hypoxia.
      2. Regardless of treatment, after 5 minutes patient develops SVT:
         1. Change rhythm generator to SVT
         2. Patient reports: heart racing and shortness of breath:
         3. Vitals: BP 105/65 HR 184 RR 26 SaO2 84% on RA or 95% on 2L oxygen Temp 97.9
         4. Physical exam: Awake and alert, complaining of heart racing, respiratory: scattered wheezing,-improved from prior, Cardiac: tachycardic, Abdomen: soft
      3. If the learner appropriately addresses the rhythm change, the patient returns to NSR. If not, the patient continues to decompensate and the session can end prior to terminal decompensation.
4. **Instructor’s Notes:**
   1. Tips to Keep Scenario Flowing and Direct Actors:
      1. Provide the patient’s chief complaint to the learners on arrival to your station. Try to only give information that would be provided from triage.
      2. Give history, demographic, and clinical examination findings only as asked, this is provided in a case narrative. Use the outline to guide your peers through the appropriate actions.
      3. Elicit differential diagnoses prior to providing imaging. Laboratory results will not be available-scenario will be completed prior to labs resulting.
      4. Encourage participation by prompting learners if necessary, but do not direct them (i.e. ask “Is there anything else you would like to add/additional tests you would like to order?”).
      5. Remind peers of the importance of re-evaluating patient, otherwise will not recognize SVT as it develops. The main focus of the case is reacting to the change in the patient condition.
   2. Runtime response notes:
      1. If asthma is not recognized or treatment is incorrect, the patient continues to worsen.
      2. If SVT is not recognized, the patient eventually becomes hypotensive, more short of breath, and has altered mental status due to hypoperfusion.
