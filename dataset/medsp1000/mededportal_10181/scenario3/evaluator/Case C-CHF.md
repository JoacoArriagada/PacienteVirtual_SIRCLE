1. **Title: Congestive Heart Failure**
2. **Target Audience:** **Medical Students.**
3. **Learning Objectives:**
4. Primary:
   1. Recognize signs and symptoms of CHF.
   2. Recognize VT/VF.
5. Secondary.
   1. For Team Leader/Peer Educator:
      1. Present a succinct case in which your peers identify the above conditions and the treatment strategies for each scenario.
      2. Guide peers through evaluation and management of an acutely ill patient.
      3. Provide a wrap-up discussion for your colleagues to highlight salient questions, physical examination findings, laboratory/imaging studies, current management and therapies.
   2. For Learners:
      1. Establishes broad differential diagnosis: Myocardial infarction, HTN emergency, toxins (cocaine, EtOH, etc.), metabolic (thyroid, DM, thiamine deficiency), valvular heart disease, infectious, idiopathic.
         * 1. Left HF: Most common cause is ischemic injury. Other causes include uncontrolled hypertension, valvular disease, dilated cardiomyopathy.
           2. Right HF: Most common cause is left heart failure. Other causes include pulmonary hypertension, PE, COPD, Right Ventricular ischemic insult.
      2. Perform a directed history and physical for shortness of breath.
      3. Recognize and interpret appropriate tests/studies for CHF patient.
      4. Implement correct treatment of CHF exacerbation.
      5. Recognize Ventricular Tachycardia/Ventricular Fibrillation.
      6. Order appropriate testing.
      7. Establish stable versus unstable VT with correct treatment.
      8. Call appropriate consultant if needed.
6. **Critical Actions Checklists:**

**CONGESTIVE HEART FAILURE Critical Action Checklist**

* Checks ABCs.
* Requests EKG, IV, O2, monitor.
* Asks the appropriate questions (directed history-taking).
* Past medical history and any previous history of “fluid in the lungs.”
* Onset, provoking/alleviating factors, quality, severity, time course.
* Chest pain, cough, Dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea/paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND), weight gain, leg swelling, salt and fluid intake, ejection fraction if known.
* Performs physical exam (asks specific questions re: findings).
* Orders appropriate tests.
* EKG – ask student to interpret: discuss criteria for diagnosing left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) (e.g. P waves taller than 25microvolts in lead II reflects right atrial enlargement. S in V2 summed with R in V5 greater than 35mv reflects LVH, etc.).
* Chest X-ray – ask student to interpret: Discuss enlarged cardiac silhouette, cephalization of lung vasculature, pleural effusions, perihilar infiltrates
* Labs: VBG, BNP, cardiac enzymes: discuss normal ranges, BNP>500 specific, BNP<100 sensitive.
* Starts appropriate treatment:
* Optimize O2 : prefer non-rebreather mask (NRB) to start, progress to CPAP/BiPAP🡪Intubation (although the students will not intubate).
* Reduce cardiac workload:
  + 1. Decrease preload:
       1. Best: Nitroglycerin (IV drip is best (10-80mcg/min); other forms include: Sublingual or paste),
       2. Diuresis: Lasix (lasix-naïve-start 20-40mg IV), time of onset: 20-90minutes
       3. Morphine 2-6mg IV push
    2. Decrease afterload: ACE, higher dose nitrates.
* Improve cardiac function: Dobutamine, Dopamine (dose effect), Milrinone.
* Consults with ICU for admission.

**Pulseless VTACH/VFIB Critical Action Checklist:**

* Rechecks ABCs.
* Obtain EKG/rhythm strip – ask student to interpret.
* Patient in UNstable VT due to hypotension and altered mental status.
* Follows ACLS protocols for UNstable ventricular tachycardia:
  1. Orders “crash cart”
  2. Follow most recent ACLS protocols (per 2012):
     1. Requests/Initiates CPR
     2. Synchronized Cardioversion at 100-200 J
     3. 1 minute of CPR after initial cardioversion
     4. Initiate Bag valve mask ventilation
     5. Repeat synch cardioversion at 200J—this should stabilize back to sinus rhythm with regain of consciousness
     6. If you chose to continue resuscitation after 2 shocks then CPR to be continued and give epinephrine 1mg every 3-5 minutes; consider lidocaine/amiodarone

-Calls Cardiac Critical Care unit for admission.

**VI. Case Narrative:**

1. Information from triage, to be provided at start of encounter:
   1. Chief Complaint: “I can’t breathe.”
   2. Demographics: W.H. a 50-year-old female.
   3. Vitals: Temp 98.8 BP 210/138 HR 110 RR 28 SaO2 85% RA
2. Scenario conditions initially, give only what is asked for:
3. HPI: She states her shortness of breath started about 4 days ago.
4. Symptoms are worse at night.
5. Props up her head with 3 pillows (new for past 3 days).
6. Often wakes up in the middle of the night with a sensation of suffocation; has to go outside to catch her breath or sleep in the recliner to get back to sleep.
7. Used to be able to walk 1 mile without difficulty. Now, can barely walk to the end of her driveway without feeling out of breath.
8. Associated with a dry cough, although over the last day she reports coughing up pink frothy sputum.
9. ROS: No fevers, palpitations, or chest pain.
10. Weight gain of about 10 pounds in the past 1-2 weeks.
11. Legs have gotten symmetrically more swollen in the past 1 week.
12. No change in diet; usual is country fried chicken with grits (loads of butter), liter of Pepsi, and fried snickers.
13. No previous cardiac evaluation: Unknown ejection fraction.
14. PMH: Once told she had hypertension, but is “diet controlled.”
15. PSH: None.
16. Meds: Ibuprofen 600mg daily for knee pain.
17. Allergies: NKDA.
18. Social Hx: Former smoker, quit 25 years ago, drinks 1 beer / week, no drugs.
19. Family Hx: Mother and father both died when she was young, so she doesn’t know.
20. Initial physical exam (only give what is asked for)
21. General: Patient appears uncomfortable, using accessory muscles of respiration.
22. Neck: Supple. +JVD, + hepatojugular reflux, trachea midline.
23. Cardiac: Tachycardic, Normal S1, S2, with S3. No murmurs or rubs. Equal pulses with beat-to-beat variation in amplitude.
24. Pulmonary: Rales at bilateral lung bases with decreased air entry at the bases.
25. Abdomen: S/NTND. No ascites. No hepatomegaly.
26. Extremities: 2+ pitting LE edema.
27. If students asks for anything else, report findings as normal.
28. **Scenario Branch Point:**
    1. See “Critical Actions Checklist for CHF” page 2:
       1. If patient is appropriately treated then patient has less shortness of breath and he feels better
       2. If patient is NOT appropriately treated then he continues to decompensate –more shortness of breath, worse hypoxia, starts to have chest pain
    2. Regardless of treatment, five minutes into the scenario patient goes into ventricular tachycardia:
       1. Change Rhythm generator to ventricular tachycardia
       2. Pt reports: worsening shortness of breath, palpitations leading to unconsciousness.
       3. VS: HR 190, BP 80/50, RR 0 Pulse ox: 94%
       4. Physical Exam: Generally: patient minimally responsive, moans to painful stimuli, Respiratory: decrease air movement diffusely, Cardiac: tachycardic, weak peripheral pulses, Abdomen: soft,
    3. Follow “Critical Actions Checklist for Ventricular Tachycardia” on page 2 regarding diagnosis and treatment
    4. Patient to regain consciousness after 2nd cardioversion

**VII. Instructor Notes**

1. Tips to Keep Scenario Flowing and Direct Actors:
2. Provide the patient’s chief complaint to the learners on arrival to your station. Try to only give information that would be provided from triage.
3. Give history, demographic, and clinical examination findings only as asked, this is provided in case narrative. Use the outline to guide your peers through the appropriate actions.
4. Elicit differential diagnosis prior to providing imaging/laboratory results.
5. Encourage participation by prompting learners if necessary, but do not direct them (i.e., “Is there anything else you would like to add/additional tests you would like to order?”).
6. Remind peers of the importance of re-evaluating patient, otherwise will not recognize V-Tach as it develops. The main focus of the case is reacting to the change in the patient condition.
7. Show the learners the imaging results for appropriate parts of the case (below).
8. Follow along and make sure the teaching checklist (critical actions) points have been addressed, if not addressed during simulation, address them during the debriefing.
9. Scenario programming
10. Optimal treatment path:
11. CHF:
    1. Recognizes signs and symptoms of CHF.
    2. Begin with giving oxygen: Nasal cannula or non-rebreather.
    3. Begin work-up CXR, labs (including BNP), EKG.
    4. Reduce Cardiac workload:
       1. Decrease preload: nitroglycerin (IV drip>SL>, paste), Diuretics (e.g. furosemide), Morphine.
       2. Decrease afterload: ACEi, higher dose nitroglycerin in higher doses.
12. After change to Ventricular Tachycardia:
    * + 1. Re-assess stable vs unstable (in this case, the patient is Unstable due to altered mental status-hence why your cardiovert).
        2. Reorder EKG.
        3. Follow appropriate ACLS guidelines (start CPR, Synchronized cardioversion at 100-200 J)
13. Potential error paths:
14. Not recognizing CHF or providing incorrect treatment, patient’s breathing worsens.
15. Does not recognize rhythm change or fails to recognize unstable VT with hypotension, blood pressure continues to decrease and pt. becomes unresponsive.
16. **Debriefing Plan**
17. Method of debriefing: Team leader-led, group style, with support items and references.
18. This discussion should be limited to 5 minutes in total and supported through reading of materials (articles, reference books, etc).
19. Highlight salient questions, physical examination findings, laboratory/imaging studies, and current management/therapies.
20. Actual debriefing materials
21. Treatment of V-Tach (list of possible sources provided below under References).
22. Additional Materials team leader discovers on their own while preparing for instruction for this case
23. Rules for debriefing: Establish at onset of debrief by group.
24. Establish goals
    1. What could be done better/differently?
    2. What was done well?
25. Questions to facilitate debriefing
    1. What are the signs of CHF?
    2. How do you initial treatment of CHF?
    3. What are the causes of decompensated heart failure?
    4. What is the initial assessment for V-tach?
    5. How is V-tach treated?
