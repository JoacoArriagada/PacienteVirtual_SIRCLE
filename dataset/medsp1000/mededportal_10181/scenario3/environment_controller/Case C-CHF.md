**IV. Environment**

1. Lab Set Up: ED, Trauma Bay, Simulation Room.
2. Manikin Set Up: Any mannequin-based simulation model, functions with low fidelity mannequin with rhythm generators or high fidelity mannequins, IV medication: epinephrine, lidocaine, amiodorone, inotropes, lasix, nitroglycerine, morphine..
3. Props: ECG, X-ray, defibrillator, Rhythm generator Airway/Code Cart, oxygen, CPAP, ventilator,
4. Distractors: None

**V. Actors**

1. Roles: Instructor – preferably medical student-instructor but can be attending physician, Senior or Junior Residents
2. Participants: Medical Students

**VI. Case Narrative:**

1. Information from triage, to be provided at start of encounter:
   1. Chief Complaint: “I can’t breathe.”
   2. Demographics: W.H. a 50-year-old female.
   3. Vitals: Temp 98.8 BP 210/138 HR 110 RR 28 SaO2 85% RA
2. Scenario conditions initially, give only what is asked for:
3. HPI: She states her shortness of breath started about 4 days ago.
4. Symptoms are worse at night.
5. Props up her head with 3 pillows (new for past 3 days).
6. Often wakes up in the middle of the night with a sensation of suffocation; has to go outside to catch her breath or sleep in the recliner to get back to sleep.
7. Used to be able to walk 1 mile without difficulty. Now, can barely walk to the end of her driveway without feeling out of breath.
8. Associated with a dry cough, although over the last day she reports coughing up pink frothy sputum.
9. ROS: No fevers, palpitations, or chest pain.
10. Weight gain of about 10 pounds in the past 1-2 weeks.
11. Legs have gotten symmetrically more swollen in the past 1 week.
12. No change in diet; usual is country fried chicken with grits (loads of butter), liter of Pepsi, and fried snickers.
13. No previous cardiac evaluation: Unknown ejection fraction.
14. PMH: Once told she had hypertension, but is “diet controlled.”
15. PSH: None.
16. Meds: Ibuprofen 600mg daily for knee pain.
17. Allergies: NKDA.
18. Social Hx: Former smoker, quit 25 years ago, drinks 1 beer / week, no drugs.
19. Family Hx: Mother and father both died when she was young, so she doesn’t know.
20. Initial physical exam (only give what is asked for)
21. General: Patient appears uncomfortable, using accessory muscles of respiration.
22. Neck: Supple. +JVD, + hepatojugular reflux, trachea midline.
23. Cardiac: Tachycardic, Normal S1, S2, with S3. No murmurs or rubs. Equal pulses with beat-to-beat variation in amplitude.
24. Pulmonary: Rales at bilateral lung bases with decreased air entry at the bases.
25. Abdomen: S/NTND. No ascites. No hepatomegaly.
26. Extremities: 2+ pitting LE edema.
27. If students asks for anything else, report findings as normal.
28. **Scenario Branch Point:**
    1. If patient is appropriately treated then patient has less shortness of breath and feels better
    2. If patient is NOT appropriately treated then patient continues to decompensate with more shortness of breath, worse hypoxia, and chest pain
    2. Regardless of treatment, five minutes into the scenario patient goes into ventricular tachycardia:
       1. Change Rhythm generator to ventricular tachycardia
       2. Pt reports: worsening shortness of breath, palpitations leading to unconsciousness.
       3. VS: HR 190, BP 80/50, RR 0 Pulse ox: 94%
       4. Physical Exam: Generally: patient minimally responsive, moans to painful stimuli, Respiratory: decrease air movement diffusely, Cardiac: tachycardic, weak peripheral pulses, Abdomen: soft,
    3. After the second cardioversion, the patient regains consciousness.

**VII. Instructor Notes**

1. Tips to Keep Scenario Flowing and Direct Actors:
2. Provide the patient’s chief complaint to the learners on arrival to your station. Try to only give information that would be provided from triage.
3. Give history, demographic, and clinical examination findings only as asked, this is provided in case narrative. Use the outline to guide your peers through the appropriate actions.
4. Elicit differential diagnosis prior to providing imaging/laboratory results.
5. Encourage participation by prompting learners if necessary, but do not direct them (i.e., “Is there anything else you would like to add/additional tests you would like to order?”).
6. Remind peers of the importance of re-evaluating patient, otherwise will not recognize V-Tach as it develops. The main focus of the case is reacting to the change in the patient condition.
7. Show the learners the imaging results for appropriate parts of the case (below).
8. Runtime response notes:
9. If CHF is not recognized or treatment is incorrect, the patient’s breathing worsens.
10. If the rhythm change is not recognized or unstable VT with hypotension is not addressed, blood pressure continues to decrease and the patient becomes unresponsive.
