# Explanation of when to use resources:

3. Purpose of each resource:

c. History and Physical: the history that is given to the learners at the beginning of the simulation case, this includes the vital signs for the start of the case as well as the physical exam. The actor playing the parent is given this form before the case so that they are able to give the history to the learners once the case starts.

d. Triage/Transport document: the document that is in the simulation room when the learners arrive, this gives them some information about the case, the age of the patient, the vital signs that have been obtained and where the patient is located within the hospital.

e. Scenario branch points: document that details how the case is intended to flow and what happens when the learners do not follow the intended sequence of events

f. Chest radiograph: shows a pediatric chest radiograph with an implantable pacemaker

g. Laboratory results: these are common labs that a learner may ask for in this scenario, in our setting we print out the labs so that we can use them when the case is run again

h. EKGs: these are the EKGs if requested. An old EKG showing ventricular paced beats will be given if asked for. A current EKG with complete heart block, bradycardia, and absence of pacing spikes

# Environment:

1. Set- Up: An emergency room medical resuscitation bay or PICU patient room. The learners may be notified that the patient has arrived (via EMS or transport team) and the heart rate is noted to be low.   
2. Mannequin Set-up: High fidelity simulation mannequin (may use child or modify scenario to use adult if child is unavailable)   
3. Props: Code cart, defibrillator with mannequin appropriate pads or paddles, chest radiograph, EKGs, laboratory results including a blood gas if requested.

# Actors:

1. The child’s parent can be played by a case instructor, this person answers the participant’s questions about the history

2. PICU/ED nurse: typically played by one of our own PICU or pediatric ED nurses, may also be played by a case instructor. The nurse functions in a typical nursing role

3. Any consultants: the team may place a consult to any subspecialist; pediatric cardiology, if in the ED may consult pediatric intensive care physician. To place a consult they may call on the phone in the room, or say out loud to whom they want to consult. They pretend to call in the room and speak to a “consultant” who is one of the case instructors. Typically the consulted team is not available to come to the bedside forcing the learners to perform the necessary procedures (in this case transcutaneous pacing.)
