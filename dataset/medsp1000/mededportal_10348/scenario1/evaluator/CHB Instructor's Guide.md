# Purpose of this module:

To teach health care professionals how to identify the signs and symptoms of complete heart block in a child with single ventricle physiology presenting with a pulse but poor perfusion. In addition, learners will review stabilization using PALS algorithms and medical management of a patient with complete heart block and poor perfusion.

The educational objectives are below.

# Educational Objectives:

Primary Objectives

1. Be able to identify/ diagnose a patient with complete heart block 2. Understand and implement the correct management for a patient with complete heart block with bradycardia and a pulse with poor perfusion

Secondary objectives

3. Be able to follow the PALS algorithm for bradycardia with poor perfusion 4. Identify signs of adequate capture during transcutaneous pacing

At the end of this session, the learner will be able to identify a patient with complete heart block, implement the correct management following PALS algorithm and explain signs of adequate capture during transcutaneous pacing.

# Explanation of when to use resources:

2. How the authors implemented the module:

When the participants arrive they will be divided into groups of 4-6. The module is to be used in a simulation setting (simulation lab or in situ) with an area able to gather to debrief after the simulation is finished. The module takes approximately 45 minutes to complete. Each group completes the simulation module (approximately 25 minutes). Once all of the groups have completed the module the case is discussed using the debriefing questions and then the PowerPoint presentation is reviewed. At the end of the discussion of the case each learner is evaluated via the evaluation sheet provided.

3. Purpose of each resource:

a. Case outline: Instructor’s Guide: provides a thorough description of the case flow, participant roles, past use, and potential pitfalls.   
b. Scenario template: an overall description of the intended audience, educational objectives, the H&P, the vital sign changes based on the assumed critical changes, the planned progression of the scenario, the debriefing questions and the evaluation of the learner.   
c. History and Physical: the history that is given to the learners at the beginning of the simulation case, this includes the vital signs for the start of the case as well as the physical exam. The actor playing the parent is given this form before the case so that they are able to give the history to the learners once the case starts.

d. Triage/Transport document: the document that is in the simulation room when the learners arrive, this gives them some information about the case, the age of the patient, the vital signs that have been obtained and where the patient is located within the hospital.

e. Scenario branch points: document that details how the case is intended to flow and what happens when the learners do not follow the intended sequence of events

f. Chest radiograph: shows a pediatric chest radiograph with an implantable pacemaker

g. Laboratory results: these are common labs that a learner may ask for in this scenario, in our setting we print out the labs so that we can use them when the case is run again

h. EKGs: these are the EKGs if requested. An old EKG showing ventricular paced beats will be given if asked for. A current EKG with complete heart block, bradycardia, and absence of pacing spikes

i. Debriefing questions: questions intended to cover the most important learning elements of this case

j. Trainee evaluation: to be completed by the lead instructor as well as all other instructors present for the case, an evaluation form should be completed for all participants

k. PowerPoint presentation: given after the discussion, this presentation is designed to solidify the knowledge that was gained by participating in this case scenario.

# Environment:

1. Set- Up: An emergency room medical resuscitation bay or PICU patient room. The learners may be notified that the patient has arrived (via EMS or transport team) and the heart rate is noted to be low. The learners should identify that the patient has bradycardia and instruct the nurse and other participants of what they need, including IV access, ekg, radiographs, labs and IV fluid.   
2. Mannequin Set-up: High fidelity simulation mannequin (may use child or modify scenario to use adult if child is unavailable)   
3. Props: Code cart, defibrillator with mannequin appropriate pads or paddles, chest radiograph, EKGs, laboratory results including a blood gas if requested.

# Actors:

1. The child’s parent can be played by a case instructor, this person answers the participant’s questions about the history

2. PICU/ED nurse: typically played by one of our own PICU or pediatric ED nurses, may also be played by a case instructor. The nurse functions in a typical nursing role

3. Any consultants: the team may place a consult to any subspecialist; pediatric cardiology, if in the ED may consult pediatric intensive care physician. To place a consult they may call on the phone in the room, or say out loud to whom they want to consult. They pretend to call in the room and speak to a “consultant” who is one of the case instructors. Typically the consulted team is not available to come to the bedside forcing the learners to perform the necessary procedures (in this case transcutaneous pacing.)

# Instructors Notes:

1. Scenario programming: Optimal management path – the patient is placed on supplemental oxygen, heart rate and blood pressure remain low and patient is poorly perfused, she is treated with epinephrine with minimal increase in blood pressure and heart rate, atropine is attempted with no response, transthoracic pacing should be initiated with expert consultation for more permanent pacemaker placement verbalized.

2. Major errors:

a. Not recognizing bradycardia: Nurse may need to point out to the learners bradycardia with poor perfusion (hypotension, depressed mental status, poor perfusion)   
b. Not recognizing that the patient has complete heart block   
c. Not treating bradycardia with medications   
d. Not initiating transcutaneous pacing when oxygen and medications have no response
