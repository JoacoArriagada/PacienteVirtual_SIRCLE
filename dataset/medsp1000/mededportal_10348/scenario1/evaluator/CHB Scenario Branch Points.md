**Pediatric Complete Heart Block – Scenario Branch Points**

Optimal management path

* Group made aware that the patient is in the ED/PICU, they form a team and evaluate the patient
* Complete vital signs are obtained and pt is noted to be bradycardic with poor perfusion
* Learner should check for pulse and provide supplemental oxygen
* Lab work is sent including CBC, BMP, CBG
* Learner should ask for ECG
* Learner should recognize hypotension with bradycardia and give epinephrine
* Minimal response to epinephrine so learner may ask for atropine
* Learner should prepare for transcutaneous pacing
* PICU Attending and Cardiology should be consulted
* Learner gives sedation/analgesia with Ketamine and begins transcutaneous pacing
* If the learner does not give Ketamine the patient will moan during pacing
* If the learner does not initiate transcutaneous pacing the patient will develop a PEA arrest and will not be resuscitatable unless she is given 3 rounds of epinephrine

Potential Complications

* Failure to form a team
* Failure to recognize bradycardia with a pulse and poor perfusion
* Failure to initiate medication (epinephrine/atropine)
* Excessive fluid boluses given for hypotension
* Failure to provide sedation/analgesia prior to transcutaneous pacing
* Failure to initiate transcutaneous pacing
* PICU attending/Cardiology not called
* Failure to plan for more permanent pacemaker (transvenous or generator change)
