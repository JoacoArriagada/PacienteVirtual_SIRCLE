1. Objectives:
   1. Recognize hypoglycemia as a stroke mimic in the Emergency Department
   2. Assess airway, breathing and circulation in a patient with altered mental status
   3. Appropriately treat hypoglycemia
   4. Avoid tPA in a patient with an alternative diagnosis to an ischemic stroke.
2. Critical Actions
   1. Identify hypoglycemia as a cause of altered mental status
   2. Assess airway, breathing and circulation
   3. Order bedside capillary blood glucose test
   4. Avoid tPA
3. Case Overview
   1. Patient presents to the ED with stroke like symptoms. The patient has hypoglycemia and a UTI, which are causing these symptoms. A bedside capillary blood glucose test is key to identifying the cause of the symptoms. If no bedside glucose test is performed the patient will seize and decompensate. The patient should be treated and admitted

8. Case course
   1. 66 y/o male presenting with altered mental status and left sided hemiparesis today he has hypoglycemia that is a stroke mimic – patient must have a bedside capillary glucose test to identify this – it will not come back with the labs. If not identified patient will have a seizure. Patient will have a seizure in CT scan if sent before bedside capillary glucose test.
   2. Patient should be given Dextrose (25 g of dextrose, 50ml of 50% solution would be appropriate) for his hypoglycemia. After Dextrose administration patient will wake up and stay awake – no complaints.
   3. If tPA is given the case must be stopped and debriefing must include emphasis on bedside capillary glucose measurement before any stroke treatment
   4. Resident should order all labs including urinalysis – can reveal that there is urinary tract infection
   5. Patient should be admitted for blood sugar monitoring and antibiotics

1. Debriefing points:
   1. All strokes must have a bedside capillary glucose immediately
   2. Keep differential diagnosis broad in strokes – may be undifferentiated
   3. Any history from a diabetic must include their insulin, dosing and when they took it
   4. Keep in mind the need for many repeat bedside capillary glucose levels in patients on oral meds such as sulfonylureas (consider octreotide for overdoses and need admission)
   5. Importance of looking for infectious processes in older people with hypoglycemia – Urinary tract infection, pneumonia
   6. Our job in the ED in strokes is to consider other diagnosis besides the “easiest” diagnosis – avoid the anchoring error simply because neurology is in the room
   7. How to correct hypoglycemia – Dextrose and then recurrent hypoglycemia and need for a 5 or 10% dextrose IV infusion (D5 or D10).
