1. Objectives
   1. At the end of this exercise the participant will be able to:
      1. Describe an approach to a critically ill patient in the Emergency Department including ABC’s
      2. Appropriately order oxygen monitor and IV access on a ill patient
      3. Recognize acute pulmonary embolus symptoms and risk factors and initiate appropriate treatment including intravenous fluids, and appropriate anticoagulation
      4. Describe the appropriate diagnostic testing for a patient with suspected PE
2. Case Overview
   * 1. 50 y/o female presents from home with severe chest pain. This is a case of a massive pulmonary embolus. The resident must do an initial ABC assessment, OMI and recognize the patient is unstable. The patient must receive fluid resuscitation, diagnostic tests must be done. and treatment initiated with heparin or equivalent anticoagulation and admission.
3. Data
   1. CXR – clear
   2. EKG – Sinus Tachycardia 135

1. Scenario Description
   1. HPI:
      1. 50 y/o female from home with chest pain. Started 5 hours ago suddenly. Worse with deep breaths, + shortness of breath. No fevers/chills. No cough. No hemoptysis. She also feels generally weak and a little dizzy. No recent surgeries. No cancer history
      2. PE risk factors: (If directly asked)
         1. Patient has some trauma to her leg while hiking about a month ago, she fell on her leg and had a bruise/abrasion to the posterior calf.
         2. Patient smokes cigarettes
      3. PMH:
         1. Asthma
      4. Meds:
         1. Albuterol
      5. Allergies
         1. NKA

5. Case Progression
   1. The patient presents in moderate distress and should be treated initially with IVF support and Oxygen. If no oxygen patient should become progressively more hypoxic and short of breath. PE should be considered in differential diagnosis and diagnostic tests should be done. If PE not diagnosed patient should code due to repeat infarct.
