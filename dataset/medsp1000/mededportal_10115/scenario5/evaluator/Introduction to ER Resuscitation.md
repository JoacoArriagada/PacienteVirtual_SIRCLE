1. Objectives
   1. At the end of this exercise the participant will be able to:
      1. Describe an approach to a critically ill patient in the ED including evaluation of airway, breathing and circulation
      2. Appropriately order oxygen, cardiac monitor and obtain IV access on a ill patient
      3. Recognize sepsis and initiate appropriate treatment including Intravenous fluids (1-2 liters), and appropriate antibiotics based on the patients history (community acquired pneumonia coverage)
      4. Describe the appropriate antibiotics to use in a patient with pneumonia in the ED including community acquired coverage versus hospital acquired pneumonia coverage.
2. Case Overview
   1. Patient presents with sepsis from pneumonia. The learner must diagnose sepsis and begin proper resuscitation. The patient should receive oxygen, intravenous fluids such as normal saline or lactated ringers (1 to 2 liters), antibiotics (appropriate community acquired pneumonia coverage) and be admitted to the ICU.
3. Data:
   1. Radiology – CXR with pneumonia
   2. EKG with sinus tachycardia
4. Scenario Description
   1. Background
      1. HPI:
         1. A 72 year-old female patient presents with a complaint of feeling generally weak and short of breath for several days. The patient arrives via EMS when the neighbor called because she “looked pretty sick” while attempting to get her newspaper. She has had a mild cough for a “long time”, but always has some cough because she smokes cigarettes. She has felt generally weak for about a week but has not passed out or had chest pain. The patient is vague about other symptoms, although she hasn’t had any abdominal pain, N/V or black/bloody stool. No recent hospitalizations.
      2. PMH:
         1. “Asthma” and a “little stroke a few years ago”
      3. Social/Family History:
         1. Smoker (40 pack/yr), Mother had “Lung cancer”
      4. Meds:
         1. Some kind of inhaler – doesn’t really use it
      5. Allergies:
         1. NKDA
5. Initial Simulator Settings:
   1. Hr 126, BP 105/65, RR 30, Sat 88%RA Temp 102.2
   2. Lungs: Crackles – right side, Wheezes – left side
   3. Monitor: Sinus tachycardia
   4. Heart: normal settings
   5. Airway: normal settings
6. Physical Exam
   1. General appearance – tachypneic in mild-moderate respiratory distress, retractions
   2. Lungs: diffuse wheezing
   3. Cardiac: tachycardia, no murmurs
   4. Abdomen: Normal
   5. Neurologic Exam: Normal
   6. Skin: flushed and warm, no rashes
   7. Extremities: no edema
   8. Remainder of exam is normal
7. Labs/results\*:
   1. CBC: WBC 18.9 x 109//L, Hct 38.9% Plt 220 x 109/L
   2. Chem: Na 138 mEq/L, K 4.4 mmol/L, Cl 104 mmol/L, HCO3 26 mmol/L, BUN 32 mmol/L Creat 1.9 umol/L, Gluc 118 mg/dl
   3. BNP: 58, INR: 1.02, CK: 88, Trop:0.02 (normal), D-dimer: 120 (normal)
8. Case Progression:
   1. The patient presents in moderate distress and should be treated initially with bronchodilators, which relieve the wheezing. Sepsis progresses quickly – the HR rises to 145 and the BP declines to 88/58. The patient will respond to a fluid challenge and will normalize if greater than one liter of crystalloid is administered. NRB O2 will increase sat to 96%. Rapid diagnosis of pneumonia by CXR should lead to the ordering of blood cultures and Antibiotics. The patient should be admitted to the ICU
