**Scenario 1: Seizure with apnea**

Objectives:

At the end of this session the learner will be able to:

* List the steps to initially approach the airway in an apneic patient
* Describe and properly use basic airway equipment such as the OPA/NPA, bag valve mask, ventimask, nasal cannula and laryngoscope

I. Scenario:

Residents are asked to come immediately to the room of a patient who just had a seizure. He is described as a 32-year-old male who has a history of seizures. When the nurse was taking his history he began to have another, it resolved on its own but now patient has sonorous snoring respirations in the bed. He is breathing spontaneously and appears to be sleeping.

II. Exam

Vital signs: RR 8, O2Sat 90%, Pulse 100, BP 150/80, Temp 98.6

Lungs: clear to auscultation bilaterally

Airway: patent but some secretions

Neurologic Exam: withdraws to pain all extremities, sleeping, some groaning to deep stimulation, pupils equal round and reactive (post-ictal characteristics)

Cardiovascular: No extra heart sounds, normal exam

Remainder of the exam is normal

III. Case Course: The goal of this scenario is for the residents to apply O2, and position the patient properly. They can use a nasal airway. If they attempt an oral airway the patient will cough. If properly positioned and O2 applied the patients status will improve and he will wake up. If the learners do not apply O2 or change position the oxygen saturation can worsen and the RN can suggest the proper treatment.
