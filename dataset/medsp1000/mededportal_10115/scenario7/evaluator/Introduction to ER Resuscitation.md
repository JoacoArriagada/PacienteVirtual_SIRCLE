1. Objectives
   1. At the end of this exercise the participant will be able to:
      1. Describe an approach to a critically ill patient in the ED including assessment of the airway, breathing and circulation
      2. Appropriately order oxygen, monitor and IV access on a ill patient
      3. Recognize anaphylaxis and initiate appropriate treatment including epinephrine (0.3mg 1/1000 IM).
      4. Recognize the need for repeat epinephrine IM injection when patient with anaphylaxis has recurrent symptoms
2. Case Overview
   1. Patient presents with anaphylaxis. The learner must initiate resuscitation and treatment. If timely intervention does not occur the patient will decompensate
3. Data:
   1. Radiology – chest x-ray normal
   2. EKG - sinus tachycardia
   3. Labs/results:
      1. Not needed in this case
4. Scenario Overview
   1. HPI: A 34 y/o female presents with shortness of breath. Pt. was at a picnic and she felt a sting. Shortly afterward the pt. felt tingling around her lips and then friends noted that she was flushed and her lips appeared swollen. Pt. presents in moderate respiratory distress with throat tightness and audible wheezing.
   2. Past Medical History:
      1. “Asthma”
   3. Social/Family History:
      1. Nonsmoker, no alcohol or drugs
   4. Meds:
      1. Some kind of inhaler – doesn’t really use it
   5. Allergies
      1. No prior allergic reactions
5. **Simulator Settings**:
   1. HR 150, BP 90/60, RR 40, Sat 88%RA Temp 98
   2. Lungs: wheezing settings diffusely
   3. Monitor: Sinus tachycardia
   4. Heart: normal
   5. Airway: normal
6. **Physical Exam**:
   1. General appearance – tachypneic in mild-moderate respiratory distress
   2. Lungs Wheezes diffusely
   3. CV: no murmurs
   4. Abdomen: Normal
   5. Skin: Flushed with some hives noted
   6. Extremities: normal pulses initially, normal
   7. Remainder of exam is normal
7. Case Progression
   1. The patient presents in moderate distress and should be treated initially with epinephrine early. The resident should administer O2 and IV but those should not delay 0.3 mg IM epinephrine administration. The patient will have some improvement with the epinephrine but then will decompensate again and require another IM epinephrine injection (and therefore admission). If no epinephrine is administered the patient will decompensate (hypotension and tachycardia, hypoxia) and expire from refractory hypotension/cardiac and respiratory arrest
8. Debriefing Points: (guideline)
   1. Initial approach to the ill patient
      1. Airway, breathing and circulation assessment
      2. Oxygen administration
      3. Monitor
      4. IV placement
   2. Approach to Anaphylaxis
      1. Recognition
      2. Allergic reaction vs. anaphylaxis (more than one system)
         1. Hives
         2. Airway
         3. Lungs
         4. GI
         5. Vascular
      3. IVF – need blood pressure support
      4. Review Epinephrine administration and dosing
         1. Adminstraion of 0.3 mg of 1/1000 concentration of epinephrine intramuscular (Epi-pen)
         2. IV epinephrine: 0.1 mg of the 1/10,000 concentration of epinephrine IV infused over 5-10 minutes or IV infusion of epinephrine in severe anaphylaxis 1:10,000 concentration (1mg/250 mL D5W) at 1-4 mcg/min
         3. Adjuncts and evidence – steroids, Pepcid, Benadryl
      5. Disposition
9. Admit vs. discharge
