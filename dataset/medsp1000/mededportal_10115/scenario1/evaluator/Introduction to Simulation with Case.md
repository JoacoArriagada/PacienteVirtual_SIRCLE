**2. Introduction to Simulation Case 1 – Allergic Reaction**

1. Goal: To introduce simulation based learning to interns utilizing a patient with an allergic reaction to peanuts. A history and physical exam should be performed as well as emphasis on the “verbalization” of findings and data by the resident. The emphasis is not on proper dosing and treatment but the administration of a case and the tools available to them as they approach their education in the simulation center
2. Objectives:
   1. At the end of this session the resident will be able to
      1. Obtain an appropriate history while participating in a simulation case
      2. Do a physical exam both verbally and physically utilizing sim man
      3. Describe a basic approach to a patient who presents to the Emergency Department with a medical complaint
      4. Feel increased comfort with the equipment available while participating in a simulation case
      5. List the elements of their participation that will lead to a successful case and debriefing during their simulation education in residency
3. Case Description
   1. HPI: A 23 y/o female presents with a rash and some wheezing after eating at a Chinese restaurant 30 minutes ago. She has a known allergy to peanuts but didn’t know the chicken she was eating had them. She has had this before and knows “just what to do”. She did not take any medications prior to arrival.
   2. ROS: Denies chest pain, fevers, cough, throat closing or any other symptoms
   3. Past Medical History: None
   4. Medications: None
   5. Allergies: Peanuts
4. Simulator Settings:
   1. VS initially: HR 110, BP 120/80, RR 20, Sat 97% RA, T 38
   2. Lungs: wheezes bilaterally
   3. Monitor: Sinus Tachycardia
5. Exam:
   1. Lungs: Diffuse wheezing
   2. Heart: tachycardic
   3. Skin: Hives over upper extremities and face, warm
   4. Abdomen: Soft non-tender,
   5. Extremities: No swelling, non-tender, good pulses
6. Trend: The patient will be forthcoming with the peanut allergy. The resident should ask a history and do an exam. If they order medicine the patient will prompt them- “I usually will take Benadryl when this happens” or “ I think I need my epi pen”. The patient will improve with this treatment.
7. Debriefing points
   1. Debriefing is a “safe” environment, confidential
   2. Debriefing is “learner led” – participation is vital
   3. Observers must critically observe and give constructive criticism to one another
   4. As adult learners – you get out what you put into it, preparation prior to sessions
   5. Verbalize everything in the cases, it takes adjustment to ask about what you can’t see
   6. Review the monitor, equipment and Sim-man based on how the resident did in the session
   7. Brief review of basic allergic reaction treatment
      1. ABC, OMI
      2. Antihistamines
      3. Epinephrine IM for systemic symptoms (dose not important in this case)
