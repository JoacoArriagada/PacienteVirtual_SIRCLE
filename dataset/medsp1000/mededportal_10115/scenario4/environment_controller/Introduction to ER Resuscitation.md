**Case 1 Altered Mental Status**
2. Overview
   1. A “frequent flier” (patient who frequently visits the emergency department) is placed in the hallway seemingly intoxicated but he has an intracranial bleed. Proper recognition that this is not just alcohol intoxication and diagnosis of the bleed with a head CT and treatment must be administered to prevent the patient from decompensating.
3. Data:
   1. CT head with subdural hematoma
   2. Chest x-ray Normal
4. Scenario Description
   1. Background
      1. HPI
         1. A 42-year-old “regular” homeless alcoholic is placed in the hallway in the back of the ED with intoxication. On resident evaluation the patient is not responsive. He cannot give any history. The nurse reassures the resident that the patient is “always like this”.
      2. Past Medical History
         1. Drug and Alcohol abuse
      3. Social/Family History:
         1. Homeless
      4. Meds:
         1. None
      5. Allergies
         1. None
   2. Initial Simulator Settings
      1. HR 60, BP 160/40, RR 12, Sat 95% on RA, Temp 96
      2. Lungs: CTA
      3. Pupils: Left 5mm and sluggish,, right is 3 mm and reactive
      4. Heart: No murmurs
   3. Physical Exam
      1. General: unresponsive, has a gag, sonorous respirations
      2. Lungs: Clear to auscultation
      3. Neurologic: withdraws to pain in all extremities, not moving, no groaning to stimulation,
      4. Head: abrasion to forehead
      5. Neck: trachea midline, intact
      6. Abdomen: soft nontender to palpation
      7. Heart: regular rate and rhythm no murmurs
   4. Case Progression
      1. The patient is severely altered. Random capillary blood glucose measurement is 101. CT of the head reveals an intracranial bleed. If CT is not done, the patient will seize and decompensate to bradycardia and cardiac arrest due to the bleed. There will be no response to naloxone.
