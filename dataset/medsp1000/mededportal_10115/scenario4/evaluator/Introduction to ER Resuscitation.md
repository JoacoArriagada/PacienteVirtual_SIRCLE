**Case 1 Altered Mental Status**

1. Objectives:
   1. By the end of this session the learners will
      1. Perform airway, breathing and circulation assessment
      2. Request oxygen, monitoring, IV placement and blood pressure measurement
      3. Correctly administer oxygen and bag valve mask patient with proper positioning
      4. Appropriately evaluate a patient with altered mental status including bedside capillary blood glucose measurement, physical exam including neurologic exam
      5. Describe a basic differential diagnosis for altered mental status
      6. Appropriately disposition a patient with an intracranial hemorrhage
2. Overview
   1. A “frequent flier” (patient who frequently visits the emergency department) is placed in the hallway seemingly intoxicated but he has an intracranial bleed. Proper recognition that this is not just alcohol intoxication and diagnosis of the bleed with a head CT and treatment must be administered to prevent the patient from decompensating.
3. Data:
   1. CT head with subdural hematoma
   2. Chest x-ray Normal
4. Scenario Description
   1. Background
      1. HPI
         1. A 42-year-old “regular” homeless alcoholic is placed in the hallway in the back of the ED with intoxication. On resident evaluation the patient is not responsive. He cannot give any history. The nurse reassures the resident that the patient is “always like this”.
      2. Past Medical History
         1. Drug and Alcohol abuse
      3. Social/Family History:
         1. Homeless
      4. Meds:
         1. None
      5. Allergies
         1. None
   2. Initial Simulator Settings
      1. HR 60, BP 160/40, RR 12, Sat 95% on RA, Temp 96
      2. Lungs: CTA
      3. Pupils: Left 5mm and sluggish,, right is 3 mm and reactive
      4. Heart: No murmurs
   3. Physical Exam
      1. General: unresponsive, has a gag, sonorous respirations
      2. Lungs: Clear to auscultation
      3. Neurologic: withdraws to pain in all extremities, not moving, no groaning to stimulation,
      4. Head: abrasion to forehead
      5. Neck: trachea midline, intact
      6. Abdomen: soft nontender to palpation
      7. Heart: regular rate and rhythm no murmurs
   4. Case Progression
      1. Airway, breathing and circulation must be assessed. It must be noted that patient is severely altered. Rapid acquisition of random capillary blood glucose measurement (101) and CT of the head. The CT reveals an intracranial bleed. If CT not done patient will seize and decompensate to bradycardia and cardiac arrest due to bleed. Intubation would be an option as well. There will be no response to naloxone.
5. Debriefing points
   1. Approach to Altered Mental Status
      1. Do not dismiss the intoxicated patient
      2. ABC
      3. Oxygen, Monitor, IV
      4. Bedside capillary glucose measurement
      5. Naloxone if opiates suspected
      6. Neurologic examination
   2. Differential diagnosis of altered mental status
      1. Hypoglycemia, hypoxia, intoxication or overdose, intracranial bleeding or other mass, infection, encephalopathy, metabolic issues
   3. Diagnosis of acute head trauma
      1. Rapid Head CT
   4. Possible Intubation
   5. Consult neurosurgery rapidly
