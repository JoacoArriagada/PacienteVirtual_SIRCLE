# Standardized Patient Checklist – Margaret Pelli

You are evaluating the way the student interacts with you as a patient across five different categories of interaction – verbal skills; manner; how an exam, treatment or procedure is handled; counseling skills or the way in which information is conveyed; and, finally, the student’s level of empathy or support and the rapport which you feel is present between you and the student. This will produce a composite score that is a measure of the student's provider relations skills in your encounter. By way of multiple assessments across a number of SP encounters, we hope to inform and shape the way the student interacts with patients in preparation for his/her entrance into professional practice as a caregiver.

In the following tables, the shaded items – the category scores (Verbal Skills Score, Manner Score, etc.) -- count towards the student’s total score; the unshaded items – “guidance questions” -- are meant to guide your thinking and should largely determine how you score the student in each of the section scores. However, they do not “count” towards the student’s final score (their points are not tallied to create the section scores), but students will be able to see how they are assessed on each question. Because your section scores are not based solely on the guidance questions, elements of the student’s style which have not been captured by the guidance questions can figure in to the category scores – in other words, your “feelings” about that student’s performance in that category should subtly influence the nuance of your scoring in that category.

# Explanation of scale used for the category scores

RUDIMENTARY (0 pts) -- Desired behavior absent or demonstrated at the RUDIMENTARY level (undeveloped, elementary). Should be reserved for behaviors that exemplify what not to do in a patient encounter.

MERGING (1 pts) -- Desired behavior is EMERGING -- present but not yet exhibited at minimally acceptable level

riting only 0 or 1 pt would benefit from corrective instruction on the scored item and are at risk for alienating patients at their current level

ACCEPTABLE (2 pts) -- Desired behavior is exhibited at an ACCEPTABLE level -- enough to not disrupt the patient-provider relationship but not enough to promote it.

EXPECTED (3 pts) -- Desired behavior is exhibited at the EXPECTED level and supporting not just a productive patient-provider relationship but patient satisfaction as well. Should be the highest score awarded in most instances.

EXEMPLARY (3.25 pts) -- Demonstration of this behavior is at an exemplary level APPROACHING IDEAL; it's better than expected and should serve as a model to others. This score should be used rarely and reserved only for the most outstanding demonstrations of specific behavior.

This instrument is based on the Doctor/Patient Interaction instrument developed by Michael Curtis of Temple University School of Medicine c.2012, used with permission of the author.
