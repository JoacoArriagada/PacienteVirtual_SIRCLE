**Instructor Guide- Margaret Pelli Standardized Patient Case**

**Resource Files**

The Margaret Pelli standardized patient case can be easily reproduced and utilized where standardized patient encounters are occurring. There are 7 case specific documents being uploaded with the submission of this case. There is also an instructor’s guide and introduction to the case being uploaded separately. The Margaret Pelli documents include:

1\_Case Document\_Margaret Pelli.pdf

2\_Medical Chart\_ Margaret Pelli.pdf

3\_Medical Chart Supplement1\_Clock Drawing Test\_Margaret Pelli.pdf

4\_Documentation Exercise \_Margaret Pelli.pdf

5\_Performance Checklist\_Margaret Pelli.pdf

6\_Documentation Grading Sheet\_Margaret Pelli.pdf

7\_Standardized patient Checklist\_Margaret Pelli

**Document 1** is the actual case document and should be studied by the standardized patient, encounter facilitator, and encounter grader prior to the encounter taking place. This document should be used for all standardized patient training as a way to ensure accuracy of case portrayal. **Document 2**, the medical chart, should be presented to the student immediately prior to entering the exam room for the encounter. **Document 3** is the clock drawing test which is to be included with the medical chart for students to have with them before walking into the exam room. **Document 4** is the documentation or SOAP note sheet. This should be used by the student following the exam room encounter for SOAP note documentation. The individual assigned to grade this case will use **Documents 5 and 6** to grade the student’s performance on the case in the exam room and on the SOAP note. Finally, **Document 7** will be used by the standardized patient to grade the student’s performance from a humanistic perspective after the encounter has ended.

*Note: The Mini Mental Status Examination should be laid out for the student to use if they deem necessary in the exam room.* *The MMSE* *can only be reproduced with original author permission. Use and dissemination of this tool must be requested on an individual basis.*

**Purpose /Objectives**

* **The purpose of the Margaret Pelli SP case is to provide students an opportunity and scenario in which they can demonstrate competence with the Mini Mental Status Exam and Clock Drawing Test.**
* **Students will administer the MMSE and the CDT and document the results in SOAP format.**
* **Assess forgetfulness/memory loss in a senior adult**
* **Conduct a cognitive screening using the Mini Mental Status Exam and the Clock Drawing Test.**
* **Document and interpret scores.**

**Conceptual Background**

The Margaret Pelli standardized patient (SP) case was developed at the University of Medicine and Dentistry of New Jersey’s –School of Osteopathic Medicine (UMDNJ-SOM) to be 1 of 3 possible SP cases used in the medical student’s year 2 geriatrics course. The case was developed by members of the New Jersey Institute for Successful Aging (NJISA) at UMDNJ-SOM, and Sima C. Bennett, MPH, RD., Director of the Clinical Education & Assessment Center. Experts in the field of geriatrics and standardized patient case development played a major role from initial planning to completion of the project. Numerous planning, brainstorming, case development, and implementation meetings took place prior to the completion and implementation of the Margaret Pelli case. All related case materials were designed by the team assembled to create this case.

**Practical Implementation**

This case provides students with the experience of conducting an in-depth functional assessment with a geriatric patient in a simulated clinical situation. Administering, assessing, and scoring an MMSE is a basic skill that all medical students should show proficiency in. This training will have an impact on students going forward in their education, training, and future career. This encounter’s difficulty is geared towards a lower level medical student trainee. All encounters will be limited to a total of 15 minutes with a 10 minute post-encounter exercise (total of 25 minutes). Students will receive an email from the Clinical Skills Assessment Center with an assigned date and time for their SP session. They arrive to the SP lab with ample time for orientation to the session and to get prepared and ready for the encounter. Students are given a medical chart for the SP case prior to entering the exam room. Once they are in the room, students will have both the MMSE and Clock Drawing Test in their possession if they deem necessary to use during the encounter. After the exam room encounter, students are given a SOAP note form to fill out in a different room. The case is scored by a second standardized patient who views the encounter. The standardized patients go through 4 training sessions leading up to the delivery of this case. All cases and topics are covered in the 2 hour long sessions, as well as inner rater reliability topics. Each SP actor also meets one on one with lab staff continually throughout the portrayal of an SP case, to insure accuracy. Quality assurance of the case portrayal are conducted by the lab director, lab staff, and course education staff prior to case delivery and while the case is being carried out. The overall grade on the encounter counts towards a portion of the student’s year 2 geriatrics course grade at UMDNJ-SOM. Each student is given a grade on their performance checklist and their documentation grading. Each item is marked as either correct or incorrect. The student’s over all case grade is made up of 80% of the performance checklist and 20% of the documentation grading.

**Successful deployment**

The case has been utilized in both the 2011 and 2012 medical students’ year 2 geriatrics courses at UMDNJ-SOM. Students are required to complete a standardized patient encounter as part of their course grade. Students are asked to perform a component of a geriatric assessment on a standardized patient in the clinical skills lab, and they do not have prior knowledge of their assigned case. Students are provided with the standardized instruments to use in each encounter. Faculty members who make up our undergraduate education team have assessed this case both during creation, and on a yearly review basis. If any changes are recommended they are brought before the team for review. Students are required to fill out course reviews at the completion of each undergraduate course. Comments and scoring about this encounter are consistently positive, with the majority of students applauding the detailed experience. We utilize a longitudinal assessment system in our institute which tracks student performance, skills, attitudes, and knowledge over the course of their medical education. When our students move on to their MS III clerkship they are required to go through a 6 case OSCE encounter. The MMSE shows up again in 2 of the 6 cases during their OSCE. Students consistently perform well on the MMSE in their 3rd year, leading to the conclusion that the training that they receive in this case goes forward with them. Over the last 2 deployments of the Margeret Pelli case student have performed well. The combined Mean (SD) is an 86 (10), with an n = 316 students.

**Limitations**

The Mini Mental Status Exam can only be reproduced with the original author permission. Use and dissemination of this tool must be requested on an individual basis. There are no other identifiable limitations to the Margaret Pelli standardized patient case. There is continued quality control measures put in place by the clinical skills lab to ensure the case is portrayed correctly. Currently, the Margaret Pelli standardized patient case will be used in the up and coming year 2 geriatrics course in 2013.
