Case 5
# Patient script: STD

You are Ms. Taylor. You are in the clinic to learn the results of the lab work ordered 2 days ago. On that date, you came to the office complaining of burning with urination, a greenishyellow, creamy vaginal discharge, and lower abdominal discomfort which had been bothering you for two days. You think you may have a yeast infection. You have been married for 12 years and have two children ages 8 and 6. You have been monogamous in your married relationship.

The patient knows very little about STD’s and when hearing the diagnosis responds:

“What exactly is gonorrhea?” “How do you get it?”   
If the student says only through sex her response is “Haven’t I heard of other ways to get it like from toilets, or hot tubs? There’s a hot tub at the gym.”   
She is still in disbelief that this could only be sexually transmitted and asks again “Are you sure?”   
When she says something like “Well I told you I only have sex with my   
husband...” and the light bulb begins to go off in her head that she got it from her husband.   
She tries to push this thought from her mind by asking more questions for   
information. For example:   
“Is this a common disease?” “Could my husband have gotten it at the Health Club?” “I don’t understand what you’re saying.” “ How long do you think I’ve had this?” “How long do you think he’s had this - could he have gotten before our marriage?” “Are there any long term effects?”   
Finally she gets very angry with her husband and shouts such things as:   
“That bastard. How could he do this to me?” “Why did he do this to me? We’ve got a great marriage…” “It’s probably that young secretary in his office.“What am I going to tell my kids? That their father is unfaithful?” “I’ll never sleep with him, again. In fact, I’ll divorce him! How could he do this to me?” “Oh god, what am I going to do? What should I do?”   
She will ask what happens from here. “How will you treat this?”   
She also wants to know what to do about her husband.

“What about my husband? Shouldn’t he get treated?”

“Will you call him and tell him? I can’t talk to him.”

“What if he doesn’t get treatment?”

“Towards the end of the session if the student has not provided HIV counseling Mrs. Taylor may start asking about the potential that she could have other STDs. Mrs. Taylor’s response will depend on the how calming and supportive the student’s response is. If the student is empathetic and makes helpful suggestions, then she will become increasingly calm. If the student takes her side and indicates that her husband is a jerk, then she will get angry at the physician for talking about her husband that way.
