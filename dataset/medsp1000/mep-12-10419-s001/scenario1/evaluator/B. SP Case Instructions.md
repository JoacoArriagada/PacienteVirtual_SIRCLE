| PHYSICAL EXAM FINDINGS |  |
| 1. Written in layman’s terms | 1. Jugular venous distension: when the vein in your neck is distended, it can be suggestive of heart failure. (2) Crackles bilaterally: when crackles are heard in your lungs, the sound is caused by “popping open” of small airways that are collapsed by accumulating fluid. (3) S3 heart sound: this extra sound that we hear when listening to your heart is indicative of heart failure. (4) Displaced point of maximal impulse: the place where your heart is heard most strongly can be displaced if your heart is enlarged – as often occurs in people with heart failure. (5) Pitting edema: fluid accumulates, especially in your ankles, when your heart is not beating with enough strength to pump it all the way back to your heart. |

| MANAGEMENT OR DIAGNOSITIC PLAN | Diltiazem should be discontinued due to heart failure diagnosis. Terazosin should be changed to a more prostate-selective option (tamsulosin, alfuzosin). Carvedilol should be changed to Metoprolol due beta-1 selectivity in patients with asthma. Continue furosemide, lisinopril, Advair, and Albuterol as previously prescribed. Monitor serum creatinine and potassium levels in 2 weeks. Titrate lisinopril and metoprolol doses upward if tolerated in 2 weeks. Counsel patient on purpose and side effects of each medication. Counsel patient on need for monitoring of blood pressure, heart rate, daily weights, and periodic blood work to check potassium and renal function. Encourage patient to purchase over the counter blood pressure machine to monitor blood pressure and heart rate at home. |
