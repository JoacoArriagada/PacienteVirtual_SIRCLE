# Case	Overview

Victoria	Owens	is	a	27-year-old ${ \mathsf { G } } 3 { \mathsf { P } } 2 0 0 2 ^ { 1 }$ at	39	weeks	gestation	presenting	to	Labor	and Delivery	for	induction	of	labor	secondary	to	poorly	controlled	insulin-dependent	gestational diabetes	mellitus2 .	Her	induction	has	been	delayed	because	of	staffing	issues	and	the	Labor	and Delivery	resident	has	been	called	away	to	assist	with	another	delivery.	Victoria	is	furious over	the	delay,	which	is	further	exacerbated	by	her	state	of	nicotine	withdrawal.	The	nursing staff	has	informed	Victoria	that	she	is	unable	to	smoke	in	the	hospital.	During	the	encounter, Victoria	is	aggravated	and	unpleasant.	She	ultimately	repeatedly	threatens	to	leave against medical	advice if	she	is	not	allowed	to	smoke.

Description	of	Affect	and	Behavior:

Victoria	is	agitated	and	unpleasant	at	the	initiation	of	the	encounter.	Moments	before,	she	had expressed	to	her	nurse	that	she	would	like	to	go	outside	to	smoke,	especially	if	she	was	needing to	wait	longer	to	see	her	physician.	The	nurse	informed	Victoria	that	she	was	not	allowed	to	go outside	to	smoke,	nor,	obviously,	was	she	able	to	smoke	in	her	hospital	room.	Victoria	has	been waiting	“hours”	to	see	a	physician	to	begin	the	process	of	her	labor	induction.	When	the interviewer enters	the	room,	Victoria	is	sitting	on	her	hospital	bed,	a	scowl	on	her	face,	arms crossed,	and	visibly	agitated.	As	the interviewer introduces	himself	or	herself	as	a	medical	student, Victoria	interrupts,	exclaiming	frustration	that	not	only	has	she	been	waiting to	see	a	doctor,	instead	“they”	send	a	medical	student	to	care	for	her.	When told of	the	delay,	she	responds	that	if	she	is	not	going	to	be	seen immediately,	she	should	at	least	be	allowed	to	go	outside	to	smoke	while	she	waits. If told that she is not allowed to leave,	Victoria	then	requests	permission	to	smoke	in	her	hospital	room.

Victoria	is	tense,	frustrated,	and	anxious,	but	not	immune	to	communication	strategies.	If	the interviewer validates	her	frustration	or	apologizes	for	the	delay,	Victoria	is	more amenable	to	the	medical	interview.	She	remains	unpleasant	for	the	duration	of	the encounter,	but	she	willingly	answers	questions	and	may	direct	her	anger	away	from	the interviewer.	However,	if	the interviewer does	not	make	any	attempt	at	establishing	rapport	or	neglects	to	demonstrate	empathic	communication,	Victoria	escalates	and	may threaten	to	leave	the	hospital.

If	the interviewer attempts	conversation	in	regard	to	smoking	habits	and	willingness	to	quit, Victoria	is	not	interested	in	quitting	at	the	present	time.	She	shakes	her	head,	rolls	her	eyes, and	avoids	eye	contact	with	the interviewer.	If	pushed,	Victoria	will	begrudgingly	share her	smoking history	(see	below	– Substance	and	Diet	History).	During	the	current	pregnancy,	she	has decreased	her	habit	from	a	pack	per	day	to	five	cigarettes	per	day.	Her	husband	has encouraged	her	to	quit	smoking	and	does	not	smoke.	However,	she	will	remind	the interviewer that	she	is	not	in	the	hospital	for	her	smoking	habit	and	does	not	wish	to	continue	further conversation	on	the	topic.	If	offered	smoking	alternatives,	such	as	a	patch	or	gum, Victoria	will	again	request	to	go	outside.	If	further	denied	this	request,	she	will unhappily	accept	the	alternative	offered.

Throughout	the	encounter,	as	the medical	interview	and	history collection progress,	Victoria	will	respond	to	questions	asked	of	her,	but	is	easily	frustrated	by	the number	of	questions	asked.	She	may	ask	why	so	many	questions are being asked and	whether	the	information	being elicited	is	really	necessary.	Overall,	she	remains	an unpleasant	and	minimally	cooperative	patient.
