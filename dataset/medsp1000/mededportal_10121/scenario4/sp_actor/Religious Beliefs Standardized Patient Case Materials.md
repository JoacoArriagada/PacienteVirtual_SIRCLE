# Case	Overview

Grace	Collins	is	a	22-year-old	G1P0	1 at	22	weeks	5	days	gestation	presenting	to	Labor	and Delivery	from	clinic	for	evaluation	of	an	elevated	blood	pressure	of	180/112, $^ { 3 + }$ proteinuria 2 , and	severe	headache,	concerning	for	pre-eclampsia	with	severe	features.3 On	Labor	and Delivery,	her	labs	confirm	HELLP	syndrome 4 .	Her	clinical	picture	is	likely	to	continue	to deteriorate	and	her	condition	is	life	threatening.	The	only	curative	therapy	is	delivery	of	the fetus,	which	will	result	in	a	non-viable	delivery.

Upon	admission	to	the	Labor	and	Delivery	unit,	discussion	of induction	of	a	non-viable	pregnancy	are	complicated	by	the	patient’s	devout	religious	beliefs and	aversion	to	pregnancy	termination.

Description	of	Affect	and	Behavior:

Grace	is	quiet	and	reserved	at	the	beginning	of	the	interview,	but	clearly	anxious	and	upset.	Her understanding	after	speaking	with	her	prenatal	care	provider	is	that	her	blood	pressure	in	clinic was	high	enough	to	be	concerned	about	her	risk	of	having	a	seizure.	The	headache	she	has been	experiencing	over	the	previous	two	days	may	also	be	related	to	a	condition	called	preeclampsia.	The	urine	test	she	took	in	the	clinic	had	a	significant	level	of	protein	to	cause	further concern.	When	questioned	as	to	what	would	be	done	in	the	hospital,	the	provider	had responded	that	the	only	“cure”	for	her	condition	would	be	delivery.	Her	prenatal	provider insisted	that	she	defer	to	the	physicians	at	the	hospital	where	Grace	was	being	transferred. Grace	is	anxious	to	understand	how	she	could	deliver	such	a	premature	baby.

She	is	compliant	with	the	interview,	but	when	the	topic	of	induction	of	labor	is broached,	Grace	becomes	distraught.	She	questions	whether	an	induction	or	D&E	is	truly	her only	option.	Grace	is	adamant	that	she	cannot	have	“an	abortion”	as	it	is	strictly	forbidden	by her	religious	beliefs.	Although	Grace	is	likely	to	be	informed	that	her	condition	is life-threatening	to	both	her	and	her	child,	she	insists	that	the	only	solution	is	to	entrust	the	life of	herself	and	her	child	to	God.	Grace	feels	that	her	predicament	is	a	test	of	her	faith	and	that clearly	the	wrong	choice	is	termination	of	her	pregnancy.	She	may	appeal	to	the	interviewer,	asking whether	he/she	has	religious	beliefs	similar	to	her	own.	Grace	attempts	to	ally	herself	with	an interviewer who	divulges	a	religious	predisposition.	Should	the	interviewer decline	to	respond	about personal	beliefs,	Grace	is	disappointed,	but	nonetheless	stable	in	her	beliefs.	She	willingly shares	her	religious	beliefs,	should	the interviewer take	the	time	to	explore	her background.	However,	if	the	interviewer	is	dismissive,	Grace	becomes	increasingly	resistant	and upset.

Although	Grace	is	resolute	and	firm,	she	is	polite	during	the	encounter.	She	remains	compliant with	any	questions	asked	of	her.	If	approached	with	the	opportunity	to	speak with	a	religious	representative	of	the	hospital,	she	welcomes	the	offer.	She	will	also	appreciate the	recommendation	of	speaking	with	her	husband,	who	is	on	his	way	to	the	hospital	from work,	and	family	before	coming	to	a	final	decision.	However,	at	no	point	during	the	encounter does	Grace	appear	to	be	amenable	to	consenting	to	the	termination.
