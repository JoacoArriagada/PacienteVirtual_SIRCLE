# Hazmat Simulation: Set-up and Preparation

4) Environment

a) Lab Set Up: “biohazards”; the goal is to recreate a biochemical research laboratory that has been contaminated due to an explosion in the building   
b) Mannequin Set Up: on floor, or possibly on table/bench located within the lab   
c) Props: Appropriate Personal Protective Equipment, AED, basic airway, backboard, basic first aid supplies   
d) Distractors: if possible, dry ice may be used to simulate vapors/fumes secondary to the explosion or possible chemical leap; lights may be off or flickering if desired

5) Actors: groups of 5 medical students

a) Roles: team leader, one student for airway, one for medication administration, two for possible chest compressions, patient and on-scene commander   
c) Action role i) Team leader – final say in all decisions ii) Patient – relays information iii) On scene commander - instructs students about building stability, provides information related to hazardous chemical exposure

# Hazmat Exposure: Simulation Narrative

# 1) Scenario Background and Triage Note (read to team by on scene commander)

At 0800 an explosion occurred in the basement of the Biohazard Laboratory and Research Building, causing significant structural damage and injuring numerous employees. It is unclear if this was due to an act of terror or resulted from equipment malfunction. The building has been evacuated and deemed stable, and Search and Rescue teams are looking for survivors.

One of the Search and Rescue teams has found a middle aged scientist on the floor of his laboratory, where he was known to work with hazardous materials and chemicals. Currently he is experiencing headaches, eye pain and some chest tightness with difficulty breathing. It is unclear at this time if his injuries are related to the explosion or from a contaminated environment. Rescue teams have requested a medical team move in and help the patient.

# Patient History (read to team by on scene commander):

Patient Name: Dr. Hyde   
APVU: Awake, oriented to self, scared   
CC: SOB, chest discomfort, headache, eye pain, dimming vision   
PMHx: none   
Medications: none   
Allergies: none

2) Initial Scenario Conditions: Students are read the scenario triage note and given 3-5 minutes to gather equipment they may need, including Personal Protective Equipment. The team will then be led to the patient by the On-Scene Commander. Students will find the patient isolated in his lab in need of medical care. Upon entering lab, patient is awake and communicating.
