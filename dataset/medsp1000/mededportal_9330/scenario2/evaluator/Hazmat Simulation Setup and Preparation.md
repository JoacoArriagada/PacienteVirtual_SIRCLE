7) Patient Physiology: Nerve agents act by inhibiting synaptic cholinesterase activity and inducing excessive cholinergic activity. Patients exposed to nerve agents will have both muscarinic and nicotinic symptoms. Nerve agents are absorbed through the alveolar-capillary membrane and distributed systemically throughout the blood inhibiting all cholinesterases. Nerve agent vapor is rapidly absorbed and produces clinical symptoms within minutes. Initially, nerve agents bind to papillary muscles and upper respiratory glands producing miosis, rhinorrhea, and salivation. Inhalation of the nerve vapor leads to bronchoconstriction and bronchorrhea. Next, circulating agent affects the gastrointestinal tract causing cramping, abdominal pain, and defecation. Inhibition of peripheral acetylcholinesterase will result in twitching, fasciculations, and weakness. As the syndrome progresses, total flaccidity, arreflexia, and apnea may develop. Excessive central cholinergic stimulation may result in coma, seizures, and central apnea [1].

# Hazmat Exposure: Simulation Narrative

# 1) Scenario Background and Triage Note (read to team by on scene commander)

At 0800 an explosion occurred in the basement of the Biohazard Laboratory and Research Building, causing significant structural damage and injuring numerous employees. It is unclear if this was due to an act of terror or resulted from equipment malfunction. The building has been evacuated and deemed stable, and Search and Rescue teams are looking for survivors.

One of the Search and Rescue teams has found a middle aged scientist on the floor of his laboratory, where he was known to work with hazardous materials and chemicals. Currently he is experiencing headaches, eye pain and some chest tightness with difficulty breathing. It is unclear at this time if his injuries are related to the explosion or from a contaminated environment. Rescue teams have requested a medical team move in and help the patient.

# Patient History (read to team by on scene commander):

Patient Name: Dr. Hyde   
APVU: Awake, oriented to self, scared   
CC: SOB, chest discomfort, headache, eye pain, dimming vision   
PMHx: none   
Medications: none   
Allergies: none

2) Initial Scenario Conditions: Students are read the scenario triage note and given 3-5 minutes to gather equipment they may need, including Personal Protective Equipment. A medical equipment bag will be provided to the team containing multiple MARK 1 kits, diazepam auto injectors, ACLS medications (epinephrine, amiodarone, etc...) and basic first aid supplies. The team will then be led to the patient by the On-Scene Commander. Students will find the patient isolated in his lab in need of medical care. They will proceed to identify and assess Hazmat and don proper level PPE. Upon entering lab, patient is awake and communicating.

a) History Patient Gives: patient complains of dimming vision and eye pain, headache, chest tightness and profuse lacrimation/salivation/rhinorrhea. Patient is mildly confused and unable to detail what chemicals/agents they work with (GCS 14).

b) Initial Exam $\mathrm { T } = 0$ minutes; see initial state on simulation algorithm) $\mathrm { T } = 9 9$ , HR 50 (sinus rhythm), BP 80/40, RR 28, O2 $8 9 \%$ Pharyngeal/tongue edema is not present. Wet/ rhonchorous breath sounds are present bilaterally without signs of pneumothorax. There is obvious rhinorrhea/lacrimation/salivation. No signs of obvious direct trauma or head injury. The patient's eyes are closed secondary to pain, however, examination will reveal miotic, reactive pupils. Skin is diaphoretic. All other physical exam findings not otherwise noted are normal.

# c) Medical Decision Making

Students will have 5 minutes to assess airway, provide oxygen and c-spine stabilization, assess breath sounds, place IV and monitor and obtain vital signs.

$ \mathrm { O 2 }$ and IV fluids will have minimal change on patient's saturation and hypotension. If a ccollar is not placed, this can discussed during debriefing but will not change the play of the case otherwise. Oxygen can be administered using NC (1-10L/min) or NRB, though the result will be the same minimal improvement regardless of route as the definitive treatment is pharmacologic. Venturi-mask is not available.

# 3) Scenario Branch Points

a) At this junction, O2 therapy has raised the Sp02 to $9 2 \%$ , and IV fluids should be administered to address the underlying hypotension. A cholinergic toxidrome should be recognized and a MARK 1 Kit (see part “b” below) or atropine (see part “c” below) should be given. If this critical action is not taken within five minutes of patient contact, the patient will develop worsening bradycardia, hypotension, tachypnea and begin to experience muscle twitching of an extremity, nausea, vomiting, defecation, urination and then rapid CNS involvement and generalized seizures requiring an advanced airway, diazepam injection and MARK 1 kit administration (see initial states (4) and 4(2) and 4(3) on iStan mannequin algorithm).

b) If students recognize cholinergic toxidrome and immediately administer the MARK 1 kit, the patient's vital signs should gradually improve (see initial state 3(2) on iStan mannequin algorithm). The students should then proceed to evacuate the patient from the lab over concern for possible vapor exposure (this may be done verbally instead of boarding and moving the iStan). Once evacuated, unknown liquid exposure has not been ruled out and the patient should be decontaminated immediately and transferred to a medical center for treatment, effectively ending the scenario. If this critical action does not occur, the students will fall behind the dose response curve and the patient may appear stable for a short time before progression of

symptoms seizures death occur (see part “a” above and states 4, 4(2) and 4(3) on iStan mannequin algorithm).

c) If students administer atropine only, there will be a mild improvement in the patient's vital signs and respiratory status (see initial state 3 on iStan algorithm), however, 2-PAM still needs to be administered to address the nicotinic effects (muscle weakness/paralysis) of the organophosphate. As in part “b” above, the patient still needs to be evacuated from the lab to remove the patient from harmful vapor exposure and decontaminated for unknown liquid exposure. If 2-PAM is not administered, the patient will clinically deteriorate leading to seizures death (see part “a” above and states 4, 4(2) and 4(3) on the iStan mannequin algorithm)

d) Following evacuation and decontamination, a focused exam should be performed patient should be sent to a medical treatment facility for definitive care.

e) Students will be debriefed on simulation.

# Instructor's Notes

# 1) Optimal Management Path

a) Students enter room in PPE, recognize patients is in distress and immediate address airway, breathing, circulation and obtain a set of vitals   
b) Student place patient on monitor, provide O2 and IVF   
c) Clinical exam findings point towards cholinergic toxicity, and atropine or MARK 1 kit is given   
d) If only atropine is provided, the need to administer 2-PAM is recognized and given   
e) Patient is removed from laboratory and decontaminated for unknown liquid exposure   
f) Patient is transferred to the ER for further treatment   
g) Simulation Ends

# 2) Potential Errors Path

a) Any actions taken by the medical team that does not involve administration of atropine and 2- PAM or the MARK 1 kits and decontamination will ultimately lead to seizures and death as described above
