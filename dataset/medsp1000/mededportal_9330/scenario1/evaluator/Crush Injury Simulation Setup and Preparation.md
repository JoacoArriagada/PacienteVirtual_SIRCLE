# Crush Injury Simulation: Set-up and Preparation

1) Title: “Crush Injury”

3) Learning Objectives

a) Primary i) Practice safe, advanced life support and extrication of a victim in a confined space emergency ii) Systematically perform BTLS patient assessment and reassessment during the scenarios iii) Demonstrate advanced clinical thinking and decision making skills in patient management as well as understand critical and required actions important to each case

b) Secondary

i) Correctly interpret patient vital signs, physical exam findings and EKGs ii) Understand the pathophysiology behind crush injury/syndrome and necessary treatment iii) Understand the utility of working as a team in the emergency scenario

c) Critical Actions Checklist

i) Assess patient's airway, breathing and circulation and address issues as necessary with reevaluation as indicated (1) Airway: intact, intubation not required (2) Breathing: Albuterol should be used given history of asthma with wheezing (3) Circulation: absent pedal pulse on deformed ankle requires prompt reduction and reassessment for return of perfusion

ii) Conduct brief history and physical exam iii) Correctly diagnose and properly treat underlying conditions

(1) Recognize sinus tachycardia and treat with isotonic IVF   
(2) Recognize significant crush injury with risk for hyperkalemia and begin prophylactic treatment with sodium bicarbonate   
(3) Proper use of immobilization (rigid c-collar, temporary extremity splint, backboard)

7) Patient Physiology: Crush Syndrome: Crush related acute renal failure is one of the few lifethreatening complications of crush injuries that can be reversed. Students will have to address this in this case. The crush syndrome affects many organs. Problems in addition to acute renal failure include sepsis, the acute respiratory distress syndrome, disseminated intravascular coagulation, bleeding, hypovolemic shock, cardiac failure, arrhythmias, electrolyte disturbances, and psychological trauma. Our patient will manifest dysrhythmias secondary to hyperkalemia. Therefore, knowing about and instituting appropriate treatment are important not only for nephrologists and trauma specialists but also for internists, cardiologists, psychiatrists, surgeons, anesthesiologists, intensivists, and generalists, all of whom may be confronted with patients with crush injuries before nephrologists become involved. Late mortality is generally attributable to rhabdomyolysis resulting in the crush syndrome, which is the most frequent cause of death after earthquakes, apart from trauma [2].

# Crush Injury Simulation Narrative

# 1) Background Information and Triage Note (read to team by on scene commander)

A severe tornado has swept through (your city) and the 160 mph winds have destroyed homes and buildings. A local Wal-Mart shopping center collapsed, trapping and injuring over 200 people. The building has been evacuated and the Search and Rescue teams are looking for survivors.

One Search and Rescue teams has found a 45 year old male seeking shelter in a supply room when the building collapsed. He has been isolated for approximately 8 hours and his legs are trapped under a 500lb beam and the team is unable to move him. Construction workers are clearing the area so they can remove the beam but the estimated time will be 45 to 60 minutes. Currently, the patient is short of breath, scared and nervous. Rescue teams have requested a medical team move in and help the patient.

Patient History (read to team by on scene commander):

Patient Name: Bill Smith APVU: Alert and oriented x4, scared CC: SOB, leg pain/unable to move legs PMHx: Asthma, Hypertension Medications: Albuterol inhaler Allergies: none

2) Initial Scenario Conditions: Upon entering the building, students will find the patient trapped under boards/beams covering the lower half of patient's body (they should be instructed as to what beams/objects they may and may not move). He is in obvious distress.

a) History Patient Gives: patient states his chief complaint is SOB and leg pain. He is scared because he is unable to move his legs. AMPLE history will reveal asthma, hypertension and PRN albuterol.

b) Initial Exam $\mathrm { T } = 0$ minutes)   
$\mathrm { T } = 3 7 . 5$ , HR 120 (sinus rhythm), BP 90/50, RR 30, O2 89%   
Alert, tachypneic and anxious   
No airway obstruction or edema, bilateral breath sounds with diffuse wheezing, strong radial pulses, unable to assess pulses in LE due to debris   
GCS 15 with no signs of head injury

c) Medical Decision Making

Students will have 5 min to begin O2, place C-collar, start an IV, put patient on monitor and administer albuterol nebulizer to address patient's wheezing. Failure to do so will result in a decline in vital signs (see state 6 in algorithm).

Oxygen, IV fluids and albuterol nebulizer will bring a slight increase in patient vital signs (see states 2 and 3 in attached document) and for the time being the victim will be considered stable

# 3) Scenario Branch Points

a) If after 5 minutes the students do not address the A,B,C's, the patient will begin to rapidly deteriorate and progress to ventricular fibrillation (see state 7) after which a code should be called and handled as deemed appropriate by faculty and instructors (it is rare for students to not provide oxygen and at a minimum start IV fluids)

b) If prior to $\mathrm { T } = 5$ minutes the team started oxygen, IV fluids (at least 500cc bolus) and started albuterol nebulizers, they will be told that a crane is now in position to remove the beam (or debris). The on-scene commander will ask the team leader if it is okay to remove the beam.

c) Patient Reassessment (prior to extrication)   
$\mathrm { T } = 3 7 . 5$ , HR 120 (sinus rhythm), BP 90/50, RR 30, O2 $9 2 \%$   
Airway remains intact, breath sounds are minimally improved with bilateral wheezing

d) Medical Decision Making: at this junction, students will have one minute to decide whether to remove the beam or to give IV bicarbonate prior to removal

if the beam is removed without giving IV bicarbonate, the patient will quickly deteriorate and progress to PEA arrest (hyperkalemia) or ventricular fibrillation (see state 7) requiring ACLS (whether the patient recovers or dies is up to the instructor).   
Other standard ACLS/paramedic medications are available (ex: calcium, insulin/dextrose) and may be used at any point, though their use prior to ventricular fibrillation should be discussed during debriefing if employed.

if 50-100 mEq IV bicarbonate are given prior to extrication, the patient will be considered stable and students should proceed with the secondary survey and removal from the building (see state 5)

# 4) Secondary Survey and removal from building

a) Patient Reassessment: following removal of the beam and addressing and treating the complications from hyperkalemia, the students should again reassess the patient (see state 5)

$\mathrm { T } = 3 7 . 5$ , HR 120 SR, BP 100/62, RR 30, O2 $9 2 \%$

Secondary physical exam: Patient remains alert and oriented x 3. Head is normocephalic and atraumatic. HEENT is unremarkable. Spine is nontender without deformity or stepoff. Heart is tachycardic but regular without murmurs. Lungs have equal breath sounds bilaterally with good air exchange and end-expiratory wheezing only (unless Albuterol not given, then wheezing remains). Chest wall is nontender without trauma. Abdomen is soft, nontender and nondistended. There is no flank bruising. Pelvis is stable. Upper extremities show some scattered superficial abrasions. Legs also have some scattered abrasions and contusions, especially below the knees. There is also a disfigured right ankle with absent DP/PT pulses. A temporary splint will be available in the student's equipment and the ankle should be stabilized. If a splint is not placed, the reduction will not remain stable and the foot will lose pulses again until a splint is placed.

Following temporary splint placement, the patient should be immobilized and placed on backboard with plan to transfer to appropriate care facility. Failure to use a backboard can be discussed in the debriefing.

# Instructor's Notes

# 1) Optimal Management Path

a) Students enter room, recognize patient is in distress, recognize potential for crush injury and obtain initial set of vitals   
b) Students proceed down ATLS algorithm and check patient's airway, place patient in c-collar, listen for bilateral breath sounds, and check pulses   
c) Patient is given O2, placed on monitor with sinus tachycardia being recognized, and started on IVF (rate of 1 L/hr.)   
d) Students recognize victim is high risk for crush syndrome and give 50-100 mEq IV bicarbonate prior to extrication to prevent dysrhythmias resulting from hyperkalemia   
e) Secondary survey discovers disfigured ankle and absent pulses, and splint is used   
f) Patient is properly immobilized and placed on backboard for transfer   
g) Simulation ends

# 2) Potential Errors Path

a) Patient is not placed in c-collar during airway assessment   
b) Bilateral breath sounds are not heard and a chest tube is placed   
c) Sinus tachycardia is not recognized on monitor and students attempt to cardiovert or defibrillate patient   
d) Patient is intubated

e) Bicarbonate is not administered prior to extrication f) Deformed ankle with absent pedal pulse is not reduced and immobilized
