**Appendix D**

**Example Case Study: Closure of a Coal Power Plant in Waukegan, Illinois**

**(Simulated Case)**

It provides background information and a description of a hypothetical meeting of two Illinois internal medicine physicians with their state legislator who represents Waukegan, Illinois. The physicians live and work in that district. The meeting occurs in early 2021 when a coal fired power plant is still operating in Waukegan. The legislature is working on a bill to promote clean energy which would phase out all coal power plants in the state. The physicians arranged the meeting to advocate for passage of this bill. In this policy advocacy, the physicians highlight the health benefits in Waukegan from shutting down that power plant.

**Background information on the coal fired power plant operating in Waukegan, Illinois (2019 data)**

The plant is located north of Chicago, Illinois on Lake Michigan and began operations in 1952. The estimated health impacts per year attributable to the plant’s fine particulate matter emissions are:

* Deaths: 13
* Hospital admissions: 1
* Asthma emergency room visits: 3
* Heart attacks: 3
* Acute Bronchitis: 8
* Asthma attacks: 154
* Work loss days: 724

The plant’s other types of toxic air, water, and land pollution are associated with additional mortality and morbidity. Its annual air emissions include (tons):

* Sulfur dioxide – 753
* Nitrogen oxides – 819
* Carbon dioxide – 1,528,941

**Background information on the Illinois clean energy legislation pending in early 2021**

* Illinois passed the Climate and Equitable Jobs Act in 2021 following a multi-year effort by advocates of clean energy.
* The law requires all fossil fuel plants in the state to close by 2045.
* While the law was being negotiated, the owner of the Waukegan power plant announced on June 17, 2021, that the plant would close in 2022.
* The law funds a “just transition” for coal mine and power plant workers and communities.
* Other states with comprehensive laws to transition to clean energy include California, New York, Massachusetts, and Washington.

**Physician Advocacy Meeting Sample Script**

1. Describe the bill or proposal.

*The clean energy legislation would phase out power plants in Illinois that burn coal or natural gas.  It would create incentives for generating energy by wind and solar power systems, and for installing battery storage.  As part of this transition, the Waukegan Generating Station would be shut down and there would be programs to redevelop the site and provide job training for the workers.*

1. Recognize objections/challenges and costs.

*Importantly, the legislation would address the needs of communities like Waukegan where power plants would close through incentives to locate clean energy production and storage as well as training for jobs in energy efficiency and clean energy.*

*Also, we understand that the costs of generating electricity by wind and solar power systems are less than the costs of operating and maintaining old coal plants like Waukegan. Supplying clean, cheaper electricity will be good for Waukegan’s households and businesses.*

1. Describe support from prior legislation.

*Closing the powerplant is consistent with Waukegan’s new Sustainability Plan unanimously passed in 2021and the fits with a proposal for the new Comprehensive Plan passed in 2018, helping guide decisions related to economic development that include environmental impacts.*
