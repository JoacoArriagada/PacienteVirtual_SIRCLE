**Appendix D**

**Example Case Study: Closure of a Coal Power Plant in Waukegan, Illinois**

**(Simulated Case)**

It provides background information and a description of a hypothetical meeting of two Illinois internal medicine physicians with their state legislator who represents Waukegan, Illinois. The physicians live and work in that district. The meeting occurs in early 2021 when a coal fired power plant is still operating in Waukegan. The legislature is working on a bill to promote clean energy which would phase out all coal power plants in the state. The physicians arranged the meeting to advocate for passage of this bill. In this policy advocacy, the physicians highlight the health benefits in Waukegan from shutting down that power plant.

**Background information on the coal fired power plant operating in Waukegan, Illinois (2019 data)**

The plant is located north of Chicago, Illinois on Lake Michigan and began operations in 1952. The estimated health impacts per year attributable to the plant’s fine particulate matter emissions are:

* Deaths: 13
* Hospital admissions: 1
* Asthma emergency room visits: 3
* Heart attacks: 3
* Acute Bronchitis: 8
* Asthma attacks: 154
* Work loss days: 724

The plant’s other types of toxic air, water, and land pollution are associated with additional mortality and morbidity. Its annual air emissions include (tons):

* Sulfur dioxide – 753
* Nitrogen oxides – 819
* Carbon dioxide – 1,528,941

The population most at risk from the emissions is disproportionately poor people of color, including thousands of children.

Within 3 miles Within 12 miles

* Population 62,372 387,393
* Children 20.944 120,319
* People of color 78% 43% (above state average of 35%)
* Poverty rate 21% 12% (state average of 14%)

**Background information on the Illinois clean energy legislation pending in early 2021**

* Illinois passed the Climate and Equitable Jobs Act in 2021 following a multi-year effort by advocates of clean energy.
* The law requires all fossil fuel plants in the state to close by 2045.
* While the law was being negotiated, the owner of the Waukegan power plant announced on June 17, 2021, that the plant would close in 2022.
* The law funds a “just transition” for coal mine and power plant workers and communities.
* Other states with comprehensive laws to transition to clean energy include California, New York, Massachusetts, and Washington.

**Physician Advocacy Meeting Sample Script**

1. Introductions of the participants/interest/expertise

*Hello\_\_\_\_\_\_\_. Thank you for meeting with us.*

*We are Dr. X and Dr. Y, internal medicine physicians who work and live in your district, and care for patients adversely impacted by the air pollution and greenhouse gases related to the emission of the power plant.*

*We and our patients are harmed by the air pollution from this outdated coal power plant. We are seeing more respiratory and cardiovascular illness related to poor air quality. Additionally, this plant and others in Illinois are major sources of greenhouse gases*. *Climate change is worsening the health of people in Waukegan now and the problem is growing.*

*We are here to discuss our concerns about the Waukegan Generating Station and our support for the pending Illinois clean energy legislation to improve the health of our community.*

1. Describe the bill or proposal.

*The clean energy legislation would phase out power plants in Illinois that burn coal or natural gas.  It would create incentives for generating energy by wind and solar power systems, and for installing battery storage.  As part of this transition, the Waukegan Generating Station would be shut down and there would be programs to redevelop the site and provide job training for the workers.*

1. Explain problem to address.

*This legislation would transition Illinois from burning coal and natural gas to clean, renewable forms of energy.  It would set deadlines for closing coal and natural gas power plants.  This bill would promote better health in Illinois and Waukegan.*

*The pollution emitted from the Waukegan Generating Station increases asthma, bronchitis, allergies, heart attacks, and other respiratory and cardiovascular illnesses. These are terrible illnesses for many people that cannot be fully remedied by medication. The community is suffering from excess deaths, hospital admissions and emergency room visits, lost workdays, and reduced school attendance. In fact, these conditions are contributing to more COVID-19 illness, hospitalizations, and deaths in Waukegan.*

*As for the coal plants’ contributions to climate change due to its emissions, our patients are vulnerable to increasing heat stress which is especially dangerous for the elderly, pregnant women, and infants. Many people in the community live in poverty without affordable access to air conditioning. Their housing is often in areas that turn into urban heat islands on hot days and do not cool off at night.*

*Moreover, the Waukegan coal power plant is polluting the groundwater and soil. Toxins from burning coal like mercury are damaging our patients’ brains and other organs.*

1. Describe risk or benefits of addressing bill (focus on health impacts)

*Closing the Waukegan coal plant will improve air quality in the area. Closing all coal and natural gas power plants in Illinois will help limit heat waves and flooding from extreme storms.*

*Our state can generate electricity in ways that do not make our residents sick and die prematurely. Our patients and the Waukegan community should not have to breathe polluted air or suffer from the heatwaves and floods associated with climate change.*

*About 390,000 people live within 12 miles of the plant, including 120,000 children. Most of the residents within 3 miles of the plant are people of color and the poverty rate is 50% higher than the state average.*

*We are seeing increased respiratory and cardiovascular diseases in our offices.  More children are missing school because they must stay home or even go to a hospital emergency room when air quality is bad. We advise our patients to check the local air quality index and wear masks to protect them from particulates, even when COVID-19 mandates end. We caution patients against working outdoors during heatwaves. The polluted air and associated medical problems in Waukegan cause huge costs for the community.*

*Also, we hear from our colleagues in obstetrics that they are seeing a rising number of preterm or low birth weight babies. These medical dangers are related to heat and bad air quality.*

*State and federal environmental regulations implemented over the past twenty years reduced the fine particulate matter and mercury emitted by coal power plants. While these regulations helped the people of Waukegan, more should be done. Advances in wind, solar and battery technologies now allow for cost-effective replacement of these old, polluting power plants.*

1. Recognize objections/challenges and costs.

*Importantly, the legislation would address the needs of communities like Waukegan where power plants would close through incentives to locate clean energy production and storage as well as training for jobs in energy efficiency and clean energy.*

*Also, we understand that the costs of generating electricity by wind and solar power systems are less than the costs of operating and maintaining old coal plants like Waukegan. Supplying clean, cheaper electricity will be good for Waukegan’s households and businesses.*

1. Describe support from prior legislation.

*Closing the powerplant is consistent with Waukegan’s new Sustainability Plan unanimously passed in 2021and the fits with a proposal for the new Comprehensive Plan passed in 2018, helping guide decisions related to economic development that include environmental impacts.*

1. Communicate relevant personal stories and supportive imagery.

*(Example) One recent patient is 15 years old with worsening shortness of breath and wheezing who lives about one mile from the Waukegan power plant and attends a neighborhood school. Testing confirmed asthma caused by inflammation in the airways worsened by breathing polluted air.  I prescribed inhalers. Even with this medication, the patient frequently experiences interrupted sleep and cannot participate in outdoor recreational activities important for overall health and required   several emergency room evaluations for asthma attacks that were severe and not remedied by the inhalers.  On days when the air quality is bad, the patient often stays home and misses school with resultant impacts on academic standing.*

1. Ask for specific action.

*Please support rapid passage of clean energy legislation for Illinois.*

1. Thank the audience, offer further information, and contact information.

*Thank you for taking the time to meet with us on this important legislation for the health of the people in your district and throughout the state. Here is a factsheet we prepared for you.  Do you have any questions or concerns that we could address?  We would be happy to provide additional information.  You can contact us by phone or email. We are also leaving our contact information to provide further information on this important issue impacting your constituents and the people of Illinois.*
