**Two human-patient simulations to illustrate acute uncal herniation**

**UNCAL HERNIATION IN THE OPERATING ROOM ("OR Scenario")**

**Patient background**

The patient is a 42 year-old previously healthy man with a 4-week history of headache, nausea, vomiting, and photophobia. He has been hospitalized for the last 2 days, receiving dexamethasone and GI prophylaxis. When the patient was seen preoperatively the night before he was sleepy but conscious, had some mild photophobia, but overall was reporting feeling better.

**Past Medical History**: benign and non-contributory to the scenario.

**Meds and allergies**: The patient does not have medication allergies. Medications have been described.

**Family/social history**: Non-contributory.

**Patient presentation and response boundaries**

- History patient gives: none. The patient is somnolent.
- Initial presentation: eyes open, but not conversant. If spoken to, the simulated patient does not answer.
- The patient is becoming increasingly somnolent.
- When the scenario advances, the patient loses consciousness and remains unable to answer questions.
