**Two human-patient simulations to illustrate acute uncal herniation**

**I. Goal**

These human-patient simulation scenarios give participants the opportunity to experience, treat, and receive feedback on their care of patients who are experiencing an acute uncal herniation (UH). The scenarios also allow participants to apply elements of effective teamwork, like sound communication techniques, workload distribution, and patient assessment and reassessment, in caring for these unstable simulated patients.

**III. Learning objectives**

After this simulation exercise, the participants will be able to:

1. Describe the signs and symptoms of an acute increase in intracranial pressure (ICP)
2. State effective strategies for treating patients with increased ICP
3. Describe the effects of the following on ICP, cerebral blood flow (CBF) and cerebral metabolic requirement for oxygen (CMRO2):
   1. PaCO2
   2. PaO2
   3. Propofol
   4. Sodium thiopental
   5. Etomidate
   6. Volatile agents
   7. N2O
   8. Fentanyl
   9. Muscle relaxants
   10. Mannitol
   11. Furosemide
   12. The reverse Trendelenburg position
4. State the rationale for avoiding and using succinylcholine in patients with increased ICP
5. Demonstrate effective communication strategies
6. Describe how the distribution of tasks can impact anesthesia crew functioning

**IV. Background and scenario overview**

This scenario can be used for team or crisis resource management training as well as focusing on an individual anesthesia care providers' response to a patient experiencing an acute uncal herniation. The scenario occurs in an operating room with a fully prepared and functioning anesthesia machine and all necessary airway devices and medications available. The simulated patient is 70 kg and 170 cm and was previously healthy.

In the OR scenario the patient experiences an acute increase in intracranial pressure (ICP) before induction of general endotracheal anesthesia for a craniotomy to remove a glioma. The patient is on the OR table, breathing room air, and fully monitored. A simulation confederate (playing an anesthesiology technician) reports that the patient is becoming increasingly somnolent. When the participant approaches the simulator the uncal herniation is triggered, representing time T=0 for the scenario.

**UNCAL HERNIATION IN THE OPERATING ROOM ("OR Scenario")**

**V. Events, actions, debriefing points and teaching points**-A table of events and actions for the OR scenario, which also includes debriefing questions and teaching points, is included with this resource. This resource is meant to facilitate reproducing this scenario in another simulation laboratory.

**VI. Assessment objectives:**

* 1. **Primary**:
     1. *Hyperventilation with a face-mask*
     2. *Place the patient in Reverse Trendelenburg position*
     3. *Call for help (neurosurgeons and anesthesia providers)*
     4. *Administer propofol*
     5. *Administer muscle relaxant (rocuronium or succinylcholine)*
     6. *Direct laryngoscopy and endotracheal intubation*
     7. *Mechanical hyperventilation and maintenance anesthesia*: this objective marks the conclusion of the OR scenario.
  2. **Secondary**:
     1. *Administer mannitol and furosemide*
     2. *Administer fentanyl or appropriate vasoactive drugs to manipulate the hemodynamics*
  3. **Critical actions checklist (Presented in the order the authors' presumed would represent optimal performance)**:
     1. Hyperventilation-Initially with a face-mask, and then continued throughout the scenario. Participants should ventilate to at least maintain normocarbia.
     2. Call for help-The patient's neurosurgeon should be notified, and experienced anesthesia providers gathered to treat this patient
     3. Reverse Trendelenburg position
     4. Administer propofol
     5. Administer and wait for muscle relaxant to have an effect
     6. Perform direct laryngoscopy and endotracheal intubation-Performed when blood pressure has been properly controlled
     7. Maintain anesthesia
     8. Administer diuretics, after consulting neurosurgeons
  4. **Time to critical actions** (times represent rough averages or our participants to date, among those who have performed those actions):
     1. UH to face-mask ventilation (FMV)-60-seconds
     2. Call for help-within the first 60 seconds
     3. UH to reverse Trendelenburg position-variably used by our participants (from never to immediately)
     4. UH to propofol-Not yet performed by attending anesthesiologists. Resident physicians-90-120 seconds
     5. UH to muscle relaxant-sometimes not performed by participants in this scenario, but when it is, within 6 minutes of the UH
     6. Muscle relaxation to direct laryngoscopy and endotracheal intubation-30-45 seconds
     7. Endotracheal intubation to maintenance anesthesia-20-30 seconds
     8. UH to diuretics-variably used by our participants (from never to immediately)

**VII. Environment**

* 1. **Lab Set Up**-the operating room, with a fully functioning anesthesia machine and all airway devices and medications drawn up into syringes and ready for use. Mannitol is in a vial (not drawn up into a syringe).
  2. **Mannequin Set Up**-We use the Laerdal SimMan 3G human-patient simulator, which can open and close its eyes, and whose pupils can be dilated and responsive to light. In this scenario, the mannequin starts with open eyes and normal and reactive pupils. It then loses consciousness (closes its eyes), and dilates the left pupil. No moulage is used. The simulator has adequate large bore IV access and an arterial line in place. The scenario starts with the patient breathing room air. Electrocardiogram, pulse oximeter and an arterial blood pressure are displayed on the vital sign monitor.
  3. **Props**-Standard OR set up.
  4. **Distracters**-none.

**VIII. Actors**

1. **Roles**:
   * 1. An anesthesiology technician
     2. A neurosurgeon
     3. More skilled anesthesia-provider help
2. **Who plays them**-A simulation technician or simulation instructor play the technician and surgeon. Other simulation participants or the simulation instructor are the skilled anesthesia-provider help.
3. **Action role**:
   * 1. Anesthesiology technician-has brought the patient into the OR, connected them to monitors, and reports to the participant that the patient is more somnolent than 10 minutes ago. They answer questions about the patient's medical history, point out the location of devices and medications, call for help and can help moderate the scenario with cues from the simulation instructor.
     2. Neurosurgeon-this actor is very anxious and concerned for their patient. They try to hurry the participants along ("Let's get him intubated, we have to get into his head, get his pressure down now!").
     3. Skilled anesthesia-provider help-often this role is played by another participant, and therefore their performance is not scripted. If other participants are not available, then this person is helpful and competent, but does not offer suggestions about the patient's management to the participant.

**IX. Case Narrative**

1. **Scenario Background**:
   1. **Chief complaint**-*Given to participants*-You are scheduled to anesthetize a patient who is having a craniotomy for tumor. The patient is a 42 year-old previously healthy man with 4-week history of headache, nausea, vomiting, and photophobia. His work-up revealed a right temporal glioma that was producing a 2 mm midline shift on computerized tomography (CT) scan. The CT revealed mild cerebral edema and sulci effacement. He has been hospitalized for the last 2 days, receiving dexamethasone and GI prophylaxis. When the patient was seen preoperatively the night before he was sleepy but conscious, had some mild photophobia, but overall was reporting feeling better.
   2. **Past Medical History**: benign and non-contributory to the scenario.
   3. **Meds and allergies**: The patient does not have medication allergies. Medications have been described.
   4. **Family/social history**: Non-contributory.
2. **Scenario conditions initially**
   1. **History patient gives**-none. As above, the patient is somnolent.
   2. **Patients' physiology**-somnolent, bradypneic (RR of 6), SpO2 of 96% on room air, BP 145/60, HR of 70 in sinus rhythm, EtCO2 42 mmHg
3. **Scenario branch points**
   1. **Changes in the patient's condition**-the patient experiences an uncal herniation after the anesthesia technician announces that the patient seems unresponsive.
   2. **Responses to treatment** (see table of actions and events)
      1. Hyperventilation (HV): the patients' condition stabilizes (BP decreases slightly, HR increases slightly, SpO2 improves to 100% in about 3 minutes, and the EtCO2 decreases to 28 mmHg if HV continues over 8 minutes)
      2. Propofol: BP decreases, HR increases
      3. Administer muscle relaxatant
      4. Fentanyl-Dose-dependent decrease in BP
      5. Volatile agent- Dose-dependent decrease in BP
      6. Direct laryngoscopy (DL) and endotracheal intubation-BP and HR increase. The increase is related to the BP at the time of DL, with a higher BP at DL resulting in large increases in blood pressure and heart rate, and a lower BP at the time of DL resulting in smaller increases
      7. Mannitol and furosemide-No change in vital signs

**X. Instructor's notes**

1. **Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer/Tips for actors**-In our experience, this scenario has not required much effort to work according to plan. If the participant is completely flailing in the scenario a neurosurgeon can arrive. For these participants they are calm and helpful (as needed). They can make the diagnosis for a participant that is not "getting it" and offer prodding or suggestions as needed ("it looks like he's herniating," and/or "I need to operate now, please get his pressure down and intubate him as soon as you can...").
2. **Scenario programming**
   1. **Potential complications paths**-Some participants can wait a long time to ventilate the patient, and allow long periods of apnea during the scenario. Some have asked for inappropriate medicines during the herniation (epinephrine, atropine, and ephedrine for example, presumably responding to the bradycardia), and some have treated the hemodynamics with vasodilators (like hydralizine or sodium nitroprusside).

**XI. Debriefing Plan**

1. **Questions to facilitate debriefing**:
   1. What signs of uncal herniation can you identify?
   2. What is causing the observed changes?
   3. What is the ultimate therapy for this patient?
   4. What is the most important action you can take to affect this patient's care?
   5. What are the beneficial effects of propofol for this patient?
   6. What would happen to this patient if you tried to perform direct laryngoscopy without muscle relaxation?
   7. What are the controversies, risks or benefits of using succinylcholine in this patient?
   8. How did you manage the patient's blood pressure during the scenario? Why?
   9. What are the benefits of mannitol and furosemide in the patient? What is the time course of these drugs' effects?
