**Two human-patient simulations to illustrate acute uncal herniation**

**UNCAL HERNIATION IN THE OPERATING ROOM ("OR Scenario")**

**Background and scenario overview**

This scenario occurs in an operating room with a fully functioning anesthesia machine and standard OR set up. The patient is a 42 year-old previously healthy man who has been brought to the OR for a craniotomy for tumor. He is on the OR table, breathing room air, and fully monitored. An anesthesiology technician reports that the patient is more somnolent than 10 minutes ago.

**Case Narrative**

1. **Scenario Background**:
   1. **Chief complaint**-You are scheduled to anesthetize a patient who is having a craniotomy for tumor. The patient is a 42 year-old previously healthy man with 4-week history of headache, nausea, vomiting, and photophobia. He has been hospitalized for the last 2 days, receiving dexamethasone and GI prophylaxis. When the patient was seen preoperatively the night before he was sleepy but conscious, had some mild photophobia, but overall was reporting feeling better.
   2. **Past Medical History**: benign and non-contributory to the scenario.
   3. **Meds and allergies**: The patient does not have medication allergies. Medications have been described.
   4. **Family/social history**: Non-contributory.
2. **Scenario conditions initially**
   1. **History patient gives**-none. The patient is somnolent.
   2. **Patients' physiology**-somnolent, bradypneic (RR of 6), SpO2 of 96% on room air, BP 145/60, HR of 70 in sinus rhythm, EtCO2 42 mmHg
