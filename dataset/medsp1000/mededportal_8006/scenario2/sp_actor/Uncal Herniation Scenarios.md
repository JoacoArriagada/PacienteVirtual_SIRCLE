**Two human-patient simulations to illustrate acute uncal herniation**

**UNCAL HERNIATION IN THE PACU**

**Patient background**

The patient is a 42 year-old previously healthy man who had a craniotomy for tumor about 2-hours ago. He has a 4-week history of headache, nausea, vomiting, and photophobia. His only preoperative medications are dexamethasone and GI prophylaxis. Immediately prior to surgery the patient was conscious, alert and oriented, and neurologically intact.

**Past Medical History**: benign and non-contributory to the scenario.

**Meds and allergies**: The patient does not have medication allergies. Medications have been described.

**Family/social history**: Non-contributory.

**Patient presentation and response boundaries**

- History patient gives: none. The patient is somnolent.
- The patient has always been a bit groggy and difficult to arouse, but seems much sleepier in the last 20 minutes.
- Initial presentation: eyes half open, pupils normal and reactive, but unresponsive to questions.
- The patient has a large, bulky bandage around the head.
- When the scenario advances, the patient loses consciousness and remains unable to answer questions.
