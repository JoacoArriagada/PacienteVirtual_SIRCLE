**Two human-patient simulations to illustrate acute uncal herniation**

**I. Goal**

These human-patient simulation scenarios give participants the opportunity to experience, treat, and receive feedback on their care of patients who are experiencing an acute uncal herniation (UH). The scenarios also allow participants to apply elements of effective teamwork, like sound communication techniques, workload distribution, and patient assessment and reassessment, in caring for these unstable simulated patients. One scenario requires the use of an advanced airway device in this simulated clinical setting.

**III. Learning objectives**

After this simulation exercise, the participants will be able to:

1. Describe the signs and symptoms of an acute increase in intracranial pressure (ICP)
2. State effective strategies for treating patients with increased ICP
3. Describe the effects of the following on ICP, cerebral blood flow (CBF) and cerebral metabolic requirement for oxygen (CMRO2):
   1. PaCO2
   2. PaO2
   3. Propofol
   4. Sodium thiopental
   5. Etomidate
   6. Volatile agents
   7. N2O
   8. Fentanyl
   9. Muscle relaxants
   10. Mannitol
   11. Furosemide
   12. The reverse Trendelenburg position
4. State the rationale for avoiding and using succinylcholine in patients with increased ICP
5. Demonstrate the proper use of a fast-track (or "intubating") laryngeal mask airway in a difficult airway scenario
6. Demonstrate effective communication strategies
7. Describe how the distribution of tasks can impact anesthesia crew functioning

**IV. Background and scenario overview**

This scenario can be used for team or crisis resource management training as well as focusing on an individual anesthesia care providers' response to a patient experiencing an acute uncal herniation. The scenario occurs in the post anesthesia care unit with all necessary devices and medications available for the participants, but not in their plain sight. The simulated patient is 70 kg and 170 cm and was previously healthy.

In the PACU scenario the patient is two-hours status-post a craniotomy for tumor and experiencing an acute increase in ICP that is causing uncal herniation. The patient has the identical size, past medical history, and starting vital signs as in the OR scenario. A simulation confederate (a PACU nurse) tells the participant the patient's history, and that although the patient has never been fully responsive in the unit, they are now completely unresponsive. If queried about narcotics or sedatives, the participants are told the patient has received only a small (2 mg dose) of morphine. Once the participant approaches the simulator the uncal herniation is triggered (time T=0 for the scenario). In the PACU version of the UH scenario, the patient has a large, bulky bandage around their head that precludes the adequate atlanto-occipital extension needed to perform conventional direct laryngoscopy. The only way the patient's trachea can be intubated is with a fast track (or "intubating") laryngeal mask airway (FTLMA).

**VI. Assessment objectives:**

1. **Primary assessment objectives**:
   * 1. *Hyperventilation with a face-mask*
     2. *Place the patient in Reverse Trendelenburg position*
     3. *Call for help (neurosurgeons and anesthesia providers)*
     4. *Administer propofol*
     5. *Administer muscle relaxant (rocuronium or succinylcholine)*
     6. *Attempt direct laryngoscopy*
     7. *Place FTLMA, and perform endotracheal intubation via that*: this marks the conclusion of the simulation scenario
2. **Secondary assessment objectives**:
   * 1. *Administer mannitol and furosemide*
     2. *Administer fentanyl or appropriate vasoactive drugs to manipulate the hemodynamics*
3. **Critical actions checklist (Presented in the order the authors' presumed would represent optimal performance)**:
   * 1. Hyperventilation
     2. Call for help
     3. Reverse Trendelenburg position
     4. Administer propofol
     5. Administer muscle relaxant
     6. Attempt direct laryngoscopy
     7. Place FTLMA, and perform endotracheal intubation via that
     8. Administer diuretics, after consulting neurosurgeons
4. **Time to critical actions** (times represent rough averages or our participants to date, among those who have performed those actions):
   * 1. UH to face-mask ventilation (FMV)-60-seconds
     2. Call for help-within the first 60 seconds
     3. UH to reverse Trendelenburg position-variably used by our participants (from never to immediately)
     4. UH to sedative/hypnotic-Again, not yet performed by attending anesthesiologists. 90-120 seconds by the resident anesthesiologists.
     5. UH to muscle relaxant-sometimes not performed by participants in this scenario, but when it is, within 3-6 minutes of the UH
     6. Muscle relaxation to direct larnygoscopy-30-45 seconds
     7. Attempt at DL to FTLMA placement-some participants have needed prompting to consider this; on average about 2 minutes from the time to attempt DL until a FTLMA is placed, and another 30-120 seconds to place endotracheal tube and remove FTLMA.
     8. UH to diuretics-variably used by our participants (from never to immediately)

**VII. Environment**

1. **Lab Set Up**-the Post Anesthesia Care Unit (PACU). All airway devices and medications are drawn up into syringes and ready for use, but are not in the participants' sight. Mannitol is in a vial (not drawn up into a syringe).
2. **Mannequin Set Up**-mannequin starts with eyes half open, pupils that are normal and reactive, but unresponsive to questions. It then loses consciousness (closes its eyes), and dilates the left pupil. No moulage is used. The simulator has adequate large bore IV access and an arterial line in place. The scenario starts with the patient breathing oxygen via a simple face-mask. Electrocardiogram, pulse oximeter and an arterial blood pressure are displayed on the vital sign monitor.
3. **Props**-Standard PACU set up.
4. **Distracters**-none.

They can also administer medications or assist with patient positioning.

2. **Chief complaint**-*Given to participants*-“Can you see this patient in the PACU, please? This is 42 year-old, previously healthy man who had a craniotomy for tumor. He came out of the operating room about 2-hours ago. **X. Instructor's notes**

1. **Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab and via computer/Tips for actors**-See previous comments.
2. **Scenario programming**
3. **Potential complications paths**-Some participants can wait a long time to ventilate the patient, and allow long periods of apnea. Some have asked for inappropriate medicines during the herniation (epinephrine, for example) and some have treated the hemodynamics with vasodilators (like hydralizine). Occasionally a participant freezes while face-mask ventilating, in which case the "surgeon" appears to tell the participant that the patient has to be intubated and taken back to the operating room. Some have asked for mannitol to be given very early, in which case the PACU nurse leaves for 3 minutes.

**XI. Debriefing Plan**

1. **Questions to facilitate debriefing**:
   1. What signs of uncal herniation can you identify?
   2. What is causing the observed changes?
   3. What is the ultimate therapy for this patient?
   4. What is the most important action you can take to affect this patient's care?
   5. What are the beneficial effects of propofol for this patient?
   6. What would happen to this patient if you tried to perform direct laryngoscopy without muscle relaxation?
   7. What are the controversies, risks or benefits of using succinylcholine in this patient?
   8. How did you manage the patient's blood pressure during the scenario? Why?
   9. What are the benefits of mannitol and furosemide in the patient? What is the time course of these drugs' effects?
