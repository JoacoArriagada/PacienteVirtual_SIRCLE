# Covering All Bases: A longitudinal case based approach to teaching critical care fellows

III. Learning Objectives

A. Primary: Key learning objectives

i. Demonstrate appropriate knowledge, clinical decision-making and management prioritization of the critically ill patient.   
ii. Demonstrate critical thought processes during the implementation of interventions aimed at stabilizing a critically ill patient.   
iii. Demonstrate compassion, professionalism and appropriate communication skills in the course of patient care.   
iv. Apply teamwork principles and shared decision making to the situations encountered.   
v. Demonstrate appreciation for the role of self-reflection, self-directed learning and practice-based improvement during the course of debriefings.

B. Secondary: Technical and behavioral goals, didactic points

i. Appropriate and timely resuscitation of an unstable, critically ill patient, including adequate IV fluid delivery and timely implementation of medications needed to stabilize the patient.   
ii. Correct interpretation of laboratory, clinical and hemodynamic data   
iii. Shared decision making with constant re-appraisal of the situation along with open communication with nursing and other physicians involved in the caring of a critically ill patient.   
iv. Demonstrate an ability to anticipate subsequent complications and accurately communicate them to all involved care givers

G. Session VII – POD #17: Anoxic brain injury and End of Life Discussion

i. Specific Goals and Objectives

a. Identify prognostic indicators in a patient with anoxic brain injury   
b. Recognize the indications for therapeutic hypothermia in a post-cardiac arrest patient   
c. Initiate end of life discussion with patient surrogate

ii. Scenario background – Provided freely to trainees a. The patient suffered a cardiac arrest 3 days ago and is failing to show any meaningful recovery of function. b. There are no other significant events in the interim.

iii. Initial scenario conditions

a. During morning work rounds, the team convenes and agrees given the current state of affairs their patient is not going to make any further neurological recovery. The fellow is asked to meet with the family when they arrive to discuss the situation.

b. Physical examination

1. There is no response to painful stimuli, no corneal reflexes, absent pupillary light reflex and no spontaneous movement or eye opening. There is a weak cough with suctioning.

iv. Scenario branch points

a. The fellow needs to explain to the members of the team what signs on physical exam in combination with the history support the notion of the patient not making a meaningful recovery from his anoxic brain injury. These include the lack of corneal reflex, lack of papillary light reflex and failure to show any meaningful response 72 hours after the arrest.   
b. The patient’s health care proxy arrives to meet with the fellow and is having a difficult time understanding the nature of the situation. The fellow needs to explain the situation in terms a lay person can understand and guide the proxy through the process of working towards setting limits of care, changing the focus to one of comfort and ensuring the patient does not suffer.
