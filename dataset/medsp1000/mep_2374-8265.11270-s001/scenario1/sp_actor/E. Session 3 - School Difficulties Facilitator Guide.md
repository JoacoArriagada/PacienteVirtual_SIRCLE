**School Difficulties: Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorders, Learning Disorders, and Anxiety**

***Facilitator Guide***

**Case 1:**

Jaden is a 7 year-old boy who presents to the clinic with his mother. She explains she’s been getting calls almost every day from his teacher complaining he is not completing his work, is running around the classroom, and “talking too much.” His mother states at home he has a lot of energy, but she doesn’t think he has trouble focusing because he can play on the iPad for hours. She does admit she has to ask him up to 5 times to follow a simple direction and that moves constantly from one activity to another. Lastly, his mother is exhausted and claims it can take Jaden up to 3 hours to fall asleep at night and is restless throughout the night, so no one in the family is getting enough rest.

**A**: 1. How long has he had the symptoms of trouble focusing or increased activity?

2. Did he ever have elevated lead?

3. What is his sleep schedule? History of sleep dysregulation or snoring?

4. Any recent stress at home?

5. Did the mother receive similar reports from his previous teachers?

6. Does the mother believe he understands and is capable of doing the school work?

Screening tool (SNAP or Vanderbilt for mother and teacher)

The main teacher (or teachers if they have multiple types of classes) and the parent

Jaden and his mother return 2 weeks later. At the end of the last visit she explained she’s gotten reports from teachers about his silly and sometimes disruptive behavior since preschool, but she believes in the past they “worked with Jaden.” She also said she knows he is capable of doing the work, because when she sits with him one-on-one and goes through each question of his homework, he answers the questions correctly. She says he has lots of friends but gets very frustrated when things don’t go exactly how he wants when they play. Today, she presents her and the teacher’s SNAP with the following scores:

Teacher SNAP: Inattention = 2.67, Hyper/Impulsive = 1.88, Combined = 2.28

Parent SNAP: Inattention = 1.88, Hyper/Impulsive = 1.67, Combined = 1.78
