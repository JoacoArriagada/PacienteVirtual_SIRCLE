**School Difficulties: Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorders, Learning Disorders, and Anxiety**

***Facilitator Guide***

**Case 3:**

Ms. V brings 10 y/o Maya in for her WCC. Ms V. shares with you that Maya’s grades are dropping and she’s “not focusing.” Maya is in 5th grade in a regular education setting. When you ask Maya about school, she looks to her mother to answer. Her mother explains her teacher is concerned that she is avoiding doing work, frequently complaining of a stomachache and asking to go to the bathroom or the nurse. Her teacher also notes that Maya is taking a long time with her assignments, often erasing and rewriting her answers. At home, Maya avoids starting her HW and when prompted by her parents, she argues with them or “shuts down.” Ms. V says she and Mr. V have started sitting with Maya or reviewing her work when she is done; if they attempt to help her or correct her work, Maya will yell or cry. Ms. V is also concerned because Maya is now saying she isn’t smart. During this conversation, Maya is quiet and looks down at the floor.

**A**: 1. When did you first start noticing these changes?

2. Have there been any changes in Maya’s sleep or appetite?
3. Have there been any other changes at home or in school?

4. Family history: Anxiety, ADHD; family education history

5. Academic functioning: Current grades vs. past grades – is there a decline?

The SCARED for parent and child

SCARED score of greater than 25 may indicate an anxiety disorder

SCARED questionnaire assesses panic disorder, somatic symptoms, generalized anxiety disorder, separation anxiety, social anxiety, and school avoidance

Ms V. reports Maya has generally done well in school, but her grades have declined this year. Ms V. acknowledges Maya is getting more work and has started to talk about the end of the year tests, what middle school she’ll go to, and even what high school she’ll go to. Ms. V goes on to explain how important these tests are and presents as anxious herself. She shares how excited they are that Maya will be the first in their family to go to college. When prompted re: mood, Maya reports she feels “good.” Ms V. denies Maya appearing sad or depressed but acknowledges that she is more irritable and cries more often, noting “she was a happier kid this summer.” Ms. V also states Maya is having difficulty falling asleep and seems very tired in the morning. She has also seen Maya keep to herself more and spend less time with her friends, which the teacher noticed as well. Ms V. can’t think of any other major changes in the last year at home/school. The SCARED is scored a 30.
