Notes for the SP:

-When the physician (the learner) introduces the topic of flu vaccination, the parent should shake their head immediately and say something along the lines of “No, no… we don’t want her to get that,” but not initially offer a reason why.

-If the physician asks why the parent is hesitant about the vaccine, the parent should at first say “Oh, I just don’t think it is right for her… maybe next time we come in we can talk about it.”

-If the physician probes to understand why the parent is hesitant about the vaccine (e.g., asks a second time), the parent can offer the reason that they “heard it actually gives people the flu.”

-Once the physician addresses that concern, the parent should also offer that they “have a 5-month-old infant at home, and won’t it put the baby at risk if Ashley gets the shot?”

-Once the physician addresses that concern, the parent should say, “well I heard the flu shot doesn’t even work this year, so it isn’t worth the risks.”

-If the physician has not already mentioned the potential side effects of the vaccine, the parent should ask “but aren’t there any side effects from getting the flu shot?”

-Once that is addressed, the parent should say “Well, I don’t want her to get the shot when she is so sick, so I guess we can’t do it today anyway. Maybe next time.”

-The physician should address that concern and make a plan going forward, most likely for the family to return to the clinic later to either get the vaccine or discuss it further. If the physician continues to push to get the vaccine today, the parent should say they would like to “think about it more” or “discuss with my spouse” and should not agree to the vaccine yet. This ends the visit.

-If the physician gets confrontational or not empathic enough or does not seem to try to understand the concerns, the parent should become defensive and less interested in discussion.
