This case centers on an 18 month old male, Timmy Baker, who presents with several days of high fever, truncal rash, strawberry tongue, and erythema of the palms and soles. His parent, who is distressed over the fact that his/her son has been sick for 5 days and has been extremely irritable, will accompany him. His visit with his primary care physician 2 days ago provided no answer to why his/her son was sick but started him empirically on Amoxicillin.

X. Actors

a. Roles: patient’s parent (mother or father)

XI. Case Narrative

a. Scenario background given to participants. Mode of arrival is by personal car. In the emergency department room are the patient, parent (mother or father), and a nurse. The nurse will bring the learner into the room, introduce the patient and family, and the chief complaint of “fever.”

b. Learner is expected to obtain missing information from the parent:

i. History of Present Illness: "My son has been terribly sick the past 5 days. He has been having a fever that won't go away and just won't stop crying. I don't know what to do. The pediatrician said this would all go away. He just looks so uncomfortable." When asked about disease progression - "Well it first started with the fever. And then I noticed the rash on his chest and back two days ago. And most recently I noticed his lips began cracking and his tongue turned bright red. He looks so uncomfortable and won't stop crying."

ii. Past Medical History: "He's an extremely healthy child. Full-term, has received all his immunizations. No reported allergies. We have been giving him Tylenol and ibuprofen for the fever."

iii. Social and Family History: Lives with mother and father, no siblings, no smokers at home, no sick contacts
