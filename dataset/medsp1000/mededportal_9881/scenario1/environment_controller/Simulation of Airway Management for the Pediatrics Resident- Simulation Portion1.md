**Simulation Portion**

**Introduction**: The conference begins with a simulation activity. The residents will be expected to participate as if the simulation is in real time. They have access to any supplies that they would in the patient care areas. Review what your simulation mannequin is capable of (breathing, heart sounds, pulses, vascular access points etc.) Allow the interns to have access to the equipment they would have in the patient care areas and give them time to familiarize themselves with the equipment. Review with them the concept of team roles during a code situation, choosing a code leader and assigning specific roles to each participant. Choose an intern to be the initial responder. During the case, the person running the simulation should not interact with the residents except to answer prompts (eg state laboratory results) and should only give information as it is requested other than the initial scenario, and they should be reminded of this prior to starting the case. All participants should be standing around the sim mannequin and not participating from their seats.

**Simulator set up**: HR 170, RR 30, Sat 90%, temp 99.5 with patient having active tonic clonic seizure

**Case Scenario**:

A 21 month old baby boy was admitted to the hospital for dehydration in the setting of an illness with vomiting and diarrhea. The mother runs into the hallway and yells “help, something is wrong with my baby!” As you are the only person standing in the hallway you decide to see if you can help. You walk in and see the baby’s arms and legs jerking in a rhythmic fashion. The mom says he was totally fine then he just started making these odd movements

**HPI:**

This is a 21 month old boy with no past medical history who has been having vomiting and diarrhea for the past 3 days. He hasn’t been able to take anything PO other than water. His parents brought him in to the hospital because he hadn’t made a wet diaper in 1 day. He has no past medical history, he has no allergies, he was born full term, has all of his vaccines and has no allergies. In the ED he received 2 IVF boluses.

**Physical Exam:**

Vital Signs: HR 170 RR 30 O2 90% on room air but with a poor tracing, Temp 100.6F

General: well appearing, having a tonic clonic seizure

HEENT: unable to assess

CV: tachycardia, regular rhythm, no murmur

Pulm: Clear lung fields bilaterally

Abdomen: Soft, NT/ND

Ext: No C/C/E, palpable pulses all extremities

Neuro: Generalized tonic clonic seizure

**Case Progression:**

* If they do not administer oxygen the new vitals will be: HR 170 RR 40 O2 75% on room air but with a poor tracing, Temp 100.6F
* If oxygen is given appropriately (ie: correct method and set up) the new vitals will be: HR 170 RR 30 O2 97% on room air but with a poor tracing, Temp 100.6F but the seizure will persist.

**Next Steps:**

* The team will likely get lab work at this point to evaluate the cause of the seizure but no labs will result within the next 5-10 minutes.
* Appropriate lab tests would be a chemistry to evaluate for electrolyte abnormalities, a fingerstick glucose level, CBC to evaluate for infectious etiology and drug levels if the patient is taking any seizure medications. None of these lab results are available within the timeframe of the case.
* There is no role for imaging at this point in the case.

**Case Progression**

* At the 5-10 minute mark, the interns should give a dose of Ativan. This will not break the seizure and the repeat vitals are HR 160 RR 20 O2 sat 88% on room air or 96% with O2 support.
* After the first dose of Ativan failed to break the seizure, 3-5 minutes later a second dose of Ativan should be given. After the second dose of Ativan the patient becomes apneic but the seizure stops. The repeat vitals are HR 120 RR 0 O2 sat 70%, which quickly falls but the patient has 2+ pulses and a normal cardiac exam.

**End points for this case**:

1. Instructor can set a time limit. We recommend 10 minutes allowing time for a debriefing after the case. However, it is important to get the interns to the point where the patient becomes apneic so they can demonstrate their airway management skills.
2. Alternatively, instructor can stop the case if the students successfully manage and stabilize the child with appropriate BVM technique.
3. If the students management does not meet the critical points, remind students that a child can be supported by bag mask ventilation for a long time while awaiting further support from the PICU team and stop the case after the students have demonstrated proper bag mask ventilation which will increase oxygen saturation
