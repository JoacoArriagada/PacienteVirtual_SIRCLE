**Simulation Portion**

**Introduction**: After the presentation is finished, there is a second simulation activity. If the group is being split in to two, they should each do the session they did not previously do. If the groups are not being split, another group of residents should do the second case to review the management that has just been discussed. The residents will be expected to participate as if the simulation is in real time. They have access to any supplies that they would in the patient care areas. Review what your simulation mannequin is capable of (breathing, heart sounds, pulses, vascular access points etc.) Allow the interns to have access to the equipment they would have in the patient care areas and give them time to familiarize themselves with the equipment. Review with them the concept of team roles during a code situation, choosing a code leader and assigning specific roles to each participant. Choose an intern to be the initial responder. During the case, the person running the simulation should not interact with the residents except to answer prompts (eg state laboratory results) and should only give information as it is requested other than the initial scenario, and they should be reminded of this prior to starting the case. All participants should be standing around the sim mannequin and not participating from their seats.

**Simulator set up**: HR 170, RR 30, Sat 88%, Temp 101, patient having stridor

**Case Scenario**:

A 6 month old baby girl was admitted to the hospital yesterday because of a cough and poor PO intake. The mom comes to find you to tell you that she is looking worse and has started to make a strange noise every time she breathes in that she has never heard before.

**HPI:**

This is a 6 month old girl with no past medical history who has been having a cough and poor PO intake for the past 3 days. Her parents brought her to the hospital because they were concerned that she could have pneumonia because of all the coughing. Her chest x-ray was normal but she was admitted for monitoring and IVF hydration. She has never been sick before, she was born full term and she received her 2 and 4 month vaccines but has still not received her 6 month vaccines.

**Physical Exam:**

Vital Signs: HR 170 RR 30 O2 88% on room air.

General: She is well appearing and in no distress.

HEENT: normal, OP clear

CV: tachycardia, regular rhythm, no murmur

Pulm: Diffuse rhonchi without crackles or wheezing, subcostal retractions, without grunting or flaring and is having stridor at rest.

Abdomen: Soft, NT/ND

Ext: No C/C/E, palpable pulses all extremities

Neuro: Alert, interactive

**Case Progression:**

* If oxygen is given via nasal cannula, the repeat vitals are: HR 170 RR 30 O2 90% but the stridor remains, even at rest
* If nebulized racemic epinephrine is given, the child improves significantly with vitals of HR 170 RR 20 O2 96% and stridor that is only audible during times of distress.
* If decadron is given, improvement will not be seen for approximately 2 hours and will not affect the initial management of this case.
  + The appropriate manner to give the steroids would be IV or IM if the patient is critically ill as they will work faster and there is less of a chance of not absorbing or vomiting.
* If steroids and racemic epinephrine are given together, the child will stabilize with vital signs of HR 170 RR 20 O2 96%
* If oxygen support, steroids and racemic epinephrine are not given, the patient will decompensate, initially with vitals: HR 180 RR 70 O2 sat 70% and then eventually become apneic HR 150 RR 4 O2 30%. At this point the only appropriate intervention would be BVM ventilation while preparing for intubation.
* If albuterol is given with oxygen, this will likely improve the oxygen saturation and decrease the respiratory rate, but this is a temporary intervention and will only help while the nebulizer is running. When the treatment finishes the patient’s symptoms will return.

**Next Steps:**

* The patient should be maintained on monitoring
* Inappropriate actions at this point would be a repeat chest xray or lateral neck xray, or any lab work, as they would both delay acute care and initial management.
* If the patient were not to respond to racemic epinephrine and steroids, they would be appropriate steps, looking for a foreign body or epiglottitis.

**Case Progression**

* In approximately two hours, her vitals are HR 150 RR 70, O2 sat 70%. She is again retracting and her stridor has returned.
* Appropriate actions at this time would be to give O2 via facemask or non rebreather and to give another dose of racemic epinephrine.
* Despite interventions, her vital signs become HR 120 RR 0 O2 sat 60%.

**End points for this case**:

1. Instructor can set a time limit. We recommend 10 minutes allowing time for a debriefing after the case. However, it is important to get the interns to the point where the patient becomes apneic so they can demonstrate their airway management skills.
2. Alternatively, instructor can stop the case if the students successfully manage and stabilize the child with appropriate BVM technique.
3. If the students management does not meet the critical points, remind students that a child can be supported by bag mask ventilation for a long time while awaiting further support from the PICU team and stop the case after the students have demonstrated proper bag mask ventilation which will increase oxygen saturation
