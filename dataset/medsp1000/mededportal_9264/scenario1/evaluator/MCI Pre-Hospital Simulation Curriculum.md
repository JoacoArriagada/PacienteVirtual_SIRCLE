# Simulation Interest Group Scenario Template

Title: Mass Casualty Triage and Tagging Scenario in the Pre-hospital Setting

II. Target Audience: Medical students, nurses, paramedics, or residents

III. Learning Objectives & Assessment Objectives

A) Learning Objectives

By the end of this course learners should be able to:

1. State the definition of a mass casualty and general response to a mass casualty/disaster.

2. Understand the basic incident command structure and out-of-hospital response system during a multiple/mass casualty event

3. Know techniques to reassess patients in a mass casualty incident using the START triage tool

4. Practice and be familiar with the Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment (START) tool in assessing multiple patients during a mass casualty using the color tagging system (immediate, delayed, minimal, expectant).

5. Use proper communication techniques during a mass casualty simulated event in communicating to victims, other providers, incident command.

B) Assessed Objectives & Critical Actions Checklist

Tagging patients according to level of severity:

Black (Expectant) Patient who are already dead, or little chance of survival, treat salvageable patient before these patients

Examples: Obvious death, non-survivable injuries such as major open brain injury, respiratory arrest, cardiac arrest

Red (Immediate) which entails life threatening injuries with immediate intervention and transport

Examples: Airway&Breathing difficulties, Uncontrolled or severe bleeding, severe medical problems, signs of shock (hypoperfusion), severe burns

Yellow (Delayed) which entails non-life threatening injuries, transport can be temporarily delayed

Examples: Major or multiple bone injuries, Back Injuries with or without spinal cord injuries, Burns without airway compromise.

Green: (Walking Wounded) which entails minor injuries requiring minimal or no treatment, can be last to be transported or self transported

# Examples: minor soft tissue injuries, minor bone injuries

VI. Case Narrative (describes what the learner will experience)

The Scenario: You are students doing a ride along with EMS (Emergency Medical Services) when the rig you are in is the first to respond to a 7 car pileup on 81. You are instructed to assist the EMS personnel in the initial assessment of the victims.

The Victims:

In CAR #1 (two people):   
Actor #1: Forehead Gash with bleeding from site (driver)   
a. Is confused   
b. Vitals: HR: 115; BP: radial; RR:20   
c. Has a seizure partway through the scenario   
d. Should be tagged as “Yellow”   
Actor #2: Broken clavicle (and visual ecchymosis) from seat belt (passenger)   
a. In pain but is more concerned about the driver’s forehead gash   
b. Vitals: HR: 110; BP: radial; RR: 25   
c. Begins screaming when victim 1 has a seizure   
d. Should be tagged as “Green”

In CAR #2 (three people):   
Actor #3: Unconscious (driver)   
a. Slumped over the wheel. Eyes open.   
b. Vitals: HR: not palpable; BP: not palpable (even carotid); RR: not breathing   
c. Should be tagged as “Black”   
Actor #4: Ejected passenger (from front seat)   
a. Lying on stomach half inside and half on the hood of the car. Does not interact, answer questions, or follows commands. Stares blankly and exhales “irregularly”.   
b. Vitals: HR: very irregular; BP: only carotid; RR: 30ish (irregular)   
c. Eventually stops breathing   
d. Should be tagged as “Black”

Actor #5: “The Distractor”

a. Seems fine (a few scrapes). But physically is dragging a student to take care of Actor #4   
b. Won’t let the student examine him/her   
c. Can be persuaded to stand by Actor #4 and hold his/her hand (but it takes a lot of persuasion). When victim Actor #4 stops breathing, starts yelling and physically drags the nearest student to help.   
d. Should be tagged “Green”

In Car #3 (single person):   
Actor #6: Posterior Hip Dislocation (driver)   
a. Sitting in driver’s seat with knee pushed against dashboard, complaining about right sided hip pain.   
b. Vitals: HR 120; BP: radial; RR: 20   
c. Is pinned and cannot move. Is concerned but not hysterical or aggressive.   
d. Should be tagged as “Yellow”

In Car #4 (single person):   
Actor #7: Wanderer   
a. Actor is found wondering around, confused, and will not communicate verbally.   
b. Vitals: HR: 100; BP: radial; RR: 15   
c. Can be convinced to stay put, but after a few minutes, looks around, gets up, and wanders around.   
d. Should be tagged as “Green”

In Car #5 (single person):

Actor #8: Traumatic Amputation of Arm   
a. Actor is found holding their arm in their car. In state of emotional shock.   
b. Vitals: HR: 120; BP: Carotid; RR: 20   
c. Is “out of it” at first, but eventually begins to yell “help” softly, then progressively louder until screaming “OMG MY ARM!!!”   
d. Should be tagged “Red”

In Car #6 (single person):   
Actor #9: Whiplash   
a. Complains of neck pain   
b. Vitals: HR 130; BP: radial; RR: 20   
c. Not hysterical. Wants rescuers to help the other victims.   
d. Should be tagged “Green”

In Car #7 (single person):   
Actor #10: Bowel Evisceration   
a. Complains of belly pain. Upset and protective of abdomen.   
b. Vitals: HR 160; BP: carotid; RR: 25 and shallow   
c. Covers abdomen with hands until persuaded to allow student to look. Student sees a bowel evisceration.   
d. Should be tagged “Red”

Once the students complete initial assessment, they are to re-assess. When 8 minutes have gone by, the doctors stop the scenario and become the second ambulance to arrive and the students must direct the incoming ambulance team to the RED tagged patients. Scenario ends when students are finished directing the ambulance crew to the RED tagged patients.

VII. Instructors Notes (what the instructor must do to create the experience)

A) Tips to keep scenario flowing in lab

- Learners/Students who will be performing triage/scene evaluation will walk into the room and the scenario will begin immediately.
- Resident/Faculty Physician can act as guides to help students/paramedics who are doing the triage in providing vital signs, physical findings, etc.

B) Tips to direct actors

- Important to emphasize to actors to create the ‘chaotic’ environment these students/providers will be walking into as in a true mass casualty/trauma situation.
- Often when preparing a victim to participate in a simulation it is the “acting” or reality part that seems to be overlooked. Moulage is helpful in creating the state of shock, but if the actors do not act accordingly, the time invested in the moulage is not helpful. The best advice is to coach the Actors on their expected responses during the preparation period. Care should be made that each victim understands the injury/wound, and back story on how it was sustained. This will insure that the victims act like what the moulage looks like.

Specific Actor Tips (see scenario above):

# Car #1

Actor #1: You are confused after having a major head injury with internal bleeding. You will not be speaking very much, but instead “moaning” and “groaning” in pain. On initial evaluation, you will not be answering questions appropriately, but speaking some unintelligible words (examples: “momma” “ouchy”). Sometime after initial assessment when not being attended by rescuers, you will start having a convulsive seizure (ie, drop to the ground and start shaking entire body). After the seizure you will be unresponsive but still breathing.

Actor #2: You will be in obvious pain from your broken clavicle but otherwise acting appropriately. After Actor #1 above has his seizure, you will become distraught and a ‘distraction’ in the scenario (eg screaming in fear, asking for help).

# Car #2

Actor #3: You will be acting ‘dead.’ No movement, no sound, no vital signs.

Actor #4: You are having agonal, shallow breaths (your last few breaths), making a few unintelligible sounds, but eventually stop breathing and are ‘dead’.

Actor #5: You are acting as a distracter. You are fine physically, but extremely distressed emotionally over your friends (Actor #3 & #4) who are not responding. Your role is to try to distract the rescuers to pay attention to your friends (#3 & #4).

Car #3

Actor #6: You are sitting in the driver seat, calm, but in pain from your hip dislocation.

Car #4

Actor #7: You are wandering the scene, in shock and disbelief, with PTSD-like stare. You are mostly calm, but in a state of shock.

Car #5

Actor #8: You have an amputated arm with bleeding that needs to be controlled by rescuers. You are “out of it” at first, but eventually begins to yell “help” softly, then progressively louder until screaming “OMG MY ARM!!!”

Car #6

Actor #9: You have a minor whiplash injury of your neck, but otherwise in no distress.

Car #7
