**"Session 1, Part A"**

Tiffany is a medical fellow working in adolescent evening clinic.  Next up on her schedule is Bryan, a 15 year old male booked for a new patient physical.  While waiting for him to check in she opens the electronic health record and finds that he was discharged from the inpatient ward last week.  Previous to this admission it does not appear he was ever at Children’s.  Review of the notes reveals he presented to the Children’s emergency room with nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and a recent history of weight loss.  He was found to be in diabetic ketoacidosis and was admitted to the inpatient endocrine service where he was started on two types of insulin – long-acting lantus and short-acting novolog per sliding scale adjusted to his carbohydrate intake and blood sugar numbers.  He did meet with the inpatient diabetes nurse educator twice.  The discharge summary includes some information on his insulin dosing, the appointment with Tiffany today, and an appointment with endocrinology in one month.   There are no other outpatient notes in the system.

**"Session 1, Part B"**

Tiffany calls Bryan’s name in the waiting room.   A woman who appears to be his mother jumps up and a young man with his eyes cast down at the floor shuffles after her.  Once in the exam room, Bryan’s mother tells Tiffany the two of them just moved to Boston a few months ago from Georgia to be near her family after she and Bryan’s father separated.  Bryan has an older brother in college in Georgia.  She tells Tiffany that Bryan is enrolled in the Boston Public Schools and that he isn’t doing nearly as well as he did at his old school.  “And now with this diabetes on top of everything else – I don’t know what I am going to do with him.  I have to stay on top of him to take these injections, and it is so confusing trying to figure out how much to take when – there’s a lot of math involved!”  When Tiffany asks her to leave the room so she can speak to Bryan alone, she seems surprised.  “His old doctor in Georgia never did that – why do you want to do that?”  Tiffany explains the adolescent confidentiality policy and mom seems satisfied and goes to the waiting room to work on getting immunization records and growth charts from his prior pediatrician faxed to the office.

**"Session 1, Part C"**

Tiffany asks Bryan what he understands about his new diagnosis and he surprises her by giving a pretty accurate summary of Type 1 diabetes – how insulin works, why he needs to take certain amounts at certain times, the risks of taking too much or not enough.  “I don’t know why my mom thinks the medicine is so hard – I’m good at math so I guess the numbers just make sense.”  However, when she reviews Bryan’s recent blood sugars from the meter his mother brought with them she notices that most of them are still too high.  When she asks him about this, Bryan admits that he just doesn’t like taking the insulin.  “It’s not the needles – I don’t really care.  I just don’t want to deal with it, and I don’t want my mom on me all the time about it.  I don’t even want to be here – I want to go back to Georgia to live with my dad.”  When they talk further she learns he hasn’t really made any friends here and that he moved too late to try out for the basketball team, which was his major activity at his old school.  Now he mostly comes home and plays videos games in his room after school.  He often skips breakfast and when he does eat, it is usually something he picks up at a fast food restaurant or something he microwaves from the freezer.  He doesn’t really feel like talking to anyone or doing homework.  He thinks there are counseling and nursing services at his school but he has never seen anyone in those offices.
