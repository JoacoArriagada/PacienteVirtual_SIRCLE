**"Session 2, Part A"**

Evelyn Smith is a nurse practitioner working in evening clinic in student health services at Georgia State University.  She is about to see an 18 year old freshman named Bryan.  The encounter form lists “skin infection – possible abscess” as the reason for the visit.  She pulls up his chart while waiting for him to check in and sees that he is enrolled in the health center’s chronic disease registry because he has Type 1 diabetes.  She clicks on the health summary that his primary medical home submitted when he enrolled last fall and begins reading.

“Wow,” Evelyn thinks to herself.  “After being diagnosed at age 15, his diabetes control was initially poor with a HbA1c of 11%.  However, after regular visits with the clinic’s mental health team to address his situational depression and regular visits with the clinic nutritionist to help him establish a regular eating schedule, his HbA1c improved to 7.5% and stayed between 7-8% until he graduated high school.  That’s a pretty remarkable transformation.”  Evelyn notes this is Bryan’s first visit to the health center and there is no HbA1c recorded here.

Evelyn sees Bryan and quickly diagnoses a boil with surrounding cellulitis on his leg.  She prescribes the appropriate antibiotic and sends him to the lab to check his HbA1c.  They chat a little bit about the year and he seems well adjusted and happy.  The next day she is dismayed when his HbA1c comes back at 12%.  She calls his cell but he doesn’t answer.

**Discussion topics for Part A**

- Components of pre-college physical
- School resources for youth with Special Health Care Needs (SHCN) (disability office, etc.)
- Transition registries

**Potential probe questions for Part A**

- What aspects of health and medical care are appropriate for an 18 year old to understand and manage?
- What are the components of the pre-college visit and how do they differ for youth with SHCN?   What should be in a summary letter?  What is a disease registry?
- For college-bound youth, what unique risks exist that youth should be prepared to encounter?  What about for youth with diabetes?
- Thinking developmentally, what contexts and milieus are relevant to Bryan now (college, fraternity) and what are the key self-care and self-management tasks that are relevant to transition readiness assessment in college?

**"Session 2, Part B"**

“Ugh,” Bryan says, hitting the decline button on his phone when he sees the caller ID for the health service. “Who was that?” asks his buddy as he passes him a beer.  “Nobody,” Bryan says and tosses his phone aside.

Bryan has received all of Evelyn Smith’s messages and knows why she is calling.  He suspected his HbA1c was going to be bad – he almost didn’t go to the lab after he got the prescription for the antibiotic.  He had seen the high numbers on his glucose meter and had just stopped using it because they were stressing him out.  He knew it was probably related to all the pizza and fries he was eating in the dining hall but that was all they served on the late-night menu and he was often there studying pretty late during the week.  Between his heavy course load during the week and rushing a fraternity on the weekend, he was too busy and stressed to deal with his blood sugar.  Plus, he didn’t like anyone seeing him with his meter – only his roommate knew about his diabetes, and that was only because he had to keep the insulin in their mini-fridge.  Now he just took his blood sugar whenever he felt really bad and took his insulin whenever he remembered and was alone in his room.

**Discussion topics for Part B**

- Substance use and chronic illness (assessment issues, special risks in youth with special health care needs)

**Potential probe questions for Part B**

- What concerns would you have about Bryan as a medical provider (i.e., physician or nurse practitioner), a mental health provider (i.e., social worker or psychologist), and a nutritionist?
- Thinking developmentally, what contexts and milieus are relevant to Bryan now (college, fraternity) and what are the key self-care and self-management tasks that are relevant to transition readiness assessment in college?
- Do the risk factors for or prevalence of mental illness or substance abuse differ in youth with SHCN?

**"Session 2, Part C"**

Evelyn Smith makes her fourth call to Bryan and gets his voicemail again.  She is growing increasingly worried about him.  She thinks back to his history of depression and wonders if she missed subtle signs of depression or substance abuse that might be contributing to his poor diabetes control.  She looks at his health summary and sees the number for his mental health provider and his PCP in Boston on the top but realizes she does not have a release of information signed by Bryan to call them.  She checks and sees there is also no release for her to speak to a parent or guardian.   She decides to call his dorm RA Rob and ask him to convince Bryan to come back to the health center.

**Discussion topics for Part C**

- HIPPA and FERPA
- Emergency psychiatric evaluations for adults, including “pink paper”

**Potential probe questions for Part C**

- Can the NP call Bryan’s prior providers or parents without his permission?  What about his RA?
- What if Bryan had alcohol poisoning or was suicidal - what emergency psychiatric resources are available for patients 18 years of age and older?  Do the requirements for psychiatric and substance abuse hospitalization change with the age of majority? Insurance?
- Thinking developmentally, what contexts and milieus are relevant to Bryan now (college, fraternity) and what are the key self-care and self-management tasks that are relevant to transition readiness assessment in college?

**"Session 2, Part D"**

A week later, Bryan shows up to the health center.  “You know at first I was really mad you told my RA about my diabetes – it’s none of his business.  But then he told me he already knew through the disability office or something.  Anyway, I’m glad he did know because last night I passed out after drinking with some of the guys on my hall.  Thankfully Rob thought to call 911 based on some training he had about college students with health problems.  Turns out I had a really bad low blood sugar and they had to give me glucagon and everything. “

Evelyn and Bryan agree he should see an adult endocrinologist in town to talk about getting his diabetes back under control.  He’s 20 minutes late for his first visit and the doctor won’t see him - which surprises him because his old doctor would always see him whenever he showed up.  The front desk put him on the cancellation call list and he is able to see the doctor within a few days, after which he starts seeing him regularly.  After cutting back on the beer and certain foods and taking his insulin more regularly, he gets his HbA1c back down to 9%.  Due to how erratic his schedule is, Bryan’s endocrinologist suggests he use an insulin pump to deliver insulin instead.  Once he starts using the pump and joins a social media group just for young adults with Type I Diabetes, Bryan is able to get his HbA1c down to 7.5%.

**Discussion topics for Part D**

- Use of technology to assist with adherence
- Research on transition outcomes

**Potential probe questions for Part D**

- How might technology help us address issues of medication nonadherence, visit noncompliance, and sharing of information between multiple health care providers?
- How might we study transition outcomes?
