**"Session 3, Part C"**

After receiving multiple letters and phone calls from the clinic, Bryan shows up to see you at the Boston Children’s Hospital Adolescent and Young Adult Clinic.  He is now 23 years old and has just moved back to Boston to work for the Celtics managing corporate ticket sales, although he still travels to Georgia frequently because his girlfriend lives there.  He loves his job because he gets to stay connected to sports, watch all the games, and eat at the arena.  He denies any symptoms of depression or suicidal thoughts, but does smoke marijuana when he feels “stressed” and notes he often forgets to take his insulin if he is high.   Otherwise he generally takes his insulin.  “My girlfriend really bugs me about it, but for some reason I don’t mind the way I did when my mom did.”

He saw an endocrinologist 6 months ago but never got a reminder call for a follow-up appointment and so didn’t go back.  He doesn’t remember the last time he saw a nutritionist and has never seen a counselor regularly.  Review of the chart notes the last two months refills for insulin were prescribed for him by the doctor of the day after he called the nursing line and stated he has no other way to get his insulin.  His last HbA1c was 10%.  When you approach him about finding an adult primary care provider and endocrinologist he says “Can’t I just keep coming here?  I really like you.”
