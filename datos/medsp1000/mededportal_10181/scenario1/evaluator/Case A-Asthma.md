1. **Title: Acute Asthma Exacerbation**
2. **Target Audience: Medical Students.**
3. **Learning Objectives:**
   1. Primary:
      1. Clinically diagnose and manage an acute asthma exacerbation.
      2. Recognize, diagnose and treat Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT).
      3. Use peer education to facilitate long-term learning and encourage life-long learning.
   2. Secondary:
      1. For Student-Instructor/Peer Educator:
         1. Present a succinct case in which your peers identify the above conditions and the treatment strategies for each scenario.
         2. Guide peers through the evaluation and management of an acutely ill patient.
         3. Provide a debriefing discussion for your colleagues to highlight salient questions, physical examination findings, laboratory/imaging studies, current management and therapies.
      2. For the Learners:
         1. Establish differential diagnoses for dyspnea.
         2. Perform a directed history and physical for dyspnea.
         3. Recognize and interpret appropriate tests/studies for asthmatic patient.
         4. Implement correct treatment of acute asthma exacerbation.
         5. Recognize and treat SVT according to ACLS algorithms
         6. Order appropriate testing.
         7. Call appropriate consultant if needed.
   3. **Critical Actions Checklist:**

**ASTHMA**

* + - Assess ABCs.
    - Request IV, O2, and monitor including pulse oximetry.
    - Ask appropriate questions (directed history-taking):
      * History of asthma.
      * Onset, provoking/alleviating factors, severity, time course.
      * History of recent steroid and inhaler use.
      * Previous hospitalization/intubation history.
      * Peak flow and triggers.
    - Perform physical examination – ask for specific physical exam findings: use of accessory muscles/nasal flaring/retractions, end-expiratory wheezing, hypoxia and cyanosis.
    - Start appropriate treatment: O2 via non-rebreather mask, Albuterol/Atrovent nebs, steroids, (may request Xopenex, magnesium, Heliox). Patient improves somewhat with treatment.

**SUPRAVENTRICULAR TACHYCARDIA**

* Reassess ABCs.
* Ask for EKG –student to verbally interpret.
* Assess for: stable vs. unstable.
* May give IV Fluids, try vagal maneuvers, adenosine (ask student for appropriate dose of 6mg, 12mg, 12mg).
* If student interprets the EKG and gives the appropriate medications, patient goes back into NSR. If not, patient continues to decompensate. At that point, can end the session prior to terminal decompensation (i.e. prior to the ”death” of simulated patient).

1. **Environment:**
   1. Lab Set Up: Emergency Department, simulation room, or trauma bay.
   2. Manikin Set-Up: Supine or sitting-up, patient with peripheral IV, nebulizer with albuterol/atrovent, prednisone both iv and oral forms, adenosine IV.
   3. Props: Xray image (provided in back of packet if ordered), initial EKG (sinus tachycardia), SVT EKG, code cart, rhythm generator or monitor set initially for sinus tachycardia, but need ability to change rhythm to SVT.
   4. Distractors: None.
2. **Actors:**
   1. Roles: Instructor – preferably medical student-instructor but can be attending physician, Senior or Junior Residents
   2. Participants: Medical Students
3. **Case Narrative:**
   1. Information from triage, to be provided at start of encounter.
      1. Chief Complaint: Shortness of breath.
      2. Demographics: LR is a 34-year-old female.
      3. Vitals: BP 148/85 HR 102 RR 26 SaO2 84% on RA Temp 97.9
   2. Scenario conditions initially (give only what is asked for):
      1. Breathing problems started 2 days ago initially with runny nose and cough. Now feeling more short of breath. Has been using her albuterol inhaler every hour without relief.
      2. Has not been on steroids for 2 years.
      3. Has never been intubated.
      4. Hospitalized multiple times as a child, but never as an adult and never in the PICU.
      5. Does not know her normal peak flow.
      6. Triggers include cats, change in seasons, dust, etc.
      7. Past Medical: Seasonal allergies, asthma, hypertension; no surgeries.
      8. Meds: Albuterol, Singulair, Hydrochlorothiazide.
      9. Allergies: Shellfish.
      10. Social Hx: 1ppd tobacco abuser, occasional alcohol and marijuana use.
      11. Family Hx: unknown, patient was adopted.
      12. Initial physical exam:
          1. Vitals: BP 148/85 HR 102 RR 26 SaO2 84% on RA Temp 97.9
          2. General: Patient appears to be in moderate respiratory distress. +Accessory muscle use, nasal flaring. Speaking in 3-word sentences.
          3. Cardiac: Tachycardic, no murmurs, rubs or gallops.
          4. Pulmonary: Diffuse end-expiratory wheezing, decreased air entry bilaterally.
          5. Extremities: No calf swelling or tenderness, no swelling.
          6. Skin: Evidence of perioral cyanosis and acral cyanosis.
          7. If students ask for anything else not pertinent, report findings as “normal.”
   3. **Scenario branch points:**
      1. See “Critical Actions Checklist for Asthma” (page 2):
         1. If patient is appropriately treated she improves initially.
         2. If patient is NOT appropriately treated she continues to decompensate with worsening shortness of breath and hypoxia.
      2. Regardless of treatment, after 5 minutes patient develops SVT:
         1. Change rhythm generator to SVT
         2. Patient reports: heart racing and shortness of breath:
         3. Vitals: BP 105/65 HR 184 RR 26 SaO2 84% on RA or 95% on 2L oxygen Temp 97.9
         4. Physical exam: Awake and alert, complaining of heart racing, respiratory: scattered wheezing,-improved from prior, Cardiac: tachycardic, Abdomen: soft
      3. Follow “Critical Actions Checklist for SVT” on page 2 regarding diagnosis and treatment
4. **Instructor’s Notes:**
   1. Tips to Keep Scenario Flowing and Direct Actors:
      1. Provide the patient’s chief complaint to the learners on arrival to your station. Try to only give information that would be provided from triage.
      2. Give history, demographic, and clinical examination findings only as asked, this is provided in a case narrative. Use the outline to guide your peers through the appropriate actions.
      3. Elicit differential diagnoses prior to providing imaging. Laboratory results will not be available-scenario will be completed prior to labs resulting.
      4. Encourage participation by prompting learners if necessary, but do not direct them (i.e. ask “Is there anything else you would like to add/additional tests you would like to order?”).
      5. Remind peers of the importance of re-evaluating patient, otherwise will not recognize SVT as it develops. The main focus of the case is reacting to the change in the patient condition.
      6. Follow along and make sure the critical actions checklist points have been addressed. If not addressed during simulation, address them during the debriefing.
   2. Scenario Programming:
      1. Optimal treatment path:
         1. Asthma exacerbation
            1. Recognize asthma exacerbation and order 3 nebulizer treatments of albuterol and atrovent.
            2. Order steroids (preferably oral).
            3. Repeatedly re-evaluate for response to treatment.
         2. Recognize change in rhythm to SVT.

i. Recognize stable SVT and attempt vagal maneuvers, but move to Adenosine when patient does not improve.

* + 1. Potential error paths:
       1. Does not recognize asthma or administers incorrect treatment, patient will continue to get worse
       2. Does not recognize SVT, patient will eventually become hypotense, more short of breath and have altered mental status (due to hypoperfusion).

2. Method of debriefing: Team leader-led, group style, with support items and references.
   1. This discussion should be limited to 5 minutes in total and supported through reading of materials (articles, reference books, etc).
   2. Highlight salient questions, physical examination findings, laboratory/imaging studies, and current management/therapies.
3. Actual debriefing materials:
   1. Treatment of SVT (list of possible sources provided below under References)
   2. Materials team leader finds in preparation for case.
   3. Example of sinus tachycardia included for comparison EKG #2
4. Rules for debriefing: Establish at onset of debrief by group.
   1. Establish goals

What was done well?

What could be done better/differently?

Questions to facilitate debriefing:

1. How do you think it went?
2. What are the goals of treatment for asthma?
3. How can you monitor improvement?
4. What is the initial treatment for asthma exacerbation?
5. What are the indications for steroid use?
6. How do you differentiate stable versus unstable SVT?
7. What different maneuvers can you try for stable SVT, unstable SVT?
